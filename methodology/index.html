Methodology

How we research, score, and report

A plain explanation of how Tia N. List works. The process is deterministic where it can be, analyst-written where it needs judgment, and transparent about both.

How we research

Continuous ingestion. Every hour we pull the Cyber category from our curated Miniflux feed list plus the r/cybersecurity hot feed. Each article is ingested exactly once (a watermark tracks what we have already seen), and non-English articles are detected and translated.

Stories, not articles. New articles are merged into stories by matching their URL, the CVE they name, or their title against what we already cover. A story is one incident: the original reporting, then updates from other outlets as they land.

The timeline is the story. The original article appears in full. Each update shows only what is new — sentences that add information the story did not already have. Reports that merely restate known facts collapse to a bare mention (source, date, link).

Analysis where it matters. Stories that go hot get an analyst write-up: what the story is, why it matters, what to watch. The daily brief at 07:15 EDT summarizes the day's landscape — the only general web search in the system is its CISA-KEV gap check, once a day, so dedup stays tractable.

How we score

Six factors, one formula. Every story is scored 0–6 from explicit factors, and the story page shows the breakdown (open "Why this score"):

Breadth (0–3): how many distinct outlets cover the story. Authority (0–2): the weight of the best outlet covering it. Severity (0–3): the real CVSS base score of the CVE(s) involved, plus KEV/active-exploitation status and content signals (zero-day, ransomware, OT/ICS, APT, breach scale). Velocity (0–2): updates in the last 48 hours. Pickup speed (0–0.8): how quickly the second source arrived after the first. Recency: a temporal multiplier that decays with a 36-hour half-life. Community (+0–1.5): reddit presence.

The formula: score = (breadth + authority + severity + velocity + pickup) × recency + community, capped at 6.0. Bands: 0–1.9 new, 2.0–2.9 warm, 3.0–4.4 hot, 4.5+ hottest. The hot ranking re-computes every hour, so a story cools off naturally as it ages.

How we report

Three feeds. Every article (the raw event stream), hot stories (score ≥ 2.0), and the daily brief (one item per digest page).

The daily brief at 07:15 EDT: an emoji headline, a one-to-two-sentence executive summary, the analyst's landscape analysis, and the stories it covers — each linking to its full timeline.

Corrections and limits. Story clustering is deterministic and can over- or under-merge at the edges; scores are a ranking signal, not a verdict. When we change methodology, this page changes; the archive never does.
