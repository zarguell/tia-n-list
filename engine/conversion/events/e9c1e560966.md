Palo Alto Networks has disclosed a critical buffer overflow vulnerability in the PAN-OS User-ID Authentication Portal (Captive Portal) that is being actively exploited in attacks. Tracked as CVE-2026-0300 with a CVSS 4.0 score of 9.3, the flaw allows unauthenticated remote attackers to execute arbitrary code with root privileges by sending specially crafted packets to exposed firewalls. PA-Series and VM-Series appliances running PAN-OS 10.2, 11.1, 11.2, and 12.1 are affected. Shadowserver is tracking over 5,800 internet-exposed VM-Series firewalls, with the largest concentrations in Asia (2,466) and North America (1,998). Palo Alto Networks describes exploitation as "limited" — a phrasing that historically indicates targeted state-sponsored operations. Patches are not expected until a staggered rollout between May 13 and May 28, 2026. Until then, the only mitigation is restricting the User-ID Authentication Portal to trusted internal network zones or disabling it entirely. Prisma Access, Cloud NGFW, and Panorama appliances are not affected. Palo Alto firewalls are used by over 70,000 organizations including 90% of Fortune 10 companies and most major U.S. banks.
