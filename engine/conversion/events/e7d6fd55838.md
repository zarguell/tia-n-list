Google Threat Intelligence Group has published a comprehensive analysis of UNC6671, a threat actor operating the "BlackFile" brand that combines high-volume voice phishing with real-time adversary-in-the-middle credential harvesting to systematically exfiltrate data from Microsoft 365, SharePoint, OneDrive, Salesforce, and Zendesk environments. The group has targeted dozens of organizations across North America, Australia, and the UK since early 2026. The attack chain: callers contact employees' personal phones impersonating IT help desk, direct them to subdomain-based phishing pages (`<org>.enrollms[.]com`, `<org>.passkeyms[.]com`), capture credentials and MFA tokens in real-time, register attacker-controlled MFA devices, then deploy Python scripts using `python-requests` to exfiltrate data via direct HTTP GETs — evading detection by generating FileAccessed rather than FileDownloaded events. In one case, the actor exfiltrated over a million files. Initial ransom demands in the millions drop to low six figures upon engagement. The BlackFile DLS went offline in late April, briefly returned May 11 with a shutdown announcement, and is now inaccessible — Google assesses this as likely rebranding rather than cessation. UNC6671 is independent from ShinyHunters (UNC6240), despite briefly co-opting their brand.
