North Korea's APT37 has been conducting a supply-chain attack through sqgame[.]net, a Chinese game-hosting platform popular with ethnic Koreans in the Yanbian region of China — a primary transit point for North Korean defectors and refugees. The campaign trojanizes APKs to deliver an Android variant of the group's known BirdCall backdoor, which ESET has documented across at least seven versions since October 2024. The Android variant functions as spyware: it extracts IP geolocation, collects contacts, call logs, SMS messages, device details (IMEI, MAC, kernel, root status), periodically captures screenshots, records audio from 7–10 PM local time via the microphone, and exfiltrates files of interest (.jpg, .doc, .docx, .xls, .xlsx, .ppt, .pptx, .txt, .hwp, .pdf, .m4a, .p12). The malware plays a silent MP3 loop to prevent process suspension. Windows victims on the same platform receive a trojanized DLL (mono.dll) that deploys RokRAT, which then loads the Windows version of BirdCall.
