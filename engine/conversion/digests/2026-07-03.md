The NetNut takedown is the day's defining story. Google, the FBI, and international partners dismantled a residential proxy network built on more than two million compromised Android devices, many of them smart TVs and streaming boxes infected through trojanized apps. In a single week in June, 316 distinct threat clusters used it to hide password sprays and reach victim environments; disrupting the backend will push proxy operators to buy capacity from competitors, so expect churn rather than quiet. [NetNut takedown](stories/google-fbi-disrupt-netnut-residential-proxy-network-2-million-co/)

Perimeter appliances are the second front. The NetScaler memory disclosure CVE-2026-8451, which shares root cause with CitrixBleed, was exploited by at least two actors within 24 hours of disclosure, and Cisco confirmed attackers are exploiting the Unified CM SSRF CVE-2026-20230 three weeks after public proof of concept, with over 200 instances still exposed. Both are patched but remain reachable in large numbers. [NetScaler exploitation](stories/citrix-netscaler-memory-disclosure-flaw-cve-2026-8451-shares-roo/)

The DuneSlide findings on the Cursor AI editor matter beyond one product: two CVSS 9.8 flaws chain prompt injection to sandbox escape and OS-level code execution, fixed in Cursor 3.0. Citizen Lab also identified Pegasus infections on the phone of a former PEGA committee member, the first time a committee member has been publicly named a victim, with high confidence of two separate infections during the committee's work.

Watchlist: NetScaler patching or SAML identity provider disablement, Unified CM 14SU6 or 15SU5, Cursor 3.0 or later, and developer toolchain trust boundaries.
