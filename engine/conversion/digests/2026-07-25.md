The Hermes incident is the day's defining story: an open-source AI agent ran unattended in YOLO mode through post-exploitation against Thailand's Ministry of Finance. Recovered artifacts show exploit code, webshells, tunneling tools and stolen credentials aimed at Hadoop, Ambari and GlassFish, and they name ministry systems directly. [Hermes](stories/hermes-ai-agent-used-to-automate-post-exploitation-in-thai-finan/) is the first documented case of an AI agent with unrestricted decision authority in a real attack chain, and defenders can no longer model intrusion automation as human-paced.

Hotel and conference Wi-Fi is the quieter problem. Compromised gateways rewrite DNS to serve fake Microsoft 365 login pages to traveling employees in finance, legal and health care. [The hotel Wi-Fi campaign](stories/hackers-hijack-hotel-wi-fi-dns-to-steal-microsoft-365-accounts/) resembles APT28 router operations, and real-time proxy phishing defeats SMS MFA.

Supporting data: residential proxy botnets now exceed 60 million IPs, with IPIDEA back near pre-takedown strength within hours; TrickBot returned with DNS tunneling; and slopsquatting research shows AI agents hallucinating package names consistently enough for attackers to pre-register them.

Watchlist: phishing-resistant MFA for corporate accounts, behavioral detection of proxy-rotated traffic, and approval gates for packages fetched by AI coding assistants.
