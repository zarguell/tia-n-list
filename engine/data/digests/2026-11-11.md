The defining story today is [Microsoft's November Patch Tuesday](stories/microsoft-s-november-2026-patch-tuesday-addresses-1093-cves-cve/), the second largest monthly release on record. [NEW] The release fixes 1,094 vulnerabilities across 287 products with 63 rated Critical, and its single zero-day is CVE-2026-124194, a moderate Outlook spoofing flaw that leaks NTLMv2 hashes when a target opens a crafted Office file and is already being actively exploited. Because the zero-day is fixed through Office updates rather than the Windows cumulative update, Office channels should move first, and CISA has already added the CVE to its KEV catalog. Heavy counts in Word, Excel and SQL Server round out an Office-first patching day, with some outlets reporting higher totals by folding in fixes shipped earlier in the month.

[NEW] CISA added six actively exploited flaws to KEV on November 10 in the [November 10 KEV additions](stories/cisa-adds-six-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-to-catalog/), and the Barracuda pair is the most actionable item in the batch. CVE-2026-116606 is a pre-authentication authorization bypass in the Email Security Gateway admin interface that allows restoring a crafted configuration backup, chained with OS command injection CVE-2026-111560 in the timezone field to yield root on appliances running versions earlier than 9.4.0.030, with exploitation observed against a small number of organizations. The batch also confirms in-the-wild exploitation of Chrome use-after-free CVE-2026-125350, Cisco BroadWorks XXE CVE-2026-20320, Openwave Webtop Media Transcoder CVE-2026-111793 fixed in 1.1.22.0, and the Outlook zero-day CVE-2026-124194, so gateway and appliance owners should patch Barracuda ESG first and restrict admin interfaces everywhere else.

[UPDATE] The [Chrome XML zero-day](stories/google-chrome-zero-day-cve-2026-125350-exploited-in-the-wild/) is now CISA KEV-listed as of November 10, so treat CVE-2026-125350 as must-patch and force-update managed fleets to Chrome 157.0.8103.50/.51 plus matching Chromium derivatives.

[NEW] Cisco's carrier voice platform is now a confirmed exploitation target in the [Cisco BroadWorks XXE story](stories/cisco-broadworks-xxe-vulnerability-cve-2026-20320/), as CISA KEV-listed CVE-2026-20320 with a November 24 federal remediation deadline. The flaw is an unauthenticated blind XXE injection in the OCI XML parser rated CVSS 7.5 that lets remote attackers read files available to the BroadWorks application account, patched by Cisco back in August 2026 with external entity resolution enabled by default. Because BroadWorks sits in telecom carrier and MSP infrastructure, any instance exposed before patching warrants compromise review covering configuration files, service parameters and application credentials.

[NEW] N-able answered the Able2Shell disclosure with a hotfix and seven CVEs, detailed in the [Able2Shell FAQ](stories/able2shell-frequently-asked-questions-for-reported-n-able-n-cent/). N-central 2026.4 Hotfix 2 shipped November 10 against researcher Sean Bales' collection of 33 alleged flaws with public proof-of-concept code, Tenable's research team published an FAQ, and security firms report exploitation attempts hitting N-central honeypots, though no confirmed compromise of a customer server had been reported as of November 10. The gap between 33 alleged flaws and seven fixed CVEs leaves residual exposure, so administrators should treat the hotfix as partial, keep management interfaces restricted, and watch for follow-up CVEs.

[UPDATE] The [OpenSSH sshole flaw](stories/openssh-on-musl-linux-has-been-letting-people-into-password-less/) is now linked to active attacks that bypass logins on musl systems and install a Python backdoor, with CISA confirming exploits as patched packages became available, so move to OpenSSH 10.6p2 or fixed Alpine builds and audit asterisked accounts for unauthorized access.

[NEW] Virtualization management joins the exploited list in the [Xen Orchestra auth bypass](stories/critical-xen-orchestra-auth-bypass-flaw-exploited-to-hijack-vm-s/), an unauthenticated flaw with no CVE yet that is being actively exploited in the wild. Sending the cookie authenticationToken=j:%7B%7D makes the REST token provider treat an empty JSON object as an unfiltered lookup and authenticate the attacker as an existing token owner with administrator privileges over managed XCP-ng hosts and their VMs, with exploitation observed from 91.211.251.112:8001 and a Tor onion service. Fixed builds are xo-server 5.213.1 (latest) and 5.211.2 (stable), so upgrade immediately, restrict the management interface, and hunt for empty-JSON-object cookies and requests to the authentication_tokens endpoint.

[NEW] Recorded Future disclosed a new hypervisor implant tied to ransomware in [GhostFrame](stories/ghostframe-a-new-esxi-implant-linked-to-settra-ransomware/), attributed to TAG-218 with operations overlapping Settra. The vmhostd-core implant needs pre-existing ESXi root access and persists via local.sh, disables the firewall and execInstalledOnly on a five-second loop, beacons over six simultaneous TCP connections across ports 53, 2222, 3389, 4444, 8443 and 9443, and steals credentials with VMkatz extraction of NTLM, Kerberos, SAM, LSA and NTDS material from guest memory and disks. TAG-218 worked VPN appliances across the US and Europe from late August through October, mostly SonicWall, Palo Alto and Fortinet, and four of those organizations later appeared on the Settra leak site, so ESXi owners should restrict management to trusted systems and hunt the documented persistence paths and six-port SYN pattern.

[NEW] Railway published a full postmortem for an October breach that turned a sandbox escape into a credential heist in the [Railway breach](stories/railway-breach-leads-to-740-000-in-fraudulent-ai-charges/). Attackers exploited an unknown flaw in build infrastructure starting October 20 to escape filesystem isolation, stole an internal credential plus a copy of Railway source code, then abused a misconfigured trust relationship to decrypt customer environment variables, potentially exposing up to 100,000 workspaces while generating roughly $740,000 in fraudulent AI usage from stolen API keys and cloud credentials. Customers should rotate every stored secret and audit usage anomalies, since the incident shows how one sandbox escape can cascade through source analysis and trust misconfiguration.

[NEW] Fortinet published eight critical advisories on November 10 in the [Fortinet November advisories](stories/fortinet-fixes-eight-critical-vulnerabilities-including-fortiman/), led by CVE-2026-126170, a CVSS 9.8 output-encoding flaw in the FortiManager and FortiAnalyzer JSON RPC API allowing unauthenticated code execution. The API is disabled by default which limits reachability, but a successful hit lands on the management plane that controls the rest of the Fortinet estate, and the coordinated October 22 hardening update means attackers can now diff fixed builds. Upgrade FortiOS to 8.0.2, 7.6.8, 7.4.13 or 7.2.14 and FortiManager and FortiAnalyzer to matching fixed lines starting with Internet-facing hosts, keep the JSON RPC API disabled, and review admin and configuration-change logs.

[NEW] Every workstation and CI runner that clones untrusted repositories is exposed in the [Git index RCE](stories/git-index-integer-truncation-rce-cve-2026-111760/), fixed in Git 2.56.1 with backports across the 2.50 through 2.55 lines. CVE-2026-111760 is an integer truncation where an extremely long path passes validation under its full name but lands on disk under an attacker-controlled short name including .git, so a routine recursive clone can swap in a malicious submodule with hooks Git then executes, and the same release fixes five related flaws including a GitLab merge-tree heap overflow CVE-2026-105437 fixed in 19.5.4. GitHub.com is patched but Git for Windows had no 2.56.1-based build as of November 10, so upgrade Git everywhere, disable recursive clones of untrusted repos where patching is blocked, and hunt cloned submodules for a .git entry that is a symlink rather than a file.

[NEW] Self-hosted mail is the target in the [Mailcow November release](stories/mailcow-2026-11-patches-five-vulnerabilities-including-cvss-9-1/), which fixes five flaws with full proof-of-concept steps public since November 10. The worst is GHSA-jgr3-fv33-x43c, a CVSS 9.1 stored template injection in the domain-wide footer where a domain administrator's HTML reaches Lua os.execute in the shared rspamd container, letting one tenant admin read or inject every tenant's mail and disable filtering platform-wide. Companion fixes close an unlimited mailbox enumeration loop, a TOTP bypass for password holders, and cross-domain quarantine reads, so update to 2026-11, limit domain-admin access per the vendor workaround, and review stored footer values and netfilter logs showing invalid-password events from 127.0.0.1.

[NEW] A Japanese bulk mail provider turned into a 77-million-address breach in the [Arara Message breach](stories/arara-message-email-service-breach-leaks-77-million-addresses/). PayCloud first disclosed about 3.01 million addresses from one shared system after October 27 reconnaissance and October 28 logins, then revised to roughly 77 million across 380 client companies and 97 servers, with about 7.13 million phishing mails impersonating a credit card company sent from compromised infrastructure between October 28 and 30 before suspension on October 31. SEGA confirmed November 9 that 2,196,044 newsletter addresses went through Arara, and a possible link to the PHP2Shell KEV flaws remains unconfirmed while the exact intrusion method is still under investigation.

[NEW] The Fireworks AI breach keeps spreading downstream in the [Notion transcripts exposure](stories/notion-ai-chat-transcripts-exposed-in-fireworks-ai-breach/), as Notion confirmed customer AI chat transcripts surfaced through its infrastructure provider and notified affected customers on November 9. Fewer than 1 percent of Notion workspaces are affected, Notion's own systems were not breached, and the investigation is ongoing. Treat this as third-party supply-chain exposure of prompts and business context, rotate Fireworks-linked credentials, and watch for Notion to confirm the full scope of accessed data.

[NEW] Check Point's year-long investigation reframes three espionage clusters as one supply-side problem in the [CopperDragon Sparrow tracking](stories/not-all-that-glitters-is-gold-tracking-copperdragon-s-sparrow-fr/), tying them to a Chinese-nexus quartermaster behind the cross-platform Sparrow framework. SilentDelivery ran selective European and Asian watering holes with fake Chrome certificate errors dropping SquadC or SparrowDoor, OceanGate hit Internet-facing Linux servers in Asia and pivoted to Windows with AnnobinDoor and trojanized SSH, and DarkWire targeted Latin American telecom and government with CopperDoor and DNS MX tunneling. Defenders should hunt framework artifacts rather than single intrusions, from Run keys and staged paths on Windows to modified SSH binaries and Reptile rootkit components on Linux, and rotate credentials after any suspected hit given keystroke and clipboard capture.

[NEW] Vibe-coded apps became public data leaks in the [Grok Build exposure](stories/grok-build-apps-exposed-confidential-data-from-35-000-sites/), reported November 10 as GhostShares 2 by Claroc Research. More than 35,000 apps built in Grok Build mode were reachable by anyone with the link, including business secrets and personal data from organizations that generated them from chat prompts, with Ars Technica reporting confidential material including SpaceXSI-linked content. Organizations using Grok Build should inventory published apps, revoke public access where it is not required, and treat already-published apps as exposed since the fix is configuration and inventory rather than a patch.

Watchlist: verify the Outlook zero-day KEV deadline and Office patch coverage, confirm N-able follow-up CVEs for the remaining alleged flaws plus any customer compromise, meet the November 24 BroadWorks KEV deadline with compromise reviews on exposed hosts, patch Barracuda ESG to 9.4.0.030 and Xen Orchestra to fixed xo-server builds while hunting token-reuse artifacts, upgrade Git fleets once a Windows build ships, update Mailcow and audit footers, rotate Railway and Fireworks-linked secrets, and inventory Grok Build publications for public exposure.
