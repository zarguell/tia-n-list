The Megalodon attribution is the day's defining story. Hudson Rock found that 331 of 978 accounts that pushed malicious commits, roughly a third, trace to machines known to be infostealer-infected, with deep dives suggesting the real rate approaches 100 percent. The attack chain starts with stolen developer credentials, not a GitHub platform bug, and the same database shows more than 24,000 companies with at least one employee whose GitHub credentials were stolen. Infostealer infection is now a supply chain emergency. [Megalodon infostealer attribution](stories/megalodon-mapped-33-of-compromised-github-accounts-trace-back-to/)

The South Staffordshire Water case is the cautionary tale on dwell time. Cl0p sat in the utility's network for nearly two years, exfiltrating 4.1 TB covering 633,887 customers, against an outsourced SOC blind to 95 percent of the network and domain controllers unpatched against ZeroLogon. The ICO fine followed a breach that needed no zero-days, only monitoring, patching, and asset management failures. [Cl0p and South Staffs Water](stories/cl0p-masqueraded-as-south-staffs-water-employee-for-two-years-ic/)

The MiniPlasma analysis is a direct hit on detection quality: rules anchored on the conhost.exe process name miss the exploit entirely when the binary is renamed, and the real signal is the registry symbolic link. Rhode Island's workers' compensation breach, disclosed four months after the January incident, adds to the pattern of delayed notification.

Watchlist: credential scanning tied to infostealer intelligence, outsourced SOC coverage audits, and detection rules built on registry artifacts rather than process names.
