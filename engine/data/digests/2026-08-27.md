The FBI seized three domains used by Chinese state-sponsored hackers to operate [QScan and QTRouter](stories/fbi-disables-china-linked-hacking-tools-used-against-us-agencies/), offensive tools deployed against U.S. federal agencies since 2018. A Justice Department announcement on August 26 identified the operator as Nanjing Xinjiuwei Network Technology Company, which sold hacking services to China's Ministry of State Security and military. According to an FBI affidavit, the group known as QTFY targeted NASA, the Federal Reserve, the National Institutes of Health, the U.S. Senate, and the departments of Energy, Justice, and Health and Human Services, along with hospitals, telecommunications providers, power companies, banks, and defense contractors. The takedown removes operational infrastructure rather than disrupting a live campaign, but the breadth of targets suggests long-running access to high-privilege brokers or exploitation of widely deployed software. Organizations in listed sectors should verify network logs for QScan or QTRouter indicators, particularly those with internet-facing services exposed since 2018.

Microsoft shipped emergency patches for two actively exploited zero-days in its [Defender antimalware products](stories/microsoft-defender-zero-days-cve-2026-41091-cve-2026-45498-activ/). CVE-2026-41091 is a privilege escalation bug in the Malware Protection Engine caused by improper link resolution before file access, chaining to SYSTEM-level privileges on affected machines. CVE-2026-45498 targets the Defender Antimalware Platform and enables denial-of-service against unpatched Windows endpoints. CISA added both to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and ordered federal agencies to remediate by June 3. Check Point Research published technical analysis showing how Defender's own signed boot-time recovery driver can be abused to delete arbitrary kernel-level security software across Windows 7 through Windows 11 25H2. The patched versions are Malware Protection Engine 1.1.26040.8 and Antimalware Platform 4.18.26040.7. The practical risk is that the attacker does not need to escalate privileges further because the Defender driver itself runs with kernel access.

Microsoft SharePoint Server remains the target of a summer-long exploitation wave across at least five flaws, with the [chain of CVE-2026-55040 and CVE-2026-63520](stories/cve-2026-63520-microsoft-sharepoint-remote-code-execution-fixed/) now confirmed under active attack. CVE-2026-55040, a JWT token validation bypass rated CVSS 9.1 and patched in July, was weaponized within one day of Rapid7 publishing its PoC on August 11. On August 24, VulnCheck researcher Jonathan Peterson released a PoC for CVE-2026-63520, the BCS-side RCE, and Defused reported seeing the full chain probed in honeypots one day later. Microsoft's advisory for 55040 still does not list exploitation, and CISA has not yet added it to KEV. Separately, CVE-2026-45659, a deserialization RCE exploited since early July, has been confirmed by CISA to be abused by ransomware gangs. SharePoint Server 2016 and 2019 hit end of extended support on July 14, 2026, with no extended security updates available. Both PoCs are public and attackers are chaining them within days of disclosure. Confirm both the July and August updates are applied and migrate 2016 and 2019 servers off unsupported versions.

[Ubiquiti patched 22 vulnerabilities](stories/ubiquiti-patches-three-max-severity-security-vulnerabilities/) across its UniFi product line on August 26, including three rated CVSS 10.0. CVE-2026-77537 is an improper input validation flaw in UniFi Protect Application allowing remote unauthenticated compromise. CVE-2026-77550 is a CRLF injection enabling authentication bypass on UniFi OS devices. CVE-2026-77554 is a command injection via improper input validation in UniFi Talk Application. All three require low-complexity attacks with no user interaction and no authentication. This brings Ubiquiti's 2026 total of critical-severity patches to six, matching the number disclosed in all of 2025. The remaining 19 vulnerabilities include seven additional improper access control issues, authentication bypass, and arbitrary command execution flaws affecting UniFi OS Server, UniFi Connect, UniFi Network Application, UID Enterprise Agent, UniFi Access Application, and UniFi Protect AI Key. Patched versions: UniFi Protect Application 7.2.105+, UniFi Talk Application 5.3.2+, UniFi OS Server 5.1.21+. Organizations running Ubiquiti UniFi infrastructure should prioritize patching given the lack of exploitation disclosure does not mean these are safe to defer.

Google pushed [Chrome 152.0.7977.64/.65](stories/chrome-152-patches-over-300-vulnerabilities/) to stable channels with 327 security fixes, ten rated Critical. Multiple sources flagged at least two as particularly dangerous, both triggerable by simply visiting a malicious page with no user interaction. The scale of this release is notable: 327 fixes in a single stable update is above Chrome's typical cadence, suggesting either a backlog of responsibly disclosed bugs reaching disclosure or a particularly productive fuzzing cycle. The Critical-rated vulns likely include type confusions and heap buffer overflows in V8 and rendering, the classes that routinely land in the exploit chain category for sandbox escapes. This update follows Chrome 151 released August 11 which fixed five high-severity use-after-free bugs. The rapid succession of two major patches in two weeks reinforces that Chromium's attack surface keeps expanding and the pace of vulnerability discovery is not slowing.

[Avada](stories/critical-avada-wordpress-theme-flaw-enables-zero-click-rce/), one of the most widely deployed WordPress themes, carries a critical vulnerability chain that enables unauthenticated remote code execution with zero user interaction. CVE-2026-18431, rated CVSS 9.8, chains six separate flaws across authorization, input-validation, trust-boundary, and file-handling mechanisms. Attackers must trigger these weaknesses in a specific sequence to escalate from anonymous access to arbitrary PHP code execution on the server. The vulnerability affects versions up to 7.16 and Fusion Builder plugin up to 3.16. Successful exploitation grants full server-side control, allowing attackers to plant backdoors, exfiltrate databases, redirect visitors, or create persistent admin accounts. ThemeFusion has released fixed versions. The attack chain requires no authentication and no clicks from victims, making mass exploitation viable.

Two critical vulnerabilities in the [Windows WatchGuard Agent](stories/critical-watchguard-agent-flaws-let-unauthenticated-attackers-ex/) allow unauthenticated attackers to execute arbitrary code with elevated privileges. CVE-2026-57910 (CVSS v4.0 9.3) is an improper authentication flaw in the agent's UDP discovery and command service where the TaskExecute event handler can be abused to force the agent to download and execute attacker-controlled code. CVE-2026-57909 (CVSS v4.0 9.4) is a related flaw carrying similar severity. Both issue SYSTEM-level access on Windows and root on Linux if the agent runs there. The agent typically runs with elevated privileges across managed endpoints, so an attacker who can reach the UDP discovery port can turn a single unpatched agent into a foothold with SYSTEM rights. WatchGuard disclosed both on August 25 with no evidence of in-the-wild exploitation, though the recent disclosure date likely reflects low awareness rather than low exploitability. Upgrade to WatchGuard Agent 1.25.13.0000 or later and restrict network access to the agent's discovery service in the interim.

SonicWall's [NetExtender Linux VPN client](stories/sonicwall-netextender-flaw-lets-attackers-write-arbitrary-files/) carries a path traversal vulnerability that lets attackers write arbitrary files to disk with root privileges. CVE-2026-66152, scored 8.8, affects versions 10.3.5 and earlier by exploiting the OPSWAT tarball handling process with directory traversal sequences. The attack is network-exploitable with low complexity and no pre-existing privileges, though it does require user interaction. A companion flaw, CVE-2026-66153 (CVSS 7.0), affects the NEService auto-upgrade mechanism via insecure temporary file handling, enabling symlink or race-condition attacks. The primary risk is on Linux systems where NetExtender is deployed with root permissions, which is common for VPN clients. A successful path traversal could give an attacker root-level persistence on a gateway or endpoint. SonicWall published advisory SNWLID-2026-0013 on August 25 with patches.

[NVIDIA NemoClaw](stories/nvidia-nemoclaw-vulnerability-lets-attackers-hijack-ai-agents-vi/), a framework that runs the OpenClaw AI agent inside NVIDIA OpenShell sandboxes, contains a critical flaw that lets attackers hijack AI agents via DNS rebinding. CVE-2026-65105 exploits NemoClaw's use of Ollama for local inference, which starts with OLLAMA_HOST=0.0.0.0:11434 binding to every network interface. Ollama on port 11434 has no authentication and relies on CORS enforcement and Host-header validation, but researchers from Oasis Security found that Ollama skips Host-header validation when bound to a non-loopback address. An attacker who gets a victim to visit a malicious page can rebind a domain to the local IP, bypass the Host-header check, and reach the Ollama API without authentication. Through this unauthenticated access, the attacker can poison the model template used by OpenClaw agents, gaining persistent control of the AI agent even after the page is closed. The attack requires only a single visit to a malicious website.

Binary Defense identified [Dindoor](stories/muddywater-linked-dindoor-backdoor-abuses-legitimate-deno-runtim/), a new Windows backdoor attributed to the Iranian APT group MuddyWater, found in infections at U.S. software and banking organizations and a Canadian non-profit starting in early 2026. Unlike most backdoors that ship a custom interpreter, Dindoor hijacks the legitimate Deno JavaScript/TypeScript runtime to execute its payload. If Deno is not present, the loader downloads it from deno.land using curl.exe, making the malware harder to spot because the runtime itself is signed and trusted. The attack chain unfolds in three stages: spearphishing for initial access, a loader that checks for Deno and launches it with a Base64-encoded script, and a final payload that checks for virtual machine or sandbox artifacts before enabling its main functions. What makes Dindoor notable is MuddyWater's pattern of hiding behind legitimate infrastructure rather than building custom tooling, requiring detection strategies that go beyond binary reputation.

A newly disclosed Rowhammer attack called [GPUThor](stories/gputhor-attack-defeats-nvidia-ecc-protection-for-root-access/) can bypass error-correcting code protections on NVIDIA GPUs, enabling denial-of-service and root-level privilege escalation. Published by the University of Toronto, GPUThor achieves far more practical bit-flip rates than past concepts like GPUHammer or GPUBreach, which became irrelevant after ECC was introduced. The attack was demonstrated against Ampere-class NVIDIA workstation GPUs with GDDR6 memory, including the RTX A4000, RTX A4500, RTX A5000, and RTX A6000, all widely used in AI and cloud infrastructure. The researchers adjusted GPUThor so its hammering follows a non-uniform pattern at a rate that avoids activating GDDR6's Target Row Refresh mitigations. Since AI model training relies heavily on GPU power, a successful Rowhammer attack could have a devastating impact on model accuracy.

Medical technology company [Boston Scientific](stories/boston-scientific-says-cyberattack-disrupted-operations-globally/) disclosed a cyberattack that disrupted some of its IT systems, causing operational disruptions globally. The company detected the incident on August 25 and says it caused a network outage and impacted access to certain operating systems and business applications, including the ability to process and ship customer orders. Boston Scientific has 59,000 employees, 13 manufacturing facilities, and manufactures stents, catheters, pacemakers, defibrillators, and endoscopes. The company activated incident response procedures and contracted external cybersecurity experts, but says the timeline for full restoration is not yet known. The incident is notable because Boston Scientific is one of the largest medical device manufacturers in the world, and the operational disruption to order processing and shipping could have downstream effects on healthcare facilities awaiting critical devices.

[Nutex Health](stories/sensitive-information-exposed-in-nutex-health-data-breach/), a for-profit healthcare operator running 28 facilities across 12 states with $875 million in annual revenue, disclosed a data breach in an SEC filing. The company said an unauthorized third party accessed and exfiltrated information from its servers, including data that may be private and/or confidential. Nutex has not yet determined what specific data was compromised or whether patients, employees, or business partners were affected. After detecting the intrusion, Nutex hired external incident-response specialists, activated its cybersecurity response plan, and notified law enforcement. The breach investigation is ongoing. Microsoft [Teams](stories/microsoft-teams-outage-disrupts-meetings-and-screen-sharing-for/) experienced a service disruption on August 26 preventing some customers from creating or joining meetings and using screen sharing. Microsoft posted an update at 7:26 a.m. IST stating that monitoring indicated the issue was largely mitigated. Japanese users began reporting failures after 9:00 a.m. local time, and Teams trended on X. One-to-one calls appeared to remain functional, suggesting the outage was concentrated in meeting services rather than a complete client failure. For security and IT operations teams, this distinction matters: failed meeting joins and creation workflows can interrupt incident bridges, SOC huddles, executive escalations, and tabletop exercises. Organizations that rely on Teams as their primary emergency communication channel should classify a broad collaboration failure as a continuity event rather than a productivity issue.
