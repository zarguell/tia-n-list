[NEW] A pre-authentication remote Linux kernel crash is today's defining story, in [NoisyTCP](stories/noisytcp-cve-2026-99288-remote-linux-kernel-crash-via-open-tcp-p/). Researcher Hyunwoo Kim disclosed CVE-2026-99288, a race in the kernel's TCP established-socket lookup that needs no credentials and no application bug, only a reachable open TCP port, so default nginx, Apache and sshd configurations are exposed after roughly 11 years unnoticed, and containers share the panicking host kernel. Virtual machines went down in 5 seconds to 2 minutes in testing, and the openwall write-up frames the outcome as remote corruption rather than a mere crash. The fix is a pair of commits, 4e2b3ef19e0e plus the refcount-ordering 5b5d8f351dd9, shipped in stable releases 6.12.117, 6.18.63 and 7.3.4, and hosts that cannot reboot should serialize the external interface to a single RX queue with RPS and RFS disabled while watching for TCP receive-path oops and panic messages.

[NEW] A sandbox fix that needs its own fix landed in [the NLTK bypass](stories/cve-2026-128138-cvss-9-8-nltk-sandbox-bypass-allows-arbitrary-ja/). CVE-2026-128138 (CVSS 9.8) defeats the 3.10.3 hardening because validation covers the per-call options list while the caller-supplied cmd list is appended unchecked, so cmd values such as -jar with a hostile JAR, or an @argfile token, or a StanfordSegmenter java_class argument, start an attacker-chosen JAR in a child JVM with the Python worker's privileges. Version 3.10.3 is affected and 3.10.4, published on PyPI on October 21 with the java cmd fix as PR 3771, is the patched release, which matters because careful teams just standardized on 3.10.3 after the August advisories. Move to 3.10.4, and where upgrades stall, strip leading dash and @ tokens from anything reaching java(cmd=...) or Stanford wrappers, hold the classpath to a fixed allowlist, never set NLTK_ALLOW_UNSAFE_JARS or NLTK_ALLOW_UNSAFE_CLASSPATH, and hunt for java children carrying -jar or @argfile under service accounts.

[NEW] The volunteer network keeps shipping under fire, in [Tor's emergency update](stories/tor-releases-emergency-security-update-to-defend-against-ongoing/). Tor 0.4.9.17, released November 13, answers the distributed denial-of-service flood overwhelming most relays with client circuits, singling out a fix to how relays' built-in DoS protections handle IPv6. This is the third build in a fast sequence after 0.4.9.15's directory-request defenses and 0.4.9.16 on November 5, and operator reports say strain persists, including relays that lost HSDir status before the update and some still struggling after it. [NEW] A worm France helped kill in 2019 is delivering malware again, in [the Retadup revival](stories/hackers-revive-retadup-botnet-shut-down-in-2019-to-deliver-coffe/). Command-and-control infrastructure tied to Retadup, dismantled with French help in 2019, is back distributing CoffeeLoader, with roughly 15,000 infection attempts recorded across the first three quarters of the year. The August 2026 variant drops a patched 7z.dll through an NSIS installer and executes it via rundll32.exe, and its new Armoury packer build issues the XOR decryption through OpenGL compute shaders after defenders learned to look for the old OpenCL-based GPU decryption. Watch for the rundll32 plus 7z.dll launch pattern on endpoints and treat removable-media propagation as an active reinfection route rather than legacy behavior.

[NEW] Old short links are pointing at new destinations, in [the Bitly hijack](stories/old-bitly-links-on-burger-king-mcdonald-s-and-fbi-posts-are-bein/). Dormant Bitly URLs published years ago by Burger King, McDonald's, FBI and Trump accounts were reclaimed and repointed at unrelated Wikipedia pages, while the original posts and archived campaign assets still resolve to whatever the current account holder chooses. [NEW] A record privacy fine rewrites the consent math, in [the Meta ruling](stories/meta-fined-887-million-in-italy-over-android-gallery-photos-and/). Italy's Garante fined Meta Platforms Ireland 805 million euros on November 14 over the Facebook for Android feature that read gallery photos, videos and metadata for roughly 20 million Italian users without specific informed consent, enabled by default behind a single permission covering several functions with different purposes. The authority rejected the argument that Ireland should own the case, holding that the ePrivacy rules transposed into Italian law have no one-stop-shop, and cited Meta's lack of cooperation in sizing the penalty at about 0.47 percent of group turnover. Meta has 60 days to disable the feature and seek separate consent and is challenging jurisdiction, so managed Android fleets should inventory full Photos permissions and confirm the gallery suggestion setting is off while watching the appeal and whether other authorities open matching cases.

[NEW] Attackers are pointing AI agents at AI infrastructure, in [the Amazon decoy findings](stories/amazon-uncovers-ai-agent-campaign-targeting-litellm-dify-and-oth/). Amazon's threat intelligence team ran decoy servers and captured attackers' own AI agents hunting internet-exposed LiteLLM, One API and Dify instances specifically for the provider keys those gateways hold, then spending the stolen keys on model calls that powered the next round of key hunting.
