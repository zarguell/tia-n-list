[NEW] GL.iNet confirmed on November 20 that an unauthorized party exploited a flaw in its GoodCloud remote management services to reach routers and KVM devices and install malware, in the [GL.iNet GoodCloud zero-day](stories/gl-inet-goodcloud-zero-day-exploited-to-plant-malware-on-routers/). The company says the attack path is now blocked but that existing implants survive, and community advisory CSA-2026-01 rates the incident critical with active exploitation and no CVE assigned. Owners should hunt for a process containing `crond -i /tmp/.log`, a shell pipe at `/tmp/f`, an empty `/etc/mp_ip_mapping_info` dated November 20 or later, and a `netstat` binary replaced by a hiding script, while noting that RemoteToHome Consulting documented a root remote shell on a GL-AXT1800 phoning home to 170.39.226.219 and a cloud message value passed into an `iptables` command without validation. The KVM placement sharpens the risk since a compromised Comet unit sits between the owner and an attached computer with screen and keyboard access and its cloud service enabled by default, so treat firmware updates and implant hunts as separate steps with beta Comet builds 1.10.2 and 1.8.6 out and stable releases scheduled for November 22.

[NEW] Researchers published Upload2Shell, a Ghostscript sandbox-escape remote code execution chain tracked as CVE-2026-125396 and scored 8.5, affecting releases before 10.08.0, in the [Ghostscript Upload2Shell flaw](stories/researchers-demonstrate-ghostscript-sandbox-escape-rce-via-craft/). Paulos Yibelo reported the Type 1 font-loader use-after-free in `.putgstringcopy` to Artifex on September 1, the fix shipped in 10.08.0 on September 8, and pwn.ai disclosed the full chain on November 21 with exploitation paths through Debian 13 CUPS print jobs, Roundcube attachments, public WordPress upload forms including Forminator and Elementor, MediaWiki, DokuWiki, Mayan EDMS, ResourceSpace, Plesk, and a Google document renderer. Hunt for the published markers `/tmp/GS_CUPS_PIPE_RCE` and `PATH_CONTROL_BYTES_AFTER=0,0,0,0`, and note the patch gap: Canonical fixed supported Ubuntu releases in USN-8949-1 on October 21, while Debian 12 stayed vulnerable and Amazon Linux and Red Hat fixes were still pending as of November 22, so any pipeline passing untrusted files to Ghostscript deserves version checks now.

[NEW] A critical unsafe deserialization flaw in the legacy Idunno.AntiCsrf library, CVE-2026-131914 rated CVSS 9.3 in versions 0.9.1.0 and the 0.9.1 Beta, is being exploited in the wild with no fix available, in the [Idunno.AntiCsrf deserialization flaw](stories/hackers-exploit-critical-asp-net-anti-csrf-library-flaw-allowing/). The bug is reachable over the network without authentication, putting any ASP.NET application still loading the old DLL in range of unauthenticated remote code execution. With no named attacker, no affected-application list, and no patched release published, the concrete step is inventory: find every deployment still shipping Idunno.AntiCsrf.dll, verify whether it faces untrusted networks, and remove or replace the library rather than relying on the CSRF checks it was meant to provide.

[NEW] Symantec's Threat Hunter Team found a 41-module PowerShell toolkit executing for just 3 minutes 12 seconds on September 20 under a webshell on an IIS server at a major African stock exchange, in the [African stock exchange intrusion](stories/major-african-stock-exchange-targeted-in-ai-assisted-attack/). The modules harvested secrets and tested credentials, a reused bearer token, and API keys against the exchange's investment API and a securities depository gateway, though Symantec could not confirm any tested credential worked, and researchers noted hallmarks of AI-assisted authorship without identifying a model. Six persistence implants observed from April 2026 were separately tied to Witchetty, linked to the TA410 umbrella, whose earlier activity used ProxyShell and ProxyLogon with webshells and Backdoor.Stegmap hidden in a Windows logo on GitHub, so review webshell artifacts and short-burst PowerShell with outbound authentication attempts against market-facing APIs.

[NEW] Attackers directed an autonomous AI penetration-testing framework across 25 operations against at least 19 organizations in August and September, gaining access or retrieving data at 10 of them, in the [China-linked AI-agent campaign](stories/china-linked-hackers-use-ai-agents-to-access-10-organizations-an/). Unit 42 reporting summarized on November 23 describes exposed credentials, persistent administrator access, military and government identities, and more than one million records, with a moderate-confidence assessment of China-nexus intelligence collection that should stay qualified until more telemetry is public. A second account of the same toolset has the agents finding and exploiting a previously unknown vulnerability in an open-source medical imaging application during university intrusions, after Amazon found a publicly reachable Cairn instance running without authentication on September 10, so watch for the underlying Unit 42 and Amazon findings plus any CVE for the imaging flaw.

[NEW] Rackspace told customers that attackers used leaked credentials to enter its Spot managed Kubernetes platform on November 17 between roughly 00:20 and 22:00 UTC, an intrusion Rackspace learned of on November 18 through a customer report, in the [Rackspace Spot breach](stories/rackspace-spot-breach-let-hackers-access-customer-kubernetes-sec/). Inside affected customer organizations the intruders could create pods, read Kubernetes Secrets, and list nodes and volumes, with Spot API and refresh tokens also exposed, so any Secret in use during the window should be treated as read. Rackspace says it revoked affected tokens, but the notice states neither how many organizations were hit nor how the credentials were obtained, leaving rotation of Secrets and tokens plus review of cluster audit records for pod creation and Secret reads as the immediate work.

[NEW] Billing platform Cleverbridge disclosed that a third party copied payment card data after unauthorized activity detected on November 4, with notices to affected individuals beginning November 20, in the [Cleverbridge payment-card theft](stories/cleverbridge-warns-hackers-stole-credit-card-numbers-cvvs-in-dat/). Its status page logged a major-impact platform availability issue from 09:53 to 19:06 UTC on November 4, a timeline that reads as the intrusion window rather than an ordinary outage, and the notice lists card numbers, expiry dates, and CVV/CVC codes, the last of which payment standards bar from post-authorization storage and which makes the records directly usable for card-not-present fraud. Exposure sits with buyers who paid through Cleverbridge as merchant of record, including customers of named vendors such as Red Hat, SUSE, Tenable, Veeam, Jamf, Sony, and Parallels, yet no affected merchant or victim count has been published, so enumerate every purchase routed through the platform, seek written scope, and reissue affected cards.

[NEW] HUMAN's Satori team reports the BADBOX botnet has rebuilt as BADBOX 3.0, generating roughly 40 billion fraudulent ad bid requests per day from infected Android-based devices, in the [BADBOX 3.0 resurgence](stories/badbox-3-0-botnet-returns-with-record-breaking-40-billion-daily/). That peak exceeded 4 percent of all connected-TV traffic on HUMAN's Defense Platform, more than triple the previous record holder VASTFLUX, and it shows the March 2025 takedown that cut off 500,000 devices with 24 apps removed from Google Play did not end the operation, which Google said had infected over 10 million devices by April 2025. Advertisers and TV operators should treat anomalous bid-request volume as the direct signal and watch for fresh infrastructure detail and any new distribution beyond low-cost Android TV boxes.

[NEW] Samsung Securities disclosed on November 23 that unauthorized access to its Voice of Customer system on November 21 exposed personal information for 8,241 customers through files attached to their inquiries, in the [Samsung Securities breach](stories/samsung-securities-hit-by-cyberattack-exposing-personal-data-of/). The disclosure names no attribution or ransom claim, so impact is bounded by whatever personal data sat in those attachments, and a second same-day report restates the scale without technical detail. Watch for a follow-up on what the attachments contained and whether South Korean regulators open a notification review.

[NEW] Korea Electric Power Corp confirmed a data breach reported on November 23 with a later report putting the loss at 1.52 million records, in the [KEPCO breach](stories/korea-electric-power-corp-reports-data-breach-affecting-over-one/). The accounts agree on scale but give no intrusion path, record categories, or actor, so with a national utility's customer population in play the risk cannot be scoped until KEPCO publishes what was exposed and on what timeline, with Korean regulator reporting as the corroboration to watch.

[NEW] The USDA's cybersecurity center uncovered a security incident that forced its Information Services Division to reset every database password in every environment, disrupting payment tools used between the agency and farmers plus staff access to loan and aid information, in the [USDA credential-reset incident](stories/usda-grapples-with-security-breach-as-it-staff-take-multiple-sys/). Three people familiar told Nextgov/FCW and Government Executive the outages stretched across multiple days with dependent application resets, and the public website was still showing downtime days after the internal email, while the operator and any data exfiltration remain undisclosed. Later tabloid framing as a food-safety cyberattack added no technical detail, so treat this as an availability and credential-reset event with unknown cause and watch for an official account of the intrusion path and confirmation that payment, loan, and aid systems are restored.
