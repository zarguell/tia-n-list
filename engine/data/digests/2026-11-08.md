Attackers siphoned about 133 million dollars from French cooperative bank Crédit Mutuel Arkéa by entering through a service provider platform and pushing fraudulent transfers out of the bank's own accounts to other banks, in the [French bank heist](stories/hackers-pull-off-133-million-french-bank-heist-via-service-provi/). Outflows began November 1, on-chain investigator ZachXBT flagged more than 106 million dollars in movement on November 2, and the bank disclosed the incident on November 4 saying customer assets were unaffected while transfer delays lingered. The stolen funds were in USDC and USDT, swapped into ETH, routed through the instant exchanges WellWallet and Kyrrex, converted into Monero and bridged across chains, with about 30 million dollars laundered by November 3 and ZachXBT stating with high confidence that North Korea's Lazarus Group was not involved. Bitquery traced 108.8 million dollars of the movement with 61 million still sitting on Ethereum, so watch whether stablecoin issuers or law enforcement freeze any of the bridged funds and whether regulators press Arkéa on the gap between the November 1 outflows and the November 4 disclosure.

The new substance on the Zyxel switch saga is failure: a November 8 Hacker News follow-up reports that Zyxel's emergency firmware does not block the GS1900 zero-day, describing the initial patch as rushed, poorly tested and insufficient, in the [Zyxel GS1900 zero-day](stories/zyxel-gs1900-switch-buffer-overflow-cve-2026-7273-added-to-cisa/). The exploited flaw is CVE-2026-107441, an out-of-bounds write in the switches' web management interface that lets an unauthenticated attacker execute operating system commands through crafted HTTP requests, which GreyNoise ties to a suspected Chinese-speaking actor called Kapibala that compromised 996 devices across 48 countries and harvested configurations plus hashed root credentials starting around August 17. Note the KEV listing in this story's title refers to the older CVE-2026-7273 buffer overflow added back on September 21, not to this zero-day, so defenders should verify GS1900 firmware against both advisories rather than trusting the first patch.

The delta on sshole is exploitation: independent reporting from Help Net Security and The Hacker News confirms attacks began within nine hours of disclosure with root access demonstrated on an Alpine honeypot, so treat CVE-2026-123158 as actively exploited and move to OpenSSH 10.6p2 or fixed Alpine builds with PasswordAuthentication set to no in the meantime, in the [OpenSSH sshole flaw](stories/openssh-on-musl-linux-has-been-letting-people-into-password-less/).

Censys ARC counted about 815 distinct ARTEX autonomous pentesting consoles exposed without authentication on default port 8787 as of November 4, with RST Labs tallying more than 1,300 exposed consoles across ARTEX, Cairn and PentAGI, in the [exposed ARTEX consoles](stories/censys-finds-roughly-815-artex-ai-pentesting-frameworks-exposed/). TechCrunch carried the exposure on November 7, and implicator.ai found about 11 percent of the exposed instances also running Cobalt Strike or other command and control frameworks, which points to live criminal reuse rather than idle researcher boxes. With forks like ScopeWeaver and BaaRai circulating after the original repository went dark in October, treat any port 8787 ARTEX exposure as an active intrusion indicator and hunt the linked Hong Kong jump infrastructure.

MUJI operator Ryohin Keikaku widened its breach disclosure in stages to up to 27 million app users plus 1.09 million MUJI Card members, in the [MUJI breach](stories/muji-app-data-breach-exposes-emails-and-payment-card-numbers-of/). The first report cited email addresses, birth dates and gender with payment card numbers potentially exposed, and the November 8 update added the card-member figure, a pattern of upward revision that argues against treating any single number as final. Watch for regulator or card-brand action and for confirmation of whether card numbers were actually taken.

Norway's aid agency Norad disclosed on November 7 that compromised accounts let attackers into its Grant Portal on November 3, potentially exposing names and email addresses of 20,614 registered users plus grant-application information from at least three organizations, in the [Norad breach](stories/norad-targeted-by-cyberattack/). Application data is prime phishing fuel against grantees and staff, so watch Norad's full accounting of what was extracted and whether the compromised accounts connect to a wider campaign against aid groups.

CTM360 detailed a gift-card fraud ring tracked as TL-2026-5511 that BleepingComputer picked up on November 7, pushing fake reward portals dressed in PlayStation, Sephora, SHEIN, Walmart and Amazon branding through celebrity-endorsed ads, in the [fake gift-card ring](stories/ctm360-exposes-fake-gift-card-scams-exploiting-trusted-brands/). Victims promised a free card land in a human-verification loop with fake progress bars and receive nothing but harvested personal data, unwanted app installs and recurring mobile subscriptions across more than 1,000 incidents. There is no CVE and no patch for a social-engineering loop, so the defense is user warnings, carrier-bill reviews and proxy hunts for the rotating reward-portal domains.

Kaniko builds before 1.25.20 can send a build's Microsoft Entra ID access token to lookalike registry hosts under CVE-2026-105769, fixed in 1.25.20, in the [Kaniko token leak](stories/kaniko-flaw-cve-2026-105769-sends-azure-entra-id-tokens-to-looka/). Any pipeline on an older Kaniko may already have handed Azure-valid tokens to an attacker-controlled endpoint, so upgrade and audit recent pushes for typo-squatted registries.

The imgproxy image proxy carries CVE-2026-123614, a critical integer overflow in its ICO loader affecting versions 4.0.0 through 4.0.15 that a crafted image can trigger for remote code execution or a crash, fixed in 4.0.16, in the [imgproxy ICO flaw](stories/imgproxy-cve-2026-123614-integer-overflow-in-ico-loader-allows-r/). The report rests on a single source with no confirmed exploitation, so treat untrusted image uploads as the exposure vector and patch on the regular cycle rather than as an emergency.

Researcher acid.vegas stood up an IRC network on TCP port 0, the port nothing is supposed to use, then scanned all of IPv4 and found real listeners answering there, in the [port 0 research](stories/the-port-that-does-not-exist-port-0-is-the-port-you-are-not-supp/). Because firewalls and monitoring widely assume port 0 is dead, those neighbors represent an unmonitored channel ripe for command and control or exfiltration, so the practical takeaway is to start logging and blocking port 0 and to watch what the other services there turn out to be.

Watchlist: verify Zyxel GS1900 firmware past both advisories, treat sshole as exploited and finish OpenSSH 10.6p2 or fixed Alpine builds, hunt port 8787 ARTEX exposure and rotate anything tied to exposed consoles, audit Kaniko registries and upgrade imgproxy to 4.0.16, warn users off gift-card verification loops, and track MUJI card-number confirmation plus Norad's full scope and any Arkéa fund-freeze action.
