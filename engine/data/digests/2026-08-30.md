A new deep-dive analysis of [Auto-Color](stories/reverse-engineering-the-auto-color-linux-backdoor/) reveals the inner workings of an emerging Linux backdoor that combines a remote access trojan with an LD_PRELOAD user-space rootkit. First documented by Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 in February 2025, Auto-Color targets universities and government offices in North America and Asia, with earliest samples collected in late 2024. The analysis shows two execution paths depending on privilege level: without root, it stores state under /tmp/cross and phones home to 146.70.41.178:443; with root, it installs itself under /var/log/cross and drops libcext.so.2 into the LD_PRELOAD chain, allowing it to hide its files and filter entries from /proc/net/tcp across all dynamically linked programs. The delivery vector remains unknown, but the rootkit's ability to conceal network connections from system monitoring tools makes detection significantly harder for defenders running standard forensics.

TA4922, a Chinese-speaking threat actor, is deploying [PackClient RAT](stories/chinese-hackers-deploy-packclient-rat-via-tax-themed-phishing-at/) through tax-themed phishing campaigns targeting organizations in mainland China and India. Proofpoint observed the activity in May and July 2026, with the first campaign impersonating the Shandong Provincial Tax Bureau and warning recipients they had been selected for a 2026 tax inspection. Emails directed victims to download a ZIP archive from gov12366[.]com containing an executable that initiates the PackClient infection chain. PackClient is a modular RAT framework marketed through Chinese-language Telegram channels, providing data-theft, surveillance, remote control, payload delivery, and persistence capabilities. The first-stage downloader checks for elevated privileges and drops a DLL invoked through rundll32.exe, making the malware adaptable to espionage, financial crime, reconnaissance, or follow-on ransomware operations.

Two different threat groups have recently attacked [Interim HealthCare](stories/two-different-groups-have-recently-attacked-interim-healthcare-e/) franchise entities, raising concerns about whether other locations in the home care franchise network face similar risks. Interim HealthCare of West Texas LLC notified HHS that 2,071 patients were affected, while the Amarillo franchise reported 666 patients impacted. The involvement of two separate groups suggests the franchise model itself may be creating attack surface, with individual locations maintaining inconsistent security postures that threat actors are exploiting at scale.

ShinyHunters claims to have stolen 284 million patient data records from [McKesson](stories/shinyhunters-claims-284m-patient-records-from-mckesson-breach/), one of the largest healthcare and pharmaceutical distributors in the United States. The breach continues to develop as the scale of claimed theft would make it one of the largest healthcare data breaches on record, with compromised data potentially spanning patients across numerous provider networks served by McKesson's distribution infrastructure. McKesson has confirmed unauthorized access to third-party applications and data exfiltration in its SEC filing, but the investigation remains in early stages.

[TerminalFix](stories/hackers-use-fake-cloudflare-captcha-to-deploy-terminalfix-revers/) continues to gain attention as additional security outlets publish analysis of the ClickFix variant that uses fake Cloudflare CAPTCHA overlays to trick users into pasting malicious PowerShell commands. The attack chain, documented by Microsoft Threat Intelligence, downloads a ZIP archive containing a legitimate signed binary and a malicious DLL for sideloading, then extracts steganographic payloads from PNG images and establishes persistence through Registry Run keys and scheduled tasks, ultimately deploying a custom reverse-tunnel implant that turns compromised Windows devices into network pivot points.

[Gogs](stories/critical-gogs-flaw-enables-remote-code-execution-through-path-tr/) CVE-2026-52813 continues to draw coverage as additional outlets report on the critical path traversal vulnerability that allows authenticated attackers to achieve remote code execution through unsanitized organization usernames. The flaw, reported by Aikido researcher Jorian Woltjer and fixed in Gogs 0.14.3, lets attackers create organizations with directory traversal sequences in the name, placing bare repositories at attacker-chosen filesystem locations and writing malicious Git hook files. With self-registration enabled by default and over 2,300 internet-exposed instances tracked by Shadowserver, the combination of default open registration and straightforward privilege escalation makes this a high-priority patch target.

The [TanStack Query](stories/hackers-compromise-tanstack-query-npm-package-to-steal-developer/) npm supply-chain compromise continues to be analyzed as security researchers dig into the tradecraft behind 10 malicious versions of @7nohe/openapi-react-query-codegen published within a 20-minute window. The attackers abused a weakness in a GitHub Actions workflow to carry valid provenance attestations, meaning attestation checks that normally signal trust cannot catch a compromised workflow itself. The payload identifies itself as "Trinitite: Sponsored by Preview 2 Effects" and uses binding.gyp during installation to execute obfuscated code via Python's class hierarchy, ultimately running a Node.js payload that steals developer credentials and can backdoor repositories.

The breach adds to the growing list of major corporations experiencing data exposure incidents affecting their workforce, and the lack of specificity about what personal information was compromised leaves employees uncertain about their exposure to identity theft or other follow-on attacks.
