[UPDATE] The delta on Ivanti is November 2 patches for two more Connect Secure zero-days chained in the ongoing attacks, CVE-2026-111357 (CVSS 8.8, missing authentication for a critical function letting an authenticated local attacker escalate privilege) and CVE-2026-118527 (CVSS 4.3, predictable identifiers enabling remote file upload to a known disk location), with fixes in 25.1.1.3 and 22.8R2.6, so anyone who stopped at 25.1.1.2 or 22.8R2.5 must upgrade again in the [Ivanti Connect Secure chain](stories/ivanti-connect-secure-cve-2026-104935-active-exploitation/). Volexity-tracked UTA0576 has chained these with the CVE-2026-104935 auth bypass for unauthenticated root since at least October 1, and CISA added the pair to KEV on October 30, which confirms patching alone never closed the path and post-upgrade hunts must still assume log-wiping intrusions.

[REVISIT] Alpine Linux disclosed November 1 that attackers tried to exploit GitLab CVE-2026-97229 against its development infrastructure on October 20 and 21, repeatedly crashing GitLab processes and coming close to success, so the project rebuilt its GitLab server from scratch and reset all passwords because missing logs and no outbound-connection records meant compromise could not be ruled out in the [GitLab CVE-2026-97229 flaw](stories/gitlab-cve-2026-97229-critical-unauthenticated-arbitrary-code-ex/). The bug is a CVSS 10.0 integer overflow in the Onigmo regular-expression compiler that gives unauthenticated remote code execution through a crafted CI/CD pipeline file, reported through HackerOne and fixed in 19.2.8, 19.3.4 and 19.4.2, and the Alpine incident is the first concrete near-miss against real infrastructure, which moves self-managed instances from hypothetical exposure to demonstrated targeting.

[NEW] Canonical disclosed CVE-2026-112816, a CVSS 10.0 authentication bypass in LXD and MicroCloud where the HTTPS listener validates only the leaf of a presented certificate chain, so anyone with network access to the API carrying any trusted client's public certificate can impersonate a trusted identity without the private key and take full cluster and VM control in the [Canonical LXD and MicroCloud bypass](stories/canonical-lxd-and-microcloud-critical-authentication-bypass-cve/). Fixed releases are 4.0.15, 5.0.11, 5.21.9 and 6.10.1 with NVD, Ubuntu, GHSA and press corroboration, though Ubuntu rates it medium priority, so treat the CVSS 10.0 and the trivially replayable public-certificate primitive as the real severity signal and upgrade Internet-facing listeners first.

[NEW] Linksys E8450 routers running firmware 1.2.00.360516 carry a second CVSS 10.0 stack buffer overflow, CVE-2026-120283, through strcpy in /www/cgi-bin/portal.cgi via the hidden_timezone argument, reachable remotely without credentials for arbitrary code execution in the [Linksys E8450 overflow](stories/linksys-e8450-critical-stack-buffer-overflow-cve-2026-120283/). ThreatAft notes the same file, the same firmware ceiling and the same vendor silence as CVE-2025-6751 sixteen months earlier, with no patch or fixed version named, so exposed units cannot be patched into safety and must be isolated from WAN management or replaced while owners hunt for portal.cgi and hidden_timezone probing.

[NEW] The November 2 Node.js advisory batches ten vulnerabilities across the 22.x, 24.x and 26.x lines plus end-of-life releases, headlined by CVE-2026-111497 (Node2Shell), a Critical 9.2 out-of-bounds write in the Brotli decoder in node:zlib, alongside Highs CVE-2026-105482, CVE-2026-105483 and CVE-2026-106069 in the [Node.js critical patch](stories/security-advisory-node-js-critical-security-patch-addressing-nod/). The same advisory layers in an eMudhra CA compromise affecting TLS certificate validation, hostname verification and X.509 chain trust, and because the Brotli decoder flaw also reaches Python stacks through brotlicffi, operators should patch every affected release line and re-verify certificates rather than assuming a runtime upgrade alone restores trust.

[NEW] The FBI formally attributed the record October 29 Binance heist to North Korean TraderTraitor actors in an October 31 advisory issued with the Secret Service and IRS-CI, naming 608 Ethereum addresses, 19 Bitcoin addresses and one Solana address holding or previously holding proceeds of the roughly 6.3 billion dollar theft that emptied multi-chain hot wallets in about twenty minutes in the [FBI TraderTraitor attribution](stories/fbi-flags-over-600-addresses-tied-to-north-korea-s-binance-heist/). The Bureau says the actors are converting assets into Ethereum and through DeFi and instant-swap services, and it explicitly calls on THORChain operators, RPC node runners, instant exchanges, DeFi services and analytics firms to refuse the listed addresses, which makes that address set live blocking intelligence rather than historical context.

[UPDATE] The delta on Chainalysis is a November 2 company statement carried by SecurityWeek that the October intrusion is not related to the Binance heist, paired with scope confirmation that no investigation content, passwords, credentials or session tokens were reached and only user IDs, full names, email addresses and organization role labels were accessed in the [Chainalysis breach](stories/chainalysis-discloses-data-breach-from-offensive-agentic-techniq/). The vendor says it is still determining per-organization impact while working with external experts against what it calls offensive agentic techniques, so customers should still audit exposed role labels for team-structure leakage, rotate platform-tied tokens as a precaution and watch for the promised per-organization breakdown.

[NEW] Polymarket will return 3.9 million dollars to traders after the supply-chain script incident, comprising about 2.7 million stolen plus 1.19 million covering roughly 5,500 wallets hit by halts or forced transfers, with all but 37 of 726 directly affected wallets made whole by October 30 in the [Polymarket reimbursement](stories/polymarket-returns-3-9-million-to-traders-as-debugbear-cites-dns/). DebugBear attributes its part to a DNS hijack serving the malicious JavaScript, and Hudson Rock linked the injected code to an attacker wallet draining positions through open sessions rather than stolen keys, so the remediation to watch is session and third-party-script controls, not seed-phrase hygiene.

[REVISIT] Reuters confirmed November 1 that a leak site run with Distributed Denial of Secrets published data on more than 41,000 Department of Homeland Security employees and contractors, including covert, undercover and surveillance roles plus CISA and ICE leadership, with records dating to at least 2023 and covering nearly a third of the ICE workforce in the [DHS employee leak](stories/leak-site-publishes-data-on-more-than-41-000-dhs-employees-obtai/). The group calling itself the Department of Peace tied the release to DHS enforcement statements, DHS acknowledged it is working to address a potential breach, and the verified presence of current CISA staff in the dataset makes this a substantive identity-exposure event for targeted phishing rather than an unverified dump claim.

[NEW] Microsoft and Google Threat Intelligence Group reporting summarized November 2 describes North Korean actors delegating large parts of post-compromise operations to ARTEX, an autonomous AI penetration-testing framework active since late August that compresses intrusion, privilege escalation and data theft sequences into seconds in the [ARTEX weaponization](stories/hackers-weaponize-artex-ai-agents-to-automate-network-intrusions/). The significance is dwell-time collapse rather than a new malware family, so the defensive response sits in EDR speed and AI-driven egress detection, and the disclosure dovetails with the CrowdStrike VAPOR PANDA campaign already tracked here as a second independent vendor sighting of the same tooling class.

[NEW] Cisco Talos reporting amplified November 2 by The Hacker News documents VOLTA-AUTODOXER, a self-deleting Windows infostealer that steals browser passwords, Microsoft OAuth tokens, cookies, payment cards, crypto seed phrases and live Discord, Telegram, Steam and Roblox sessions, then does a second Gmail-centered sweep and pipes the bundle through OpenRouter to Claude Opus 4.6, falling back to Kimi K2 and Gemini 2.5 Flash, delivering the AI-written victim ranking as AI_Analysis.txt inside volta_dox.zip to a Telegram channel in the [VOLTA infostealer](stories/volta-infostealer-uses-claude-gemini-and-kimi-ai-to-profile-vict/). Talos has not confirmed in-the-wild use and the sample looks built for sale, but the artifacts are immediately huntable, including the VOLTA-AUTODOXER string, OpenRouter LLM traffic, and the AI_Analysis.txt and volta_dox.zip filenames in Telegram-bound uploads.

[NEW] Kaspersky reporting describes NetamLoader targeting custom Windows 10 builds used by an Asian government, using .NET AppDomainManager injection for in-memory execution and persistence through malicious .config files in the [NetamLoader campaign](stories/netamloader-targets-custom-windows-10-builds-used-by-asian-gover/). No victim count or country is named yet, so the actionable items are the injection mechanism and the government-build targeting profile while waiting on sample hashes and IOCs from follow-on vendor reporting.
