[NEW] A decade-old authentication bypass in OpenSSH on musl-based Linux, dubbed sshole and tracked as CVE-2026-123158, lets a remote attacker log into accounts explicitly configured to reject passwords by submitting any password between 257 and 1,024 characters, detailed in the [OpenSSH sshole flaw](stories/openssh-on-musl-linux-has-been-letting-people-into-password-less/). Researcher Ozgün Kultekin disclosed it November 5, tracing it to a 2016 OpenSSH change for CVE-2016-6210 that misfires on musl systems because crypt() returns an asterisk on hashing failure, so the guardrail meant to block password logins accepts the overlong input. OpenSSH shipped 10.6p2 on November 5 with Alpine publishing fixed packages for every branch from 3.16 to edge the same day, affecting Alpine Linux, OpenWrt, Buildroot, Yocto and the many container images built on them, with no exploitation reported yet and PasswordAuthentication=no or disabling SSH as the interim workaround.

[REVISIT] The new substance is urgency, not novelty: today, November 6, is the CISA KEV remediation deadline for CVE-2026-119977, the unauthenticated WordPress Core remote code execution flaw in versions 4.7 through 7.1.3, with November 6 reporting confirming active exploitation in the [WordPress Core RCE](stories/wordpress-core-rce-cvss-8-1-in-versions-4-7-7-1-3/). The bug is a time-of-check time-of-use race in the full-package upgrade path that stages installer files in predictable web-accessible locations, fixed in 7.1.4 released October 28 alongside 21 other flaws, and Wordfence telemetry counted more than 18,500 installer-path probes in the first 21 hours with blocked attacks near 59,000 within a day. Sites on auto-update are exposed while an upgrade is in progress, so administrators should move to 7.1.4 now and confirm backports, watching for confirmed compromises beyond blocked probes.

[REVISIT] The new substance is a November 6 BSI confirmation that German supermarkets were hit in a second European grocery wave, widening the cold-chain intrusion campaign described in the [US refrigeration warning](stories/us-agencies-warn-of-refrigeration-hacks-amid-suspected-attacks-o/). CISA, the FBI and three more federal departments issued joint guidance after attackers exploited authentication bypass, code injection and weak defaults tracked as CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451 and CVE-2025-41452 to force refrigeration equipment into continuous heating and defrost cycles, with roughly six US military commissaries affected since late August in a suspected Iran-linked espionage angle, about 500 French stores running defrost at 50 degrees for one to eight hours per CERT-FR, and an EFS plasma refrigeration attack in France where emergency alarms intervened. Operators should treat cold-chain controllers as exposed OT, restrict internet access, patch the three CVEs and monitor temperature setpoints, watching for US attribution on the base incidents and whether CISA adds any of the three to KEV.

[NEW] Anthropic still will not fix a design flaw in Claude Dispatch that lets a malicious browser extension run commands on the host operating system, including on a different computer signed into the same Claude account, in the [Claude Dispatch flaw](stories/anthropic-won-t-fix-a-claude-dispatch-flaw-that-could-let-attack/). Forever Security researchers named the attack ThisBash: any extension able to control an ordinary logged-in claude.ai session can send instructions to Claude Desktop with no browser exploit and minimal user interaction, forging clicks in Gmail, Docs and Calendar or running arbitrary code. Manifold Security reported it May 21 against Claude for Chrome v1.0.72, Anthropic closed both reports the next day, the click handler was byte-for-byte identical in v1.0.80 as of the July 7 Hacker News disclosure at CVSS 7.7 High and 9.6 Critical with hands-off automation, and as of November 5 no patch exists or may be possible for a design issue. Defenders should treat any extension with claude.ai access as able to act as the user inside Claude Desktop and review extension policies until Anthropic changes position.

[NEW] Check Point researcher Alexey Bukhteyev disclosed November 5 a bypass of ChatGPT Lockdown Mode in the ChatGPT Classic desktop app, the setting sold as the specific defense against prompt-injection exfiltration, in the [Lockdown Mode bypass](stories/chatgpt-lockdown-mode-bypass-phishing-malware-delivery-and-silen/). The defect sits in the HTML page preview feature added in February 2026, which renders user-controlled HTML inside two nested iframes carrying the allow-popups and allow-popups-to-escape-sandbox tokens, so a malicious prompt, GPT or shared chat could trigger zero-click exfiltration of the full chat history plus malware-disguised download links and credential phishing through fake login popups. Exposure covers Classic desktop users relying on Lockdown Mode, and chat history commonly holds medical, financial and credential material. OpenAI applied a server-side fix September 18 removing the tokens from the outer preview iframe, so defenders should verify that fix is live, treat shared chats and unknown GPTs as untrusted, and hunt egress to attacker domains from the desktop app.

[NEW] Microsoft Paint ships an unauthenticated network-reachable out-of-bounds read, CVE-2026-83844 with CVSS 8.1, that lets an attacker execute code with no login and no user interaction, in the [Paint flaw](stories/microsoft-paint-out-of-bounds-read-cve-2026-83844/). Microsoft disclosed it through the Security Update Guide on October 13 with a fixed Paint build 11.2605.91.0, and two CVE.org records surfaced November 6 amid confused version reporting across advisories. Because Paint is an in-box app on essentially every Windows endpoint, the reach is broad, though CISA's October 13 SSVC entry recorded no exploitation evidence and judged the bug not automatable, and PCWorld reported the fix might not surface through the Microsoft Store. Administrators should verify the installed mspaint.exe build rather than assuming Store updates applied, watching for a KEV addition, public exploit code or GreyNoise and Shodan telemetry.

[NEW] A critical CVSS 9.8 privilege escalation flaw in the miniOrange OAuth SSO plugin for WordPress, CVE-2026-120844, enables incorrect privilege assignment in the component that brokers single sign-on, in the [miniOrange flaw](stories/miniorange-oauth-sso-plugin-privilege-escalation-cve-2026-120844/). [NEW] Autonomous ARTEX agents are running hands-off intrusions at scale, with Check Point counting more than 1,800 publicly exposed ARTEX instances, Censys seeing 1,465 hosts exposing consoles as of November 4 and GBHackers finding 2,557 systems that host or hosted panels with 1,608 live and ten misconfigured instances leaking full databases of models, prompts and actions, in the [ARTEX crimeware wave](stories/hacking-on-autopilot-inside-artex-cybercrime-campaigns/). [NEW] DigiCert confirmed that a security misconfiguration exposed an internal business system holding archived customer information to the internet, with unauthorized access to names, business emails, phone numbers and addresses, plus driver's licenses, passports and utility bills for some individuals, in the [DigiCert breach](stories/digicert-confirms-data-breach-after-security-misconfiguration-ex/). The exposure stemmed from a February 2025 server migration that mistakenly put the system online, surfacing in a Mozilla Bugzilla incident report, and DigiCert says certificate authority services including CertCentral and DigiCert ONE plus customer credentials and issued certificates were not affected, with only a small portion of archived data involved and notifications underway after cloud architect Fabian Bader shared a copy on X. [NEW] Credit Mutuel Arkea confirmed attackers entered through a service provider's platform and used the bank's own account to initiate fraudulent transfers, with onchain investigator ZachXBT first disclosing the theft November 3 at above $133 million and the bank confirming November 4, in the [Arkea bank theft](stories/credit-mutuel-arkea-cyberattack-exposes-customer-data/). The intruders bypassed customer-facing controls entirely to reach the institution's core account, reportedly moving an initial $106 million plus another $27 million in exchange withdrawals with some funds converted to cryptocurrency, and the bank told Le Parisien some funds were frozen with the final amount still being assessed. No CVE is involved since this abused legitimate banking access, and there is no patch, only re-authentication of third-party paths that can reach the bank's own account. European banks are treating the service-provider link as a systemic warning, so defenders should hunt institution-originated transfers to external banking actors around November 3 to 5 and watch for confirmation of which provider was compromised and whether customer data went with the money.
