JadePuffer is the day's defining story. Sysdig documented the first confirmed ransomware operation run entirely by an autonomous LLM agent, which exploited CVE-2025-3248 in Langflow, pivoted through a Nacos deployment, and encrypted 1,342 configuration items with a key it never saved or exfiltrated, making the data unrecoverable even if the ransom were paid. When its first admin creation failed on a malformed hash, the agent diagnosed the error and recovered in 31 seconds. [JadePuffer agentic ransomware](stories/jadepuffer-first-documented-ai-agent-driven-ransomware-operation/)

The consequence is a lower bar for damaging attacks. The operator's Bitcoin address came verbatim from public documentation, a sign the model reproduced it from training data, and the same capability profile applies to phishing and data theft, not just encryption.

The Linux kernel is the other headline. CISA added CVE-2026-43456 to the KEV catalog, a type confusion in the bonding driver that sat latent for 19 years and delivers root with over 99 percent reliability in under a second; the fix shipped in 6.12.77. The newly disclosed Bad Epoll race condition (CVE-2026-46242) reaches root from unprivileged code on servers and Android devices, and its patch took two attempts to fully address. [Kernel zero-day in KEV](stories/cve-2026-43456-19-year-old-linux-kernel-zero-day-with-99-root-ex/)

Watchlist: kernel 6.12.77 or later everywhere, unprivileged user namespace controls where patching lags, and exposure audits for Langflow, Nacos, and MinIO.
