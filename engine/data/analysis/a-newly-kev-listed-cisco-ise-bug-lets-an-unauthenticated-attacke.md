Cisco Identity Services Engine (ISE) has two actively exploited zero-days being used in the wild right now. CVE-2026-76460 lets an unauthenticated attacker get root on ISE appliances without any credentials, scoring a perfect 10.0 CVSS. This is the second actively exploited zero-day Cisco has confirmed in as many days, following CVE-2026-20192 which also targets ISE. Both are now in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog with federal patch deadlines.

The broader Cisco advisory covers ten CVEs total: CVE-2026-20192, CVE-2026-20234, CVE-2026-20282, CVE-2026-20283, CVE-2026-20284, CVE-2026-20331, CVE-2026-20332, CVE-2026-76423, CVE-2026-76460, and CVE-2026-76461. The root-access bugs are the most critical since they require no authentication. Cisco has shipped patches and is urging immediate upgrades to ISE or ISE-PIC versions 3.5 Patch 4, 3.4 Patch 7, 3.3 Patch 12, 3.2 Patch 11, or 3.1 Patch 12. No workarounds exist except using infrastructure access control lists to restrict traffic.

ISE is the access control and posture assessment platform for enterprise networks. Root compromise means an attacker can intercept authentication traffic, modify posture policies, or pivot laterally across the network. The fact that two separate zero-days are being exploited simultaneously suggests either multiple threat actor groups targeting the same platform or a coordinated campaign. Organizations should check access logs for suspicious usernames and review distributed deployment logs for anomalies.

With 21 sources covering this across security publications, threat intelligence platforms, and news outlets, this is one of the most widely tracked Cisco vulnerabilities in recent months. The score of 10.0 reflects the maximum severity, active exploitation, and broad coverage.
