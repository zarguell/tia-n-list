Cisco Identity Services Engine (ISE) has two actively exploited zero-days being used in the wild right now. CVE-2026-76460 lets an unauthenticated attacker get root on ISE appliances without any credentials. This is the second actively exploited zero-day Cisco has confirmed in as many days, following CVE-2026-20192 which also targets ISE. Both are now in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog.

The broader Cisco advisory covers ten CVEs total across ISE, including CVE-2026-20234, CVE-2026-20282, CVE-2026-20283, CVE-2026-20284, CVE-2026-20331, CVE-2026-20332, CVE-2026-76423, CVE-2026-76460, and CVE-2026-76461. The root-access bugs are the most critical since they require no authentication at all. Cisco has shipped patches and is urging immediate upgrades.

ISE is the access control and posture assessment platform for enterprise networks. Root compromise means an attacker can intercept authentication traffic, modify posture policies, or pivot laterally across the network. With 21 sources covering this across security publications, threat intelligence platforms, and news outlets, this is one of the most widely tracked Cisco vulnerabilities in recent months. Organizations running ISE should patch immediately and check logs for signs of exploitation.
