The FBI and RCMP seized domains linked to NightmareStresser, a DDoS-for-hire platform that has been operational since at least 2022. The U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Alaska announced the takedown, targeting infrastructure that paying customers used to launch hundreds of thousands of attacks or attempted attacks against targets worldwide. The operation is part of the ongoing Operation Power Off series of DDoS-for-hire takedowns.

NightmareStresser was a booter/stresser service accessible via standard web interfaces, allowing anyone with a cryptocurrency wallet to purchase denial-of-service attacks against指定 targets. The service abstracted the technical complexity of launching volumetric, protocol, and application-layer DDoS attacks behind a simple dashboard. These services are frequently used for extortion, competitive sabotage, and personal disputes.

The seizure adds to the growing list of law enforcement actions against DDoS-for-hire infrastructure, following takedowns against services like Webstresser, vDOS, and others. However, the DDoS-for-hire ecosystem remains resilient, with new services frequently emerging to replace seized ones. The RCMP's involvement signals cross-border coordination between U.S. and Canadian law enforcement on cybercrime enforcement.
