Revolut disclosed a data-security incident in which an attacker impersonated a government agency using a fraudulent email account. The attacker obtained valid domain-authentication credentials, so Revolut staff treated the request as legitimate and responded with customer records. Revolut says its core systems, mobile app, and customer accounts were not compromised, and biometric facial telemetry was not involved.

The exposed data is extensive: names, dates of birth, occupations, postal addresses, email addresses, phone numbers, passport or driving-license copies, verification selfies, account statements, IBANs, account-status details, wallet reference numbers, withdrawal records, and full transaction histories. Attackers have since begun publishing stolen customer ID documents and are threatening daily data releases.

This is a social-engineering breach, not a technical compromise, but the impact is comparable. Statched KYC documents and selfies enable identity fraud, targeted phishing, and crypto scams. Revolut users should expect impersonation attempts using their real financial details and transaction patterns. Financial institutions routinely process government data requests, and this incident highlights the risk of that process being weaponized when domain-level authentication passes inspection.
