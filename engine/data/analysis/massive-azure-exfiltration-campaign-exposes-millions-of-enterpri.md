A threat actor using the alias TheHatman is selling massive internal employee directories on cybercrime forums, claiming the data was extracted directly from Microsoft Azure and Entra ID tenants using compromised credentials. The dumps cover multiple Fortune 500 companies, with McDonald's, Vodafone, Tata Consultancy Services, HCL Technologies, Kyndryl, Gap Inc., and InterContinental Hotels Group named so far, spanning IT services, hospitality, telecommunications, retail, and logistics. Researchers who reviewed samples assessed the data as highly legitimate, citing corporate email addresses and field names matching standard Azure directory exports, with millions of enterprise records offered.

The campaign matters for two reasons. First, the claimed attack path is credential-driven identity compromise of Azure and Entra ID tenants, which points to weak MFA enforcement and standing credential reuse in large enterprises; TCS, HCL, and Kyndryl are themselves IT services and managed-infrastructure providers, so their directories could amplify downstream supply-chain targeting. Second, sold employee directories are raw material for phishing, credential stuffing, and targeted social engineering against the named organizations.

Watch for victim organizations confirming or denying the claims, for the actor publishing additional tenants, and for evidence of whether the data was genuinely pulled via compromised credentials or assembled from prior breaches. No CVEs are attached, so the exposure is operational. Enterprises with Entra ID tenants should treat forum claims of directory sales as a prompt to audit sign-in logs for anomalous token use and re-enforce MFA.
