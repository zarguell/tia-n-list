ENGIE, the French energy group operating in 30 countries with over 90,000 employees and about EUR 71.9 billion in annual revenue, disclosed that attackers stole customer data after breaching a marketing platform used by business partners for customer prospecting and marketing of energy offers. ENGIE's internal IT systems were not compromised; the company cut off the attackers' access. The incident is limited to customers in France. The data is limited to customers' contact details and certain information relating to their energy contracts; no banking data was affected. Breach notification letters sent to affected customers list first and last names, mailing address, email address, phone number, Delivery Point (PDL) or Metering and Estimation Point (PCE), customer ID, and contract number. ENGIE did not attribute the attack to a specific threat group, and no ransomware or extortion gang had claimed the breach before publication.

This is a third-party exposure rather than a compromise of ENGIE's own systems, but the stolen identifiers are enough to make targeted fraud credible. A contract number, PDL or PCE identifier, and customer ID let an attacker build a convincing energy-bill or account-support pretext, so the absence of banking data does not remove the risk. The number of affected customers was not disclosed, which means the blast radius is still unknown and defenders should assume a broad France-wide customer set rather than a narrow subset. This disclosure also stands apart from the separate 2025 Scattered LAPSUS$ Hunters leak of records from Engie Records, the retail energy supply subsidiary of ENGIE North America: that was a Salesforce instance claim, while this incident is a partner marketing platform with no extortion claim attached.

Defender actions should focus on access and phishing rather than a vendor patch: affected customers should treat any unsolicited contact that references a contract number, PDL, or PCE as suspicious, enable multi-factor authentication on ENGIE customer accounts, and verify support requests through official channels. ENGIE and its partners should audit and revoke access to the marketing platform, reset partner credentials, enforce least privilege on customer-data exports, and review that tool's access logs for the unusual data access activity ENGIE described. Phishing hunts should key on the exact fields in the notification letters, especially phone numbers, email addresses, mailing addresses, and the PDL, PCE, customer ID, and contract number combinations.

The assessment would change if ENGIE discloses the number of affected customers, if a threat group is attributed or claims the data, if customers report phishing that uses contract identifiers, or if evidence emerges that the compromise extended beyond the partner marketing platform into ENGIE systems.
