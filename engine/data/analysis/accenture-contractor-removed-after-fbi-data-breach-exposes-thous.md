The FBI removed an Accenture contractor after a breach of the bureau's PeopleSoft jobs platform exposed personal data on thousands of employees. FBI cyber chief Brett Leatherman put the cause on record: a security failure on a platform managed by a third party, after the contractor failed to implement a security patch explicitly issued to secure it. Reuters identifies the system as Oracle PeopleSoft and the integrator as Accenture, which holds the patch management and custom code role at the bureau. The FBI has not named the missed patch, but the only public PeopleSoft patch track that fits is CVE-2026-35273, the CVSS 9.8 unauthenticated remote code execution flaw in PeopleTools 8.61 and 8.62 that Oracle disclosed in an out-of-band alert on June 10, 2026 and that CISA KEV-listed on June 12. The stolen data reportedly includes addresses, phone numbers, spouse details, intelligence and surveillance roles, and private medical information.

This was not a zero-day surprise, and that is what makes it damning. Mandiant and Google Threat Intelligence documented ShinyHunters, tracked as UNC6240, mass-exploiting this exact flaw from May 27 through June 9, 2026 across more than 100 organizations, 68% of them US higher education, and in late September the group renewed the campaign with URL-encoded requests that bypass WAF rules keyed to the literal /PSEMHUB/ path. Any owner that answered the June alert with a firewall rule instead of the patch was re-exposed in September, four months after a fix existed. The deeper failure is structural: the FBI carried the harm, exposure of surveillance roles and medical records is targeting and blackmail material, not spam-list filler, while the patching obligation sat with an integrator that never applied the fix. Procurement accountability, not attacker sophistication, is the story, and it is why this breach outranks the other thirty reported today.

Defenders running PeopleSoft should act on three fronts. Inventory PeopleTools builds first: 8.61 and 8.62 need Oracle's June 10, 2026 Security Alert fix, and the September 2026 Critical Patch Update shipped 16 additional PeopleSoft patches worth applying in the same pass. Where patching lags, disable the Environment Management Hub outright, because WAF rules matching the literal /PSEMHUB/* path are demonstrably bypassable via URL encoding. Hunt for the documented tradecraft: inbound requests to /PSEMHUB/ and /PSIGW/HttpListeningConnector, MeshCentral agents and azurenetfiles[.]net staging masquerading as Azure, connections to 142.11.200[.]186-190, 108.174.202[.]99, or 176.120.22[.]24, zstd-compressed SSH exfiltration, fanout bash scripts moving with stolen credentials, and README-IF-YOU-SEE-THIS-YOUVE-BEEN-HACKED.TXT dropped in WebLogic directories. Federal teams should verify whether their integrator, not just their own patch pipeline, applied the fix, since that is precisely where the FBI's chain broke.

Two developments would change this assessment. Confirmation of the second, unpatched PeopleSoft zero-day ShinyHunters claimed in September, which BleepingComputer could not verify at the time, would widen exposure beyond PeopleTools 8.61 and 8.62 and reset the remediation clock. Formal assignment of the missed patch to a specific build and date would convert a personnel action into a procurement enforcement case with implications for every agency relying on the same integrator. A leak of the claimed 2 TB to 3 TB taken from FBI-managed GovCloud systems would confirm scope and put applicant and employee data into circulation.
