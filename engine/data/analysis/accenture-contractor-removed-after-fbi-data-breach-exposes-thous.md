The FBI has removed an Accenture contractor from its assignment after a breach exposed personal data on thousands of bureau employees. Reuters reported the finding on Tuesday, citing two sources, and FBI cyber chief Brett Leatherman confirmed that the incident resulted from a security failure on a platform managed by a third party after a contractor failed to implement a security patch explicitly issued to secure it.

Reporting identifies the affected system as Oracle's PeopleSoft human resources platform, with Accenture responsible for software patch management and custom code maintenance at the FBI. Oracle had provided the patches that were never applied. ShinyHunters claimed responsibility for the intrusion last month, saying it exploited PeopleSoft to break into the FBI's jobs site, and Google recently warned that the group had been targeting vulnerable PeopleSoft instances to steal data. Stolen data reportedly includes employee addresses, phone numbers, spouse information, intelligence and surveillance roles, and private medical information.

The case matters beyond its high-profile victim. It ties a major breach to a specific, preventable failure: a vendor missed a patch the vendor of the underlying platform had already shipped. That puts contracting and oversight practices under scrutiny, since the government agency carried the reputational and workforce harm while the patching obligation sat with an integrator.

Watch for whether Accenture faces contractual or procurement consequences, whether the Justice Department widens the review to other Accenture-managed systems, and whether ShinyHunters follows through on leaks. Any new PeopleSoft exploitation reported elsewhere will now be read against this case as a benchmark for third-party patch accountability.
