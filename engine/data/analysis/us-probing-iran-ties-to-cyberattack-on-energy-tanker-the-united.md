The United States is investigating whether Iran was behind cyberattacks targeting two energy tankers, according to reporting from EuropeSays. The investigation examines potential Iranian state-sponsored involvement in disruptions to maritime energy transport, a critical chokepoint for global oil supply chains. The attacks targeted tankers carrying crude oil, raising the stakes for both cybersecurity and physical safety of maritime operations.

Iran has a documented history of cyber operations against maritime and energy infrastructure, including previous attacks on shipping companies and oil facilities. Attribution in maritime cyber incidents is complicated by the convergence of IT and OT systems on vessels, the international nature of shipping registries, and the difficulty of distinguishing state-sponsored activity from criminal or hacktivist operations. The investigation is being conducted at the federal level, suggesting initial indicators point toward a nation-state actor.

This incident fits a pattern of escalating cyber activity targeting energy transportation infrastructure. Organizations in the maritime and energy sectors should review their network segmentation between IT and OT systems, monitor for anomalous vessel tracking data, and coordinate with CISA and sector-specific information sharing organizations.
