IDScan, an identity verification company, confirmed on September 4, 2026 that hackers accessed customer data stored in its cloud platform. The company said it learned on or around September 1 that certain data may have been accessed without authorization. The exposed information includes customers' full names and driver's license or other government-issued identification numbers. BleepingComputer reported that multiple lawsuits had been filed against IDScan after hackers allegedly breached the company and offered access to a database containing more than 153 million driver's license scans. IDScan's breach notification was published on September 4 but was configured with a noindex directive, instructing search engines not to index the page.

The scale of this breach is notable: 153 million driver's license scans represent a significant portion of the U.S. driving-age population. Driver's license data is a high-value target for identity theft because it provides the photo ID, full name, address, and date of birth that financial institutions and government agencies use for identity verification. Unlike credit card numbers, driver's license data cannot be easily rotated or replaced.

Organizations that use IDScan for identity verification should monitor for unauthorized use of their customers' identity documents. The noindex configuration on the breach notification page is a red flag that suggests IDScan may have been attempting to minimize the public visibility of the incident. The ongoing investigation has not yet determined the full scope of data accessed or the attack vector.
