SAP confirmed suspicious activity in its development environment on November 26, 2026 and is handling the incident internally under the name Project Granite. Der Spiegel reported that attackers targeted SAP's GitHub presence and may have used agentic AI systems. Unnamed sources cited by the outlet said employee authentication tokens were stolen and used to copy and modify source code. SAP has not confirmed the token theft, identified the repositories involved or said whether proprietary code was accessed, so those details remain media-sourced claims rather than company findings.

Handelsblatt reported that SAP restricted GitHub access, halted automated software-development workflows and replaced signing keys. Those measures are the clearest reported operational artifacts of the response, although the specific actions were reported by media rather than detailed in SAP's public statement. SAP told BleepingComputer and Recorded Future News that its investigation had found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data or downstream compromise of customer systems. None of the cited reporting established that altered code reached software delivered to customers.

The main risk is potential damage to source-code integrity and to the credentials and signing infrastructure around SAP's development process. No threat actor has been named and there is no public claim of responsibility; the reported use of AI is also unconfirmed. Watch for SAP to identify affected repositories, establish the scope of token use, validate signing keys and build outputs, and state whether any modified code entered customer releases. Until then, source-code theft and tampering should be described as reported, not confirmed by SAP.
