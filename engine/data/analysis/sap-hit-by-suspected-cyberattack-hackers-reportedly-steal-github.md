SAP confirmed suspicious activity in its IT environment on November 26 and said it had taken measures to protect customers and secure services. The company told SecurityWeek and BleepingComputer that its investigation had found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data. Internal communications placed the activity in GitHub, and German outlets reported that SAP restricted GitHub access, suspended automated development workflows and replaced signing keys.

Der Spiegel reported the incident under the internal codename Project Granite and said sources claimed attackers stole employee authentication tokens and used them to copy and alter source code. Handelsblatt and other outlets repeated the token-theft account, but SAP did not confirm those details, name the affected repositories or identify a threat actor. The reporting also does not establish that altered code entered software delivered to customers.

The concern is the integrity of enterprise-software development and release infrastructure rather than a confirmed customer-data breach. Watch for the repository scope, whether signing keys or build artifacts were exposed, evidence of tampered commits in released products and any customer guidance. Until SAP or investigators substantiate the token and code-tampering claims, those elements should remain attributed reports rather than established facts.
