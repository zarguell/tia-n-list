SAP told Recorded Future News on Friday 27 November 2026 that it had identified suspicious activity in its environment and immediately moved to protect customers and secure services, without describing the nature or extent of the intrusion. The company's statement says the investigation has found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data. Der Spiegel reported the incident a day earlier from internal company communications, and the German Press Agency (dpa), which reviewed portions of the same material, reported that SAP had warned employees about suspicious activity in its GitHub environment several days before the story became public and advised them to assess whether their login credentials needed changing. SAP ran the response under the internal codename Project Granite.

The reported mechanism, still unconfirmed by SAP, is theft of employee authentication tokens from the GitHub-hosted development environment, which sources say let attackers copy and modify source code. SAP has not named affected repositories, has not identified an actor, and has not said whether altered code reached anything shipped to customers. Earlier coverage added that the company restricted GitHub access, suspended automated development workflows and replaced signing keys, which is consistent with a token-compromise response but does not establish code tampering in released products.

The material risk here is release-pipeline integrity, not confirmed customer data exposure. The questions worth tracking are the repository scope, whether build signing material or artifacts were reachable from the compromised accounts, whether released binaries contain commits that do not match SAP's own history, and any customer guidance on patching or credential rotation. Until SAP or an investigator substantiates the token theft and code modification, those elements remain attributed press reports rather than established facts, and the no-customer-data line should be read as a statement about business data rather than a clean bill for the development chain.
