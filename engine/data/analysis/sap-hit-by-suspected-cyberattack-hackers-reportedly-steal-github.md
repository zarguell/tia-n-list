SAP confirmed on November 26, 2026 that it had identified suspicious activity in its IT environment and taken measures to protect customers and services. Internal communications placed the activity in GitHub, and German reporting named the incident Project Granite. Der Spiegel reported, citing company insiders, that attackers stole employee authentication tokens and used them to copy and alter SAP source code. SAP has not confirmed the token theft, named the repositories or said whether proprietary code was accessed.

Handelsblatt reported that SAP restricted GitHub access, suspended automated software-development workflows and replaced signing keys. SAP told SecurityWeek and BleepingComputer that its investigation had found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data, and no report showed that modified code reached software delivered to customers. No threat actor or public claim of responsibility has been identified. The reported use of agentic AI remains an insider-sourced claim, not a confirmed detail.

The significant risk is the development trust boundary: a stolen developer token can turn source repositories, build automation and release signing into a supply-chain problem even when customer data remains untouched. Watch for SAP's scope assessment, evidence of altered commits or build artifacts, and confirmation of which signing keys were rotated and when automation resumed. Customers should wait for SAP guidance on affected products rather than assuming that the absence of customer-data access rules out tampered software.
