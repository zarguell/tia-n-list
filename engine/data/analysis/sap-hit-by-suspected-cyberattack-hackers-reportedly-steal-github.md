SAP has confirmed a cybersecurity incident involving its development infrastructure after German reporting described an operation dubbed Project Granite. SecurityWeek reported that Der Spiegel cited SAP insiders and an internal employee notice, while DPA reviewed internal communications indicating the suspicious activity was found in GitHub. An insider cited by Der Spiegel said attackers obtained employee authentication tokens and used them to copy and alter source code. Those token theft and source-code claims remain reports, not confirmed findings from SAP.

SAP told SecurityWeek it detected suspicious activity and took measures to protect customers and secure services. It said that, based on its investigation to date, it had found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data. The account does not establish whether attackers reached proprietary code, and Handelsblatt reported that SAP's response included replacing signing keys, restricting GitHub access and suspending automated development operations across affected teams.

The source-code angle matters because SAP publishes developer tools, sample applications and open-source projects on GitHub, but the reports do not show that altered code entered software delivered to customers. The concrete watch points are SAP's forensic determination of which repositories and tokens were accessed, whether any code changes reached build or release pipelines, and whether the company revises its customer-data assessment. For now, the confirmed facts are a development-infrastructure incident and disruptive containment measures; source-code theft, alteration and AI assistance are not independently established by the event record.
