The UK National Cyber Security Centre reported on Tuesday a "marked rise" since September in AI-driven attacks. The warning came from three agency staff, including chief technology officer for architecture David Chismon, incident handler Kris B, and lead data scientist Ellen T. Organisations across multiple sectors have reported incidents involving what the NCSC calls agentic offensive frameworks, software built for red teaming that is now being used against real targets. Artex, a framework the NCSC said received the most attention, was reportedly used in last month's compromise of the European Commission.

The observed pattern is automated discovery and exploitation of internet-facing assets in quick succession, with impacts ranging from outages to system compromise. The NCSC said the change is in speed, persistence, and cost, driven by cheap capable open models and illegal API resellers, not in novel attack techniques. The agency explicitly said none of the exploited weaknesses are new and that the tools offer no capability a typical threat actor lacks. That framing puts the emphasis on patching known flaws and maintaining basic controls rather than on new defensive categories.

For defenders, the actionable signal is the compression of the window between vulnerability discovery and exploitation attempts across every exposed asset. The reports describe attackers sweeping all internet-facing systems rapidly, so coverage and patch latency matter more than the use of AI itself. The two accounts of the NCSC statement agree on the timeline, the frameworks, and the conclusion that the underlying weaknesses are already known.
