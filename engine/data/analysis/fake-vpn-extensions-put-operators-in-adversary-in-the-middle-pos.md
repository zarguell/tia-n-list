Socket documented a Chrome Web Store operation of 737 fake VPN and proxy extensions tied to at least 40 developer accounts, with 274 impersonating 66 well-known brands including Proton VPN, NordVPN, Surfshark, ExpressVPN and Cloudflare 1.1.1.1. Installs reached nearly 75,000, mainly Russian-speaking users seeking to bypass blocked services. BleepingComputer's coverage adds detail: 520 extensions configured Chrome to route all browser traffic through the operator's SOCKS5 proxies on port 1082, and 104 resolved their proxy hostnames through Cloudflare or Google DNS-over-HTTPS to hide the operator's domain.

The effect is a full adversary-in-the-middle position over victims' browsing. The relay server can read every destination, every TLS SNI value, the source IP, and any request body sent over plain HTTP. A shared analytics account across the publisher network ties the operation to a single actor. Socket could not analyze 212 extensions because they were already removed from the store when analysis ran.

The delivery channel is the story: users hunting for tools to bypass restrictions are prime targets for fake tooling, and the store cleanup is racing re-publication under fresh accounts. Defenders should audit installed extensions against the impersonated brand list and treat free VPN extensions as a distribution vector. Watch whether the operator republishes under new developer accounts and whether the SOCKS5 infrastructure is reused in later campaigns.
