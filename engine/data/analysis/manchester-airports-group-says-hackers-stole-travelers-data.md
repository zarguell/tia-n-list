Manchester Airports Group (MAG) confirmed that hackers breached its systems and stole customer data from Manchester, London Stansted, and East Midlands airports. The compromised information includes email addresses, phone numbers, vehicle registration numbers, postcodes, and details from Wi-Fi sign-ups, car park bookings, lounge reservations, and Fast Track purchases. No payment card data was accessed, and airport operations were not disrupted. MAG suspended its online Manage My Booking service as a precaution and redirected customers to phone support.

The breach affected a system handling data for over 66 million annual passengers across the UK's largest airport operator. MAG said it contained the intrusion, engaged external security experts, and notified law enforcement. Scott Helme highlighted the incident as emblematic of poor security hygiene across many organizations, noting the breach required no sophisticated hacking. MAG employs 40,000 people and generates 1.5 billion pounds in annual revenue.

The incident underscores the persistent vulnerability of customer-facing reservation and booking systems at critical transportation infrastructure. With three airports and tens of millions of passengers affected, the data is valuable for phishing campaigns targeting travelers. MAG advised affected customers to watch for suspicious emails and SMS messages.
