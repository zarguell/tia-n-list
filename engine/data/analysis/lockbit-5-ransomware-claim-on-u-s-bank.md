LockBit listed U.S. Bank on its data-leak site on August 20, 2026, claiming a breach and threatening to publish stolen data unless the bank made contact, with reporting pointing to a September 3 deadline roughly 14 days after the listing. The listing included no file samples, counts or details about affected systems, customers or employees. AhnLab's ASEC financial-sector roundup for August 2026, published September 8, independently records that LockBit listed usbank.com as a victim with a public disclosure deadline while stating the leak's actual scale and breach path remain unverified.

U.S. Bank is investigating and has not confirmed a compromise. A spokesperson said there is no indication internal systems are impacted and no evidence of unauthorized network access, and the bank has not said whether it received a demand or what data was allegedly taken. The listing landed while LockBit's 5.0 branch, publicly relaunched in September 2025 with cross-platform Windows, Linux and ESXi support, targets the financial sector; ASEC's same report catalogs parallel unverified claims against QNB Finansbank, Allianz Thailand, Alipay, Robinhood and Bank of Baroda, most flagged as forum claims without confirmation.

The incident is a textbook double-extortion listing: a named tier-one US bank, a countdown, and no evidence attached. That pattern keeps pressure on the victim regardless of breach reality, and the ASEC corroboration confirms the listing itself rather than any stolen data.

Watch for whether the deadline passes with or without publication, any bank confirmation or SEC/customer notification, and affiliate chatter that either supports or debunks the claim. Until then the factual record is a leak-site claim plus a bank denial, not a confirmed breach.
