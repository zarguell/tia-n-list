CVE-2024-40766 is an improper access control flaw in SonicWall SonicOS management access and SSLVPN, rated CVSS 9.3 and patched on August 22, 2024 in advisory SNWLID-2024-0015 for Gen 5, Gen 6, and Gen 7 devices on 7.0.1-5035 and older. The vendor added the line "potentially being exploited in the wild" on September 6, 2024, and kept appending attacker IPs to the advisory through November 2024. CISA put the CVE in the KEV catalog on September 9, 2024 with a September 30 federal remediation deadline and a known-ransomware-use flag. The new report is not a prediction: an MDR team describes multiple Akira cases with a SonicWall device present in recent weeks, pacing roughly 30 percent ahead of last year's total.

The exposure does not decay because the patch structurally cannot reach every device. Roughly 213,900 SonicWall VPN and management interfaces are still reachable from the internet, and NSA 2600 plus Gen 5 appliances that are end of life will never receive a fixed build, so those devices stay vulnerable until owners buy new hardware. For the victim this bug is a front door with no trace at the app layer: in the 2024 caseload Arctic Wolf documented, time from SSL VPN login to encryption ran as short as 1.5 to 2 hours, and the joint advisory revised November 13, 2025 attributes about $244.17 million in claimed proceeds to Akira as of late September 2025. That is why this story outranks any freshly disclosed CVE today: a new bug is a risk, this is a measured, ongoing loss with a two-year-old fix sitting unused.

Inventory first: any firewall on SonicOS 5.9.2.14-12o, 6.5.4.14-109n, or 7.0.1-5035 or older is vulnerable. Upgrade to 5.9.2.14-13o, 6.5.4.15.116n (6.5.2.8-2n for SM9800, NSsp 12400, NSsp 12800), or above 7.0.1-5035; Arctic Wolf cites 7.0.1-5072 as fixed for Gen 7. Where patching must wait, disable WAN management and SSLVPN from the internet or restrict both to trusted sources, force password resets on all locally managed SSLVPN accounts, and enable MFA and account lockout. Hunt in firewall logs for SSLVPN logins from hosting-provider ASNs, event ID 238 or 1080 (remote user login allowed) followed by 1079 login and IP assignment, typically on default port 4433, and treat appliance log deletion as an incident trigger. Alert or block the IPs SonicWall lists in the advisory (88.119.175.104, 45.149.172.51, 172.86.116.8, 216.146.25.208, 194.48.154.67, 162.120.71.224, 45.61.157.15), and on endpoints watch for .akira and .akiranew extensions, akira_readme.txt or fn.txt ransom notes, and the w.exe encryptor hashes published in AA24-109A.

Two developments would change this assessment. A revision to SNWLID-2024-0015 or AA24-109A adding 2026-era IOCs would signal the campaign has re-tooled against current infrastructure rather than riding the 2024 playbook. And if the 30 percent detection growth holds through year end, Akira finishes 2026 as the dominant initial-access driver in MDR data; any new SonicWall CVE chained against estates still unpatched for 40766 would make the exposure math worse, not better.
