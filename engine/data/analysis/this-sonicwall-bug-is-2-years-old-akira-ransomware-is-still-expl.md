The Akira ransomware group continues to exploit CVE-2024-40766, a critical SonicWall firewall vulnerability with a CVSS score of 9.3, despite a patch being available for two years. SonicWall fixed the flaw in August 2024, but MDR teams report that Akira intrusion cases involving SonicWall devices are running roughly 30% ahead of 2025 totals. CISA added the CVE to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog in September 2024 and updated its joint Akira advisory in November 2025.

The vulnerability exists in SonicWall's VPN and management interface components. Roughly 213,900 SonicWall VPN and management interfaces remain reachable from the public internet, providing a large attack surface for exploitation. Akira actors chain this flaw with credential harvesting and lateral movement techniques to gain initial access before deploying ransomware. The pattern is straightforward: unpatched perimeter devices remain the initial access vector for a ransomware group that has been prolific throughout 2025 and 2026.

Defenders running SonicWall appliances should verify patch status immediately, particularly for any devices with internet-facing VPN or management ports. The persistence of exploitation two years after a fix underscores the gap between vulnerability disclosure and actual remediation in enterprise environments. Organizations that have not yet migrated away from unpatched SonicWall devices face an active and growing threat from a group that has demonstrated willingness to target critical infrastructure and healthcare organizations.
