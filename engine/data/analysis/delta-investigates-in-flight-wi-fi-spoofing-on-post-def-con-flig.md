Delta Airlines is investigating an in-flight Wi-Fi spoofing incident on flight 591 from Las Vegas to Atlanta, a Monday flight carrying passengers who had attended DEF CON. Social media posts detailed a passenger using an unidentified device to set up a rogue network named "Delta WiFi Fast" that could have intercepted passengers' sensitive data. ACARS messages show the crew told ground personnel that a passenger was "trying to scam the other passengers."

Delta said cabin crew deactivated the aircraft's Wi-Fi for roughly 30 minutes, that flight safety was never in question, and that no aircraft operating systems were affected. The airline is partnering with federal law enforcement and aviation regulators on the investigation. The FBI's Atlanta office and the FAA have confirmed awareness of the incident.

The story matters on two levels. Operationally, it is a live demonstration of a long-standing attack class: spoofing a legitimate network name to harvest credentials or data from users who connect without verifying. Defensively, it shows that security conferences are now followed by real-world targeting of attendees, with a flight from DEF CON as the venue. Travelers should treat in-flight Wi-Fi as untrusted, avoid entering credentials over it, and use VPNs or verified captive portals.
