NightEagle, the APT group Kaspersky tracks as APT-Q-95, has moved from Asian targets to Russian businesses, and the 16 September 2026 GERT report lays out the mechanics. In most incidents the group entered through corporate VPNs using valid stolen credentials, with sessions originating from Russian addresses linked to Cloudflare WARP tunnels and European virtual infrastructure providers. The signature move is the GhostContainer backdoor on Microsoft Exchange, a .NET assembly whose Stub class reads C2 commands from x-owa-urlpostdata headers and patches amsi.dll and ntdll.dll to blind AMSI and the Windows event log, running entirely in memory. Delivery was not confirmed, but Kaspersky assesses with high confidence that the attackers extracted Exchange machine keys from the ASP.NET configuration and overwrote the VIEWSTATE parameter to launch the payload, a chain assembled from public parts: an exploit for CVE-2020-0688, the GhostWebShell class from ysoserial, and the Neo-reGeorg tunnel. No new Exchange vulnerability appears in this campaign; Kaspersky detects the implant as Trojan.MSIL.GhostContainer.gen.

The dates are the argument. CVE-2020-0688 was published 2020-02-11 with NVD scoring it CVSS 3.1 8.8, so the bug at the center of a 2026 espionage campaign is six years past its fix, and patch state alone decides less than it seems: GhostContainer's Stub carries code copied from machinekeyfinder-aspx that reads the validation key at runtime, meaning the VIEWSTATE technique runs on key material an attacker can harvest in memory rather than on a missing update that a scanner would flag. The group QiAnXin disclosed on 2025-07-04 stole nearly a year of email from Chinese semiconductor, quantum, AI and military organizations; the same operator class is now doing this to Russian companies with a toolchain of stolen VPN logins, a 2020 n-day and GitHub utilities, which means exposure is defined by internet-reachable Exchange and VPN estates everywhere, not by the two geographies named. The follow-on toolset tells you the objective: Impacket atexec, netsh portproxy rules, rdp2tcp over RDP virtual channels, Microsoft dev tunnels exposing port 3389, BlueKeep CVE-2019-0708 exploitation in one incident to mint an account in the Administrators and Remote Desktop Users groups, Kerberos tickets with the Forwardable, Proxiable, Renewable flag combination, then DCSync, ending at the domain controllers and every hash in the directory.

Remediation has no new patch, so precision matters. Confirm every Exchange server carries the February 2020 CVE-2020-0688 fix, and treat any host missing a 2020 update as presumptively exposed to CVE-2019-0708 (fixed May 2019) as well. Rotate and force MFA on VPN credentials, since initial access rides valid logins, and prioritize accounts that authenticated from WARP-linked or European VPS addresses. The hunt has specific anchors: in Microsoft-Windows-RemoteDesktopServices-RdpCoreTS/Operational.evtx, events 132 and 148 whose channel name is rdp2tcp or random alphanumerics instead of XPSRD, cliprdr or Microsoft::Windows::RDS::DisplayControl; DNS or TLS to *.devtunnels.ms; netsh interface portproxy add v4tov4 commands in scheduled-task history (the report's sample forwards listenport=443 to port 445); IIS and Exchange logs carrying x-owa-urlpostdata requests or the /wEPDwUKLTcyODc4 VIEWSTATE marker; downloads from github.com/mirror-js/mirror-js and github.com/browserthemes/resourcepack; and the report's MD5s: 1dcafb7f8448683281106b06dd22409a (AdobeSync.exe), 1f3034b706c78b35d8e34044e68c693a (adobe_32.exe), 3ecd1cd627d0340c92901a478a7caad8 and 631fb131a56caf4ca0f287ed73e876ab (App_Web_Container_1.dll), 4aa9fb1bf9223dfcdac920759bc7a3c7 (1c-office-plugin.exe, 1cbroker.exe, trueconf.exe). Add DCSync detection on replication of the Domain-Password object and flag TGS requests pairing Forwardable with Proxiable and Renewable.

Two developments would change this assessment. First, confirmation of the Exchange delivery method: if a zero-day surfaces, patching regains primacy; if key theft alone explains it, the durable fix is credential and machineKey rotation plus inbound RDP-to-Exchange request filtering, and Kaspersky stating the method is undetermined leaves both doors open. Second, scope reporting: victim counts or dwell times for the Russian cases (QiAnXin measured roughly a year of email access in 2025), or evidence the Asia-to-Russia shift extends further, would widen the exposure picture, and a public GhostContainer port or weaponized CVE-2020-0688-plus-key-theft package would drop the entry bar for imitators who lack NightEagle's discipline.
