NightEagle (APT-Q-95), active since at least 2023, has expanded its targeting from Asian organizations to Russian businesses. Kaspersky's Global Emergency Response Team documented the campaign, which uses compromised VPN credentials as initial access. VPN connections traced to Russian Cloudflare WARP tunnel IPs and European VPS infrastructure. After gaining a foothold, NightEagle deploys the GhostContainer backdoor on Microsoft Exchange servers. The backdoor is a .NET assembly stitched together from public tools: the Neo-reGeorg tunnel, an exploit for CVE-2020-0688, and the GhostWebShell class from ysoserial. Kaspersky assesses with high confidence that attackers extract Exchange cryptographic keys from ASP.NET configuration, then overwrite the VIEWSTATE parameter to inject the payload in memory, leaving minimal disk artifacts.

The group combines legitimate and offensive tooling to blend with normal traffic. Post-exploitation involves RDP tunneling and Active Directory techniques for lateral movement. GhostContainer's functionality splits across three .NET classes: a Stub that processes C2 instructions, a Manager handling the encrypted command channel, and a Payload class executing deployed tools. NightEagle's expansion to Russia signals broadened geographic ambition beyond its original Asian focus. The use of Exchange VIEWSTATE injection is a well-documented but under-patched technique, making it effective against organizations that have not addressed CVE-2020-0688 or migrated away from on-premises Exchange.
