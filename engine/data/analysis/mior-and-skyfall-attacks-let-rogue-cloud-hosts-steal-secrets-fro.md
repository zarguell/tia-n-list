Mior (CVE-2026-0462, CVSS 4.0 5.9) and Skyfall (CVE-2026-40671, CVSS 4.0 4.9) are two attacks published between 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-13 that break AMD SEV-SNP confidential VMs. Mior redirects the Platform Security Processor's initialization writes for the Reverse Map Table into attacker-controlled device memory, leaving protections uninitialized so a malicious hypervisor can read or modify guest memory and forge attestation reports; it requires hypervisor and BIOS/UEFI control plus a PCIe device such as a GPU. Skyfall builds on Transient Scheduler Attacks (first disclosed 2025-07-08) to sample decrypted guest state from an aborting VMRUN, recovering register values and disk-encryption keys and reading three fixed cache lines per targeted page at offsets 0x0c0, 0x380, and 0x3c0; it needs only a malicious host or hypervisor. A third identifier, CVE-2026-18448 (CVSS 4.0 5.6), covers Skyfall's System Management Mode leak through missing TSA mitigations in EDK II firmware. Mior was tested on an AMD EPYC 9135 (Zen 5) and Skyfall on an EPYC 7313 (Zen 3) plus EPYC 9124 and 9334 (Zen 4).

The blast radius is the confidential-computing customer base, not the whole cloud. SEV-SNP is the isolation boundary that lets tenants run sensitive workloads on a host they do not trust; Mior and Skyfall show that boundary fails when the host controls the hypervisor and, for Mior, the firmware. The realistic exposure is narrower than the headlines suggest: Mior needs a BIOS patched to leave memory-routing registers unlocked and a PCIe device, and the IEEE S&P program committee noted that Skyfall's VM attack needs a quiet core, rapid failed VM starts, and a guest that does not crash or time out. Still, both teams released code, and Mior's proof of concept needs only a BIOS patched to leave those routing settings unlocked. The argument for urgency is attestation: Mior can forge the signed report that tells a tenant which software runs, so a compromised host can present a clean measurement while running attacker code, defeating the one check tenants can actually perform.

Remediation is firmware, and it is not in tenants' hands. AMD's Mior advisory (AMD-SB-3059) ships SEV firmware dated 2026-08-12 and targets platform initialization firmware for December 2026 on EPYC 7003, 8004, 8005, 9004, and 9005, with embedded models in February 2027; the Skyfall advisory (AMD-SB-3061) ships microcode dated 2026-08-12 and targets platform firmware for November 2026, with the Instinct MI300A patched 2026-09-21 and embedded models between January and April 2027. The EDK II SMM issue (AMD-SB-7072) needs the earlier TSA mitigations from AMD-SB-7029 plus an EDK II or platform firmware patch targeted for November 2026. Tenants should verify their provider's firmware level and the TCB values for SNP attestation listed next to each fixed firmware version in the Mior advisory, and treat a confidential VM on unpatched host firmware as readable by that host. Hunt for the documented primitives: hypervisor-driven VMRUN failures, unexpected debug enablement on confidential VMs, attestation reports whose measurement matches a benign image, and SMM state reads via performance monitoring counters.

Watch whether cloud providers actually deploy the November and December 2026 firmware across fleets and whether AMD clarifies if the August 12 microcode and SEV firmware fixes work standalone or only as part of the BIOS update, since neither advisory lists a workaround. Also watch for in-the-wild use: neither advisory states whether either flaw has been exploited, but both research teams published code.
