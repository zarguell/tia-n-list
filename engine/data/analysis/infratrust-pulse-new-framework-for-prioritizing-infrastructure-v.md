Eclypsium launched InfraTrust on July 22, 2026 as a knowledge base for hardware, firmware, networking, and edge device risk, paired with a monthly InfraTrust Pulse report. The first report counted 61 advisories from 14 vendors, including 6 critical advisories and 26 remotely exploitable, unauthenticated vulnerabilities, with several flaws listed in CISA KEV. The stated purpose is to rank infrastructure issues by exploitability, exposure, and real-world risk instead of CVSS score alone, with attention to Russian and Chinese state activity against routers, VPNs, and firewalls.

The November Pulse report covers 373 advisories from 23 infrastructure vendors published between October 15 and November 19, spanning 3,880 vulnerabilities. Ninety-eight advisories were rated critical, and 38 covered flaws in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. Thirty-three of those 38 fixed exploited flaws in bundled third-party components, mainly PHP, Node.js, and OpenSSH. The report's point is that an upstream fix does not end the exposure: appliance vendors have to rebuild their products before customers can install a patched version.

The report uses PHP2Shell, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, as the example. Those flaws are now being exploited against Palo Alto Networks and Check Point firewalls, where attackers chained them with other bugs to get root and Check Point reported destructive attacks. The metric to watch is the gap between upstream patches and vendor builds for bundled software, since that window determines how long internet-facing management interfaces remain exposed after a public fix exists.
