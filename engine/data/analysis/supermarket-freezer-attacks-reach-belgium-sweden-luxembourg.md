Overnight into November 2, 2026, refrigeration systems at supermarkets across Belgium, Luxembourg, Sweden, the Netherlands, Ireland, and Estonia showed sudden temperature rises, with some freezers set as high as 50°C. Retailers discarded spoiled stock and several stores opened late or stayed closed. More than 115 stores were affected, including roughly 40 Delhaize stores in Belgium and Luxembourg and six Coop stores in Sweden. Sweden's national cybersecurity center is responding, and the refrigeration contractor running Delhaize's Belgian installations called the events a cyberattack. No group has claimed responsibility and no authority has named a suspect or entry point.

The incidents follow a destructive French supermarket refrigeration attack on October 11-12, 2026, in which attackers remotely manipulated controllers to force defrost cycles. Reporting linked affected French environments to remote management software used with older refrigeration controllers, including Danfoss equipment. Claroty Team82 disclosed three vulnerabilities in the Danfoss AK-SM 800A controller (CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451, CVE-2025-41452) in August 2026, including an authentication bypass via a hidden code-of-the-day mechanism, and Danfoss published an advisory for CVE-2025-41450 in March 2025. However, no link between those disclosed flaws and the actual exploit chain has been confirmed.

The concrete artifact to track is the multi-country store impact and the open question of the entry point. Watch for any authority naming the controller vendor or remote-management software involved, and for a claimed threat actor, which would shift this from suspected to attributed.
