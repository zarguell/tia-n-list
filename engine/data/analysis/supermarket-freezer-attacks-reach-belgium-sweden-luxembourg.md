Supermarket refrigeration systems in Belgium, Luxembourg, and Sweden were disrupted by a suspected cyberattack over the weekend of November 1-2, 2026. Temperature settings were remotely changed to as high as 50°C, forcing retailers to discard stock. Sweden's national cybersecurity center and a refrigeration contractor for Delhaize both describe the events as a cyberattack. Dozens of stores across multiple chains were affected, with some staying closed through the morning.

No group has claimed responsibility, and authorities have not named a suspect or an entry point. The pattern suggests remote access to industrial control interfaces for refrigeration, which places these systems in the same exposure class as other operational-technology attacks against food and logistics infrastructure. Because the attack caused physical spoilage and business interruption rather than data theft, the economic impact is direct and measurable.

Watch for any attribution or technical details from Swedish or Belgian authorities that reveal the intrusion path. Retailers and refrigeration contractors should review remote-access controls on cooling systems and segment OT networks from corporate IT. Monitor for follow-on attacks in neighboring countries.
