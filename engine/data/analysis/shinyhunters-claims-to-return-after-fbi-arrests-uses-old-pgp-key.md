Someone claiming to represent ShinyHunters announced a return on 23 November 2026 through a new Tor leak site and a PGP-signed post from a 2020 Exploit forum account titled "The Darkest Day". The notice appeared in Russian and English, rejected other self-described members and denounced a former member said to be cooperating with law enforcement. BleepingComputer verified that two messages were signed with historical ShinyHunters PGP keys, including one used to authenticate communications in August 2025. Those keys dated to 2020 and had been revoked in March, so the signatures prove control of old key material, not that the original operators are back.

The announcement followed arrests in the Netherlands, Jordan and the United States over two months. Dutch police arrested 24-year-old Amsterdam resident Pepijn van der Stap, known online as Umbreon, on 15 September. Jordanian authorities detained Saif al-Din Khader, known as Rey, whom sources said was walking investigators through his devices and cooperating with the FBI. The group had claimed in September that it breached FBI systems through a new Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability and stole 2 to 3 TB of employee data. Separately, the FBI attributed an exposure of bureau employees' personal information to an Accenture contractor that failed to apply an available patch. Those two claims should not be treated as one verified intrusion.

Okta Threat Intelligence reported that the vishing cluster it tracks as O-UNC-053 moved victim listings from the ShinyHunters site to a new brand called INCEPTION. The cluster used vishing and a phishing kit to take SSO credentials and MFA factors, with ShinyHunters listings appearing 1 to 28 days after phishing domain registration. Okta said the kit could not bypass phishing-resistant methods such as Okta FastPass and FIDO2/WebAuthn passkeys, and it could not validate the published data or confirm who operates INCEPTION. Watch for new victim posts, independent confirmation of the FBI breach claim, and whether the old keys sign any fresh extortion material.
