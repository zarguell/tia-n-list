A message posted on November 23 on a new Tor leak site and from a ShinyHunters account on the Exploit forum claims the extortion group has returned after two months of arrests. The post, titled "The Darkest Day" in one report, was signed with historical ShinyHunters PGP keys from 2020, one previously used with BleepingComputer in August 2025 and another reported as revoked in March. DataBreaches notes that the original key had leaked months earlier. Control of the key and account shows access, not that the same operators are back, and the new notice disavows other people claiming ShinyHunters membership.

The arrests form the backdrop. Dutch police detained a 24-year-old Amsterdam man identified as Pepijn van der Stap, Jordanian authorities detained Saif al-Din Khader, known as Rey, who was reportedly cooperating with the FBI, and a Canada-based cybersecurity expert was also arrested. The group had claimed an FBI breach through an Oracle PeopleSoft zero-day and said it held two to three terabytes of bureau data, claims that were never publicly verified; Reuters reported the underlying intrusion stemmed from an Accenture contractor failing to apply an available patch.

Okta Threat Intelligence reported that victims of the O-UNC-053 vishing cluster began appearing on a leak site branded INCEPTION instead of the ShinyHunters site used from March through September 2026. The cluster used voice phishing and a kit that captured SSO credentials and MFA factors, and Okta said phishing-resistant methods such as FastPass and FIDO2 or WebAuthn passkeys blocked it. The November 25 Securityweek report says INCEPTION's operators deny ties to ShinyHunters. Watch for victim postings on the new site, independent validation of the signing keys' custody and any law enforcement statement about who controls the revived brand.
