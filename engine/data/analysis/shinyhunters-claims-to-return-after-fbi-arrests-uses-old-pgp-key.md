A Tor site launched on November 23, 2026, and an Exploit forum post titled The Darkest Day claim ShinyHunters has returned about two months after arrests in the Netherlands, Jordan and the United States. Both posts were signed with two ShinyHunters PGP keys created in 2020 and revoked in March, and BleepingComputer verified the signatures against historic keys, including one used to authenticate its August 2025 communications. The notice denounced a former member said to be cooperating with law enforcement and named the 2020 Exploit account as the only official outlet. The backdrop is the group's September claim of an FBI breach through an Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability, reported as 2 to 3 terabytes of FBI worker data and never publicly verified.

Signature validity is not continuity. Those keys had circulated among numerous people, so the proof establishes access to old key material, not that the original operators are back; that matters because a brand with a contested signature can be reused by the next crew with the same keys. The consequential signal is the extortion supply chain under the brand. Okta tracked O-UNC-053 from March 2026 feeding the ShinyHunters leak site, with targeted organizations listed 1 to 28 days after phishing domain registration, then saw the same cluster's targets surface on INCEPTION after the old site went offline. INCEPTION named seven alleged victims in its first five days from November 16, including AMD, PepsiCo and Cigna Healthcare, none publicly confirmed. This is a dispute over who can monetize an intact vishing operation, not a restored gang, and the exposed population is every user who answered one of those support calls across technology, healthcare, finance, retail and legal firms.

Move password and MFA resets behind phishing-resistant authentication, Okta FastPass or FIDO2/WebAuthn passkeys, and require positive caller verification at the help desk before issuing one-time codes or approving push prompts; Okta states its phishing kit cannot bypass those methods. Restrict sensitive SaaS applications to managed devices. Hunt DNS, proxy and identity logs back to October 21 for legal-themed lookalike domains such as `legal-<target>[.]com`, `legaldept-<target>[.]com`, `<target>[.]legal` and `<target>-legal[.]com`, Cloudflare-fronted infrastructure in AS13335, and account takeovers using AS19318, AS210558 or AS399486. No CVE or fixed PeopleSoft build is named in the events, so patch verification has to follow the advisory for the affected deployment rather than a version claimed in this reporting.

Two developments would change the assessment. First, INCEPTION publishing data independently tied to the FBI PeopleSoft theft, or new listings that fit the 6 to 19 day gap between legal-themed domain registration and publication, would show the vishing cluster and the revived brand share more than history. Second, arrests or court filings identifying who held the 2020 keys would settle the continuity question; another statement signed with the same revoked keys would not.
