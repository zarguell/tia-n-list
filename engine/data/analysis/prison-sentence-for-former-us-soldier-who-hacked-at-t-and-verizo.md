Cameron John Wagenius, a 22-year-old former US Army soldier, was sentenced to 70 months in prison for hacking AT&T and Verizon systems and leaking the call detail records of a government official. Court documents say he operated under the moniker kiberphant0m, hacked the carriers' databases between April 2023 and December 2024 while on active duty, and in November 2024 publicly disclosed the official's records while threatening to release more. He conspired with others to defraud at least 10 organizations, obtaining credentials with the SSH Brute utility and similar tools, exfiltrating data, extorting victims on private and public forums, and selling stolen data on cybercrime forums. The group attempted to extort at least $1 million. He was arrested in December 2024 and admitted sharing the phone records in February 2025.

The case is a rare full look at the lifecycle of telecom record theft: intrusion, extortion, public leak, and resale, run by an insider with military training and access. It also shows how carrier call-detail data becomes leverage against government personnel, not just a privacy problem.

Watch the proceedings against his co-conspirators, which may name additional victims and buyers of the stolen records, and any follow-on enforcement against the forums where the data was sold. The sentencing also gives defenders a benchmark for how US courts treat active-duty insiders in cyber extortion cases.
