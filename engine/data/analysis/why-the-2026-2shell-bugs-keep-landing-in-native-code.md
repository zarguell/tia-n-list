Five named memory-safety bugs landed in foundational software in about a month: PHP2Shell in the PHP Zend engine (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619), Brotli2Shell in the Brotli C decoder (CVE-2026-111497), Java4Shell in HotSpot's Base64 intrinsic (CVE-2026-114938), nginx2root in nginx HTTP/2 (CVE-2026-123007) and Ruby2Shell in Ruby's case_map code (CVE-2026-132825). Four are normally reached through a memory-safe language or framework, but all five bugs live in the C, C++ or hand-written assembly layer underneath. The fixes named in the reporting are PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35, Brotli 1.0.10, 1.1.1 and 1.2.1, OpenJDK 17.0.21.1, nginx 1.31.9 and 1.30.7, and Ruby 3.3.14, 3.4.13 and 4.0.10.

The reach matters more than the branding. GreyNoise saw the first successful PHP2Shell exploitation against a sensor at 05:14:01 UTC on October 28, 2026, and Unit 42 counted more than 22.6 million potentially vulnerable instances, with October 29 reporting of active exploitation against internet-facing PAN-OS management interfaces. A Dream Research chain joined the PHP bugs to the Fragnesia kernel privilege-escalation flaw CVE-2026-46300 and obtained root on 25 servers. Hunt.io found 616 PHP2Shell-related files in one open directory, including exploit kits and scanners. Harry Sintonen extended the Brotli exposure to git-remote-https when libcurl is built with Brotli support, and GitHub's advisory lists PyPI brotli and brotlicffi versions from 1.0.3 up to 1.2.1 as affected.

The newest entries show how narrow a trigger can be. Ruby's case_map writes two bytes for sharp-s in 14 single-byte encodings while checking for only one byte of room; the researchers reached String#upcase pre-authentication through a multipart _method field tagged ISO-8859-1 in Rack::MethodOverride and confirmed the one-byte write on Ruby 3.4.12. On AVX-512 VBMI CPUs, the HotSpot Base64 intrinsic writes 62 bytes past the output buffer, with temporary workarounds of disabling Base64 intrinsics or capping AVX at level two. GitLab patched the Ruby bug in 19.6.1. Watch for more advisories that name the native boundary explicitly, and for exploitation that moves from tailored server configurations toward reliable pre-auth chains.
