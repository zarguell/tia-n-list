In a year-long investigation, Check Point Research identified three operational clusters it names SilentDelivery, OceanGate and DarkWire, all running the same Sparrow malware framework across Latin America, Asia and Europe. The clusters hit telecommunications providers, government organizations, military entities, research institutions and critical infrastructure. Check Point tracks the entity behind the framework as CopperDragon, which it assesses is a "digital quartermaster" supplying tooling to several China-nexus actors including FamousSparrow, Earth Estries, TropicTrooper and UAT-9244. The clusters differ in toolset and initial access, ranging from compromised internet-facing infrastructure to highly selective watering-hole attacks against government, shipping, IT and research sites.

The connective tissue is the Sparrow framework itself. Check Point reports Windows and Linux variants, passive backdoor capabilities, and a link to the Melofe backdoor, with Melofe tied in through shared Reptile rootkit code and reused self-signed certificates. Earlier public reporting supplies the lineage: ESET first documented FamousSparrow's SparrowDoor backdoor, Kaspersky saw Tropic Trooper deploy the CrowDoor variant via DLL search-order hijacking, Cisco Talos tracked UAT-9244 using TernDoor against South American telecoms, and ESET documented SparroWocky, later identified by Check Point as CopperDoor and tied to the DarkWire cluster.

The significance is that what looked like separate campaigns from different vendors are facets of one tooling supply chain feeding multiple espionage teams. That reframing lets defenders pivot on shared infrastructure, rootkit code and certificates rather than treating each intrusion as novel. Watch for follow-up IOCs on the CopperDoor/CopperShell implants, the watering-hole domains in the shipping and government sectors, and whether Earth Estries and UAT-9244 activity shows the same infrastructure as SilentDelivery or OceanGate in subsequent reporting.
