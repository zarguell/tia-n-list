Shell confirmed it is investigating a potential security incident after the Clop ransomware group listed the energy giant on its dark-web leak site, claiming to have stolen 89GB of corporate data. The allegedly stolen material includes engineering drawings, facility photographs, project roadmaps, testing reports, and other internal documents. Shell has activated incident-response procedures and is working with security specialists but has not confirmed compromise or verified the data's authenticity.

Clop listed Shell as one of 43 new victims likely targeted through exploitation of CVE-2026-12569, a critical improper input validation vulnerability in PTC Windchill and FlexPLM, industrial product lifecycle management systems. The same campaign also targeted General Electric and Philips, with Clop claiming to have stolen backups, system files, drawings, diagrams, and blueprints from both companies. GE and Philips have not confirmed the claims.

The targeting of PLM systems is significant: these platforms store the intellectual property backbone of manufacturing and engineering operations. Even without system encryption, the theft of engineering drawings, facility scans, and project roadmaps from an energy company with 85,000 employees across 70+ countries creates serious commercial and national security implications. Shell reported no operational disruption, but the leak-site posting is designed to pressure ransom negotiations. Organizations running PTC Windchill or FlexPLM should patch CVE-2026-12569 immediately and audit for signs of unauthorized access to their PLM environments.
