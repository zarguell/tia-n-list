The Clop ransomware gang claims it stole 89GB of data from Shell and listed the energy major on its leak site as one of 43 new victims. Shell confirms it is investigating a potential incident, with the allegedly stolen files said to include engineering drawings, scans of facility testing reports, photos, and project plans. Clop is also claiming data from General Electric and Philips in the same campaign, none of whom have confirmed or denied.

The campaign targets internet-exposed PTC Windchill and FlexPLM product lifecycle management instances, exploiting CVE-2026-12569, an improper input validation flaw. PTC began patching on June 17 and issued a private advisory urging IOC reviews after warning of heightened threat activity on June 26. CISA added CVE-2026-12569 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on June 25, confirming active exploitation and ordering federal agencies to secure instances within three days; Germany's BSI issued a midnight emergency warning to PTC customers. Ransom-ISAC and ReliaQuest both confirmed Clop is deploying JSP webshells to steal data from compromised PLM platforms.

This matters because Windchill and FlexPLM sit at the center of engineering and manufacturing data for aerospace, defense, automotive, and retail customers, and Shell is a high-profile critical infrastructure target. If the claimed files are real, Clop holds design and facility data that goes beyond credentials. Watch for confirmation from GE and Philips, whether any of the claimed victims push back on the leak posts, and whether the stolen engineering data surfaces in extortion or resale. PLM instances remain the weak point; organizations running Windchill or FlexPLM should assume exposure and audit for webshells and unusual data egress.
