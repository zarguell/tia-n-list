Federal prosecutors in the Southern District of New York unsealed an indictment charging Jacob Soall, Sharon Hazan and Guy Sheetrit with wire fraud, aggravated identity theft and money laundering for an AI deepfake scheme that impersonated corporate executives and attorneys to steal about $129 million. The defendants, based in Spain, the UK, Israel and the United Arab Emirates, targeted thousands of companies worldwide over more than a year and a half, according to the indictment filed in a U.S. District Court in New York. Sharon Hazan also carried a 2008 conviction for conspiracy to commit bank fraud, and the three were arrested between June and September.

A successful attack subverts the victim's payment approval layer, so the money moves before anyone realizes the request was faked, and perimeter controls alone cannot stop it. The indictment names hospital systems in Missouri and California, a bank in Rhode Island, a financial technology company in the United Kingdom, a mutual fund company in Massachusetts and a pet food company in Florida, which shows any organization with accounting staff exposed to email is in scope rather than a single sector. This story rates attention because it is a charged, nine-figure campaign with named defendants and an unsealed indictment, not an unreported or theoretical threat, and the Associated Press and Independent coverage appeared on the morning of November 6, 2026 ahead of the first SecurityWeek record.

Defenders should verify every payment instruction change and wire request through a separate channel before acting, and enforce multi-factor authentication on email and accounting systems. Hunt activity should focus on wire transfer approval logs, email authentication events and accounting department mailbox access for the wire fraud and identity theft patterns the indictment describes. Because the events carry no specific IoC names, file hashes or log paths, detections should be built around transaction anomalies and compromised executive or vendor email rather than a malware signature.

Watch for the indictment to move toward trial and for additional defendants or victims to be named given the multi-country scope and thousands of companies allegedly targeted. Any evidence that the same deepfake impersonation technique is being reused against new targets would change the assessment from a discrete charged scheme to an active repeatable campaign.
