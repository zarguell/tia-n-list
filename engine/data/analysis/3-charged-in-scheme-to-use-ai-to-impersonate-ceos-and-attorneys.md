Jacob Soall (41), Sharon Hazan (54) and Guy Sheetrit (42) were indicted in a U.S. District Court in Manhattan for using AI to impersonate corporate executives and attorneys in a scheme prosecutors say stole about $129 million. The indictment charges wire fraud, aggravated identity theft and money laundering. Court records place the scheme between July 2024 and February 2026, targeting thousands of companies worldwide, including hospital systems in Missouri and California, a Rhode Island bank, a UK financial technology company, a Massachusetts mutual fund and a Florida pet food company.

The defendants allegedly used AI deepfakes of voices and images to pose as chief executive officers and lawyers at major international law firms, typically approaching accounting staff as the CEO and then introducing an impersonated attorney to make the requested payment look legitimate. Victim losses cited by the Justice Department include almost $11.6 million at a UK fintech firm, $5.2 million at a Florida pet food company, $4.8 million at a Missouri hospital system, $4.6 million at a Massachusetts mutual fund, nearly $3 million at a California hospital system, $2.8 million at a New York investment company and nearly $2.7 million at a Rhode Island bank.

The three defendants are from Spain, the UK, Israel and the United Arab Emirates and were arrested between June and September. Hazan has a 2008 New York conviction for conspiracy to commit bank fraud, and the case docket remained sealed as of the unsealing announcement. Deepfake-based impersonation of executives and counsel is now a proven wire-fraud vector at multi-million-dollar scale.
