Federal prosecutors in the Southern District of New York unsealed an indictment charging Jacob Soall, 41, Sharon Hazan, 54, and Guy Sheetrit, 42, with wire fraud, aggravated identity theft and money laundering in an AI deepfake scheme that impersonated corporate executives and attorneys to steal about $129 million. Over more than a year and a half the defendants, operating from Spain, the UK, Israel and the UAE under nicknames including "Rocky Balboa," "Superman Cash" and "Fat Boy," targeted thousands of companies worldwide. Victims named in court documents include hospital systems in Missouri and California, a Rhode Island bank, a UK financial technology firm, a Massachusetts mutual fund company and a Florida pet food company, with accounting departments as the usual entry point. Hazan carried a 2008 New York conviction for conspiracy to commit bank fraud. All three were arrested between June and September, and the case docket remained sealed as of Thursday evening.

The method is what makes this a pattern threat rather than a one-off fraud. Faked executive and attorney identities subvert the payment approval layer itself, so funds move before anyone flags the request, and perimeter controls cannot catch a wire that looks properly authorized. Any organization whose accounting staff takes payment instructions by email is in scope regardless of sector, which the victim list confirms.

Verify every payment instruction change and wire request through a separate channel before acting, lock down email and accounting systems with multifactor authentication, and hunt wire approval logs and mailbox access records for impersonation patterns. Watch for the case to move toward trial with more victims or defendants named, and for signs the same deepfake impersonation playbook is being reused against new targets.
