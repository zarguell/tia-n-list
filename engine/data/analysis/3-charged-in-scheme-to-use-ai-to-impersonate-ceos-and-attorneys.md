Three people have been indicted in New York for using AI-generated deepfakes to impersonate corporate executives and attorneys in business-email-compromise scams that stole about $129 million. Jacob Soall, 41, Sharon Hazan, 54, and Guy Sheetrit, 42, face charges of wire fraud and aggravated identity theft, plus money laundering, according to an indictment filed in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York. Prosecutors say the scheme ran for more than a year and a half and targeted thousands of companies worldwide.

Victims named in court documents include hospital systems in Missouri and California, a bank in Rhode Island, a financial technology company in the United Kingdom, a mutual fund company in Massachusetts, and a pet food company in Florida. The defendants, based in Spain, the UK, Israel, and the United Arab Emirates, often targeted accounting departments and used nicknames including Rocky Balboa, Superman Cash, and Fat Boy. The three were arrested between June and September, and Hazan had a prior 2008 conviction for conspiracy to commit bank fraud.

The case is one of the larger publicly charged AI deepfake fraud schemes, with the $129 million loss figure and the Southern District of New York indictment as concrete anchors. Organizations should treat executive or attorney requests arriving only through voice or video channels with heightened verification, since the indictment shows attackers converted synthetic impersonation into sustained wire fraud. The sealed docket means further defense details are not yet public.
