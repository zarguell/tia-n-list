RATHat is an Android banking trojan that pairs Google Gemini AI with Android Debug Bridge (ADB) abuse to steal credentials and give attackers remote device control. The malware spreads through malicious ads, SMS phishing, and deceptive download pages disguised as cryptocurrency trading or streaming apps. After installation, it abuses Accessibility Service access to enable Developer Options and wireless debugging, reads the pairing code from the screen, and connects to the local ADB daemon under Android UID 2000 without an external computer. Operators then deploy a native Go service into /data/local/tmp that maintains its own command-and-control channel and runs a reverse tunnel through an FRP client, giving remote shell access separate from the original malicious app.

The Gemini component navigates unfamiliar Android interfaces automatically, reducing operator workload and making the malware more adaptable. The operator panel applies AI to identify higher-value victims from stolen SMS data, which means campaigns are actively prioritized rather than random. Target regions include Europe, Latin America, and Southeast Asia. This is a notable shift: generative AI is no longer just a developer convenience but an embedded malware capability that increases both automation and targeting precision.

There is no specific CVE assigned to RATHat because it is malware, not a product vulnerability. The defense priority is user awareness and endpoint protection: restrict sideloading, monitor for Accessibility Service abuse followed by Developer Options activation, and scan for unexpected ADB wireless pairing events and processes running from /data/local/tmp. Watch for additional variants that integrate newer AI models and expand target geography beyond the current campaign footprint.
