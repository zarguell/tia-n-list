The sample arrived as an archive named "my new program called 2.rar" wrapping a nested ZIP, TokenGrabberBuilder.zip, with a folder called "TokenGrabber Builder". Inside are two components: a Python-based builder and an embedded infostealer payload. The builder follows a MaaS-style model, separating the malware-generation process from the final payload so the same builder produces many customized binaries for different operators.

Operators generate customized Windows executables compiled with Nuitka or PyInstaller, with the webhook configuration for exfiltrated data integrated into the build process itself rather than patched in afterward. The resulting stealer drains browser credentials and tokens and ships them to an operator-controlled webhook endpoint. Aggregated coverage of the same builder at threadlinqs counts 26 IOCs against only 9 detections, which is the operationally relevant number.

Low detection on a commodity builder matters because it lowers the entry cost for low-skill operators: a unique recompiled build per customer defeats hash-based blocking, and the stolen material flows to webhooks that sit behind ordinary cloud endpoints. Watch for fresh builds from the same builder family, hunt on the published IOCs, and flag unsigned or packed Nuitka/PyInstaller executables that open outbound connections to webhook URLs shortly after execution.
