BleepingComputer reported on September 9 that over 36,000 Plex Media Server instances remain exposed to the internet without patches for recently disclosed vulnerabilities. The article references multiple flaws patched by Plex but does not name specific CVEs in the available content. Plex issued an earlier advisory urging immediate updates to both the media server and desktop client. The 36,000 figure comes from internet-wide scanning data showing unpatched, internet-reachable instances.

The attack surface here is straightforward: self-hosted Plex installations that administrators have exposed via port forwarding or UPnP without keeping the software current. An attacker who chains the disclosed vulnerabilities could gain code execution on the host system. Plex is widely deployed in home labs and small business media setups, and many users run it on always-on systems with access to internal networks. A compromised Plex server could serve as a pivot point into a home or small-office LAN.

The German-language outlet android-digital.de picked up the story on September 10, confirming the 36,000 figure and emphasizing the urgency of updating. No public exploitation has been reported yet, but the combination of a large exposed population and published vulnerability details makes mass exploitation a realistic near-term risk. Plex administrators should update immediately and audit their network exposure.
