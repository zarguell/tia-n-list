Kiteworks urged customers worldwide to temporarily shut down servers for a six-hour window on Saturday 26 September 2026 after receiving credible law-enforcement intelligence that a cyberattack targeting Kiteworks systems could be imminent. The advisory was preventive; the company stated it had no evidence of a confirmed compromise. Coverage from BleepingComputer and German technology publication Heise noted the customer email cited credible threat intelligence, with follow-up coverage framing the warning as originating from federal authorities, and Heise publishing the German-language original roughly ten minutes before English translations appeared. A follow-up on 27 September referenced a potential nine-hour window and 12 IOCs, though no breach was confirmed.

The incident highlights how file-sharing vendors are responding to targeted threat intelligence with preemptive shutdowns rather than post-breach remediation. The absence of confirmed compromise, combined with law-enforcement sourcing, suggests intelligence-driven defense rather than observed intrusion. The shift from a six-hour to a nine-hour advisory indicates the threat assessment evolved between 25 and 27 September.

Customers should treat any vendor-imposed server blackout as a sign of credible targeting, verify system integrity after restart, and review access logs around 26 September for any unauthorized service-principal or administrative access. Monitor Kiteworks and BleepingComputer for confirmation of whether the threat materialized.
