Kiteworks, the secure file-transfer vendor formerly known as Accellion, told customers on September 25, 2026 to take systems offline based on what CISO Frank Balonis described as credible threat intelligence from federal law-enforcement authorities indicating an imminent attack, possibly involving zero-day vulnerabilities. The company coordinated time-zone staggered windows on Saturday September 26 (a six-hour window 04:00 to 10:00 in Central Europe, overnight Friday to Saturday for the US East Coast, described overall as a nine-hour precautionary shutdown), asked self-hosted customers on-premises and in AWS and Azure to power down, and shut down the systems it hosts itself. heise broke the story; Germany's BKA declined comment and BSI did not respond that Friday.

On September 27 Kiteworks lifted the recommendation and brought everything back online, still maintaining there was no evidence of compromise. The scoped explanation followed on September 28: the threat centered on a severe vulnerability in the Advanced Forms secure data collection product, which is enabled for fewer than 1 percent of customers, under 50 organizations, and is confined to that product. DPE, file collaboration, file transfer, email encryption, APIs, and MFT were reportedly unaffected. Kiteworks says all known vulnerabilities are fixed in version 9.5.1, reports no evidence of exploitation, and is working with partners including Mandiant to share intelligence.

The context that makes this credible rather than theatrical is Kiteworks' history as Accellion, whose FTA product was mass-exploited via zero-days in 2021 and whose customers remain attractive targets for large-scale data theft. A vendor ordering a global outage on law-enforcement intel is a rare, expensive signal. Watch for a CVE and patch details for the Advanced Forms flaw, disclosure of whether the threatened actor was caught in the act, and any confirmation from Mandiant or authorities about the threat's origin.
