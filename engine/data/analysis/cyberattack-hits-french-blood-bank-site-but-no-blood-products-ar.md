French authorities said a cyberattack on several blood banks attempted to raise cold-storage temperatures to extremely high levels but was stopped before any blood products or samples were damaged. The French Blood Establishment, EFS, reported that temperature and defrosting thresholds were raised on Monday at three of its plasma cold-storage sites, in a statement released late Tuesday. Monitoring systems detected the change and staff acted in time, so no blood products were lost and no health consequences were identified, according to the EFS.

Le Canard Enchaîné, which first reported the cyberattack, said the intruders tried to reset temperatures that normally run at minus 30 Celsius (minus 22 Fahrenheit) to as high as 110 Celsius (230 Fahrenheit). The attack hit a supplier of refrigeration equipment at three EFS sites. The stored plasma bags, intended for transfusions and for manufacturing medicines, were transferred or secured.

The incident follows reports that supermarkets across multiple European countries had refrigerators and freezers remotely taken over this week, spoiling tons of food at a cost of millions of dollars or euros. A French-language report from infonewscyber.fr confirms the EFS site was hit with no blood products compromised. The events show an active intrusion chain targeting refrigeration infrastructure in the healthcare and food cold chain, with temperature manipulation as the primary impact vector.
