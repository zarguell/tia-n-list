A cyberattack on Ontario's court system exposed personal information of individuals involved in court proceedings. The breach targeted C-Track, an online case management platform owned by Thomson Reuters Canada. Thomson Reuters detected unauthorized activity in one of its cloud environments on June 30, 2026, but later determined the intrusion began in March. The company brought in cybersecurity experts, notified law enforcement, and created a dedicated notification website for affected parties.

The breach is particularly concerning because C-Track handles sealed, confidential, and redacted court records. Thomson Reuters confirmed that "certain confidential, redacted or sealed information may have been impacted for certain affected courts." This means individuals involved in sensitive legal matters, including criminal cases, family law proceedings, and civil litigation with sealing orders, may have had their information exposed. The scope of affected records remains unclear, and Thomson Reuters could not estimate how many Ontarians might be impacted.

The incident extends beyond Ontario. C-Track systems serving court systems in Alabama, Kentucky, Ohio, Montana, Nevada, North Dakota, New Hampshire, South Carolina, Tennessee, Wyoming, and the U.S. Virgin Islands were also compromised. New Hampshire's judicial branch indicated the breach affected records from 2002 to 2015. Ontario's three chief justices, including Ontario Chief Justice Michael H. Tulloch, issued a joint public statement on September 2. Thomson Reuters stated that C-Track remains fully operational and safe to use, and that additional cybersecurity measures have been implemented. Organizations relying on third-party legal technology platforms should treat this as a reminder that vendor breaches can expose the most sensitive institutional data, and that contractual security requirements and incident response coordination with vendors need to be proactive rather than reactive.
