Maileva, a digital subsidiary of France's national postal operator La Poste Group, disclosed a cyberattack that exposed the personal data of about 22 million people. La Poste confirmed the intrusion on Saturday, with the reporting surfacing Sunday, 15 November 2026. The company said the attack took place in mid-September, and the exposed data includes first names, last names and postal addresses.

The scale is the headline: 22 million affected individuals is a mass-exposure event tied to a subsidiary handling digital and mail-adjacent services for one of France's largest institutions. Maileva specializes in dematerialized mail and secure document workflows, which makes the compromise sensitive even though the confirmed leaked fields are, so far, limited to names and addresses rather than payment or credential data.

What to watch is the scope. Reports to date detail only names and postal addresses, and no ransom claim, actor attribution, or exploit vector has been confirmed publicly. A follow-up from La Poste or the CNIL (France's data-protection authority) on the exact data categories and the number of truly affected records would firm up the picture. Any expansion into more sensitive categories, or confirmation of how the mid-September intrusion occurred, would raise the significance of an already large breach.
