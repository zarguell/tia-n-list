Maileva, the business-mail and printing subsidiary of La Poste, confirmed the theft of names and postal addresses for about 22 million people after a mid-September cyberattack. The service had been offline from September 23 at 07:00 until September 29 after what Docaposte described as unauthorized access attempts aimed at sending illegitimate mail. FrenchBreaches and Cyberattaque.org first reported the incident from a customer notice; national press picked it up on November 14 and La Poste told AFP on Saturday that the breach was under judicial investigation.

The 22 million figure counts recipients of letters produced for roughly 70,000 business customers, not people with Maileva accounts. Maileva printed and mailed 180 million documents in 2024. Named affected clients include social landlords Altéal and Flandre Opale Habitat and Foch hospital in Suresnes. Foch patients were told that payment reminders sent through Kiwee.care and printed by Maileva may have been read, exposing name, address, invoice number, care date and amount for letters dated September 9 to 23. Flandre Opale Habitat warned that invoice amounts and personal data in letters from the past ten years may have been accessed.

The exposed fields are contact details and letter contents rather than passwords, but the scale makes targeted postal fraud plausible. Le Lab Technique has already framed the risk around scams that use real names and addresses. Watch for further client notifications, details on how the attacker entered Maileva's systems, and any confirmation of whether fraudulent letters were actually sent.
