Maileva, a mailing and document subsidiary of Docaposte within France's La Poste group, confirmed in mid-November 2026 that a cyberattack in mid-September exposed the personal data of about 22 million people. La Poste confirmed the theft on Saturday November 14, and Le Figaro and Le Monde reported it the same day, describing the exposed fields as first names, last names, and postal addresses. Coverage from Security Affairs and AFP framed it as one of the largest data breaches reported in France, and a judicial investigation is under way.

The intrusion surfaced well before the scale was known. Maileva was offline from 07:00 on September 23 until September 29 after what Docaposte described as unauthorized access attempts aimed at sending illegitimate mail, a detail first revealed on September 24 by Cyberattaque.org and FrenchBreaches. Downstream clients began notifying affected individuals in early November. Flandre Opale Habitat warned tenants on November 3 that administrative and financial information such as invoice amounts was taken, and Hopital Foch notified patients on November 6 that payment reminder letters it prints through Maileva may have been read for reminders issued between September 9 and 23, exposing name, address, invoice number, care date, and amount.

The pattern matters because Maileva sits in a document supply chain that many organizations trust without direct oversight. Because La Poste is a national operator, the exposed records aggregate patients, tenants, and general account holders, which raises the risk of targeted fraud and convincing phishing that references real invoice and care details. What to watch is whether the judicial investigation identifies the actor and whether more client organizations disclose downstream exposure, since the initial notification chain suggests the affected population may keep expanding as Maileva's customers audit what passed through the service during the intrusion window.
