Maileva, the Docaposte subsidiary of France's La Poste, confirmed the theft of names and postal addresses of about 22 million people after a mid-September 2026 cyberattack. The service was offline from 23 September at 07:00 until 29 September after what Docaposte called unauthorized access attempts aimed at sending illegitimate mail. Cyberattaque.org and FrenchBreaches first reported the incident on 24 September from a client notice. Le Figaro and Le Monde published national accounts on 14 November, and a judicial investigation is under way.

Most of the 22 million are recipients of letters rather than Maileva account holders. The company prints and mails payslips and invoices for about 70,000 business users and handled 180 million documents in 2024. Named clients include social landlords Altéal and Flandre Opale Habitat and Foch hospital in Suresnes. Foch told patients on 6 November that payment reminders sent through Kiwee.care between 9 and 23 September could have been read, exposing names, addresses, invoice numbers, care dates and amounts. Flandre Opale Habitat warned tenants that invoice amounts and letter contents from the past ten years may have been accessed.

Maileva says it corrected the flaw and that no data was modified or deleted. The exposed material combines names, addresses and the contents of specific mailings, which is useful for targeted fraud against tenants and patients. Watch for further client notices refining the ten-year scope, the outcome of the judicial investigation, and any evidence that fraudulent letters were actually sent.
