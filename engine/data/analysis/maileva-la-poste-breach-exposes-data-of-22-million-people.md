Maileva, the business-mail unit of La Poste subsidiary Docaposte, confirmed that a mid-September 2026 attack exposed names and postal addresses of about 22 million people. Le Figaro and Le Monde reported the confirmation on 14 November, and a judicial investigation is under way. The scale comes from Maileva's role: it prints and mails payslips, invoices and other letters for roughly 70,000 business customers, handling 180 million documents in 2024, so most of the 22 million affected people never had an account with Maileva. An earlier notice says the service was offline from 23 to 29 September after unauthorized access attempts aimed at sending illegitimate mail.

Downstream notices show what was readable. Flandre Opale Habitat told tenants on 3 November that invoice amounts and possibly personal data in letters sent over the past ten years were exposed. Hospital Foch said on 6 November that payment reminders sent through Kiwee and printed by Maileva could have been read, exposing name, address, invoice number, care date and amount for reminders issued between 9 and 23 September. A new French-language post adds that travelers of Izilo in Lorient had personal data exposed in the same leak, extending the list of affected customer organizations.

Because the data is largely names, addresses and billing detail, the near-term risk is targeted fraud rather than account takeover. Watch for further customer disclosures from social landlords, hospitals and other mail customers, and for any finding on whether fraudulent letters were actually sent during the September access.
