Maileva, the business mail subsidiary of Docaposte within the La Poste group, has confirmed that a breach in mid-September 2026 exposed the names and postal addresses of about 22 million people. La Poste told AFP on 15 November 2026. The company prints and mails payslips, invoices and other correspondence for roughly 70,000 business clients, handling around 180 million documents in 2024, so most of the 22 million affected are not Maileva customers at all: they are the recipients of letters sent on behalf of those business clients.

The outage came first. Maileva was offline from 23 September at 07:00 until 29 September after what Docaposte described as unauthorized access attempts aimed at sending illegitimate mail, first revealed on 24 September by cyberattaque.org and FrenchBreaches, who obtained the notice Maileva sent to its clients. The data theft was only confirmed two months later, alongside the admission that names and addresses had been taken. Affected clients named so far include the social landlords Altéal and Flandre Opale Habitat and the Foch hospital in Suresnes.

The sharpest detail is at Foch, where the attacker reportedly read payment reminders containing invoice numbers and amounts. That access ran through Kiwee.care, a receivables firm that uses Maileva for printing and mailing, which means the exposure crossed a data-processing boundary rather than staying inside one client relationship. Watch three things: whether Docaposte confirms fraudulent mail was actually sent during the outage window, whether the count holds at 22 million or grows as client-by-client notification proceeds, and what CNIL makes of a processor that took six weeks to confirm the nature of the data lost.
