Italy's data protection authority, the Garante, fined Meta EUR 805 million (about $887 million) over a Facebook for Android feature that pulled photos and videos from users' phone galleries without explicit consent. The feature, "Get suggestions from gallery when browsing Facebook," was enabled by default and accessed Italian users' photos, videos, and related metadata. Investigators found Facebook requested gallery access through a single permission covering multiple app functions with different purposes, which the Garante said fell short of the free and informed consent required under the European privacy directive protecting the smartphone as part of a person's private sphere.

The penalty was levied on Meta Platforms Ireland Limited and equals roughly 0.47 percent of the group's global turnover, with Meta's lack of cooperation during the investigation cited as an aggravating factor. Meta challenged Italy's jurisdiction over the matter. The Garante gave the company 60 days to disable the feature and to seek separate, specific consent for any continued use, and noted Italy has about 20 million Android users in scope. Meta made no immediate comment at the time of the decision.

This is one of the larger European privacy penalties against a US platform and turns on default-on data collection through bundled permissions rather than a classic breach, which makes it a reference point for how regulators treat consent-by-default design. Watch Meta's jurisdictional challenge and whether the 60-day remediation deadline forces a feature change or a broader consent-flow rework across other markets.
