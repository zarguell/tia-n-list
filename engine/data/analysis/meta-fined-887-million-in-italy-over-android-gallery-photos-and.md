Italy's Garante fined Meta Platforms Ireland EUR 805 million, about $887 million, over the Facebook for Android feature "Get suggestions from gallery when browsing Facebook", which read photos, videos and metadata from users' galleries without specific, informed consent. The decision was announced on 14 November 2026 and covers roughly 20 million Android users in Italy. Investigators found the feature enabled by default and that gallery access rode one app-wide permission covering several functions with different purposes. The penalty is about 0.47 percent of group turnover, and the Garante cited Meta's lack of cooperation. Meta has 60 days to disable the feature and seek separate consent, with reporting due at 30 and 90 days.

The investigation opened in July 2025 after a report from the Centro Tutela Consumatori Utenti di Bolzano and included technical analysis by the authority. The feature had been available since 2023. The Garante found the bundled authorization could let Facebook read an entire photo library, usually on a permanent basis, with no record of what was actually used. It rejected Meta's argument that the Irish lead regulator should own the case, holding that the ePrivacy rule in article 122 of the Privacy Code has no one-stop-shop mechanism. The proceeding explicitly did not cover any use of gallery content to train AI.

This is a consent enforcement action, not a breach. There is no CVE, no intruder and no exfiltration, so the practical audit is configuration based. On managed Android fleets that allow Facebook, check which devices hold a full Photos and videos permission, confirm the gallery suggestion setting is off because it shipped enabled, and review any other permission bundle that mixes unrelated functions behind one prompt.

Watch the 60-day deadline for the disable-and-consent change, Meta's challenge to Italian jurisdiction, and whether other national authorities open cases on the same consent-by-default reasoning. A parallel case would show the fine is a template rather than a one-off. Coverage spans the regulator's own statement, AP, Securityweek, The Hacker News and Therecord, all describing the same penalty.
