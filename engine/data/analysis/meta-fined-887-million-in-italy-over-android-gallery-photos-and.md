Italy's data protection authority, the Garante, fined Meta €805 million (about $887 million) for using photos and videos from Android users' galleries in Facebook without specific consent or adequate disclosure. The authority said the feature, called "Get suggestions from gallery when browsing Facebook," violated a European privacy directive incorporated into Italian law that treats the smartphone as part of a person's private sphere and requires free and informed consent for such access.

Investigators found the feature was enabled by default and that Facebook requested gallery access through a single permission covering multiple app functions with different purposes. The Garante gave Meta 60 days to disable the feature and seek separate consent, and said Meta's lack of cooperation factored into the penalty, which the authority put at roughly 0.47% of the group's global turnover. The fine was imposed on Meta Platforms Ireland Limited, which challenged Italy's jurisdiction.

The action is one of the largest European privacy penalties and turns on default-on data access and bundled permissions rather than a breach. Watch Meta's appeal of the jurisdictional challenge, whether the gallery-suggestion feature is withdrawn in Italy, and whether other EU regulators treat default-on gallery access as a consent violation.
