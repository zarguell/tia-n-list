Italy's Garante fined Meta Platforms Ireland EUR 805 million (about $887 million) on 14 November 2026 over the Facebook for Android feature "Get suggestions from gallery when browsing Facebook", live since 2023, which read photos, videos, and metadata from Italian users' galleries without specific consent. The feature was on by default, and gallery access rode a single app-wide permission covering multiple functions, presented behind a generic "enable other functions" notice. The Garante grounded the penalty in art. 122 of the Italian Privacy Code, which transposes the ePrivacy directive and treats the smartphone as part of the private sphere. This is not a breach: no CVE, no intruder, no exfiltration. It is a consent-by-default enforcement action.

The events read as a privacy fine, but the consequential part is jurisdictional. The Garante rejected Meta's argument that the Irish authority should own the case, holding that the ePrivacy regime has no one-stop-shop, which means Meta Ireland cannot funnel every EU privacy matter through Dublin and is exposed to parallel action from every national authority. The sum, roughly 0.47 percent of global turnover, shows penalties now scale to revenue rather than to fixed caps. About 20 million Italian Android users sat in scope, and the Garante noted a permission granted in full can give the app permanent read access to the whole photo library with no way for the user or the regulator to see what was actually used. The proceeding explicitly excluded any AI-training use of gallery content, which Meta denies, leaving that question open rather than settled.

There is no patch and no network telemetry here; the hunt is a permission and configuration audit. On managed Android fleets that allow Facebook, inventory which devices hold a full Photos and videos app permission and confirm the "Get suggestions from gallery when browsing Facebook" setting is off, since the Garante found it defaulted to on. Treat the single bundled gallery authorization as the failure mode: the exposure is a consent flow, not a vulnerability, so log paths and IoCs do not apply. Meta has 60 days to disable the feature by default and request separate, specific consent, and must report the initiatives started within 30 days and the measures adopted within 90. Outside Italy, expect the same consent-by-default argument to be tested against other national DPAs.

Two developments would change the assessment. First, Meta's appeal on jurisdiction: if the one-stop-shop rejection survives, national DPAs across the EU gain a template to fine Meta Ireland directly. Second, the Garante reserved a separate inquiry into a 2026 feature that processes gallery content in the cloud, a materially larger data flow than on-device suggestions and a candidate for a second, higher-value proceeding.
