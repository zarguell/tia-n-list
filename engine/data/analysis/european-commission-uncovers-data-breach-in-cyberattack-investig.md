The European Commission confirmed on Monday that the October 22 intrusion into its systems also breached EU Academy, the online learning platform used by people outside the Commission, and leaked user personal information. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier said the confirmed leak includes names and email addresses and that the exact scale is still under investigation. Access to EU Academy was suspended during the investigation and the European Data Protection Supervisor was notified. Affected individuals were not contacted one by one; a general notice was posted on the platform instead.

The first disclosure described an AI-assisted attack using the ARTEX penetration testing framework that disrupted several Commission applications, most of which were later restored, and also affected the internal Sysper HR platform. At that stage the Commission said it had found no evidence that Commission staff personal information was compromised. It still says no ransom demand was received and no actor has been attributed. The EU Academy finding narrows that earlier statement: staff data was not shown to be affected, but a public-facing learning platform did lose user data.

The open questions are how many EU Academy accounts were exposed, whether anything beyond names and email addresses was accessed, and when the platform will return. With attribution and scope unresolved, the incident shows how a breach first framed as service disruption can become a confirmed personal data leak. Watch for the Commission's follow-up on scope and for any EDPS action.
