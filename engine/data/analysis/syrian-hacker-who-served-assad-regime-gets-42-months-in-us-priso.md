Ahmad Umar Agha, a Syrian man tied to the Syrian Electronic Army and Assad's inner circle, was sentenced to 42 months in federal prison after more than a decade on the run. Prosecutors say the hacking conspiracy began as early as August 2011 and continued until January 2024, directed by members of Assad's circle including Syria's General Intelligence Directorate commander. Agha compromised approximately 47 targets including media outlets, NGOs, businesses, and U.S. and allied government bodies.

The operation had multiple motives: silencing reporting on regime atrocities, intimidating opponents, collecting intelligence, and attempting to persuade U.S. service members to refuse orders. A notable incident was the 2013 takeover of the Associated Press Twitter account to post a fabricated bombing claim that triggered a brief $136 billion flash crash in the Dow.
