Ahmad Umar Agha, a 42-year-old Syrian man, was sentenced to 42 months in federal prison for a hacking operation prosecutors say was organized and led by members of former President Bashar Assad's inner circle, including the commander of Syria's General Intelligence Directorate. The conspiracy ran from August 24, 2011 until January 31, 2024 and targeted approximately 47 media entities, businesses, nongovernmental organizations and U.S. and allied government bodies.

The most damaging intrusion came on April 23, 2013, when Agha accessed the Associated Press Twitter account and posted a false claim that President Barack Obama had been injured in two explosions at the White House. The post triggered a flash crash that briefly erased $136 billion in market value.

Motives cited by the Justice Department include punishing news organizations for reporting on Syrian government atrocities, spreading fear among the regime's opponents, collecting intelligence, and urging U.S. servicemembers to refuse their orders. No new technical indicators were reported in the events for this story; it is a sentencing development on a long-running, state-linked intrusion campaign.
