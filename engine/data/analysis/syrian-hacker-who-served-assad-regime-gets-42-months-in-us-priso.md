Ahmad Umar Agha, 42, was sentenced to 42 months in US federal prison for his role in the Syrian Electronic Army hacking operation directed by figures close to Bashar Assad, including Syria's General Intelligence Directorate chief. The conspiracy ran from August 2011 to January 2024 and targeted about 47 entities, including media outlets, NGOs, and US and allied government bodies. The most damaging intrusion came in April 2013, when Agha took over the Associated Press Twitter account to post a false claim that President Obama had been injured in White House explosions, triggering a flash crash that briefly erased $136 billion in Dow Jones value. Agha also targeted the Marine Corps recruiting site to urge Marines to defy orders.

This sentencing closes a 12-year gap since charges were first filed, underscoring the long timelines for prosecuting state-linked cyber operators. Watch whether this case affects how the DOJ pursues remaining Syrian Electronic Army members and whether Assad-linked cyber infrastructure faces further sanctions.
