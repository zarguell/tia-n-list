The CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence discovered a breach of an unclassified system used to analyze open-source intelligence, according to CyberScoop, Reuters, CNN, the Associated Press and the New York Times, which broke the story Tuesday. A source familiar with the incident told the AP the issue has been resolved and that no classified information was involved. ODNI said classified systems and critical capabilities were not disrupted.

What was taken remains unspecified. CNN said the timing, the responsible party and even whether the intrusion was fully remediated were unclear; CyberScoop reported only a limited disruption. The affected network pulls from news, social media, commercial satellite imagery and private data brokers. Huntress manager Ben Bernstein noted that access to analysts' searches and tracked topics can reveal intelligence priorities and blind spots without touching a classified file.

The incident lands after an internal NSA network for classified communications was offline for a time and while the FBI investigates an apparent hack of a national law enforcement database. CNN said this hack did not appear related to the FBI case. Watch for attribution, a clearer account of what data left the OSINT system, and whether information belonging to other agencies on the shared platform was exposed. ODNI oversees 17 US intelligence agencies.
