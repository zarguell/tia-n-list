The CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence discovered a breach of a network used for open-source intelligence analysis. The New York Times first reported it on Tuesday, and CyberScoop, Reuters, CNN and the Associated Press confirmed the incident through people familiar with it. Officials said classified networks and classified information were not involved; a source told the AP the issue has been resolved. Who was responsible, when the intrusion happened and what was copied remain unknown.

The affected system supports OSINT work, pulling in news reports, social media, satellite imagery and commercial data feeds from private brokers. Huntress analyst Ben Bernstein said an intruder could see which subjects analysts track and what they search for, exposing intelligence priorities and blind spots without touching a classified file. Reuters reported that information at other government agencies was also believed to have been exposed, and CNN noted ODNI oversees 17 US intelligence agencies.

What to watch is attribution and any accounting of the accessed material. CNN said this incident did not appear related to the apparent hack of the FBI national law enforcement database. Separate incidents in the same window included an internal NSA classified communications network that was offline for a time, according to AP reporting.
