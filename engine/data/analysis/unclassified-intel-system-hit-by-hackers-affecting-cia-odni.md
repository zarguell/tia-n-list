The CIA, the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, and, according to GBHackers, the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency discovered a breach of an unclassified system used to analyze open-source intelligence. The New York Times first reported the intrusion on November 24. A source told the Times that the hack did not involve classified networks or classified information and that no other systems were currently at risk. AP said the issue has been resolved, while CNN, citing four sources, said it was unclear when the hack occurred or who was responsible.

BleepingComputer reported that the source told the Times the attack was carried out with the ARTEX AI-powered penetration testing framework. The affected network handles public and commercially available material, including news reports, social media, commercial satellite imagery and private data feeds. ODNI said critical capabilities and classified systems were not disrupted, and the agency oversees 17 US intelligence agencies. No agency has identified what material was taken or whether any data were stolen.

The exposure matters because an OSINT platform shows analysts what they search for and which topics they track. Huntress manager Ben Bernstein told CyberScoop that this can reveal intelligence priorities and blind spots without touching a classified file, and that such networks are hard to secure because they must stay reachable from the public internet. Watch for an attribution statement, a clearer account of accessed data, and any link to the NSA internal network outage or the FinCEN intrusion disclosed hours after the first report.
