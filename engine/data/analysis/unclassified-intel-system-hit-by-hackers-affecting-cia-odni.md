The CIA, ODNI and, in some reports, the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency found a breach of an unclassified system used for open-source intelligence analysis. The New York Times first reported it on November 24, and CyberScoop, Reuters, AP and CNN carried confirmations from people familiar with the matter. A source quoted by CyberScoop said the hack did not touch classified networks or classified information and that no other systems were at risk. AP reported that the issue has been resolved.

The affected system ingests public and commercially available material such as news, social media, satellite imagery and paid data feeds. Huntress manager Ben Bernstein noted that access to analyst queries could reveal intelligence priorities and blind spots without exposing a classified file. One report said the intrusion used the ARTEX AI-powered penetration testing framework, but attribution, timing and whether data was taken remain unconfirmed across the reporting.

Watch for a clearer account of what was accessed, which agency owns the affected network and whether the NGA overlap is confirmed. The incident sits alongside a FinCEN system outage, an NSA internal communications network outage and an FBI check of a national law enforcement database, so shared infrastructure or a common access path is a reasonable question, not an established finding.
