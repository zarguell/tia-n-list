The New York Times first reported on November 24 that the CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence found a breach of a network used to analyze open-source intelligence. CyberScoop confirmed the report. AP sources said the affected system handles public and commercially available material from the internet, commercial satellites and private data brokers. Officials said classified networks and classified information were not involved, and a source told AP the issue has been resolved.

CNN cited four sources for the same intrusion and said it was not clear when the hack happened, who carried it out, or whether it had been resolved. GBHackers reported that the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency was also affected. BleepingComputer's account said the attack used the ARTEX AI-powered penetration testing framework. Reuters later reported CIA and ODNI breaches of unclassified systems, with later coverage placing the exposure on a network the agencies share for OSINT work.

The immediate risk is analytic rather than classic secret theft, since OSINT systems show what analysts search for and which topics they track. Huntress manager Ben Bernstein told CyberScoop that public and commercial inputs can still reveal intelligence priorities and blind spots. Several outlets placed the incident next to the reported FBI national law enforcement database hack, a temporary NSA classified communications network outage, and the same-day FinCEN disclosure. What remains unconfirmed is the actor, the exact material taken, and the initial access path.
