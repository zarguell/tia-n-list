The CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, with the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency named in some follow-up coverage, discovered a breach of an unclassified system used for open-source intelligence analysis. The New York Times first reported the intrusion on November 24, 2026. CyberScoop, Reuters, AP and CNN corroborated the core account. The system handles public and commercial material, including news, social media, satellite imagery and paid data feeds. ODNI said critical capabilities and classified systems were not disrupted, and an AP source said the issue had been resolved. The compromised material, timing and responsible actor remained unclear.

One follow-up report said the intrusion used ARTEX, an AI-powered penetration testing framework. The CIA and ODNI did not confirm that detail in those reports, so it remains a lead rather than established attribution. CNN, citing four sources, said the incident did not appear related to the apparent hack of the FBI's nationwide criminal information database. Huntress manager Ben Bernstein said the intelligence value lies in seeing what analysts search for and track, which can reveal priorities and blind spots without exposing a classified file. ODNI oversees 17 U.S. intelligence agencies, according to CNN.

Watch for identification of the system owner, the scope of any data access or theft, confirmation of NGA involvement, and independent evidence tying the intrusion to ARTEX. The breach emerged alongside an NSA internal communications network outage and FinCEN's decision to take systems offline after unauthorized access, but the reports do not establish a common actor or shared infrastructure. Those incidents should remain separate unless technical or investigative evidence links them.
