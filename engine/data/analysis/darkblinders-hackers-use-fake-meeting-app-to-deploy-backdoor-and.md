DarkBlinders, an Iran-nexus actor that Dream Research Labs links to UNC5795, Dust Specter, and APT34, ran an espionage operation from August through October 2026 against a high-profile individual in Israel and a government cloud environment in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq. Delivery used two paths: a trojanized fake video-meeting client called StarkMeet, presented as version 3.2 through an Inno Setup installer that drops a decoy under %LOCALAPPDATA%\StarkMeet and malicious components under %LOCALAPPDATA%\Microsoft\RuntimeBroker, and credential-harvesting pages that impersonated Outlook Web Access for Kuwait's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the GCC Secretariat General plus Google Drive sharing links themed around the Kurdistan Regional Government and its Ministry of Electricity. The backdoor is an AppDomainManager-based loader: a legitimate signed vshost.exe renamed RuntimeBroker.exe loads RuntimeBroker.dll, which derives a victim identifier from SHA256("Peaky Blinders 2.1" plus UserSID, MachineName, and UserDomainName), beacons host metadata to the PeakyBlindersTeam/myLic GitHub repository, and only decrypts the in-memory second stage RuntimeBrokerApi.dll after the operator supplies host-specific activation material. RuntimeBrokerApi.dll then polls PeakyBlindersTeam/myCode roughly every 63 seconds for tasking and runs PowerShell through an embedded PsProxy.dll without spawning powershell.exe, while persistence sits under HKCU\SOFTWARE\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run as MicrosoftRuntime with the first check at 121 seconds and hourly thereafter. Roughly ten distinct hosts appear in the available check-in data.

The reason this story matters more than the routine vulnerability roundup is that it describes a live, hands-on-keyboard operation with confirmed compromises rather than a theoretical bug, and its architecture is built to stay quiet: registration and activation are deliberately separated, so a host can sit dormant in myLic with no second-stage activity and still be one operator decision away from full access. The credential path needs no endpoint malware at all, since a successful OWA or Drive submission exposes email, contacts, shared files, and linked cloud services without triggering an EDR alert, which means environment-wide mailbox and cloud-credential resets are warranted for any organization that saw these lures. The GitHub-based channel also blends into normal enterprise traffic, and the selective activation model means absence of observed malicious traffic is not evidence of a clean estate. Exposure is narrow but high-value: recipients who installed StarkMeet 3.2 or submitted credentials to the lookalike portals, with the registered-host count suggesting targeted, small-scale operations against individuals and government cloud tenants rather than mass exploitation.

Defenders should treat this as tradecraft rather than a patchable CVE, so response centers on blocking and hunting. Block the StarkMeet decoy domains stark-meet.com, stark-meet.online, and stark-meet.website, the PeakyBlindersTeam GitHub repositories, and the Cloudflare Worker fallback, and hunt for %LOCALAPPDATA%\Microsoft\RuntimeBroker\RuntimeBroker.exe, the MicrosoftRuntime Run key, and the Global mutex whose name is the full hexadecimal SHA-256 of "GSC" plus the victim identifier. Telemetry to review includes GitHub API calls to api.github.com for PeakyBlindersTeam/myLic and PeakyBlindersTeam/myCode on a roughly 63-second cadence, plus PowerShell runspace activity that does not spawn powershell.exe. Recovered artifacts to search for include StarkMeet.zip, RuntimeBroker.dll, RuntimeBrokerApi.dll, PsProxy.dll, and SHELBYLOADER, and cloud tenants should be reviewed for unusual persistence or data exfiltration. Organizations that received the GCC or Kuwait Ministry of Foreign Affairs lures should reset affected credentials and audit mailbox rules and shared-drive access.

What would change this assessment is recovery of the original StarkMeet delivery message or meeting invitation, which would settle the unconfirmed initial-access vector, and any evidence that the actor has migrated to new infrastructure after GitHub's takedown of the prior repositories, as happened with the earlier Blinder Tunnel campaign. A new wave expanding the impersonation targets beyond Kuwait and the GCC to additional Gulf diplomatic entities would also raise the stakes, as would publication of detection rules that move this from targeted hunting to broad visibility.
