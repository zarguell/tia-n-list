In 2024, T-Mobile's security team physically cut a network cable to sever access by Salt Typhoon, the Chinese state-sponsored espionage group targeting telecommunications providers worldwide. According to Bloomberg reporting, the action came after months of incident response during which defenders could not fully contain the intrusion through conventional network-level controls. The physical disconnection was a last resort to guarantee the threat actor lost connectivity.

The incident underscores the severity of Salt Typhoon's campaign against US telecoms. The group, linked to China's Ministry of State Security, has been attributed to intrusions at multiple US carriers and is believed to have accessed metadata and in some cases call content of targeted individuals. T-Mobile's decision to resort to a physical air-gap suggests the adversary had established persistence that software-based remediation could not reliably eliminate.

This is a reference case for incident response at the boundary of network and physical security. When a sophisticated actor achieves deep persistence inside critical infrastructure, conventional containment may be insufficient. Expect other telcos to review physical kill-switch procedures. The broader Salt Typhoon campaign remains ongoing; CISA and FBI advisories on telecom-sector mitigations are still in effect.
