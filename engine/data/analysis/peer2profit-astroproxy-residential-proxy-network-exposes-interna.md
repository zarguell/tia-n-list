Silent Push research revealed that PEER2PROFIT, a bandwidth-sharing application that pays users roughly $0.28/GB to share their internet connection, actively supplies residential proxy capacity to ASTROPROXY, which resells it at $7.60/GB. Researchers confirmed the pipeline by enrolling a controlled device in PEER2PROFIT and detecting its IP in ASTROPROXY's proxy pool within hours. During a 72-hour enumeration, Silent Push observed 117,224 unique IPs across ASTROPROXY's pools, with the residential pool averaging 1,071 new addresses per hour.

The enterprise risk goes beyond outbound abuse. While ASTROPROXY blocks direct requests to private IP ranges, researchers found that internal network resources can be reached through specific HTTP request patterns and URL structures. This means employee devices running PEER2PROFIT (installed voluntarily or through organizational shadow IT) can become unauthorized proxy exit nodes that expose corporate IP space and internally reachable services. The rapid IP rotation (over a thousand new addresses per hour) makes traditional IP reputation controls ineffective.

The finding matters because residential proxy networks are increasingly weaponized for credential stuffing, account takeover, and fraud, but they also create a bidirectional exposure: the same infrastructure that lets attackers route through residential IPs can let them reach back into corporate networks from inside the perimeter. Security teams have limited visibility into bandwidth-sharing apps on employee devices, and the monetization model ($0.28 to $7.60 per GB spread) ensures ongoing growth of the network.
