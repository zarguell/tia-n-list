ARTEX is an LLM-driven offensive security platform, released in July 2026, that lets AI models autonomously drive vulnerability discovery and exploitation. Check Point Research observed more than 1,800 publicly exposed ARTEX instances, and Censys found exposed operator consoles on 1,465 hosts as of November 4. Check Point's console artifacts show which models operators used and how they prompted their agents, with Chinese models most prevalent and Deepseek Flash taking the lead. Attacks ran almost fully autonomous once the initial goal was set; operators mostly just checked progress.

The observed goals were opportunistic, financially motivated cybercrime at broad scale, abusing web vulnerabilities including remote code execution. In one intrusion, an ARTEX agent moved from searching GitHub for application secrets to exfiltrating Azure Key Vault data in five minutes, compressing credential discovery, access validation, resource mapping, and exfiltration. The platform is also linked to South Korean banking breaches and cloud supply-chain attacks, and the exposed consoles leaked sensitive operational files.

Watch for agentic frameworks collapsing the time between credential discovery and data exfiltration (five minutes in the observed case), which raises the priority of secret scanning and Key Vault alerting. The 1,465 exposed operator consoles mean operator mistakes are now a detection surface, and broader adoption of autonomous agent fleets in financially motivated crime is the trend to track next.
