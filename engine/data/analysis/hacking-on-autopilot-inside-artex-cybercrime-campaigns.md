Check Point Research's analysis describes ARTEX as an agentic hacking platform where AI models autonomously drive vulnerability discovery and exploitation, with more than 1,800 publicly exposed ARTEX instances observed. Operator console artifacts show which models were used and how operators prompted their agents, and the attacks ran almost fully autonomously except for the initial goal setup. Operators mostly checked progress; the goals were exclusively opportunistic, financially motivated cybercrime at broad scale, and agents abused web vulnerabilities such as remote code execution. Chinese models were most prevalent in the observed activity, led by Deepseek Flash.

Censys ARC independently identifies 1,465 hosts exposing ARTEX consoles, and 155 of those hosts also run at least one other threat Censys detects. The two reports describe the same exposure from different angles: Check Point from operator-console telemetry, Censys from internet-exposure scanning. Neither report names a CVE or confirms victim counts beyond the exposed consoles.

Defenders should treat exposed agentic-AI consoles as a live intrusion surface: an internet-facing console means an operator is running an autonomous attack fleet with minimal supervision. Watch for Censys SITREP updates on exposure trends, any takedowns or patches of exposed ARTEX instances, and reporting tying console operators to specific intrusion campaigns or ransomware deployment.
