GEEKOM confirmed that a malware-flagged Realtek LAN driver package was hosted on an outdated support page for its mini PCs. The file remained online after the company migrated to a newer support system, meaning search-engine results could still surface the legacy page and its poisoned download. GEEKOM said the issue was limited to old support pages with no evidence of infection in factory-installed Windows images or the current support portal. Only users who manually downloaded from the outdated page would have been exposed.

The incident is notable because driver packages are trusted software components. Users download them expecting vendor-signed installers for Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or audio hardware. A compromised driver installer in that context is a classic supply-chain attack vector: the victim believes they are patching hardware and has no reason to question the file. That GEEKOM's own infrastructure served the payload, even on a legacy page, undermines the trust model that keeps driver updates safe.

Reporting links the malicious package to Asruex command-and-control infrastructure via a network contact. However, GEEKOM's public statement does not confirm an Asruex attribution, identify the specific domain contacted, or provide technical indicators such as file hashes, IP addresses, or traffic logs. That connection should be treated as a reported indicator requiring independent validation rather than a confirmed finding. The vendor has not explained how the malware reached its servers in the first place.

Defenders should watch for follow-up technical disclosure from GEEKOM or independent researchers, particularly IOC lists and detonation reports for the affected driver package. Organizations using GEEKOM mini PCs should verify whether any machines in their fleet were sourced from the legacy support page. The broader pattern of abandoned vendor support pages hosting stale, potentially compromised downloads is worth monitoring across other hardware vendors.
