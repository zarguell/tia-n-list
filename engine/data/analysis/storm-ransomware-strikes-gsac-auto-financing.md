The Storm ransomware group claimed a successful attack on GSAC Auto Financing (gsacfinance.com), a significant player in the U.S. auto financing industry, on September 3, 2026. The group threatened to release sensitive data unless the company engages in negotiations. In a separate but related development, Storm also claimed a breach of Macquarrie Corporation following a third-party compromise, indicating the group is actively targeting the financial services sector across multiple victims simultaneously.

Storm's threat actor statement is direct: "Negotiations have failed. GSAC Auto Financing's data will be fully leaked unless immediate contact is made." This language suggests the group has already exfiltrated data and moved past the initial negotiation phase. The auto financing sector handles sensitive financial data including Social Security numbers, loan applications, and payment histories, making any breach in this industry particularly consequential for affected consumers.

The dual claims against GSAC and Macquarrie within the same week suggest Storm is running concurrent operations against mid-market financial firms. Organizations in the auto financing and lending space should review their third-party risk posture, as the Macquarrie breach reportedly originated from a third-party compromise. Monitoring for leaked data on Storm's leak sites and dark web forums is advisable for any organization in this sector.
