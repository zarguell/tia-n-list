Microsoft's September 2026 Patch Tuesday addressed 974 CVEs, making it the largest single security update in Microsoft's history. The release includes 119 critical-severity bugs and patches for two zero-day vulnerabilities that were already being exploited in the wild. One of the exploited zero-days was used in the TerminalFix campaign, which hit Berlin's Senate network before awareness training could catch up with the threat. Four distinct espionage clusters were observed leveraging vulnerabilities from this release cycle.

The TerminalFix campaign is particularly notable because it targeted a government entity and exploited the gap between patch availability and user awareness. The two zero-days in the wild indicate that at least two vulnerability chains were actively weaponized before Microsoft's disclosure. The sheer volume of 974 CVEs reflects the expanding attack surface across Windows, Office, Exchange, Azure, and other Microsoft products.

CISOs face a prioritization challenge with this volume of fixes. The two exploited zero-days and the 119 critical bugs should be patched first, followed by any vulnerabilities with public exploit code. Organizations should also review their exposure to the TerminalFix campaign indicators and check for signs of the four espionage clusters' activity in their networks.
