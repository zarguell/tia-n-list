Italy and Switzerland warned on 25 November 2026 about large-scale cyberattacks in which autonomous AI agents scan exposed endpoints, exploit vulnerabilities and misconfigurations, and use compromised credentials to reach internal networks. The alert names four offensive frameworks seen in recent breaches: ARTEX AI, CyberStrikeAI, DeepSeek Harness Red Team and PentAGI. CSIRT Italia says multiple agents worked in parallel to compromise many systems within hours, and describes one DeepSeek Harness setup where a coordinator assigned five agents tasks spanning information gathering, exploitation and persistence.

The warning matters because the agents linked low-impact flaws into complex attack chains and turned exposed systems into footholds, instead of stopping at automated scanning. The agencies told critical infrastructure operators to reduce internet-facing exposure, prioritize patching and monitor for unusual activity. That advice treats agent speed as an operational problem for defenders, since the observed campaigns adapted their modules and moved from discovery to persistence in a single run.

What to watch is whether the named frameworks show up in incident telemetry and whether the coordinator pattern spreads beyond the reported campaigns. The alert gives no victim count, CVE or actor attribution, so its concrete value is the tooling list and the observed behavior. Absent those details, the useful check for operators is exposure: which endpoints are public, which are unpatched, and which credential leaks would let an agent chain a minor bug into a network foothold.
