A scan of ICS and building automation systems near more than 1,000 U.S. data center locations found 548 internet-exposed devices running end-of-life products that will not receive future security patches. The affected lines are Tridium NiagaraAX 3.x, Trane Tracer SC, and Carrier WebCTRL version 7.0, platforms used to manage HVAC, cooling, power monitoring, and environmental controls in the facilities that keep servers online.

The broader survey identified 6,300 high-confidence internet-accessible devices, including BACnet controllers, Niagara platforms, programmable logic controllers, and power management interfaces, filtered from 73,847 raw Shodan records through five validation layers, about 8.5% of initial results. A compromised building automation controller on a data center campus is a physical infrastructure risk: an attacker who reaches the BAS layer can disrupt cooling or power, a denial-of-service vector against facilities hosting critical infrastructure.

Watch for the report's full device and location data and whether any of the 548 EOL devices sit near known operational tech exploitation. The exposure is chronic rather than a single event, so the near-term signal is which of these legacy lines, if any, get picked up by scanner-driven campaigns or OT-focused actors.
