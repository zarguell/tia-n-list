CVE-2024-37079 is a pre-authentication heap overflow in the DCERPC implementation that VMware vCenter Server carries in its fork of the Likewise Open library, scored 9.8 and reachable by any actor with network access to the appliance. The vulnerable libdcerpc.so runs inside the VMware Certificate Management Service, Directory Service and Authentication Framework, listening on TCP 2014, 2012 and 2020 respectively. Broadcom patched it on June 17, 2024 in VMSA-2024-0012 together with CVE-2024-37080 and CVE-2024-37081, with fixes landing in 8.0 U2d, 8.0 U1e and 7.0 U3r plus an asynchronous patch line for Cloud Foundation 5.x and 4.x. ZDI's writeup of August 28, 2024 credits Hao Zheng and Zibo Li of Qi'anxin's TianGong Team as the original discoverers and pins the trigger down precisely: a DCERPC Bind or Alter Context request carrying exactly 169 presentation context elements makes an unsigned subtraction wrap, and the resulting oversized copy corrupts the heap.

The score is not the story here, the clock is. CISA added CVE-2024-37079 to the KEV catalog on January 23, 2026, nineteen months after the patch, marking exploitation as active and the bug as automatable, and the cybertop post that generated this story repeats the actively exploited warning in August 2026. An organization hit by this bug in 2026 does not have a zero-day problem, it has two years of patch debt on the management interface of its vSphere cluster, where remote code execution means control of the virtualization layer every workload runs on. The KEV automatable flag and the fixed, fingerprintable trigger size cut both ways: mass exploitation is cheap to run, and the same packet shape is cheap to detect, so an unpatched appliance is simultaneously a breach candidate and a place where a compromise would be visible only if someone were looking. Among the vCenter advisories of recent months, this one is evidence that the 2024 DCERPC bug wave never finished clearing out of estates.

The patch targets are exact: 8.0 U2d or later on the 8.0 line, 8.0 U1e or later on 8.0 U1, 7.0 U3r on 7.0, and the Cloud Foundation asynchronous patch from the advisory; ESXi hosts are not affected. The advisory carries no workaround, so until patching completes, restrict TCP 2012, 2014 and 2020 to management networks and apply the appliance VAMI firewall as a compensating control. The specific hunt comes from ZDI: parse DCE/RPC on those three ports and flag packet types 0x0b (Bind) or 0x0e (Alter Context) with a nonzero auth_len and 169 or more presentation context elements, a combination with no legitimate sender. One patching trap is documented in Broadcom's FAQ: on 7.0 U3q and U3r, custom TLS cipher configurations abort the vpxd-svcs service start, and the precheck is described in KB 369485. Any appliance that missed the February 13, 2026 KEV deadline deserves forensic triage, not just a patch queue entry.

Two developments would change this assessment. CISA flipping the ransomware-campaign-use field from Unknown, or adding the forensic-triage flag it applies to confirmed intrusion vectors, would turn patch debt into incident response across every laggard estate. And public exploit code that removes the friction ZDI described, the exact 169-element arithmetic and the multi-step authentication negotiation, would widen the attacker set beyond careful operators to commodity scanning, a step the KEV record's automatable marking already anticipates.
