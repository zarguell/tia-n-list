The Gentlemen, tracked as GOLD SHERWOOD, is a Russian-speaking ransomware-as-a-service group that emerged in mid-2025, reportedly after affiliates split from established groups such as LockBit and Qilin. Acronis and Sophos research describe a fast, destructive playbook: affiliates steal data, disable security tools and backup services, then encrypt systems across victim networks, with a median of about two days between first post-compromise activity and encryption and some attacks moving from initial access to ransomware deployment in under 24 hours. The group operates double extortion, threatening to publish stolen files before encrypting. It offers affiliates up to 90 percent of ransom revenue, has claimed more than 800 victims across the US and Europe, and produces cross-platform variants for Windows, Linux, NAS, and BSD. Affiliates gain access through exposed firewall vulnerabilities or stolen VPN credentials, including a Fortinet SSL VPN login with compromised credentials where multi-factor authentication was not enabled, then move laterally via Remote Desktop Protocol, targeting file servers and domain controllers and storing tools in C:\PerfLogs. EDR-killing tooling includes a custom dropper abusing vulnerable Safetica ProcessMonitor and Lenovo BootRepair.sys drivers in a bring-your-own-vulnerable-driver attack. The group's own infrastructure was breached, giving researchers rare visibility into affiliates, and the leak site had listed 683 victims by the end of July 2026, including 169 added in July alone. The group is linked to CVE-2025-70795.

The most recent leak feed entry added ImmoVia Hausverwaltung to the victim list alongside other organizations, consistent with continued activity into late October 2026. The group targets opportunistically across industries rather than focusing on a single sector.

Defenders should prioritize exposed firewall and VPN hygiene, enforce multi-factor authentication on remote access, monitor for lateral movement via RDP and tool staging in C:\PerfLogs, and watch for BYOVD-based EDR tampering. Because encryption can follow initial access within hours, detection and containment speed matter more than perimeter hardening alone. Organizations with Fortinet SSL VPN or similar edge devices should verify patching and credential hygiene.
