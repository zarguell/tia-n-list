A phishing campaign tracked by Fortra's Intelligence and Research Experts (FIRE) has been abusing Windows mshta.exe since June 2026, targeting Spanish-speaking users at global organizations. The attackers send emails disguised as invoices or judicial notices, with subjects like "Facturacion" and "Aviso Judicial," originating primarily from accounts hosted by Italian provider libero.it. A notable element is the apparent abuse of Microsoft's spam-confidence-level system: messages categorized as high-confidence phishing reportedly received an SCL:-1 value, allowing them to bypass quarantine and reach recipients' inboxes directly.

The attack chain begins when a victim clicks a shortened URL in the phishing email. Links redirect through services including goo[.]su, abrir[.]link, and abre[.]ai before landing on a malicious delivery page hosted through archivogratuito[.]online. Victims are prompted to open an HTA file such as Doc_02º.HTA. Once launched, mshta.exe, a signed Microsoft binary, executes the HTML Application, enabling system reconnaissance and staged deployment of credential-stealing malware. Because mshta.exe is a legitimate signed binary, the activity blends with normal Windows operations and evades many endpoint detection rules.

The campaign is notable for its combination of infrastructure abuse (Italian hosting, SCL bypass) and living-off-the-land execution. The SCL:-1 bypass is particularly concerning as it undermines a core email defense layer. Fortra reports the operators regularly recompile malware samples to evade detection. Organizations should consider blocking mshta.exe execution from email-originating processes, monitoring for the identified redirect domains, and reviewing Exchange or Microsoft 365 email filtering rules for SCL override anomalies.
