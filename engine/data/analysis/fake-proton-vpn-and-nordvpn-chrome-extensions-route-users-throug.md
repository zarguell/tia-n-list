Socket's threat research team identified a network of fake Chrome VPN extensions impersonating trusted brands, including Proton VPN, NordVPN, Surfshark, ExpressVPN, Cloudflare 1.1.1.1, AmneziaVPN, and AntiZapret. In total 737 free VPN and proxy extensions were tied to at least 40 Chrome Web Store developer accounts, and 274 of them impersonated 66 known VPN and privacy brands. The campaign accumulated 75,486 displayed installs, with 516 extensions still live carrying 58,318 installs at collection time. Instead of providing a VPN, the extensions route browser traffic through a separate SOCKS5 proxy network controlled by the operators.

The operation primarily targets Russian-speaking users seeking access to blocked services such as Instagram and ChatGPT, which means the abuse model is surveillance and traffic interception of users who think they are buying privacy. The mitigation is Chrome Web Store hygiene: verify publisher identity, check extension permissions, and treat free VPN extensions from unknown developers as high risk. Users in the targeted region should use first-party VPN clients and avoid proxy-extension shortcuts.
