A threat actor leaked a database of over 41,000 Department of Homeland Security employee records, including names, work emails, phone numbers, job titles, office locations, and internal system access details. The data covers Customs and Border Protection, Immigration and Customs Enforcement, Homeland Security Investigations, and about 1,000 CISA employees. The actor, using the handle Department of Peace, claimed the data was stolen from an Okta single sign-on system. Okta denied any compromise of its own infrastructure, and DHS said it is actively addressing the report.

The leak includes an online search tool, increasing the risk of targeted phishing and social engineering against DHS staff. Because the data reveals which systems and applications each account can access, attackers could craft highly specific lures using the exposed organizational context. Partners and contractors should treat this as a credible data exposure and review authentication and access logs for any anomalous use of DHS-related accounts or services.
