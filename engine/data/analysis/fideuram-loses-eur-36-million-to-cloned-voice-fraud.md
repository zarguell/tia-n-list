Fideuram, the wealth management arm of Intesa Sanpaolo, was hit by a cloned-voice fraud that first reporting put at 36 million euros. Redhotcyber's account framed the aftermath as the start of a "Human ZeroTrust" challenge for the bank. Newer reporting by Insicurezza Digitale reconstructs the attack as a WhatsApp voice message followed by a phone call with a cloned voice, and puts the loss at 95 million euros. The discrepancy between the two figures is unresolved in the collected coverage and matters for how serious this incident actually is.

Either number makes this one of the largest documented voice-cloning frauds against a financial institution. The technique defeated the implicit trust in a familiar voice on a familiar channel, which is exactly the control most treasury and client-payment processes still rely on. Italian banks are a bellwether here because their client onboarding and payment flows lean heavily on phone authorization.

Watch for Fideuram or Intesa Sanpaolo to confirm an official loss figure, for details on which internal process authorized the transfers, and for Banca d'Italia or EU banking regulators to respond with voice-verification or callback requirements. If the 95 million figure holds, expect this case to be cited in every deepfake-fraud control pitch for the next year.
