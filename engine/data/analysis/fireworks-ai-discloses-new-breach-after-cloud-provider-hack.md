Fireworks AI disclosed two linked October 2026 incidents: an October 6 breach where unauthorized use of internal credentials exposed environment variables holding API keys and tokens for a limited number of customer accounts, and an October 23 breach of a third-party cloud provider hosting Fireworks R&D machines that an unauthorized party used on October 24 to reach Fireworks development machines and run an unauthorized workload. Fireworks says the activity is tied to a financially motivated group pursuing LLMJacking, reselling stolen access to large language models through illicit routing gateways.

A November 9 update expanded the scope to account and billing information (billing and shipping addresses, invoice and payment history, account balance, credit usage; no payment card numbers), plus incomplete model weights and datasets for certain customers; Fireworks says it does not store payment card numbers. Notion, a Fireworks customer, confirmed that AI chat transcripts were exposed for fewer than 1% of workspaces after Fireworks alerted it on November 2 about an exposed dataset initially described as synthetic test data; Notion cut traffic to Fireworks, deleted data from the vendor's systems, and halted training runs by November 3, while Fireworks confirmed customer data access on November 8.

What to watch: the investigation remains ongoing and at least two customers told Forbes they had already moved off Fireworks; customers should rotate API keys and review third-party credentials. The case illustrates supply-chain risk in AI inference: a cloud-provider compromise cascaded into customer data and model artifacts.
