Fireworks AI, an inference platform for open-source models such as Mistral, Meta's Llama, and Google's Gemma, disclosed a second security incident on top of an October 6 breach. The first incident involved unauthorized use of internal credentials that exposed environment variables containing API keys and tokens stored as secrets in evaluation jobs for a limited number of customer accounts; Fireworks revoked the compromised keys and engaged a forensics firm, and at the time said it had not detected access to customer models, datasets, personal information, or inference traffic. During that response, Fireworks discovered a second incident: a third-party cloud provider hosting its research and development machines was breached on October 23, and an unauthorized party used that access on October 24 to reach Fireworks development machines and run an unauthorized workload. The expanded disclosure adds resource metadata, billing and shipping addresses, invoice and payment history, account balances, and credit usage, plus incomplete model weights and datasets for some customers. Fireworks says it does not store payment card numbers. The company attributes the activity to a financially motivated cybercriminal group pursuing LLMJacking, which steals access to large language models and resells capacity through illicit routing gateways. Notion notified its own customers that they were affected through the third-party AI vendor.

The cloud-provider supply chain is the recurring pattern here: the second path into Fireworks systems came not through Fireworks' own perimeter but through a hosted R&D environment, which is a common blind spot in AI infrastructure. Fireworks links both incidents to the same LLMJacking monetization model. Two customers told Forbes they had already moved off the platform, with one citing poor communication, so trust and retention are already eroding. The exposure of incomplete model weights is the most sensitive element, since even partial weights can reduce the cost of reconstructing a proprietary model.

What to watch: whether the investigation shows that complete model weights or training data were accessed, since only incomplete weights are confirmed so far; the full customer scope and which organizations beyond Notion were affected; and whether Fireworks' key-rotation and third-party-credential review guidance reaches all affected users. Platform trust in AI inference providers depends on whether this disclosure is the last revision or another expansion. The investigation remains ongoing.
