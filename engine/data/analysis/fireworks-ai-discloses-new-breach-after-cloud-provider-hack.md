Fireworks AI disclosed a second security incident in which attackers reached its development machines through a compromised third-party cloud provider. The cloud provider hosting Fireworks R&D machines suffered its own incident on October 23, 2026, and on October 24 an unauthorized party used that access to run an unauthorized workload on Fireworks development machines. The intrusion exposed customer resource metadata, billing and shipping addresses, invoice and payment history, account balances, credit usage, and incomplete model weights and datasets for certain customers. This followed an earlier October 6 incident in which internal credentials were used to access environment variables in evaluation jobs for a limited number of accounts, an intrusion Fireworks linked to a financially motivated LLMJacking group selling unauthorized large language model access through illicit routing gateways.

A successful attack here means more than leaked billing records: partial model weights and datasets are proprietary artifacts that can accelerate model distillation or reconstruction, while billing addresses, invoice history, and credit usage give attackers a ready-made profile for targeted fraud against AI customers. The exposure is described as limited to certain accounts, but the second incident shows that revoking compromised credentials and attacker-created keys after the first breach did not close the path, because the attacker pivoted through the cloud provider instead. That supply-chain pivot is what separates this from a routine credential leak: the control plane failed at the provider boundary, not inside Fireworks code.

Forbes reported on November 9 that two customers told it they had already moved their business off Fireworks systems, with one describing a series of very bad hacks and poor communication. Fireworks says it does not store payment card numbers, and the investigation remains ongoing. Defenders should rotate all Fireworks API keys and any third-party credentials identified in customer notices, review environment variables used in evaluation jobs, revoke attacker-created keys even where no suspicious activity is visible, and restrict workload and R&D access at the provider boundary.

Watch for expanded scope disclosures as the investigation continues, particularly whether complete datasets or full model weights were later found accessible. Also watch for law enforcement or provider-side action against the unnamed cloud provider and the LLMJacking group, which would clarify whether the October 23 and October 6 intrusions share one actor.
