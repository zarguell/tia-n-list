Healthcare breaches at Clover Health Investments and AngMar Management Services have exposed data on more than 250,000 people. Clover Health, based in Jersey City, New Jersey, was hacked in early July when attackers used social engineering to compromise three non-managerial employee accounts. The company said stolen information included PII and PHI such as names, dates of birth, insurance identifiers, and account numbers. In mid-September Clover told HHS that 138,677 people were affected. AngMar Management Services, based in Mansfield, Texas, identified suspicious activity in mid-July and confirmed in early September that hackers stole patient PII and PHI including Social Security numbers, diagnosis details, medical history, health insurance information, prescription details, and dates of service. Both incidents were added to federal breach tracking.

The Clover incident shows how social engineering of non-managerial staff can bypass technical controls and lead to large-scale health data theft. The AngMar breach expands risk to business-process vendors that handle home health and hospice operations, a sector with extensive third-party exposure. Watch for additional HHS updates and whether either organization confirms ransomware involvement; the AngMar event references The Interlock ransomware group.
