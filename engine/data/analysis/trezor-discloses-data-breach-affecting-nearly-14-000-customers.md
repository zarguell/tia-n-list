Trezor disclosed a data breach affecting nearly 14,000 customers after ShipMonk, its shipping provider and logistics partner, was compromised. Attackers accessed order data: 11,742 customers had full exposure of name, email, phone number, and shipping address, and 1,947 had partial exposure of name, city, and email. Affected customers received orders between May 10 and August 8, 2026, across the United States, the United Kingdom, Sweden, Colombia, Brazil, Italy, and Portugal. ShipMonk informed Trezor of the unauthorized access on Monday, August 10, 2026; Trezor's own systems and services were not compromised.

The exposure is exactly the dataset a crypto-phishing campaign needs: hardware wallet owners with verified order history, real names, and direct contact channels. Trezor itself flags that scammers can use the leaked information for phishing attempts. The breach is a logistics-vendor compromise rather than a wallet or key-material incident, so funds are not directly at risk, but the social engineering surface for these customers is now substantial.

Watch for phishing impersonating Trezor support or shipping notifications, especially anything referencing the breach window, order numbers, or wallet recovery. Customers in the affected cohort should treat unsolicited contacts as hostile, verify any recovery-seed or firmware prompts against official channels, and enable hardware wallet protections like passphrases if not already set.
