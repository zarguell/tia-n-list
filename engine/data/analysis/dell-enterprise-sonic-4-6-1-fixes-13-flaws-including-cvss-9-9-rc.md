Dell advisory DSA-2026-451, dated 2026-11-19, covers 13 proprietary-code defects in Enterprise SONiC Distribution and names 4.6.1 as the fixed release; versions before 4.6.1 are affected. The lead issue, CVE-2026-103782, was published on 2026-11-23 with CVSS 3.1 score 9.9 and vector AV:N/AC:L/PR:L/UI:N/S:C/C:H/I:H/A:H, describing argument injection that a low-privileged remote attacker can turn into arbitrary command execution on a switch. SecurityOnline's report at 2026-11-25T01:00:41Z says two flaws in the batch permit arbitrary command execution. Nothing in the advisory, CVE records, or event reports exploitation, and OpenCVE showed KEV not listed and EPSS unavailable at publication.

The mechanism matters more than the score. PR:L still requires a usable low-privileged account with network reach to the switch management plane, so credential hygiene and segmentation decide whether a 9.9 is reachable at all. Scope is every Enterprise SONiC Distribution switch still running a build before 4.6.1, and no installed-base count appears in the material reviewed, so exposure has to be expressed as a version boundary rather than a device total. That combination, a 13-fix bundle, a network operating system, and a management-plane RCE with a six-day public lead before this story was first seen at 2026-11-25T07:25:34Z, is why it earns desk attention over a single library CVE. Command execution under the network operating system gives an attacker a foothold on the device that controls traffic forwarding, and the changed-scope vector means consequences can extend beyond the vulnerable component.

Upgrade affected switches to Enterprise SONiC 4.6.1; no workaround appears in the advisory material found. Until upgrades land, restrict management-plane access, including gNMI Dial-in sessions and CLI services, to dedicated networks, and remove low-privileged accounts that do not need switch access. The published guidance starts with switches whose management interfaces are reachable from less trusted networks. Hunt switch management audit records and gNMI Dial-in sessions for commands or argument strings issued by low-privileged accounts that do not match normal automation, and check those accounts for recent use. No log path, port, or IOC name is published in the advisory, so the hunt should key on account and management-session telemetry rather than invented filenames.

The assessment changes if a working exploit, a patch diff naming the affected management component, or telemetry showing scanning or exploitation of SONiC management interfaces appears. A CISA KEV listing, a first EPSS score, or a Dell update that narrows the affected range below 4.6.1 would also change the priority. Absence of exploitation reporting so far is a snapshot, not evidence that attackers have ignored these switches.
