Cleverbridge disclosed on November 20 that an unauthorized third party copied payment card data from its platform. The company detected unusual activity on November 4, took systems offline to contain the incident, and brought them back online the same day. Its status feed shows a major-impact platform availability issue beginning at 09:53 UTC on November 4, a fix deployed by 10:24 UTC, intermittent regional problems later in the afternoon, and closure at 19:06 UTC. The subsequent investigation concluded that card data stored on the platform had been copied.

The public disclosure does not enumerate the stolen fields, but a notification shared with BleepingComputer lists payment card numbers, expiration dates, and CVVs (CVV/CVC codes). That combination is enough for card-not-present fraud and makes the affected customers' notification letters the main source of practical detail. The company sells e-commerce and subscription billing services to software vendors including Red Hat, SUSE, Tenable, Veeam, Jamf, Sony, and Parallels, but has not said which of those vendors' customers are affected.

The useful watch items are the downstream notices from the software vendors that use Cleverbridge, any count of affected cardholders, and whether the theft is confined to the November 4 intrusion. Until the impacted resellers are named, the exposure is difficult to scope from the outside, and cardholders should rely on the individual notifications rather than the generic incident page.
