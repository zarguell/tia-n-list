Cleverbridge, a commerce and billing platform used by software and SaaS vendors, says a third party copied payment card data stored on its platform. The company detected unauthorized activity on 4 November 2026 and took systems offline to contain it, with all systems back online that day. Notices to affected customers began on 20 November. A new social post points back to Cleverbridge's incident update and restates the 4 November attack date. The public disclosure does not name the affected merchants or give a victim count, though Cleverbridge lists customers including Red Hat, SUSE, Tenable, Veeam, Jamf, Sony and Parallels.

The notification shared with BleepingComputer is more specific than the public page: it lists payment card numbers, expiration dates and CVV/CVC security codes. Cleverbridge's status feed records a major-impact platform availability issue opening at 09:53 UTC on 4 November, a fix deployed at 10:24 UTC, intermittent regional problems through 14:20 UTC, and closure at 19:06 UTC with resolution stated since 14:25 UTC. That window is the intrusion timeline, not an ordinary outage. CVV/CVC is sensitive authentication data that payment standards bar from post-authorization storage, so its presence in the copied set raises questions about what the platform retained.

No patch applies to a data-theft incident. Buyers who paid through Cleverbridge should obtain written scope from the company and reissue affected cards rather than rely only on issuer monitoring, given that numbers, expiry dates and CVV/CVC were taken. Defenders with checkout or cardholder-data systems touching Cleverbridge should hunt the 4 November window for privileged access, bulk exports and new accounts. The assessment changes if Cleverbridge names affected merchants, publishes a victim count, or explains how CVV/CVC was available to copy; corroborated fraud against notified cards or a forensic finding on the intrusion path would also change scope.
