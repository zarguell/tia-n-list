Cleverbridge disclosed that a third party copied payment card data stored on its e-commerce platform. The company detected unauthorized activity on November 4 and took systems offline to contain it; all systems were back online that day. Notices to affected customers began on November 20. The public incident page does not say which card fields were taken, but a notification shared with BleepingComputer lists card numbers, expiration dates and CVV/CVC codes.

The platform handles checkout and subscriptions for software and SaaS vendors including Red Hat, SUSE, Tenable, Veeam, Jamf, Sony and Parallels. Cleverbridge has not named the businesses whose customers were affected. Its status feed records a major-impact incident starting at 09:53 UTC on November 4, a deployed fix at 10:24, intermittent regional problems at 13:51 and 14:20, and closure at 19:06 with resolution since 14:25. That timeline shows the availability incident and the data theft overlap, but it does not explain the intrusion path.

Watch for the customer-by-customer scope, which card fields appear in individual notices, and whether the company publishes an access method or actor detail. The presence of CVV/CVC codes in the stolen set is the sharpest concrete point in the reporting. There is no exploit, CVE or campaign attribution in the event material, so the case rests on the company's disclosure and the notification obtained by BleepingComputer.
