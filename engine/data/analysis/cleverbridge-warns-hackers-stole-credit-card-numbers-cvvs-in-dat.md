Cleverbridge, an e-commerce and billing platform used by software and SaaS vendors, says a third party copied payment card data stored on its platform. The company detected unauthorized activity on 4 November 2026, took systems offline to contain it and brought all systems back the same day. Notification of affected customers began on 20 November. Cleverbridge lists clients including Red Hat, SUSE, Tenable, Veeam, Jamf, Sony and Parallels, but has not said which of those businesses' customers are affected.

A notification shared with BleepingComputer is more specific than the public page: the stolen information includes payment card numbers, expiration dates and CVV/CVC security codes. The public disclosure only says payment card data was copied and directs customers to individual notices for details. CVV/CVC data should not be retained by merchants after authorization, so its presence in the copied set raises questions about how Cleverbridge stored card records and what systems the intruder reached.

Watch for clarification on the number of affected customers, the retention practice that left CVV/CVC codes available, and any linked fraud reports from the named vendors' user bases. For now the concrete facts are the 4 November detection, the 20 November notification start and the card data categories in the shared notice.
