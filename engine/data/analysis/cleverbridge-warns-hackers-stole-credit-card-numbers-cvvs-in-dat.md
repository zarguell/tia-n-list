Cleverbridge, a merchant of record and billing platform used by software and SaaS vendors, says a third party copied payment card data stored on its platform. It detected unauthorized activity on 4 November 2026 and took affected systems offline; its status page logged a major-impact "Platform availability issue" opening at 09:53 UTC, a fix deployed at 10:24 UTC, intermittent regional problems through 14:20 UTC and closure at 19:06 UTC. Notices to affected individuals began on 20 November. The notice shared with BleepingComputer lists card numbers, expiry dates and CVV/CVC codes, while the public page names no fields.

That status-page timeline is the intrusion window rather than an ordinary outage, and it was public while Cleverbridge was still investigating what had been copied. Exposure sits with buyers who paid through Cleverbridge, including customers of named vendors such as Red Hat, SUSE, Tenable, Veeam, Jamf, Sony and Parallels, yet no affected merchant or victim count has been published. CVV/CVC is sensitive authentication data that payment standards bar from post-authorization storage, so the copied fields suggest the intruder reached a store that should have held no reusable security codes and that the records are directly usable for card-not-present fraud.

No patch applies; this is a data-theft incident. Organizations that used Cleverbridge as merchant of record should enumerate every purchase and subscription routed through it, obtain written scope from Cleverbridge and reissue affected cards rather than relying on issuer monitoring alone, given that numbers, expiry dates and CVV/CVC were taken. Hunt the 4 November 09:53 to 14:25 UTC window for privileged access, bulk exports or new accounts touching checkout and cardholder-data stores, and brief support staff on notices sent via TransUnion/Cyberscout, which Cleverbridge calls legitimate but says will never request card details, passwords or security codes.

The assessment changes if Cleverbridge names the affected merchants and publishes a victim count, or explains how CVV/CVC was available to copy, since either would turn an opaque downstream incident into a scoped one. Corroborated fraud against the notified cards or a forensic finding on the intrusion path would also raise severity.
