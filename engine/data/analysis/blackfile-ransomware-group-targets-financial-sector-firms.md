BlackFile, tracked by Google Threat Intelligence Group as UNC6671 and linked to The Com cybercrime network, has been actively targeting financial sector firms since early 2026. The group impersonates IT support staff in vishing and social engineering campaigns to gain initial access, then splits extortion demands across four brand names sharing infrastructure: Redact, Pink, Helix, and Falcon. GTIG analyst Austin Larsen confirmed continued targeting of financial organizations with expansion into med tech, describing the group as "big-game hunting" against large enterprises.

The extortion playbook has evolved. BlackFile now claims to exfiltrate and delete data rather than simply threatening to leak it, and has adopted a tiered ransom model: initial demands around $3 million, negotiated down to sub-$1 million settlements. Several payments were confirmed in recent weeks. The group impacts organizations across healthcare, technology, transportation, logistics, wholesale, retail, and hospitality.

What to watch: BlackFile's brand-splitting strategy (Redact, Pink, Helix, Falcon) means organizations may face extortion demands from seemingly unrelated actors using the same underlying infrastructure. Financial sector defenders should correlate indicators across all four brand names. The shift toward data-deletion claims rather than pure leak threats raises the stakes for recovery planning.
