Microsoft released 974 security patches in September 2026, its largest single Patch Tuesday batch on record, obliterating the previous mark of 570 set in July. The 2026 patch total now exceeds 2,600 CVEs, more than double the previous annual record of 1,245 set in 2020, with three months remaining. One hundred thirteen of the fixes carry a critical severity rating.

Two zero-day vulnerabilities are confirmed actively exploited in the wild: CVE-2026-81963 and CVE-2026-85880, both privilege escalation flaws on Windows systems. The Threadlinqs threat brief also flags 20 wormable remote code executions within the batch. Microsoft credits AI-assisted discovery for accelerating the volume of identified vulnerabilities, but the practical burden of testing and deploying nearly 1,000 fixes in a single cycle is straining enterprise security teams.

The sheer scale elevates this from routine patching to a prioritization crisis. Organizations should triage the two actively exploited zero-days and the 113 critical-severity fixes first, then work through the remaining high and moderate patches on a risk-weighted schedule. The privilege escalation zero-days are particularly dangerous when chained with initial-access vectors like phishing or exposed RDP.
