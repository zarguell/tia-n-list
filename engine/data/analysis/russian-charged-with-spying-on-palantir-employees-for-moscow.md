US prosecutors charged Russian national Igor Andreevich D'iu with conspiring to act as a Russian agent, acting as an agent of a foreign government without notifying the attorney general, interstate stalking and identity document fraud. FBI Counterintelligence assistant director Roman Rozhavsky said D'iu was recruited, directed and funded by the Russian government through Telegram. He entered the United States illegally from Mexico after travelling from Russia via Istanbul.

The indictment does not name Palantir, describing instead a publicly traded technology and defense company whose software Ukraine uses in the war with Russia. DOJ photos show D'iu photographing laptop screens at Starbucks and Verve Coffee Roasters tables near Palantir's Palo Alto offices. The alleged surveillance included dossiers on employees and their families and a nearly 30-mile pursuit of a female worker who sought help from a police patrol car. Palantir did not respond to a request for comment.

An Italian follow-up post restates the DOJ case and adds no new technical detail. Watch for court filings on how the dossiers and electronic surveillance were collected and whether any company systems were accessed.
