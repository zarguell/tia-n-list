Since mid-August 2026, a phishing operation tracked by Material Security's Threat Research team as Operation CloudFire has been harvesting live Google sessions from corporate users. The messages arrive as Google Calendar invites, often sent by BCC and sometimes appearing to come from an organization the recipient has already corresponded with. Pretexts include voicemail notifications, RFP and RFI requests, court summonses, and missed-payment notices. Most links point to Google itself, typically through Google's own /url redirector with the real destination in a query parameter, though other waves used share.google, Drive file links, and Intuit transaction notification links. To any link scanner, the domain, certificate, and reputation all look legitimate and trusted.

The attacker runs a live reverse proxy in front of the genuine Google sign-in page. The victim enters a password and approves a real prompt while the operator watches the session in real time, and the server relays SMS codes, authenticator codes, Google's Tap-Yes number matching, and phone-based account recovery to Google as each arrives, so none of the second-factor checks stop the takeover. Material Security says it has taken down hundreds of attacker domains. Because the abuse chains legitimate Google infrastructure, defenders should treat calendar invites from known contacts as potentially hostile and watch for session hijacking alerts rather than relying on link reputation alone.
