AlphaHunt published a game-theory analysis of a problem familiar to defense contractors: insider-style access can form before the security workflow has a stable employee, device, or account to monitor. DPRK IT-worker schemes, suspected Iran-linked recruitment operations, and supplier compromise are distinct threats that expose the same gap, since workforce and delegated authority are built from evidence scattered across teams that rarely compare records.

The analysis maps the players, incentives, and trust transitions and argues for stronger assurance at high-consequence handoffs rather than uniform scrutiny of every applicant. It proposes an authority register spanning HR, vendor management, payroll, assets, and identity. The concrete takeaway for defenders is to correlate hiring, access requests, and payment data at the points where a new identity actually receives authority, because that is where the first sign of a fake or recruited insider appears.
