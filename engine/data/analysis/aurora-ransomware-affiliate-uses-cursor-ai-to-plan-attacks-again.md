CloudSEK documented an Aurora ransomware affiliate whose exposed open directory revealed months of operational data. The affiliate targeted more than 20 organizations across nine countries between April and July 2026, gaining domain-level or interactive access at 17 victims. The unprotected Linux home directory, served on port 8888, contained victim folders, Kerberos tickets, credential dumps, Active Directory data, shell-history files, Cursor AI chat logs, exploit tools, and Aurora ransomware binaries. Four organizations later appeared on Aurora's leak site.

The key finding is the affiliate's use of Cursor, an AI coding assistant, to plan attacks in Russian. The recovered chat logs show sustained discussions of Active Directory Certificate Services abuse and domain-compromise paths across multiple victims. The affiliate followed a repeatable playbook: using NetExec for lateral movement, Kerberos ticket manipulation for privilege escalation, and SOCKS proxy infrastructure (VPS in Germany and the US) to hide direct connections. CloudSEK assessed with high confidence this was a direct Aurora affiliate conducting intrusions, not an initial-access broker. The use of AI tools to accelerate attack planning is an operational efficiency gain that lowers the barrier for affiliates to run complex, multi-victim campaigns.
