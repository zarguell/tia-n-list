North Korea's government websites were hit by multiple waves of DDoS traffic starting around October 20, 2026, knocking over 40 official sites offline for roughly 32 hours. Monitoring firm Kentik tracked the synchronized inbound traffic surges, and director Doug Madory cited aggregate traffic data confirming the disruptions. The story matters because sustained, multi-wave DDoS against state infrastructure signals a coordinated operation rather than a simple protest or script-kiddie event, and it raises questions about whether the traffic originated externally or from compromised internal nodes.

Watch for follow-up attribution from Kentik or Korean security agencies identifying the source networks. If the attackers reveal themselves or the traffic patterns match prior campaigns, the incident could escalate from nuisance to geopolitical messaging.
