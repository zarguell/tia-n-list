The DeadLock ransomware operation has moved its infrastructure onto blockchain-backed services. Microsoft's research describes a setup where the Polygon blockchain stores configuration data and leak-site posts, and the victim chat page resolves its current proxy address by querying a smart contract instead of using a fixed Tor URL. DeadLock also uses the decentralized Session network for victim communications and hosts stolen files on Wasabi cloud storage.

The design means operators can rotate their chat and C2 infrastructure with a blockchain transaction, with no change needed on the victim side, which makes takedown and sinkholing far harder. DeadLock emerged in mid-2025, uses double extortion, and by July had listed 80 mostly European victims across IT, mining, transportation, manufacturing, hospitality, and consumer goods. Microsoft observed deployment by affiliates previously linked to the Lynx and INC ecosystems. Organizations should expect blockchain-anchored resilience to become more common in ransomware operations and should not count on infrastructure seizure to end a campaign.
