TeamUpdraft disclosed unauthorized access to teamupdraft.com over the weekend of October 17-18, 2026, believing its customer database was accessed. That database contained connection keys for UpdraftCentral, UpdraftVault, and UpdraftClone along with customer account information. Attackers used valid UpdraftCentral connection keys to send remote commands to connected WordPress sites, uploading, activating, and deleting malicious plugins; because the requests were properly signed with trusted keys, affected UpdraftPlus installations could not distinguish them from legitimate dashboard commands. Nethemba discovered the campaign on their own infrastructure and published the first technical analysis with indicators of compromise and a read-only checking script, and Patchstack confirmed the scope, noting releases 1.26.11 and 2.26.11.26 were impacted.

The effective mitigation at publication was deleting UpdraftCentral keys, since no plugin update stopped the attack. Site owners who use UpdraftPlus should rotate or delete connection keys, audit installed plugins for unauthorized additions, and check for newly created admin accounts.

Watch for a vendor fix that invalidates compromised keys server-side and for confirmation of how many sites were backdoored.
