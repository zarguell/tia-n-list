Attackers distributed fake OpenAI, SpaceX, and Anthropic event invitations that led to registration pages tying together two phishing-as-a-service platforms. Kali365 handled Microsoft device-code phishing while VoidProxy acted as an adversary-in-the-middle reverse proxy to intercept Google sign-ins and exfiltrate authentication session cookies. On the Microsoft side, VoidProxy was reused as a second stage to harvest credentials and session cookies after Kali365 had already captured an authentication token. Push Security reported that it detected and blocked visits to these pages in late October 2026. No vulnerability or CVE is involved; the chain is entirely social engineering against login and device-code flows.

A successful compromise means the victim's Microsoft and Google corporate accounts are fully exposed: stolen session cookies let the attacker read mail and access cloud resources without the password, and multi-factor authentication does not invalidate a live session. The events show corporate employees were the target, and that one lure page simultaneously enrolled victims into two separate PhaaS tracks. The distinguishing factor is the double-dip: device-code capture is not the end goal but a stepping stone to a second-stage VoidProxy session theft. That chaining is what separates this campaign from the dozens of single-platform phishing stories published daily.

Defenders should block known Kali365 and VoidProxy infrastructure at the web proxy and firewall, and treat any device-code authentication prompt that arrives outside a normal sign-in flow as hostile. Review Microsoft sign-in logs for device-code authentication events and Google account activity for sessions originating from unfamiliar proxy IPs. Instruct users that no legitimate AI event registration asks them to enter a device code at a Microsoft sign-in page. After any suspected visit, force a session reset and require MFA re-enrollment for the affected Microsoft and Google accounts.

Watch for the Kali365-VoidProxy pairing to be reused against other SaaS providers beyond Microsoft and Google, and for the Zoom-event abuse pattern documented by ANY.RUN in July 2026 to be merged with this event-invitation lure. Also watch whether the Anthropic-impersonation email campaign reported by Cofense converges with this infrastructure, which would signal a shared operator rather than coincidental lures.
