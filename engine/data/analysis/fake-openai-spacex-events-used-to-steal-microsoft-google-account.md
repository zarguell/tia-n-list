Attackers are sending fake invitations to OpenAI, SpaceX, and Anthropic events to lure employees into signing in with corporate accounts and surrendering Microsoft and Google credentials and authentication sessions. A Push Security report says the registration pages use two phishing-as-a-service platforms: Kali365 for Microsoft device-code phishing and VoidProxy as an adversary-in-the-middle reverse proxy that intercepts Google sign-ins and steals session cookies. In the Microsoft chain, VoidProxy is also used as a second stage to capture credentials and session cookies after authentication tokens are obtained.

This is an active campaign, not old research: Push Security said it detected and blocked visits to the malicious event pages in late October 2026. The same lure appeared in July when ANY.RUN documented attackers abusing legitimate Zoom event pages to promote fake Meta, OpenAI, and Anthropic events that led to device-code and adversary-in-the-middle phishing.

Employees should treat unexpected event invitations offering free access to premium AI services as suspicious, especially any request to complete a device-code sign-in. Security teams should watch for device-code authentication events and adversary-in-the-middle proxy traffic targeting Microsoft and Google login flows.
