APT28 (tracked by Symantec as Swallowtail, also called Fancy Bear and BlueDelta) breached a NATO naval shipbuilder and a second NATO member state using the HOOKEDGE backdoor and a previously undocumented reflective loader, according to Symantec's Threat Hunter Team. The first confirmed infection at the naval shipbuilder occurred on July 24, 2026, with attackers returning repeatedly over a week in hands-on-keyboard activity; malicious activity was last detected on September 24, 2026. The campaign used malicious Microsoft Word attachments delivered by email, followed by credential theft and in-memory code execution, targeting not just the shipbuilder but also a military aircraft engine manufacturer and a law enforcement agency.

This is not routine espionage noise. A naval shipbuilder sits at the intersection of NATO operational capability and supply-chain integrity; sustained hands-on access over a week gives a GRU-linked actor time to map internal networks, steal design or logistics data, and stage future disruption. The selection of targets spans defense manufacturing and law enforcement across two unnamed European countries, which aligns with Symantec's assessment that the operation is part of a broader Russian intelligence-gathering effort focused on European government and defense organizations. The July-to-September dwell window exceeds the brief intrusion profile of commodity ransomware and matches state-aligned persistence patterns.

Defenders should hunt for HOOKEDGE indicators and the new reflective loader mentioned by Symantec, review email attachments from late July through September 2026 in defense-sector environments, and inspect network logs for repeated return connections to compromised hosts. Patch Microsoft Office to close the feature-bypass vulnerability (CVE-2026-21509 is referenced in related Swallowtail activity from earlier in 2026) and restrict outbound webhook-style communications from production networks. Monitor for credential-use anomalies linked to the law-enforcement and shipbuilder victim profiles, since the attackers explicitly attempted login-credential theft.

Watch for Symantec or Recorded Future to publish additional technical indicators of the reflective loader and any confirmed exfiltration volume. A correlation with the earlier August 2026 HOOKEDGE disclosure by Recorded Future's Insikt Group could confirm whether the same infrastructure spans both campaigns, which would raise the priority of this assessment.
