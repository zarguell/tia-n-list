CISA has published new guidance on deploying cyber decoys within critical infrastructure environments. The advisory frames decoys as a complement to Zero Trust architectures: assets that appear to be legitimate systems, accounts, or data but exist solely to distract adversaries, detect their presence, and collect threat intelligence. The guidance recommends placing decoys where users rarely interact, configuring them to produce high-fidelity alerts, and designing them to lure attackers into downloading non-sensitive data while their operations are observed in controlled environments.

The guidance positions decoy techniques as incremental and cost-effective, requiring no major architectural changes. Organizations should deploy decoys that produce misleading recon results, direct adversaries to controlled environments, and generate actionable intelligence on adversary TTPs. The approach targets the reality that Zero Trust assumes some level of access has already been gained.

This matters because CISA's advice carries weight across federal and critical infrastructure sectors. Decoy deployments are a low-cost detection layer that can surface lateral movement and credential abuse early. Security teams running lean SOC operations should evaluate whether their monitoring covers deception-based signals alongside traditional telemetry.
