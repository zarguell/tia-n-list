The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives is investigating a cybersecurity incident that the Justice Department has designated a "major incident" under federal guidelines. The disclosure came after the Qilin ransomware group listed ATF on its dark-web leak site and claimed to have compromised the agency. ATF has not attributed the incident to Qilin or confirmed whether ransomware was involved.

ATF said the affected system operates separately from its main computer network. The bureau found no indication the incident spread to its broader network, its electronic firearms application platform (eForms) or any other ATF system. The eForms platform handles National Firearms Act applications for silencers, short-barreled rifles and machine guns. The Record reported the intrusion before ATF's public statement.

Qilin provided no proof of its claims, consistent with the group's pattern of listing victims without substantiation. The "major incident" designation triggers specific federal reporting and remediation requirements under OMB guidelines. The separation of the compromised system from the main network and eForms platform limits the blast radius, but the designation suggests the incident affected systems handling sensitive law enforcement data, potentially including investigation targets as The Record's earlier reporting indicated.
