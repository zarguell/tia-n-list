Breach of Brevo, Trezor's third-party email marketing provider, gave attackers access to Trezor's opt-in newsletter database of roughly 347,000 email addresses. On September 9, threat actors used that access to send fake "critical security alert" emails from help@trezor.io claiming a hardware microcontroller vulnerability in Trezor wallets' STM32 chips could expose seed phrases to brute-force attacks. The phishing emails directed recipients to download a malicious app that asked for their wallet backup.

Trezor took down the phishing domain within 20 minutes, limiting the blast radius to approximately 2,500 users who clicked the link before it was disabled. The company confirmed no other Trezor systems or customer data beyond the email list were compromised. Brevo disclosed that 120 of its customer accounts were affected in the broader incident, though Trezor is the only known crypto wallet vendor impacted.

The 347,000 email addresses remain exposed and could be used in future phishing campaigns. Users who received the emails should be aware that any "security alert" from Trezor asking them to download software or enter a seed phrase is fraudulent. Trezor has stated it will never ask for seed phrases via email. The incident underscores the supply-chain risk posed by third-party marketing platforms handling sensitive customer lists for financial services companies.
