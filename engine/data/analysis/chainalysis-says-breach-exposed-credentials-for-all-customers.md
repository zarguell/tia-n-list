Chainalysis disclosed that attackers who broke into its systems in October ultimately exposed credentials for all customers. The company first said on October 30 that attackers using offensive agentic techniques accessed names, email addresses, user IDs and organizational role labels. A November 4 update expanded the impact sharply: names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all Chainalysis users were exposed to the attacker in what the company called an on path attack. All customer passwords were reset, and customers whose KYT accounts may have been accessed without authorization are being notified directly.

The scope matters because of who uses these tools. Chainalysis provides blockchain tracing used by law enforcement, government agencies, financial institutions and crypto businesses for fraud, ransomware and money laundering investigations. The company shut down KYT transaction monitoring, address screening, Reactor, Data Solutions and Chain Beta while responding. It states it has found no evidence that investigation graph data was accessed or that blockchain attribution repositories were altered.

The live tokens are the artifact to watch, since active authentication tokens can be replayed where not yet expired or rotated. Customers should have rotated API keys and integration credentials, reviewed access logs for the October to November 4 window, and treated related inboxes as highly phishable given the attacker holds names, emails and org roles. The next signal is whether Chainalysis or customers report secondary abuse of the stolen tokens.
