Chainalysis disclosed an attack on its systems and internal tools that exposed credentials and live tokens for all customers. The firm provides blockchain analysis tools used to trace cryptocurrency transactions and investigate fraud, ransomware, and money laundering. Its customers include law enforcement, government agencies, financial institutions, insurance companies, and cryptocurrency businesses. Attackers accessed customer names, email addresses, user IDs, and organizational role labels using what Chainalysis described as offensive agentic techniques with AI tools.

Why it matters: Chainalysis initially said the impact was limited to non-sensitive identity metadata, but later updates revealed that usernames and password hashes for all customers were exposed in an on-path attack, more commonly known as a man-in-the-middle attack. In a Wednesday update the company said that on Monday it discovered names, usernames, password hashes, and live tokens for all users exposed to a malicious actor. It found no evidence that attackers accessed investigative graph data or altered blockchain attribution repositories.

What to watch: whether the exposed live tokens were abused to access customer investigation data, any further disclosures about the interception method, and follow-on targeting of Chainalysis customers.
