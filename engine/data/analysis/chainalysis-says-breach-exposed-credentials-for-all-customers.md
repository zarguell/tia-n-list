Chainalysis disclosed that an attack on its systems and internal tools exposed usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all customers. The company sells blockchain analysis tools used to trace cryptocurrency transactions and investigate fraud, ransomware and money laundering, and its customers include law enforcement, government agencies, financial institutions, insurance companies and cryptocurrency businesses worldwide.

The disclosure follows a Friday report that attackers using AI tools, which Chainalysis described as "offensive agentic techniques," accessed customer names, email addresses, user IDs and organizational role labels. Chainalysis first described the impact as limited to non-sensitive identity metadata, then revised it after discovering on Monday that names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all users had been exposed to a malicious actor in an on-path attack, commonly known as man-in-the-middle.

Chainalysis said it found no evidence that attackers accessed investigative graph data or altered or manipulated its blockchain attribution repositories. Watch for whether exposed live tokens are used against customer accounts, which downstream investigations may be affected, and any customer notification or credential-reset guidance.
