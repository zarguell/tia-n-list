Chainalysis disclosed that attackers breached its systems and internal tools, exposing credentials and live tokens for all customers. The company first reported on October 30, 2026 that attackers using AI tools and what it called offensive agentic techniques had accessed customer names, email addresses, user IDs, and organizational role labels. It initially described the impact as limited to non-sensitive identity metadata.

A November 4 update widened the scope: on the preceding Monday the company discovered that usernames, password hashes, and live tokens for all Chainalysis users had been exposed to a malicious actor in an on-path attack, more commonly known as a man-in-the-middle attack. Chainalysis said it found no evidence that attackers accessed investigative graph data or manipulated its blockchain attribution repositories.

In response the company shut down KYT transaction monitoring, address screening, Reactor, Data Solutions, and Chain Beta. Its customers include law enforcement, government agencies, financial institutions, insurance companies, and cryptocurrency businesses worldwide. Bitcoin News reported on November 5, 2026 that the expanded disclosure followed the initial October 30 report.
