ChainScript is a newly identified Node.js-based RAT distributed through fake installers for Spotify, Zoom Workplace, and Microsoft Teams. The attack uses ClickFix-style social engineering: victims are tricked into copying and running a PowerShell command that downloads and executes the malware disguised as a legitimate application setup. Two independent reports from The Hacker News and Hackread confirm the campaign.

The RAT's use of Polygon blockchain smart contracts is the novel element. ChainScript communicates with or stores configuration on-chain, which gives the operators a decentralized C2 infrastructure that is difficult to takedown. Smart contract interactions are also harder for network monitoring tools to flag as malicious since they resemble normal Web3 traffic.

ClickFix campaigns have surged throughout 2026 because they bypass traditional email gateway defenses entirely. There is no malicious attachment to sandbox and no URL to blocklist until the campaign domain is identified. The fake installer approach adds another layer since victims believe they are installing software they chose. Defenders should monitor for unexpected Node.js processes and PowerShell commands that download executables from unfamiliar domains.
