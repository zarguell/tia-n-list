The HexMage campaign has compromised more than 40 e-commerce storefronts across at least 15 countries, using Ethereum smart contracts on the Sepolia testnet to deliver and rotate payment-card skimmers. Attackers inject malicious JavaScript server-side into victim sites, hiding it inside fake Google Tag Manager blocks. When a shopper reaches checkout, the loader pulls the ethers.js library from jsDelivr and retrieves skimmer payloads from Ethereum smart contracts. This EtherHiding technique makes infrastructure resilient: defenders cannot easily remove blockchain-stored data the way they would take down a malicious domain.

The campaign targets WooCommerce, PrestaShop, Magento, and WordPress installations. Researchers linked the activity to a cluster operating since at least April 2026. The skimmer captures card details at the point of sale and exfiltrates them to attacker-controlled endpoints. Because the delivery mechanism sits on-chain, takedowns require cooperation with blockchain infrastructure rather than traditional hosting providers.

This is the latest evolution of Magecart-style client-side skimming, but the Ethereum layer adds genuine operational complexity for defenders. Organizations running e-commerce sites should verify the integrity of their checkout-page JavaScript and monitor for unexpected network calls to Ethereum-related domains or contracts. The campaign demonstrates that blockchain abuse is moving beyond crypto theft into traditional payment fraud.
