Claroc Research reports that more than 35,000 apps built with Grok were exposed to anyone on the public internet. The research, published Nov. 10 as GhostShares 2, found that the apps could be opened by anyone with the link, including internal data from organizations that built them with xAI's Grok Build mode. Ars Technica separately reported that Grok Build leaked confidential information, some of it belonging to SpaceXSI, the company formerly called xAI.

The exposure matters because Grok Build lets people publish apps directly from a chat prompt, and the reports describe business secrets and personal data sitting in published apps that anyone could open. That turns a coding-agent feature into a data-disclosure risk for customers who adopt it, including the organizations whose internal records were found in the exposed apps.

Organizations using Grok Build should audit published apps and their sharing settings, revoke public access where it is not required, and treat already-published apps as potentially exposed. Because the exposure stems from published apps rather than a single compromised account, the fix is a configuration and inventory problem as much as a patch.
