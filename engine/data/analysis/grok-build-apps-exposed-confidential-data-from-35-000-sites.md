Claros researchers found more than 35,000 apps built with xAI's Grok Build coding agent that were publicly reachable, with 94.7 percent of the live apps they tested opening without any sign-in. The default sharing setting on Grok Build is "Anyone with the link," and the researchers opened the first page of each app address once with no account. They found internal data from 30 identifiable organizations, 26 of them with more than 1,000 employees, plus 24 apps belonging to SpaceXSI and X staff. Ars Technica covered the work and SpaceXSI and X took their apps down after the report.

The exposure is a configuration default rather than a memory-safety bug, which makes it both mundane and broad. The post, titled GhostShares 2, documents the method, two test apps the researchers built to confirm Grok's defaults, and what a security team can do when employees ship AI-generated apps into production. The practical exposure is the usual AI coding pattern: an employee prompts an agent to build an internal tool, the agent applies a permissive sharing default, and the resulting URL is indexable or guessable enough to be found.

The SpaceXSI sales plans are the attention-grabbing detail, but the more durable finding is the population of enterprise apps sitting behind share links that were never reviewed. Anything that treats a share link as an access control is exposed to this class of error, and the same applies to comparable agentic builders.

Watch for xAI changing the Grok Build default or adding tenant-level sharing policy, for the Claros team naming further victims, and for whether enterprises start inventorying AI-built apps the way they inventory SaaS. A leaked index of 35,000 app URLs, if one surfaces, would turn this from a spot check into a searchable list.
