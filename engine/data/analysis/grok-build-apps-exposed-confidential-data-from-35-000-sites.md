Claroc Research found that more than 35,000 apps built with xAI's Grok Build coding agent were publicly accessible without authentication. The team resolved 43,601 names under grok.me, opened 43,551 addresses from the public internet with no sign-in, and found 37,571 still online, of which 35,578 (94.7 percent) opened for anyone; only 1,993 returned HTTP 401. The exposure included internal business plans, ad proposals, medical exam results, employee names and ID numbers, a sales territory map of 594 accounts, and X advertising proposals marked confidential, with 24 apps built by staff at X and SpaceXSI among those exposed.

The root cause is a design default: Grok Build's publish panel preselects "Anyone with the link," and published apps receive their own hostnames that passive DNS and subdomain discovery can enumerate, so randomized URLs provide no real access control. SpaceXSI removed its 24 staff apps after Claroc's report but did not take down other users' apps. Organizations whose employees build apps with AI tooling should inventory deployments outside IT's view and verify sharing defaults before publishing.
