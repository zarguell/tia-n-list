Roman Khlynovskiy, a 43-year-old Kazakh national, appeared in federal court in Knoxville after extradition from Italy. Prosecutors allege he belonged to the 8-Digits Team, an affiliate of the INC and Lynx ransomware operations, and took part in a campaign against US hospitals, schools and healthcare providers in at least six states. The indictment says the group breached networks, exfiltrated data and threatened publication to force payment. DOJ quotes the conspirators claiming more than 50 terabytes of data and over 70 million confidential health records, with more than $500 million in ransom demands.

He faces five counts: conspiracy to commit computer fraud and identity theft, conspiracy to commit wire fraud, computer extortion, conspiracy to launder money, and threats to disclose nonconsensual intimate images under the TAKE IT DOWN Act. If convicted, the maximum sentence is 27 years. DOJ credits Italian, Ukrainian and Lithuanian authorities with assisting the arrest and extradition. INC has been operating as a ransomware-as-a-service group since July 2023, and its leak site lists more than 1,000 victims.

The case matters less as a technical disclosure than as an example of affiliate-level accountability across borders. The alleged scale, especially the 70 million health records claim, comes from prosecutors and should be read as an accusation rather than a measured breach total. Watch the court record for the specific victim counts and whether the charged TAKE IT DOWN count produces a legal precedent for ransomware actors who threaten to publish intimate images.
