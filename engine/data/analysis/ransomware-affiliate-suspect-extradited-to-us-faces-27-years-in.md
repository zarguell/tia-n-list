Roman Khlynovskiy, 43, a Kazakh national, made an initial appearance in federal court in Knoxville, Tennessee after extradition from Italy. Prosecutors allege he was a member of the 8-Digits Team, a group affiliated with the INC and Lynx ransomware operations, and that the conspiracy hit schools, hospitals and healthcare entities in at least six US states. The attackers breached networks, stole data and threatened public release to force payment. The Department of Justice quotes the conspirators boasting of more than 50 terabytes of data and over 70 million confidential health records, with more than 500 million dollars in ransom demands.

Khlynovskiy faces up to 27 years if convicted of conspiracy to commit computer fraud, identity theft, wire fraud, extortion, money laundering and threats to disclose non-consensual intimate images. INC Ransom began as a ransomware-as-a-service operation in July 2023 and its leak site lists more than 1,000 victims across education, healthcare, government and industry. The case matters less for one defendant than for the scale it attributes to a single affiliate network: healthcare records were the pressure instrument, not an incidental target.

Watch whether the extradition produces further charges against 8-Digits Team members or infrastructure seizures tied to INC and Lynx. The record counts are assertions from the indictment, not a confirmed tally of affected patients, so later filings or victim notices may revise them. Prosecutors in Knoxville are also likely to disclose which designated healthcare entities and school systems were extorted, which would give defenders a clearer picture of the initial access patterns used against those sectors.
