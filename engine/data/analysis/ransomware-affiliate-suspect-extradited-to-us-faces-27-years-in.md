Roman Khlynovskiy, a 43-year-old Kazakh national, made his initial appearance in federal court in Knoxville, Tennessee on 16 November 2026 after extradition from Italy. The Justice Department charges him with five counts: conspiracy to commit computer fraud and identity theft, conspiracy to commit wire fraud, computer extortion, conspiracy to launder money, and threats to disclose nonconsensual intimate images under the TAKE IT DOWN Act. The maximum sentence is 27 years. Prosecutors place him in the 8-Digits Team, an affiliate group tied to the INC and Lynx ransomware operations. DOJ credited Italian, Ukrainian and Lithuanian authorities with assisting the arrest and extradition.

The indictment alleges breaches of hospitals, schools and healthcare providers in at least six states, followed by exfiltration and threats to publish stolen files. The conspirators claimed more than 50 terabytes of data and over 70 million confidential health records, and the campaign generated more than $500 million in ransom demands. INC Ransom began operating as a ransomware-as-a-service in July 2023 and its leak site lists over 1,000 victims across education, healthcare, government and industrial sectors. The TAKE IT DOWN count, added through a law signed in May 2025, reflects the intimate-image threat element alongside the extortion case.

The development is an enforcement action, not a new intrusion campaign, and none of the reporting ties Khlynovskiy to a newly discovered victim or active infrastructure. What to watch is whether US prosecutors unseal further charging documents against other 8-Digits Team members, whether the alleged victim states and healthcare entities are named, and whether INC or Lynx leak-site activity changes after the extradition. For defenders, the useful artifact is the attribution chain between 8-Digits Team, INC and Lynx, which can inform extortion tracking and victim notification decisions.
