Roman Khlynovskiy, a 43-year-old Kazakh national, made his initial appearance in federal court in Knoxville after extradition from Italy. The Justice Department alleges he was a member of the 8-Digits Team, a group affiliated with the INC and Lynx ransomware operations. Prosecutors say the group targeted victims in at least six US states, including hospitals, schools and other healthcare entities, breached their networks, exfiltrated data and demanded payment in promises not to publish it. A November 17 report from The Record repeats the accusation and adds companies to the list of targeted organisations without adding new case facts.

The indictment, quoted across DOJ material and follow-up coverage, cites boasts of more than 50 terabytes of stolen data and over 70 million confidential health records, with more than $500 million in ransom demands. Khlynovskiy faces five charges: conspiracy to commit computer fraud and identity theft, conspiracy to commit wire fraud, computer extortion, conspiracy to launder money, and threats to disclose nonconsensual intimate images under the TAKE IT DOWN Act. The maximum penalty is 27 years. The DOJ release credits Italian, Ukrainian and Lithuanian authorities with assisting the arrest and extradition.

The case is a concrete enforcement action against an affiliate ecosystem whose INC leak site is reported to list over 1,000 victims, with INC described as a ransomware-as-a-service operation that emerged in July 2023. It also shows healthcare extortion being prosecuted through both computer-crime counts and the intimacy-abuse statute, and it puts an individual defendant in court over attacks on schools and hospitals in multiple states. Several outlets, including 2 News Nevada and DataBreaches.Net, covered the same Knoxville appearance on November 16 and 17.

Watch the trial schedule, any cooperation details that surface in filings, and whether the DOJ links evidence from the extradition to other INC or Lynx affiliates. Victim notifications for the healthcare and school entities would be a further signal, though the reporting identifies the affected states only as at least six and does not name them.
