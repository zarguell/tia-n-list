The DOJ and FBI seized seven domains supporting two hacking platforms operated by Integrity Technology Group, a China-based contractor US agencies link to the Chinese government and to the Flax Typhoon botnet operation. MicroScan is a vulnerability-scanning platform; the FBI affidavit says it was used alongside a botnet of Mirai-infected devices to identify targets. FishHub supports spearphishing and delivered malware capable of stealing files or giving attackers remote control. The court-authorized seizures were granted in the Western District of Pennsylvania and announced Thursday with unsealed documents. Early reporting counted six domains before the seven-domain figure settled.

The action coincided with a joint advisory from the FBI, CISA, NSA, and international partners. It warns that Chinese government-linked actors enabled by Integrity Tech combine automated scanning, large-scale botnets, and hands-on exploitation to steal data from organizations worldwide, including US critical infrastructure. Documented tactics include scanning tools, cross-site scripting, and password spraying against Microsoft Exchange servers, with persistence established through VPN software. Targets cited include government, manufacturing, healthcare, law enforcement, and education organizations. Integrity Tech was sanctioned in 2025 and was the subject of a 2024 botnet takedown, so this is the third US action against the same enabler.

The FBI's message, via Cyber Division Assistant Director Brett Leatherman, is that China relies on contractor companies to expand the reach of its cyber operations and that disrupting them raises the cost of attacks. Watch for how quickly MicroScan and FishHub infrastructure reconstitutes, whether indictments follow, and for defenders using the advisory's IOC set to hunt for prior Integrity Tech-enabled intrusions.
