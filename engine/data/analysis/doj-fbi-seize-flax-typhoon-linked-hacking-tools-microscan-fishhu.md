The Justice Department and FBI seized the internet domains behind two hacking platforms, Microscan and FishHub, operated by China-based Integrity Technology Group and used by the Flax Typhoon intrusion set. The court-authorized seizures, unsealed in the Western District of Pennsylvania, were announced October 9 alongside a joint advisory from CISA, the FBI, the NSA and international partners. Outlets in the cluster report either six or seven domains taken; the underlying action targeted the infrastructure of the two tools, which handled vulnerability scanning on one side and spearphishing with malware delivery on the other.

Integrity Tech was sanctioned by the US in 2025 and was the target of a 2024 DOJ takedown of its large Mirai-based IoT botnet, so this is the third US action against the same contractor. The advisory describes a repeatable pattern: contractors supply automated scanning, large-scale botnets and hands-on exploitation to China-linked actors, with XSS, password spraying against Microsoft Exchange servers and persistence through VPN software. Reported victims include a South Carolina power company, NGOs, Japanese and Polish airports, and Taiwanese critical infrastructure entities and universities, across government, manufacturing, healthcare, law enforcement and education sectors. The FBI also says stolen email access was brokered to third parties, which broadens the blast radius beyond the contractor's own campaigns.

The story matters because it attacks the contractor layer that gives Chinese state hacking its scale, and because Microscan and FishHub are scanning platforms whose telemetry reveals targeting. Disruption is usually temporary for infrastructure of this kind; the domains can be re-registered elsewhere. Watch for Integrity Tech rebuilding the platforms, additional designations against enabler firms, and whether KEV or advisory updates add the specific Exchange and VPN flaws the advisory references.
