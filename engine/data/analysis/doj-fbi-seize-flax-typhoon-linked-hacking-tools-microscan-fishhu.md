On October 8 the Justice Department and FBI seized six internet domains supporting two hacking tools, Microscan and FishHub, operated by China-based Integrity Technology Group, the contractor tied to the Flax Typhoon cluster. Microscan scans networks for security weaknesses; FishHub supports spear phishing and delivers malware that steals files or grants remote control. The court-authorized seizures were granted in the Western District of Pennsylvania and announced with unsealed court documents.

The company is a repeat target. US authorities sanctioned Integrity Technology last year and ran a 2024 takedown against the botnet it operated. The same-day advisory from CISA, FBI, NSA, and international partners states that Chinese government-linked actors enabled by the firm combine automated scanning tools, large-scale botnets, and hands-on exploitation to steal data from organizations worldwide, including US critical infrastructure. Techniques named: vulnerability scanning, cross-site scripting, and password spraying against Microsoft Exchange servers, with persistence established through VPN software. Sectors hit include government, manufacturing, healthcare, law enforcement, and education. IC3 published a companion advisory (CSA 261008).

The significance is the supplier model: the FBI's message, via Cyber Division Assistant Director Brett Leatherman, is that the PRC relies on contractor and enabling companies to expand the reach and scale of its cyber operations, and that disrupting enablers raises cost. Seizing the tooling domains degrades access for every customer of the contractor, not one intrusion.

What to watch: whether the seizures cause measurable drops in the associated scanning and phishing infrastructure, additional designations against Integrity Technology or its staff, and attribution detail linking Microscan/FishHub artifacts to specific victim campaigns.
