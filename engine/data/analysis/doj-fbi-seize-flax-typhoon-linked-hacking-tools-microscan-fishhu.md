The Justice Department and FBI seized six internet domains on October 8 that supported Microscan and FishHub, two hacking tools operated by Integrity Technology Group, the China-based contractor the United States sanctioned in 2025 and targeted in a 2024 takedown operation against its botnet. The court-authorized seizures came out of the Western District of Pennsylvania and landed the same day as joint advisory AA26-281A from the FBI, CISA, NSA, and seven international partners including the UK NCSC, Australia's ACSC, and Japan's National Police Agency. Microscan is a Python web application holding more than 1,300 penetration testing scripts, in use as early as 2017; FishHub supports spearphishing and delivers malware that steals files or grants remote control of victim systems. The advisory is evidence, not forecast: it draws on technical material recovered during multiple FBI investigations, with victim access observed since at least mid-January 2021.

The action matters because the target was supply, not a single victim. Integrity Tech arms customers whose activity tracks to Flax Typhoon, Ethereal Panda, and Red Juliett, so the six domains were shared infrastructure for many simultaneous campaigns and their loss raises cost across the contractor's whole customer base at once. The exposed population is wide: victims in government services, critical manufacturing, healthcare and public health, and IT, plus law enforcement, education, and religious organizations across North America, Southeast Asia, and Africa. CISA's acting executive assistant director for cybersecurity, Chris Butera, framed the endgame as pre-positioning inside critical infrastructure networks, including OT systems, to disrupt critical functions at a time of the actors' choosing. What the timeline alone does not reveal is the tooling's vintage: five of the eight successfully exploited CVEs were newly added to the KEV catalog with this advisory, anchored by 2015-era ProFTPD 1.3.5 and ISC BIND bugs, so networks with disciplined modern patch cycles can still carry every one of these exposures on forgotten edge gear.

The hunt list is specific. The FBI attributes the domain dns.studiocloud.xyz to Integrity Tech as the HTTP channel for a payload named live700_v1.exe, which spawns a masquerading process called DiagTrack.exe; alert on any DiagTrack.exe creation outside Microsoft's own telemetry path. Persistence rides on legitimate SoftEther VPN clients downloaded by PowerShell or curl and wget, often named conhost.exe or dllhost.exe and configured to reconnect at startup, so inventory VPN software installs on servers and edge devices and treat unexplained SoftEther as compromise until proven otherwise. Credential access comes from EBurst password spraying against Exchange and Office 365 through the ECP, EWS, OAB, OWA, RPC, and MAPI interfaces, and reconnaissance concentrates on ports 21, 22, 53, 80, 443, and 1080 with tools such as Fscan, Nmap, dirsearch, and WPScan. Download the AA26-281A STIX XML and JSON feeds, which include the microscan.me domain family registered in December 2017, and prioritize the eight listed CVEs: CVE-2014-6278 in GNU Bash through 4.3, CVE-2015-3306 in ProFTPD 1.3.5, CVE-2015-5477 in ISC BIND 9.x, CVE-2016-3081 in Apache Struts 2.3.x, CVE-2019-11510 in Pulse Connect Secure, CVE-2021-22205 in GitLab, CVE-2021-3199 in ONLYOFFICE DocumentServer, and CVE-2023-22894 in Strapi.

Two developments would change the assessment. Fast replacement infrastructure, meaning equivalent scanning and phishing capacity reappearing under new domains within weeks, would confirm that customers treat Microscan and FishHub as rentable commodity and that the seizure bought time rather than disruption. Escalation against the company itself, through new designations or charges naming Integrity Tech personnel, would signal that this third US action against the same supplier had shifted from degrading infrastructure to dismantling the business. Track both through the advisory's IOC set and any follow-on Treasury or DOJ announcements.
