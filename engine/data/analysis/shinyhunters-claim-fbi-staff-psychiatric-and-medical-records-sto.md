The breach of the FBI's job portal is real by every third-party check available. Reuters examined a 5,000-row sample of the claimed two to three terabyte trove and judged it plausible: names, addresses, phone numbers, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, emergency contacts, and assignments to specific field offices and units doing intelligence, security, or counterespionage work. The BBC reviewed health screening samples containing blood and urine test results and specific allergy information, and 404 Media reports members of the FBI's Remote Operations Unit, the team that builds exploits and covert-access tools, are among those listed. ShinyHunters claims data on nearly all of the bureau's roughly 60,000 current and former employees, taken from fbijobs.gov, which the group defaced with ASCII art of the Pokemon Umbreon.

On the entry path, the group says it exploited a flaw in Oracle cloud storage, possibly Oracle PeopleSoft CVE-2026-35273 (CVSS 9.8, KEV-listed since June), the same flaw Mandiant warns ShinyHunters is mass-exploiting worldwide, bypassing WAF rules with URL encoding such as /%50SEMHUB/ instead of /PSEMHUB/. The group threatened publication within five days, then told 404 Media it never intended to publish and framed the theft as leverage to force retraction or correction of an FBI report attributing sextortion to the group. The deadline passed unmet, but publication was never the only monetization path for data like this.

Law-enforcement pressure is the backdrop and the likely motive shift. Dutch police re-arrested 24-year-old Pepijn van der Stap in mid-September while he was on probation from a four-year 2023 sentence served as Umbreon; KrebsOnSecurity reports his laptop contained information on two murders planned abroad. Mandiant estimates the group is on track for roughly $100 million in 2026 extortion payments, the FBI has publicly called on remaining members to come forward, and it dropped threat videos aimed at the group after the arrest. The core harm is counterintelligence: a roster mapping names to units is a targeting package for any hostile service, and a roadmap for harassment of individual agents. Watch for the FBI's confirmed scope, whether the trove is sold rather than published, exploitation reporting on unpatched PeopleSoft estates, and follow-on arrests from van der Stap's devices.
