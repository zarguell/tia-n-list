ShinyHunters claims it stole personnel data on current, former and prospective FBI employees, defacing FBIjobs.gov with a "seized by ShinyHunters" banner on September 22, 2026. The group says it used an Oracle PeopleSoft pre-auth zero-day for remote code execution on the jobs portal at apply.fbijobs.gov, claims a 2 to 3 TB haul including lateral movement onto FBI-managed AWS GovCloud servers with access to HR, MedLink and CJIS services, and demands the FBI retract its May 15, 2026 IC3 PSA (I-051526-PSA) within one week. A spokesperson told The Register the operation is "protecting our business as any other business would do."

The claims have partial third-party verification. 404 Media examined a roughly 5,000-line sample; Reuters reviewed a spreadsheet listing names, addresses, phone numbers, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, emergency contacts, and field office and unit assignments including intelligence and counterespionage roles. Reuters later confirmed psychiatric and medical evaluation records among the material, and Malwarebytes reported blood test results and doctors' notes surfacing. CyberScoop spoke to researchers who reviewed samples containing agents' personal contact information, family member details, office and duty assignments, and personnel specialties; one called the trove "a roadmap" that lets anyone with a grievance locate FBI employees at home. Reporting also indicates the trove includes members of the FBI's Remote Operations Unit, the team that builds exploits and covert-access tools.

The FBI says it is actively and aggressively investigating and has not confirmed the type, volume or attribution; the defaced jobs site was still offline as of September 28, and the New York Times reports fears of lasting harm to bureau employees. The claimed initial access vector ties this to the group's PeopleSoft campaign (CVE-2026-35273), which Google tracks as renewed mass exploitation by UNC6240.

Watch for full publication on the leak site, FBI confirmation of scope, and any dating that distinguishes a genuine FBI-specific intrusion from rebranded data from the broader PeopleSoft victim pool. The non-financial demand pattern, forcing removal of a law-enforcement publication while framing extortion as business, is itself notable for how extortion groups negotiate.
