A financially motivated threat actor is using open-source AI agent frameworks to attack online retailers at scale, stealing over 600,000 credit card records. The campaign has been active since at least July and continued through September 22. In just five days, the attacker compromised at least 27 companies and launched more than 100 attacks. Researchers at Gambit identified the operation, which relies on three AI tools: Strix for scanning (146 runs against 138 hosts, 633 scanning hours between August 23 and 31), Cairn for autonomous exploitation, and Hermes for campaign orchestration using claude-opus-4.6. Hermes contains a persona called "SOUL - Red Team Operator" with 121 skills, 78 of them attack-related. The attacker appears to be Chinese-speaking and targeted tens of companies daily.

Findings indicate the actor stole over 600,000 valid card details from two companies and deployed skimmer malware on five other retailers to collect payment data. This is a significant escalation: rather than using AI only for reconnaissance or phishing, the campaign automates the full kill chain from discovery to post-exploitation. Organizations running e-commerce platforms should review web skimmer defenses, audit AI-agent access to production environments, and look for indicators tied to Strix scanning patterns and Hermes orchestration artifacts.
