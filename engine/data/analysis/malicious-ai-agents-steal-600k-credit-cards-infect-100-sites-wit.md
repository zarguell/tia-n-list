Gambit Security reconstructed an active campaign that uses open-source AI agent frameworks to attack online retailers at scale, stealing more than 600,000 credit card records. The campaign has run since at least July and was ongoing as of September 22. Three tools divide the labor: Strix, an open-source penetration testing framework, for scanning and vulnerability discovery; Cairn, an autonomous exploitation engine tasked with objectives like obtaining a shell or admin access; and Hermes, an orchestration layer running claude-opus-4.6 with a persona named "SOUL - Red Team Operator" and 121 skills, 78 of them attack-related.

The scale comes from Gambit's access to the operator's own staging server. Between September 10 and 15 the operator launched 105 attack projects and compromised at least 27 companies. More than 600,000 unexpired card records came from two victims, 488,372 of them US-issued, and skimmers were confirmed on 19 named victims plus over 100 further infected sites. Strix ran 146 times against 138 hosts between August 23 and 31, accumulating 633 scanning hours at an average cost of $25.46 per completed scan. The operator appears to be based in China, and the toolkit includes a Database Wipe After Extraction skill. Gambit published five IPs, eleven skimmer and C2 domains, and nine skimmer URLs as indicators.

This is the clearest documented case of AI agent frameworks driving the full intrusion chain for financial gain rather than in a lab demo. E-commerce operators should sweep their environments against the published IOCs, monitor checkout pages for unauthorized script injection, and assume commodity scanning at this price point will keep expanding. Watch for copycat operators adopting the same Strix and Cairn stack now that the economics are public.
