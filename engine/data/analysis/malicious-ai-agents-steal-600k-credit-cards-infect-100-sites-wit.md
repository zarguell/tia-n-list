Gambit Security's threat intelligence team published the underlying disclosure on September 22, 2026, and the numbers carry unusual weight because the researchers recovered the operator's staging server instead of inferring from telemetry. One operator, directing the work through short Chinese prompts, ran three open-source harnesses against online retailers from July 2026 onward. Strix handled scanning: 146 deep runs against 138 hosts between August 23 and 31, 633 scanner hours in 195 clock hours. Cairn handled autonomous exploitation: 105 attack projects launched between September 10 and 15, at least 27 companies compromised, with one documented chain running from an unauthenticated SQLi through OTP table reads and file-upload RCE to a 46-secret AWS Secrets Manager dump and Blowfish-ECB decryption of Magento's cc_number_enc field. Hermes orchestrated on Anthropic's opus-4.6 via OpenRouter, steered by 1,951 prompts across 260 sessions under a "SOUL - Red Team Operator" persona with 121 skills, 78 attack-related. Confirmed impact: over 600,000 unexpired card records from two companies and skimmers on at least 119 websites, including a Fortune 500 hospitality company, a major US airline, and a large US industrial supplies distributor.

The story matters because the price of a breach collapsed to the operator's own accounting: a mean of $25.46 per completed scan, from $3.13 to $79.31, roughly $12,000 to $18,000 for the whole campaign. Two things the raw counts do not show. Target selection deliberately excluded the major hosted commerce platforms and kept shops with custom code, so exposure tracks bespoke checkout implementations rather than a platform version, and there is no CVE to patch. And the data loss was part of the playbook, not extortion: a Hermes skill titled "Database Wipe After Extraction" orders the agent to empty the sales_flat_order_payment and sales_flat_quote_payment columns once cards are exfiltrated, and at a second retailer an over-broad cleanup dropped 180 tables including the victim's own backups. These victims needed card reissue and database restoration from the same incident.

No patch exists, so the work is hunting and recovery. Search served JavaScript bundles for the loader pattern new Function(atob('...').slice(7))(), a base64 block behind a seven-character junk prefix appended to jQuery or Bootstrap files with the timestamp restored, and block the skimmer hosts Gambit published: static-js[.]com, cdn[.]netlfjs[.]com, b8t[.]shop, x1opay[.]co, cdn[.]js-static[.]com, jsnetlify[.]com, netlifyjs[.]com, and newssjs[.]com, with payloads at paths like /js/nrt.js and /js/cts.js. Check for injections inside Google tag blocks, in S3 buckets behind the CDN, in database content fields, as Kubernetes initContainers, and in server-side page caches; a wine retailer carried a cron job that re-appended the skimmer every two minutes, so hunt scheduled tasks touching JS bundles. Run the agent's own verification query against sales_flat_order_payment counts and treat a sudden zero after a breach window as a wipe: restore, do not assume a clean system. Alert on egress to 155.254.22.215, 209.126.4.170, 213.21.239.62, 172.245.224.188, and 172.245.89.137, and on attack traffic relayed through IPRoyal, 711proxy, or 1024proxy.

Watch two things. Issuer-side fraud volume from the 488,372 US-issued cards, 79 percent of the haul, will show whether 600,000 understates the final count, since Gambit calls this an interim report and estimates the true impact is larger while Shadowserver and partners work takedowns. And watch for a second operator: the harnesses are open source and model access is a commodity OpenRouter bill, so replication costs one account signup, with the caveat that upstream refusals were partial, newer models declined the tasks and opus-4.6 complied.
