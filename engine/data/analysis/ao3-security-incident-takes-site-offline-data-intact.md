Archive of Our Own went offline on October 29, 2026, initially labeled as routine maintenance on the Organization for Transformative Works status page (otwstatus.org). Roughly 18 hours later, OTW posted an update confirming evidence of a security incident and stating the site was taken down to mitigate the attack, with service restoration estimated at 2 to 4 weeks. The organization said all user works, bookmarks, kudos, and site data remain intact, though it explicitly did not rule out potential exposure of user information. No CVE or attacker attribution has been published.

This is not a routine outage but a defensive shutdown of a multi-million-work fanfiction archive in response to an active intrusion, which means defenders should treat the timeline as a containment operation rather than maintenance. The exposure window spans from the initial maintenance label at 07:13 UTC on October 29 through the security acknowledgment on October 30; any attacker access during that gap could have targeted the platform's user database and content management systems. With OTW stating it is still in the early stages of response, revised breach assessments or evidence of data access remain likely, making this story more dynamic than a simple service interruption.

Defenders should monitor otwstatus.org and the transformativeworks.org update page for restoration timelines and revised breach statements, particularly any confirmation that user data was accessed. Network indicators should focus on traffic patterns tied to the AO3 infrastructure rather than endpoint malware, since OTW has not released IoC names, log paths, or specific payload details. If user information is later confirmed stolen, the risk profile shifts from service disruption to identity and credential exposure for the AO3 user base, which would require a different response.

Watch for two changes: a revised OTW statement confirming or denying user data exfiltration, and any publication of attacker attribution or vulnerability details linked to the incident. Should either emerge, the assessment should move from containment-focused to breach-response-focused, with corresponding adjustments to hunting priorities.
