A campaign tracked as Spring Ring targeted more than 150 employees across at least 10 organizations between January and April 2026 by abusing Microsoft Teams for social engineering. Attackers created external Teams accounts with names like "Help Desk," "IT Assistance," and "Network Support" using attacker-controlled .onmicrosoft.com tenants to appear legitimate. They initiated chats then placed voice calls, impersonating IT technicians for 10 to 15 minutes to build trust.

The voice interaction was central. Rather than relying solely on malicious links, attackers used live conversations to persuade victims to launch legitimate remote-support software such as Quick Assist or third-party RMM tools. After gaining remote access, they ran whoami /groups and net group /dom for reconnaissance, then used PowerShell to download an obfuscated RAT from san-sid[.]com. In a second campaign variant, attackers used NTLM relay techniques after obtaining NTLMv2 hashes through the social engineering chain.

This campaign demonstrates a shift from email-based phishing into collaboration platforms where users have higher trust. The combination of voice calls, legitimate RMM tools, and custom malware makes detection harder than traditional credential phishing. Organizations should enforce policies restricting external Teams communication, monitor for unusual RMM tool execution, and alert on NTLM relay indicators. The use of .onmicrosoft.com tenant names that mimic corporate IT departments is a detail worth including in user awareness training.
