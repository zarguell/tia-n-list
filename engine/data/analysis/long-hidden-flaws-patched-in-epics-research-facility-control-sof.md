CISA published advisory ICSA-26-300-01 on October 27, 2026 covering four vulnerabilities in EPICS Base 7.0.10 and earlier, the central component of the open-source EPICS control-system toolkit used at particle accelerators, fusion experiments, and other large research facilities, including ITER's fusion reactor control system and Argonne's Advanced Photon Source. The flaws were discovered by Cipher, an autonomous security research system from Causal Security, in under four hours. The most severe is CVE-2026-114595 (CVSS 9.8), an integer overflow during network request processing that can lead to a wrongly sized memory allocation and potentially memory corruption, denial of service, or remote code execution. CVE-2026-114596 (CVSS 8.2) can cause an out-of-bounds write leaving the process unstable or causing denial of service, CVE-2026-114597 (CVSS 7.5) is an out-of-bounds read that could crash the application and leak nearby memory, and CVE-2026-114598 (CVSS 7.5) is a type-confusion issue. CISA recommends updating to EPICS Base 7.0.11.

Because EPICS Base underpins control systems at major research facilities, the 9.8 integer overflow is particularly concerning for environments where availability and safety are critical. The vulnerabilities require no credentials or user interaction according to the advisory summary, so internet-exposed or network-reachable EPICS deployments should be patched promptly and segmented from untrusted networks until updated.
