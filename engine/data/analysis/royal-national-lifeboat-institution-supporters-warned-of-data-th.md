The Royal National Lifeboat Institution (RNLI), the UK's lifeboat search and rescue service, has warned supporters that their personal data was stolen following a breach of a third-party CRM system. The RNLI disclosed that attackers compromised the customer relationship management platform used to manage supporter information, though the specific data elements exposed and the number of affected individuals have not been detailed.

Third-party CRM breaches remain a persistent risk because organizations often share donor, supporter, and customer data with SaaS platforms that may have different security postures than the primary organization. The RNLI breach follows a pattern of nonprofit and charity organizations being targeted through their technology supply chain, where smaller vendors may lack enterprise-grade security controls.

Supporters should monitor for phishing attempts referencing RNLI donations or membership, as breached supporter data is frequently used for targeted social engineering. The RNLI should conduct a thorough review of its third-party data processing agreements, assess the security posture of all vendors with access to supporter data, and implement data minimization practices to limit what is shared with CRM platforms. Organizations with donor databases should treat this as a reminder to audit their own vendor risk management processes.
