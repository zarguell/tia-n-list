Microsoft's September 24, 2026 report profiles Storm-2570 as a cross-ecosystem ransomware affiliate that deploys multiple ransomware families with consistent tradecraft. The merged coverage ties the operation to Qilin, DragonForce, and Anubis deployments; the load-bearing observation is that one affiliate, not several groups, sits behind brands defenders often track as unrelated threats. The report walks the attack chain from initial foothold to impact and ships Defender detections and hunting queries alongside it.

German coverage of the same report makes the right point: the notable part is not which ransomware lands but the repeated pattern across deployments, which is what makes the affiliate trackable. Affiliate operations rotate payload brands faster than defenders rotate detection logic, so brand-level IOCs decay quickly while the tradecraft underneath persists.

Watch for Storm-2570 adding or dropping ransomware families while reusing the same access tooling, and use Microsoft's hunting queries to find the affiliate's foothold behavior rather than its final payload. If your organization checks only for the current brand name in SIEM rules, that gap is the practical takeaway.
