Uber is notifying roughly 150,000 US drivers that personal data was stolen through Fireworks AI, a third-party AI cloud provider that held Uber records. A Texas attorney general filing puts the affected count at 149,346. Uber says it was notified by the provider on October 9, learned on October 13 that personal information had been accessed, and determined that stolen provider credentials were used on October 7. The exposed data includes driver's license images, names, addresses, dates of birth, license numbers and government-issued ID numbers.

Uber says the data sat in an environment created and managed by its Cloud Security team. Alerts were enabled but did not fire because the attacker used valid credentials, and the provider credentials did not grant access to other Uber systems, according to the company. Uber cut its data connections with the provider and filed notices in California, Iowa, Massachusetts, South Carolina, Texas and Vermont, offering free credit monitoring and fraud assistance to affected drivers.

Fireworks AI disclosed its own compromise in parallel. Attackers reached its development environment through another third-party cloud provider on October 23 and ran an unauthorized workload on October 24, taking environment variables with API keys and tokens, along with account and billing data, datasets and incomplete models for a limited set of accounts. Notion said fewer than 1% of workspaces were affected. Watch for downstream credential abuse and for revised victim counts from other Fireworks customers.
