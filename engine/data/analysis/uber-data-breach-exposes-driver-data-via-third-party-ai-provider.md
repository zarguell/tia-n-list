Uber is notifying 149,346 US drivers that names, addresses, dates of birth, driver's license numbers, government-issued ID numbers and images of licenses were exposed through Fireworks AI, the third-party AI provider that held Uber records. Uber was told on October 9 that the provider's internal credentials had been compromised and used to reach Uber's account, and determined on October 13 that the data had been accessed. Notification letters dated November 10 went to affected drivers, with filings in states including California, Iowa, Massachusetts, South Carolina, Texas and Vermont.

The upstream timeline matters for attribution. TechCrunch and The Hacker News report that attackers reached Fireworks' development environment through an unnamed third-party cloud provider on October 23, then ran an unauthorized workload on October 24. An earlier October incident at Fireworks involved environment variables holding API keys and tokens; the later activity touched account and billing information, secrets, datasets and incomplete models for a limited set of accounts. Fireworks said payment card numbers were not stored by the company and that fewer than 1% of Notion workspaces were affected.

Uber said the stolen data sat in an environment created and managed by its Cloud Security team, and that alerts were enabled but did not fire because the attacker used valid credentials. The company cut data connections to the provider and is offering credit monitoring and fraud assistance. A 17 November BleepingComputer recap held the count at roughly 150,000 drivers and restated the October 7 credential use, October 9 provider notice and October 13 determination. Watch for phishing aimed at drivers with their license and ID numbers, and for scope revisions as the Fireworks investigation continues.
