Uber is notifying 149,346 drivers that their names, addresses, dates of birth, driver's license numbers, government ID numbers and driver's license images were taken from an environment its Cloud Security team created and managed. Uber was told on October 9 that a third-party provider had internal credentials stolen and used to reach the Uber account, and its October 13 investigation found those credentials had been used on October 7. Reporting by TechCrunch and BleepingComputer identifies the provider as Fireworks AI, which separately disclosed that attackers reached its development machines through another cloud provider on October 23 and ran an unauthorized workload on October 24.

Uber says alerts were enabled on the affected environment but did not fire because the attacker used valid credentials, and that the stolen credentials did not give access to other Uber systems. It cut the provider's data connections after being notified. Notice letters went out on November 10, and filings cover California, Iowa, Massachusetts, South Carolina, Texas and Vermont; the Texas filing gives the 149,346 figure. Affected drivers are being offered free credit monitoring and fraud assistance. Fireworks said the wider incident touched account and billing data, secrets, datasets and incomplete models for a limited set of accounts, and Notion said fewer than 1% of its workspaces were affected.

The useful watch items are identity abuse rather than a password reset at Uber. Valid-provider-credential access bypassed the detection that would have caught a new login or a direct intrusion, and the stolen data is identity-document material that can be reused well after the notification cycle. Watch whether more state filings change the victim count and whether Fireworks publishes a root cause for the October 23 cloud-provider compromise, since that entry point is still unspecified in the available reporting.
