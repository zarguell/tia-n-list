Uber is notifying 149,346 US drivers that names, addresses, dates of birth, driver's license numbers, government-issued ID numbers and images of licenses were exposed through Fireworks AI, the third-party AI provider that held Uber records. Uber was told on October 9 that the provider's internal credentials had been used to reach its account, and determined on October 13 that the data had been accessed. Notification letters dated November 10 went to affected drivers, with filings in California, Iowa, Maine, Massachusetts, South Carolina, Texas and Vermont. A Swedish report from Redaktionen on November 18 restated the count as almost 150,000 drivers.

The upstream incident matters for the timeline. TechCrunch and The Hacker News report that attackers reached Fireworks' development environment through an unnamed third-party cloud provider on October 23, then ran an unauthorized workload on October 24. An earlier October incident at Fireworks involved environment variables holding API keys and tokens; the later activity touched account and billing information, secrets, datasets and incomplete models for a limited set of accounts. Fireworks said it does not store payment card numbers and that fewer than 1% of Notion workspaces were affected. Uber's own notice says compromised provider credentials were used on October 7 to reach the data.

Uber says the stolen records sat in an environment created and managed by its Cloud Security team, and that alerts were enabled but did not fire because the attacker used valid credentials. The company cut data connections to the provider and is offering credit monitoring and fraud assistance. Watch for phishing aimed at drivers using their license and ID numbers, and for any revision to the affected count as the Fireworks investigation continues.
