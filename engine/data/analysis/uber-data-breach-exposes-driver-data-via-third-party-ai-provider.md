Uber is notifying roughly 150,000 US drivers that personal information was taken through Fireworks AI, a third-party AI infrastructure provider that held Uber records. A filing with Maine's attorney general puts the count at 149,346. Uber says the provider reported stolen internal credentials on 9 October 2026, and its own investigation determined on 13 October that data connected to the provider account had been accessed. The stolen material includes names, addresses, dates of birth, driver's license numbers, government-issued ID numbers and images of driver's licenses. Uber cut data connections with the provider after being notified and is offering credit monitoring and fraud assistance.

The provider side of the incident is broader than the Uber notification. Fireworks disclosed that attackers reached its development machines through an unnamed third-party cloud provider hosting its R&D environment on 23 October, then ran an unauthorized workload on 24 October. Access included environment variables holding API keys and tokens, plus account and billing information, secrets, datasets and incomplete models for a limited set of accounts. Notion said fewer than 1% of its workspaces were affected. Fireworks says it does not store payment card numbers.

The detail that matters for detection is that the attacker used valid provider credentials: Uber says alerts were enabled but none fired. The data sat in an environment Uber's Cloud Security team created, but access was mediated by the vendor. Notification letters went out on 10 November, with filings in California, Iowa, Massachusetts, South Carolina, Texas and Vermont. Watch for revised victim counts from the provider investigation and for credential-theft follow-on against drivers whose license images and ID numbers were exposed.
