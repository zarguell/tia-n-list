Uber is notifying about 149,346 US drivers after attackers used compromised credentials at Fireworks AI, a third-party provider that held Uber records, to access data on October 7. The provider told Uber on October 9 that internal credentials had been stolen and used to reach the company's account, and Uber determined on October 13 that personal information had been accessed. Exposed material includes driver license images, names, addresses, dates of birth, license numbers and government-issued ID numbers. Uber says valid credentials were used, so its alerts did not fire, and it cut the provider's data connections after learning of the incident. Filings went to attorneys general in California, Iowa, Massachusetts, South Carolina, Texas and Vermont, with credit monitoring and fraud assistance offered to affected drivers.

Fireworks separately described an October 23 compromise through a third-party cloud provider hosting its development machines, followed by an unauthorized workload on October 24 that expanded the customer impact to account and billing information, secrets, datasets and incomplete models. Notion said fewer than 1 percent of its workspaces were affected. Fireworks said payment card numbers were not stored by the company and were not impacted. The two timelines do not line up exactly with Uber's October 7 access date, so the vendor account theft and the later development-environment intrusion may be separate stages of one incident or distinct problems.

The useful signal is credential trust rather than a break of Uber's own systems: a vendor account created a clean-looking data access path inside an environment Uber's own Cloud Security team built and managed. Watch for additional state filings, any revision of the 149,346 count, and whether either company names the upstream cloud provider or the route used to steal the initial credentials. Affected drivers should expect license-number misuse and targeted follow-on fraud that references Uber-specific details.
