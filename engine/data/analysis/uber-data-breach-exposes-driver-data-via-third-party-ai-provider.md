Uber is notifying roughly 149,346 US drivers that personal data was taken through Fireworks AI, a third-party provider that held Uber records. Attacker use of compromised provider credentials began on October 7, Uber was told on October 9, and its investigation confirmed access to Uber data on October 13. Notification letters went out on November 10, with filings in states including California, Maine, Iowa, Massachusetts, South Carolina, Texas and Vermont. Exposed fields include names, addresses, dates of birth, driver's license numbers, government ID numbers and images of driver's licenses.

Fireworks AI reported its own related compromise: attackers reached development machines through a third-party cloud provider on October 23 and ran an unauthorized workload on October 24, gaining access to secrets, datasets and incomplete models for a limited set of accounts. An earlier Fireworks notification covered environment variables holding API keys and tokens. Notion said fewer than 1% of its workspaces were affected by the same incident. Fireworks says it does not store payment card numbers.

The breach matters because the attacker used valid provider credentials against an environment Uber's Cloud Security team had created and managed, and alerts were enabled but never fired. Uber severed data connections with the provider and is offering credit monitoring and fraud assistance. Watch for updated victim counts from state filings, further customer disclosures tied to Fireworks, and any evidence that the stolen license images and ID numbers are being used for fraud.
