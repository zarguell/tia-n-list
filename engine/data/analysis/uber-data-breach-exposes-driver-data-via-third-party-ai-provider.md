Uber is notifying roughly 150,000 US drivers that personal data was stolen after attackers used compromised credentials at Fireworks AI, a third-party AI cloud provider that held Uber records. The Texas attorney general filing puts the count at 149,346. Notices filed in California, Iowa, Massachusetts, South Carolina, Texas and Vermont say Uber was alerted by the provider on October 9, learned on October 13 that personal information had been accessed, and determined that stolen provider credentials were used on October 7. Exposed records include driver's license images, names, addresses, dates of birth, license numbers and government-issued ID numbers.

Uber says the data sat in an environment created and managed by its Cloud Security team. Alerts were enabled but did not fire because the attacker used valid credentials, and the provider credentials did not grant access to other Uber systems. Uber cut its data connections with the provider and is offering free credit monitoring and fraud assistance. Fireworks AI disclosed its own compromise in parallel: attackers reached its development environment through another third-party cloud provider on October 23, ran an unauthorized workload on October 24, and took environment variables containing API keys and tokens, account and billing data, datasets and incomplete models for a limited set of accounts. Notion said fewer than 1% of workspaces were affected. Fireworks said payment card numbers were not stored and were not impacted.

Watch for revised victim counts from other Fireworks customers, downstream abuse of the stolen API keys and tokens, and any widening of the October 7 access window as the investigation continues. The chain matters more than the first count: one cloud provider's stolen credentials reached Fireworks' development environment, which in turn held Uber driver records, and valid-credential access left the normal alerting path blind.
