Uber is notifying about 150,000 drivers after a third-party services provider compromise exposed personal data the company had shared with that provider. Uber learned of the incident on October 9 and cut data connections, then determined on October 13 that attackers had used compromised provider credentials on October 7 to reach the data. The Texas Attorney General filing puts the affected count at 149,346. Other notifications went to California, Iowa, Massachusetts, South Carolina and Vermont.

The accessed material includes images of driver licenses, names, addresses, dates of birth, driver license numbers and government-issued ID numbers. Uber said the data sat in an environment built and managed by its Cloud Security team, and that alerts were configured but did not fire because the actor used valid credentials. The provider's credentials did not reach other Uber systems, according to the notice. Earlier coverage names the provider as Fireworks AI, whose own October incident involved an unauthorized workload on development machines reached through a third-party cloud provider.

The practical risk is identity fraud against a large driver population, with license and government ID images in scope. Uber is offering credit monitoring and fraud assistance. Watch for whether the provider is formally identified in amended filings and whether the investigation changes the count or the categories of data involved.
