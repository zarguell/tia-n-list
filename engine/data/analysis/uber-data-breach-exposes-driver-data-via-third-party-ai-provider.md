Uber is notifying about 149,346 US drivers after attackers used compromised credentials at Fireworks AI, a third-party AI provider that held Uber records, to access data on October 7. The provider told Uber on October 9, and Uber determined on October 13 that personal information had been accessed. Exposed material includes driver's license images, names, addresses, dates of birth, license numbers and government-issued ID numbers. Uber says valid credentials were used, so its alerts did not fire, and it cut the provider's data connections after learning of the incident.

Fireworks separately described an October 23 compromise through a third-party cloud provider hosting its development machines, followed by an unauthorized workload on October 24 that expanded the customer impact to account and billing information, secrets, datasets and incomplete models. Notion said fewer than 1 percent of its workspaces were affected. Uber has filed notices in California, Iowa, Massachusetts, South Carolina, Texas and Vermont and is offering credit monitoring and fraud assistance.

The useful signal here is credential trust rather than a break of Uber's own systems: a vendor account created a clean-looking data access path. Watch for additional state filings, any expansion of the affected count, and whether Fireworks identifies the cloud provider or the initial access route. Affected drivers should expect license-number misuse and targeted follow-on fraud.
