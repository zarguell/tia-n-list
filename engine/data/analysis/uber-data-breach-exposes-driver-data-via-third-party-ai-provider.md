Uber is notifying thousands of U.S. drivers that personal and driver's license information was stolen in an October 2026 breach at Fireworks AI, a third-party AI cloud provider that held Uber records. Uber said it was alerted by the provider on October 9, confirmed on October 13 that personal information had been accessed, severed its data connections, and filed breach notices with several state attorneys general including California, with the notice letter dated November 10, 2026.

The incident sits inside a wider compromise of Fireworks AI itself. Attackers first reached Fireworks development machines through an unnamed third-party cloud provider that hosted its R&D environment on October 23, 2026, then ran an unauthorized workload on October 24. A separate earlier October incident exposed environment variables holding API keys and tokens for a limited number of accounts. Fireworks' follow-on investigation found additional malicious activity touching account and billing information, secrets, datasets, and incomplete models for a limited set of customers; Notion reported fewer than 1 percent of workspaces affected, and Fireworks stated payment card numbers are not stored and were not impacted.

The case is a supply-chain lesson: a vendor's R&D environment and secret stores became the path to customer data, and the downstream victim learned of exposure only after the provider's own delayed disclosure. Affected drivers should expect phishing and identity-fraud attempts using license numbers and personal details, while customers of Fireworks AI should rotate any credentials and tokens that transited the platform and verify whether their data was in the limited affected set.
