Federal contractors that handle controlled unclassified information are about to face government-wide security and breach reporting requirements. Pending rules would mandate that any unauthorized access to CUI, including access resulting from a cyberattack, be reported to the federal government within 72 hours of discovery, and would impose minimum electronic security standards for protecting the data. CUI covers material below classification level, including personal data such as Social Security numbers and technical details that could expose vulnerabilities in critical infrastructure.

Procurement attorneys cited by CyberScoop describe the change as a sea change for the contractor base. The rules could land as early as the end of 2026 and likely no later than the end of the current presidential term. They extend beyond the Defense Department, acting as the government-wide companion to existing DoD rules on the same subject, and their 72-hour reporting window is deliberately aligned with CISA's forthcoming CIRCIA rules requiring critical infrastructure owners and operators to report major cyber incidents.

Contractors should watch for the final rule text and its effective date, since the reporting clock and minimum standards will apply contractually once in force. Alignment with CIRCIA timelines suggests companies preparing for one regime can reuse much of that work for the other, but firms working only with civilian agencies have no equivalent obligation today and will need new detection and reporting capability.
