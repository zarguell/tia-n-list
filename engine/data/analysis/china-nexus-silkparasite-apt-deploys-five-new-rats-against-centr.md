SilkParasite is a China-nexus espionage campaign (medium confidence attribution) targeting government bodies across Central Asia. Researchers uncovered the operation after investigating a suspicious infection at a Central Asian government organization involved in economic policy in late 2025. The campaign had likely been operating for nearly a year before detection. SilkParasite uses seven RAT families: five previously undocumented (DriveSilkRAT, CookiETagRAT, NomadRAT, GoginRAT, NodeEdgeRAT) and two known families (SpiceRAT and BloodAlchemy, linked to ShadowPad and the Deed RAT lineage).

The C2 infrastructure is notable for abusing Google Drive as a command-and-control channel. By tunneling RAT traffic through Google Drive, the malware blends into cloud activity that enterprises inherently trust, making network-level detection significantly harder. The campaign targets government bodies involved in economic decision-making, consistent with Chinese state-sponsored intelligence collection priorities in Central Asia.

The breadth of custom RAT tooling, the use of trusted cloud services for C2, and the sustained operation before detection indicate a mature, well-resourced threat actor. Organizations in Central Asia and adjacent regions should audit for the five new RAT families and monitor Google Drive API traffic for anomalous patterns. The link to ShadowPad lineage (via BloodAlchemy) connects this activity to a broader Chinese APT toolkit used across multiple regions.
