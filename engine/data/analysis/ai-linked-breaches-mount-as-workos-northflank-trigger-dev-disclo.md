Over the past month at least a dozen AI developer platforms were hit, with the most recent incidents involving CommandCode, Northflank, Trigger.dev, Chainstack and WorkOS. Across all incidents the attackers appear to have been hunting for AI access tokens they could monetize, relying on AI agents to speed up and streamline their attacks. Initial access varies from compromised credentials to third-party dependencies and vulnerability exploitation, and the incidents do not appear to be related.

Cloud Security Alliance points out that intruders are reaching AI provider credentials through the intermediary layer of hosting platforms, evaluation services and developer consoles, where that layer holds credentials on behalf of many downstream customers and a single intrusion can expose keys for many inference accounts at once. Wiz attributes the surge to the rise of LLM-jacking and LLM broker infrastructure, which make stolen AI access tokens easy to monetize. Defenders should treat AI platform credentials as high-value secrets and rotate any keys touched by the affected services.
