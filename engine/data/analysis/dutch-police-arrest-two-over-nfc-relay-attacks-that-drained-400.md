Dutch police arrested a 23-year-old from Vaassen and a 25-year-old from Ede on October 6, 2026 over a bank helpdesk fraud scheme that used AI-generated audio and NFC relay to drain more than 400,000 euros, roughly 425,600 dollars, from 170 victims of all ages. The suspects called victims claiming suspicious account activity, then had them download an application and hold their debit card against the phone so the app could read the NFC chip data used for contactless payments. That data was relayed to a second attacker standing at an ATM to withdraw funds. Investigators took the fraudsters' servers offline and seized equipment.

The case combines social engineering with a technical relay step, showing how contactless payment data can be turned into cash without the physical card leaving the victim's hand. The two suspects divided labor between infrastructure and coordination, and the use of AI-generated voices lowered the barrier to impersonating multiple banks.

Banks and consumers should treat unsolicited calls asking to scan a payment card as fraud, and institutions should review whether contactless limits and NFC transaction monitoring would catch relay-style withdrawals. The arrests remove one operation but the technique is replicable, so awareness and transaction controls remain the main defenses.
