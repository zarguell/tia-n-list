TSMC confirmed a data breach on November 12, 2026, when an attacker began selling what they claim to be employee personal data on an underground forum. The seller listed 46,000 records totaling 6.9GB of data and set an asking price of 3 BTC. Security researcher Jian Yongxiang first reported the incident, noting the breach was allegedly traced back to a cloud provider serving TSMC.

TSMC responded to the incident but has not found evidence of system intrusion. The company stated it is investigating whether the data originated from a third-party service provider rather than a direct compromise of TSMC infrastructure. The discrepancy between the seller's claim and TSMC's internal assessment is common in supply chain breaches where vendors hold employee data on behalf of the primary organization.

The incident highlights ongoing supply chain security challenges in the semiconductor industry, where TSMC operates as the world's leading foundry. If confirmed, this breach would join a pattern of attacks targeting chip manufacturers and their contractors, including previous incidents affecting equipment suppliers and IP partners. The 3 BTC ransom demand (approximately $180,000 at current rates) suggests the attacker expects the data to contain valuable credentials or proprietary information beyond basic PII.
