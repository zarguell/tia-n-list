Keio Corporation, a major Japanese private railway operator with 85 km of track, 69 stations, 25 hotels, over 2,200 employees, and about $2.6 billion in annual revenue, confirmed a ransomware attack that disrupted some business systems. The company shut down its network to limit damage after confirming the attack in the early hours of September 26, 2026. The impact appears confined to the hospitality side rather than train operations, though customer-facing services at Keio Plaza Hotel Tokyo may face delays. BleepingComputer reports the company contacted police and is working with external experts; at the time of reporting no ransomware group had claimed responsibility.

The breach matters because of the target size and the potential for customer or partner data exposure. No CVEs are involved, and no technical vulnerability is named; this is a ransomware intrusion with network shutdown as containment. Watch for the investigation results regarding whether attackers accessed customer or business partner information, claims from ransomware groups, and updates from Japanese authorities. The primary sources are BleepingComputer and the Keio company statement.
