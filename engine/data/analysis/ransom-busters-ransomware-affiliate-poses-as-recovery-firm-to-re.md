GuidePoint Security's GRIT team uncovered a double-extortion tactic where a ransomware affiliate impersonates a third-party recovery service called "Ransom Busters." The group contacts victims before their attacks become public, claiming to have breached ransomware infrastructure, recovered decryption keys, and deleted stolen backups. The price: a fee to the "recovery firm" rather than the original ransomware operator. GRIT assesses with moderate confidence that Ransom Busters is the affiliate itself, siphoning payments away from the RaaS platform.

The scheme spans at least three ransomware families: DragonForce, Settra, and Anubis. What makes it distinctive is the social engineering angle. Instead of relying solely on a ransom note, the affiliate positions itself as a savior third party that has allegedly compromised the attacker's panels. Victims who believe the offer may pay twice: once to the fake recovery service and again when the original operator re-extorts with stolen data. The affiliate's advance knowledge of the attack timeline is the red flag that gave it away.

The breadth of sources (four outlets covering it within hours) and the scoring velocity suggest this is gaining traction in the community. Watch for follow-up reporting on whether any victims actually paid, and whether RaaS operators respond by tightening affiliate identification or issuing public denouncements. This pattern could proliferate if it proves profitable.
