GuidePoint Security's GRIT team identified a ransomware affiliate impersonating a recovery service called "Ransom Busters" to intercept extortion payments from victims. The scheme targets organizations already hit by DragonForce, Settra, or Anubis ransomware. Before the victim's breach becomes public, the affiliate sends emails claiming to have breached the ransomware gang's infrastructure, offering file recovery, data deletion, and decryption keys in exchange for a separate payment. GRIT assesses with moderate confidence this is the same affiliate running the original attack.

The tactic exploits victims' desperation during active incidents. By positioning as a third-party rescuer, the affiliate creates urgency and confusion, potentially diverting payments that would go to the original ransomware operator. The moderate-confidence assessment suggests GRIT has linked infrastructure or payment addresses between the "Ransom Busters" emails and the underlying ransomware operations but lacks definitive proof.

Three sources covered this (Malware.News, GBHackers, CyberPress). No CVE involvement. The story matters because it represents an evolution in ransomware monetization: affiliates are now running double-extortion schemes against both victims and their own operators. Incident response teams should warn clients about unsolicited recovery offers during active ransomware engagements. The DragonForce/Settra/Anubis attribution suggests this affiliate operates across multiple ransomware-as-a-service programs.
