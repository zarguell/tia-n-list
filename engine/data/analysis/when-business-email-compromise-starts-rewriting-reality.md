Rapid7 opened a research series with Zimbra Collaboration Suite by reporting it found over 50 vulnerabilities in the product. The finding reframes business email compromise: instead of the familiar playbook of breaching one mailbox and quietly monitoring it, several of the flaws let attackers actively rewrite an environment by impersonating senders without credentials, controlling inbox visibility, and altering shared documents and calendars.

Exploitation history shows this is not theoretical. CVE-2024-45519, unauthenticated command injection in the postjournal service, was abused on September 28, 2024 with base64 payloads stuffed into CC fields, per Proofpoint, and CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 3. CVE-2025-27915, stored XSS in the Classic Web Client triggered by a crafted .ICS attachment, was used as a zero-day against Brazilian military targets to steal mail and silently set forwarding rules. CISA has repeatedly added Zimbra bugs to KEV over the last three years.

Watch the follow-up installments of the series for technical detail, and treat unpatched or internet-exposed Zimbra instances as a standing BEC foothold, since the same bug classes that enabled past KEV-listed exploitation now extend to sender impersonation and inbox manipulation.
