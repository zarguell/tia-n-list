Rapid7's series opener on Zimbra Collaboration Suite counts more than 50 vulnerabilities, and the ones it leads with change what business email compromise means technically: an attacker who can impersonate senders without holding credentials, control what a victim sees in the inbox, and alter shared documents and calendars is not reading the approval chain, that attacker is authoring it. This lands on a platform with a documented exploitation record. CVE-2024-45519, unauthenticated command injection in the postjournal service scored 9.8, was hit in the wild with base64 payloads in CC fields on September 28, 2024 per Proofpoint and reached CISA's KEV catalog five days later. CVE-2025-27915, stored XSS in the Classic Web Client triggered by a crafted .ICS attachment, ran as a zero-day against Brazilian military targets before Zimbra shipped 9.0.0 Patch 44, 10.0.13, and 10.1.5 on January 27, 2025.

The reason this story outranks the day's other vulnerability noise is that email is the system of record for obligations, approvals, and intent in nearly every organization, and these bug classes corrupt the record itself rather than skirting it. A finance team can be defrauded by someone who never observed the approval process, because the process's artifacts can be manufactured. Calendar and Briefcase manipulation adds manufactured context to manufactured correspondence, which is why Rapid7 frames the work as psychological operations and calendar warfare, and it breaks detections built for the classic BEC pattern: forwarding-rule alerts, impossible travel, and phishing lures all assume the attacker reads while the platform writes. The victim pool is broad and precedented. Volexity counted over 1,000 backdoored Zimbra servers worldwide in August 2022 after CVE-2022-27925 was chained with the CVE-2022-37042 authentication bypass, a medium-rated RCE that mass exploitation turned into an unauthenticated breach tool, and Zimbra's advisory table shows this series already shipping as discrete fixes credited to Rapid7's Jonah Burgess: CVE-2026-73571, delegated send impersonation, in 10.1.17, plus CVE-2026-50054, CVE-2026-50055, CVE-2026-10631, and CVE-2026-73570 in 10.1.20. The realistic exposure state for a Zimbra fleet is version drift across that patch ladder, not one missing update.

Defenders should pin each build against the vendor table instead of assuming a single patch covers the series. CVE-2024-45519 is fixed in 9.0.0 Patch 41, 10.0.9, 10.1.1, and 8.8.15 Patch 46, released September 4, 2024. CVE-2025-27915 is fixed in 9.0.0 Patch 44, 10.0.13, and 10.1.5. The 2026 impersonation, delegation, forwarding, and EWS fixes land in 10.1.17 and 10.1.20, and 10.1.20 also covers CVE-2026-73570, command injection in the SNMP monitoring component that applies only when SNMP notifications are enabled. Kudelski Security's advisory offers restricting or disabling the postjournal service as a bridge while unpatched. Hunt on the known signatures rather than trusting patch state: proxy and mailbox logs for POSTs to /service/extension/backup/mboximport returning 401, the 2022 mass-exploitation marker Volexity documented; unexpected JSP files under /opt/zimbra; the message store for Base64-encoded .ICS entries, guidance Zimbra itself gave after the Brazilian military campaign, whose payload added a mail filter named Correo forwarding to a Proton address and pulled mail through the Zimbra SOAP API every four hours; and every forwarding rule and delegation grant, since CVE-2026-50055 is precisely a forwarding-restriction bypass. StrikeReady caught the .ICS zero-day by flagging attachments over 10KB containing JavaScript, a static rule cheap to add upstream, and admin and webmail interfaces belong off the internet throughout.

Two developments would change the assessment. The next installments of the Rapid7 series will show whether these bugs chain, and chaining is what history says to expect: CVE-2022-27925 plus CVE-2022-37042 turned a medium RCE needing admin credentials into unauthenticated mass exploitation within weeks in 2022. And watch CISA's KEV and public exploit repositories for the new identifiers, because Zimbra's postjournal bug went from Proofpoint telemetry to KEV in five days in 2024; the same cadence for CVE-2026-73571 or CVE-2026-50055 would convert a research story into an emergency patch cycle for every exposed Zimbra instance.
