Rapid7's Zimbra research reframes business email compromise from passive mailbox monitoring to active environment manipulation: over 50 Zimbra Collaboration Suite vulnerabilities let attackers impersonate senders without credentials, control inbox visibility and alter shared documents and calendars. The FBI's IC3 tallied about $3.05 billion in reported BEC losses for 2025, and most cases involve no malware at all, so the ability to rewrite messages and calendar state raises the stakes for finance and approval workflows.

The same Zimbra stack has a long record of exploited flaws. CVE-2022-27925 mboximport RCE, enabled by the CVE-2022-37042 authentication bypass, was mass-exploited in 2022 with over 1,000 backdoored instances including government and military targets. CVE-2024-45519, a CVSS 9.8 command-injection flaw in the postjournal service, was exploited one day after a public proof of concept to deploy web shells; fixes shipped in 8.8.15 Patch 46, 9.0.0 Patch 41, 10.0.9 and 10.1.1. CVE-2025-27915, a stored XSS triggered by a crafted .ICS attachment, was used as a zero-day against a Brazilian military organization from January 2025, stealing mail and contacts and forwarding messages to a Proton address.

Defenders should prioritize patching internet-exposed Zimbra instances, disabling or restricting postjournal where possible, and monitoring for suspicious mboximport requests, webshells and mail-filter rules such as forwarding to external addresses. The combination of BEC's trust-based fraud and server-side mail manipulation makes Zimbra a high-value target, so assume compromise for unpatched public instances and hunt with the published Snort signatures, YARA rules and IOCs.
