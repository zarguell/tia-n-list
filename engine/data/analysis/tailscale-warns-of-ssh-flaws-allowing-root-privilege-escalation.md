Tailscale SSH and serve components contain 11 findings collectively called Tailworm, with six rated high severity. Four of the flaws target Tailscale SSH, which performs Unix-socket forwarding with root privileges; an attacker who compromises one device permitted to reach another over SSH can escalate to root on the target server and steal keys that add rogue devices to the tailnet. The research was published by Cyberdione Labs (Erica Windisch) before patches were available, covering Tailscale client 1.104.2 and headscale 0.29.5.

As of the October 26 reports, no patch release was available, though Tailscale had unreleased commits addressing some findings. The company released version 1.104.4 fixing a batch of SSH/Serve flaws (TS-2026-014 through TS-2026-017), which narrows but may not fully resolve the Tailworm surface. Watch for a complete patch cycle and any evidence that the SSH forwarding bypass is being used in targeted attacks against self-hosted headscale environments.
