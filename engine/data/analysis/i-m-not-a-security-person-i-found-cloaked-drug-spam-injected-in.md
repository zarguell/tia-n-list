A health blogger discovered that stonevillenc.org, the legitimate website for the Town of Stoneville, North Carolina, is serving cloaked spam pages for drug and peptide products. The site uses user-agent detection: crawlers like Googlebot receive full-length spam articles (85 KB pages about peptides), while real users get JavaScript redirects to WhatsApp numbers with pre-filled "I want peptide catalog" messages. The spam operation is actively maintained, with the redirect URLs rotating from three to nine numbers between observations.

The pages are winning organic search results and appearing in Google's AI Overview, cited as coming from "Town of Stoneville, NC." Searches for terms like "sam sulek retatrutide" return ten of these spam pages on the first results page, all attributed to the town's domain. The AI Overview treats them as authoritative sources because they originate from a government domain, amplifying the reach of the cloaked content.

This illustrates a real-world SEO poisoning attack against a small municipality. The town likely lacks the IT resources to detect or remediate the compromise. For security practitioners, it demonstrates how compromised government domains can be weaponized to lend credibility to spam, and how AI-powered search features can amplify the damage by citing compromised authoritative sources as trusted.
