The U.S. Department of Justice has charged 17 Iranian nationals connected to the Mabna Institute for a years-long campaign that stole $3.4 billion worth of intellectual property, academic research, emails, and proprietary data from American organizations. Nine of the defendants were previously indicted in March 2018 for hacking over 300 universities and private companies. The newly charged eight individuals operated under the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), using credential theft, spear-phishing, and network intrusions against universities, corporations, and government agencies.

The DOJ has posted rewards of up to $10 million for information leading to the arrest of five of the defendants, signaling that these individuals are considered high-priority targets with firm attribution. The Mabna Institute has been linked to prior campaigns including "Operation APT35" and the "Alan Turing" operation, both attributed to IRGC-linked actors.

This indictment continues the U.S. strategy of charging foreign hackers even when physical arrest is improbable. The legal framework enables asset seizures, travel restrictions, and sanctions. Organizations in academic and government sectors that were targeted during this period should review whether their data was compromised in the Mabna Institute campaigns, as the scope of affected entities spans multiple years and sectors.
