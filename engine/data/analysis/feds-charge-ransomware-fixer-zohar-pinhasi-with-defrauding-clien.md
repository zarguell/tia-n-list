Federal prosecutors charged Zohar Pinhasi, owner of MonsterCloud, with wire fraud for running a ransomware “fixer” scheme that defrauded clients out of over $19 million. Rather than using proprietary decryption tools as advertised, Pinhasi allegedly paid more than $8 million in ransoms to attackers and then billed victims for the recovered keys - charging about $150,000 in one case after paying an $8,200 ransom. The indictment undermines trust in third-party ransomware recovery services and highlights the legal risk for intermediaries who conceal ransom payments behind inflated service fees.

Pinhasi, also known as Zack Silver and Zack Green, could face decades in prison if convicted. The case reinforces a hard rule for incident response: verify any vendor’s decryption claims independently and prefer law-enforcement reporting over unverified remediation vendors. Organizations that used MonsterCloud should review past recoveries for hidden ransom flows and assess whether client data was handled by unknown third parties.
