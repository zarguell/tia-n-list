RatHat is an Android banking Trojan that uses a live AI assistant to dynamically interact with the victim's device. Unlike traditional Android malware that relies on hardcoded scripts or fixed overlay templates, RatHat accesses the Android accessibility tree and interprets screen content in real time, deciding where to tap, scroll, or input data based on what it sees. Zimperium's zLabs and MalwareBytes both published analyses of the threat.

The malware also abuses Android Debug Bridge (ADB) capabilities, including wireless debugging, to gain shell access. This combination of AI-driven navigation and ADB exploitation makes RatHat unusually adaptive. It can vary its interaction path depending on device settings, installed apps, and the specific screens presented, defeating behavioural detection that relies on predictable attack sequences.

Infection begins with social engineering: smishing messages and malicious ads redirect victims to fake download pages impersonating streaming services or browsers. Users are prompted to sideload an APK outside official stores. Once installed, RatHat requests accessibility permissions and begins exfiltrating banking credentials, PINs, and one-time passcodes. The use of a live AI model for screen interpretation is a notable escalation in mobile threat sophistication, moving beyond static automation to something closer to a remote operator's decision-making.
