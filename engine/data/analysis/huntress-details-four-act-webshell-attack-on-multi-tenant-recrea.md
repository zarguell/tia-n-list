Huntress published a four-act incident write-up on September 30, 2026 covering an intrusion observed starting September 10, 2026 against three web servers of a shared, multi-tenant recreation management platform used by municipalities and parks organizations. The attacker spent roughly six hours failing against the first server with brute force on two login pages, IIS 8.3 tilde enumeration, WebDAV method probes, upload-handler parser bypasses, and forced browsing. Access finally came through the front door: register a member account, abuse the member file upload function, and plant 14 files including .aspx webshells in the /documents/MemberFiles/ directory.

Once inside, the actor extracted the IIS applicationHost.config, scraped web.config files for connection strings, machineKeys, and API keys, then used stolen SQL credentials with sqlcmd to hunt cardholder data. Payment data was found in Fortis payment webhook logs holding card numbers, expiration dates, and CVVs, and the actor searched for AuthorizeNet, Fortis, CardConnect, BluePay, PayPal, Braintree, and Stripe integrations. On the second and third servers the tradecraft sharpened: webshells copied and renamed to css_bundle.aspx, jquery.validate.min.aspx, and webresource.aspx, PowerShell timestomping to inherit legitimate file timestamps, and failed attempts to drop shells into mxmerchant and fortis directories. The PowerShell user agent carries a zh-CN locale, pointing to a mainland China base, and Huntress assesses AI-generated scripts were used, with instruction text such as "Fast plant: candidate paths only, no drive recurse" left as comments.

The fourth act followed a premature return of the cleaned third server to production. The actor returned, found its old webshells remediated, uploaded new ones, and trojanized the jquery.mousewheel-3.0.6.pack.js asset loaded by the /auth/default.aspx page. Base64-encoded planters in C:\Windows\Temp (plant_ccrtc_mousewheel.ps1, plant_ccrtc_fast.ps1, ccrtc_sdk_init.js) injected a browser agent from chat.ririmochii[.]workers[.]dev, converting visiting browsers into encrypted WebRTC and WebSocket C2 clients through fk.bubuneeko[.]workers[.]dev for real-time credential harvesting. Watch for tenant-side review of payment integration logs, use of the published IOCs (webshell keys wf9x and m7Qx2pL9, the listed SHA-256 hashes and workers.dev C2 domains), and other shared municipal platforms where upload functions trust self-registered accounts.
