Russian APT Star Blizzard, linked to Russia's FSB Centre 18, has updated its tactics with a new malware delivery technique called RedFlick. The group relies on large-scale spear-phishing campaigns targeting academia, defense, government, NGOs, and think tanks in the US, UK, NATO countries, and states neighboring Russia. In attacks observed between January and August 2026, Star Blizzard launched over a dozen campaigns using RedFlick lure attachments that pose as Ukrainian authorities or reputable think tanks. The infection chain requires a single user interaction: after a response to the initial phishing email, the attacker sends a password-protected archive that triggers malware execution. Microsoft reports the group creates accounts on compromised websites to send tens to hundreds of phishing emails per campaign. The advisory notes sustained targeting of Ukrainian individuals and international organizations supporting Ukraine.

The RedFlick technique lowers the barrier to compromise compared with previous multi-stage chains. Organizations in defense, academia, and NGOs should treat unsolicited Ukrainian-themed documents with extra scrutiny, audit email authentication on legacy domains, and verify contact details independently before opening password-protected attachments. No specific CVEs accompany this campaign; the threat is behavioral and infrastructure-based rather than vulnerability-driven. Watch for additional Microsoft updates on Star Blizzard infrastructure indicators.
