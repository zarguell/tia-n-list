Star Blizzard (FSB Centre 18 / SEABORGIUM) replaced ClickFix with RedFlick, a single-click malware delivery chain described in Microsoft's September 29, 2026 report and confirmed in a September 30 bleepingcomputer summary. A target replies to an initial attachment-free email; the follow-up delivers a password-protected RAR or ZIP containing a VHDX disk image or an LNK disguised as a PDF. Opening it runs a script that fetches an MSI, which creates scheduled tasks pulling down CosmicPulse, a Python backdoor that stores its AES key under HKCU\Software\Classes\.mollis. The chain mutated through 2026: by July the PowerShell stage hid inside a downloaded PDF behind a cAB magic-header marker, and by mid-August the group used steganography and unique ZIP files per target.

Microsoft counted at least 13 large-scale campaigns from January to August 2026 affecting over 100 organizations, mainly in the US and UK, sent in waves of tens to hundreds of emails. The reply-gated second stage means clean mail logs prove nothing; mounted VHDX files carry no Mark-of-the-Web, so SmartScreen never prompts. The group now sends from accounts on compromised cPanel and WordPress sites and places the impersonated organization in the local part of the sender address, defeating sender-reputation filtering. There is no patch: this is tradecraft. Hunt for scheduled tasks named Internet Quality Test Connection, Network Configuration Manager, System Health Monitor, or SecureDNSObserver; the .mollis registry key; conhost.exe spawning curl; and ssh.exe with PermitLocalCommand. Defender covers Trojan:Script/RedFlick and Backdoor:Python/CosmicPulse. Restrict outbound SSH and WebDAV, enable ASR rules, keep Safe Attachments and Safe Links active, and train staff that a reply to an unsolicited message producing an archive with a password delivered as an image is the trigger.
