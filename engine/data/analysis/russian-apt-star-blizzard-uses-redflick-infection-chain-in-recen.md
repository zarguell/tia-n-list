Russian state-sponsored APT Star Blizzard, believed to be subordinate to FSB Centre 18, has updated its tactics with a new malware delivery technique called RedFlick, according to Microsoft. The group launched over a dozen campaigns between January and August 2026, using large-scale phishing that requires a single user interaction. If a recipient responds to the initial email, Star Blizzard sends a second message containing a password-protected RAR or ZIP archive that triggers malware execution.

The APT has targeted Ukrainian individuals and institutions, international NGOs, think tanks, governments, and financial institutions supporting Ukraine. It creates accounts on compromised websites to send tens to hundreds of phishing emails per campaign, likely through a mass-mailing platform. The report does not introduce a CVE, but the operational detail - a single-interaction infection chain - is the key defensive takeaway: users should treat unexpected responses to initial outreach as high-risk triggers.
