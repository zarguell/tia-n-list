The Silver Fox threat actor is running an active campaign targeting China-based organizations across healthcare, manufacturing, gaming, technology, logistics, government, and education sectors. Attackers operate convincing counterfeit download pages for brands including Razer, Microsoft Edge, Kaspersky, Calibre, SteelSeries, Baidu Netdisk, Sogou, and draw.io, using look-alike domains like pc-razerzone[.]com[.]cn and app-microsoft-edge[.]com[.]cn.

The campaign uses dynamically generated ZIP archives (with names like app_setup, zinst, zintall, intsoft, and innstll) that allow attackers to change file hashes while retaining the same archive name. This defeats simple hash-based detection. After extraction and execution, a wrapper executable drops malware into randomized folders under C:\Users\Public, C:\ProgramData, or C:\Program Files (x86) with randomized filenames to evade identification.

Microsoft has not attributed the activity to a nation-state actor, but the infrastructure and payload delivery suggest a well-resourced operation. The broad targeting across critical sectors in China indicates either intelligence collection or broader monetization goals. Organizations should block the identified domains and monitor for the documented archive naming patterns.
