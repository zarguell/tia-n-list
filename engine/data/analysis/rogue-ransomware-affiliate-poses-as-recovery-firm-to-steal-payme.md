GuidePoint Security's GRIT team disclosed a ransomware affiliate running a double-extortion scheme under the name "Ransom Busters," posing as an independent recovery service. The operation contacts victims before attacks become public, offering to decrypt stolen data and delete it from servers for $20,000 to $60,000. The affiliate claims access to RaaS infrastructure from DragonForce, Settra, and Anubis, which raises the question of how they obtained that access in the first place.

GRIT's evidence points to Ransom Busters being the attacker itself. Across two separate incidents, the same toolset appeared: SoftPerfect Network Scanner, s5cmd, and the Remotely remote monitoring tool. The same backdoor account (password "Numlock!123") and the same attacker-controlled hostname "DESKTOP-BBETH6K" were used in both cases. The affiliate appears to be monetizing the same attack twice: once through the ransom, and again through a fraudulent recovery fee.

The tactic is notable because it exploits the chaos and information asymmetry of a ransomware incident. Victims who receive unsolicited recovery offers before the breach is public have limited means to verify the legitimacy of the service. Organizations should treat any unsolicited ransomware recovery offer as suspicious and cross-reference with their incident response provider before paying. The reuse of identical tooling and infrastructure across incidents suggests this is the same actor repeating a playbook rather than an isolated scheme.
