France attributes the mid-October sabotage of supermarket refrigeration systems to Shahid Kaveh, a cyber-offensive unit tied to the IRGC's electronic and cyber center. The intrusions ran overnight October 11-12, when attackers compromised industrial refrigeration controllers and remotely raised defrost setpoints, spoiling perishable stock. French authorities now put the scope at roughly 700 retail locations, including Casino, Leclerc, Coopérative U, Metro, Carrefour, Intermarché, smaller butcher shops, a slaughterhouse, and cooperative food-processing sites, with at least 15 fires and tens of millions of euros in food losses.

CERT-FR published a technical report on the incident, and France's Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs issued a formal condemnation naming the Islamic Republic of Iran. Paris prosecutors opened a judicial investigation into organized cybercrime aggravated by service to a foreign power. The Foreign Minister summoned the Iranian ambassador, called the operation unprecedented in scale and aggressiveness on French soil, and pledged European-level sanctions plus further measures.

Watch items: whether CERT-FR releases fuller technical indicators that defenders can use to harden industrial refrigeration and OT controllers; whether additional European sanctions follow; and whether the same Shahid Kaveh tooling appears against other retail or cold-chain targets. The gap between the initial 250-350 store estimate and the revised ~700 figure suggests the full blast radius took weeks to establish, so further victim disclosures are possible.
