France has formally attributed the October 11 to 12, 2026 cyberattack on industrial refrigeration controllers to Iran, naming Shahid Kaveh, an offensive cyber unit tied to the electronic and cyber center of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. The French Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs said the intrusion remotely raised defrost settings to high temperatures across roughly 700 retail locations, spoiling food and causing at least 15 fires. Paris prosecutors opened a formal investigation into organized cybercrime with the aggravating circumstance of acting in the interests of a foreign power, and Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot said France will seek European Union sanctions while summoning the Iranian ambassador.

A CERT-FR technical report identifies the affected operators: Casino, Leclerc, Cooperative U, Metro, Carrefour and Intermarche, plus smaller butcher shops, a slaughterhouse and cooperative food processing facilities. French authorities put losses in the tens of millions of euros. Earlier reporting had put the count at 250 to 350 supermarkets; the official figure is now about 700 out of roughly 10,000 stores nationally. Tehran denies any involvement.

The operational significance is that this is the first publicly attributed large-scale sabotage attack on French soil, and the mechanism was mundane. The controllers were reachable and were changed to a damaging configuration rather than exploited through a novel flaw. CISA, the FBI and other U.S. agencies have since issued guidance urging operators of commercial cold chain and industrial refrigeration systems to isolate OT networks, remove or secure remote access, patch critical flaws and replace end-of-life devices, citing both the French supermarket attacks and earlier intrusions against French blood banks. CISA acting executive assistant director for cybersecurity Chris Butera said every organization depending on refrigeration should assume it could be a target.

What to watch is whether EU sanctions materialize against named individuals or entities, since an attribution without a listing is mostly rhetorical. Also worth tracking is copycat activity against cold chain operators elsewhere, the same class of internet-exposed controllers with hard-coded or weak credentials, and whether the Paris judicial investigation produces indictments that expose the access method used against the French retailers.
