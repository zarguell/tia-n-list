Attackers compromised refrigeration controllers overnight on October 11 to 12 and pushed defrost cycles until cold rooms reached about 50°C, ruining stock at roughly 700 French retail locations and causing at least 15 fires. France attributed the campaign on November 9 to Shahid Kaveh, a cyber unit tied to Iran's Revolutionary Guards, and Paris prosecutors opened an investigation for organized cybercrime in the interest of a foreign power. CERT-FR named Casino, Leclerc, Coopérative U, Metro, Carrefour and Intermarché among the affected chains, along with butcher shops, a slaughterhouse and food processors.

The exposed equipment was largely legacy Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC hardware, with later alerts adding Copeland and Dixell XWEB consoles. Claroty Team82 disclosed CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451 and CVE-2025-41452 in the AK-SM 800A web interface, including a code-of-the-day authentication bypass and command injection through the SMTP configuration page; CISA advisory ICSA-25-140-03 lists fixes in R4.2 and R4.3.1. Le Monde reported defrost cycles stretched from one hour to eight. An IPinfo scan from October 6 to 7 counted 2,528 internet-facing Danfoss StoreView dashboards in 73 countries, 1,331 of them in France, with 85 percent of French systems carrying known flaws and only 195 fully patched.

A second wave on November 2 hit more than 115 stores in Belgium, the Netherlands, Sweden, Luxembourg, Ireland and Estonia, with some freezers again set near 50°C and no group claiming responsibility. A Dutch Cybercrimeinfo analysis published November 24 restates the warning chain: CERT-FR on October 12, Belgium's CCB a day later and again on November 2, a joint ENISA, European Commission and Europol call on November 4, then CISA and the FBI on November 5. Watch for controller replacement rather than patch-only remediation, and for the unresolved attribution dispute: Iran denies the accusation and US intelligence reportedly assesses Iranian involvement only with low confidence.
