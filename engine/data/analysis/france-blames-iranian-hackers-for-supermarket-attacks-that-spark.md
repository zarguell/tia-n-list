France has linked cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration systems overnight on October 11-12 to Iranian state-backed hackers. The Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs issued a statement on Monday blaming Shahid Kaveh, a cyber-offensive unit affiliated with Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, and Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot repeated the accusation publicly. Hackers compromised industrial refrigeration controllers and changed defrost settings to high temperatures, disrupting food preservation at more than 700 retail locations, an estimated 250 to 350 of them supermarkets. Stores discarded fresh and frozen products, at least 15 fires were sparked, and French authorities put food losses in the tens of millions of euros. Affected names include Casino, Leclerc, Cooperative U, Metro, Carrefour, Intermarche, Monoprix, smaller butcher shops, a slaughterhouse, and cooperative food-processing facilities.

The technical trail points to refrigeration controllers exposed online. France's CERT-FR published a technical report naming Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC series controllers along with Copeland and Dixell xweb consoles, and urged operators to remove internet exposure and apply firmware updates. CISA issued ICS advisory ICSA-25-140-03 covering CVE-2025-41450, an authentication bypass through datetime-based password generation in Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA devices prior to R4.2, with Danfoss fixes in R4.2 and later R4.3.1. Claroty Team82 described a separate authentication bypass using a predictable code-of-the-day mechanism on the Danfoss AK-SM 800A web management interface. Desk reporting cited roughly 2,752 Danfoss systems exposed online, about 1,590 of them in France.

What to watch: confirmation of any intrusions beyond refrigeration controls, progress on firmware patching across the exposed installed base, and whether France's attribution triggers further sanctions or defensive guidance from CERT-FR and CISA. The broad exposure count means unpatched controllers remain a live target.
