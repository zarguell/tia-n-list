France's Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs attributed October cyberattacks on industrial refrigeration systems at roughly 700 retail locations to Shahid Kaveh, a cyber-offensive unit affiliated with the electronic and cyber center of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC). The attacks occurred overnight on October 11-12, when hackers compromised industrial refrigeration controllers and remotely changed defrost settings to high temperatures, affecting Casino, Leclerc, Cooperative U, Metro, Carrefour, Intermarche, plus smaller butcher shops, a slaughterhouse, and cooperative food-processing facilities. French authorities say the attacks caused at least 15 fires and tens of millions of euros in food losses; earlier estimates put affected supermarkets at 250 to 350. Iran denies the accusations.

This is a state-sponsored operation against food supply chain operational technology with physical consequences, including fires and spoiled food. Following the attacks, CISA, the FBI, and other U.S. agencies urged critical infrastructure organizations to secure industrial refrigeration and cold-chain controllers, isolate OT systems, secure or remove remote access, patch critical vulnerabilities, and replace end-of-life devices. CISA Acting Executive Assistant Director for Cybersecurity Chris Butera warned that every organization depending on refrigeration and monitoring systems should assume it could be a target, across healthcare, chemicals, transportation, and food and agriculture sectors.

Watch for further affected sites, the CERT-FR technical report and its attribution evidence, and whether Iran's denial escalates into broader diplomatic or cyber tensions. Cold-chain operators globally should treat remote refrigeration controller access as a priority hardening target.
