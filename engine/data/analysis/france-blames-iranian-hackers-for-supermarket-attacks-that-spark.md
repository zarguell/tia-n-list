French authorities have attributed the October 11-12 cyberattacks on industrial refrigeration systems at roughly 700 retail locations to Shahid Kaveh, a cyber-offensive unit tied to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Attackers compromised refrigeration controllers and raised defrost temperatures, spoiling food and causing at least 15 fires, with losses estimated in the tens of millions of euros. Affected retailers include Casino, Leclerc, Coopérative U, Metro, Carrefour, and Intermarché, plus smaller butchers, a slaughterhouse, and food-processing cooperatives.

The operation is a state-backed attack on civilian food supply rather than a conventional data breach, which raises the stakes for operators of cold-chain and OT environments. France's CERT-FR published a technical report and the Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs condemned the attacks. Separately, a Danfoss ICS advisory (ICSA-25-140-03) documents an authentication bypass in Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA controllers, and U.S. agencies have urged critical infrastructure operators to harden refrigeration controllers against remote tampering.

Defenders should isolate OT refrigeration networks, remove or secure remote access, patch controllers such as the Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA series (fixed in R4.2/R4.3.1), and replace end-of-life devices. The French case shows that temperature manipulation alone can destroy inventory and start fires, so monitoring of controller setpoints and defrost schedules should be treated as a safety control, not just an IT concern.
