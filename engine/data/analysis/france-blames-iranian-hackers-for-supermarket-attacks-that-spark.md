France has attributed the October cyberattacks that disrupted refrigeration at roughly 700 retail locations to Shahid Kaveh, a cyber-offensive unit tied to the electronic and cyber center of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. The attacks ran overnight on October 11-12, 2026, when hackers compromised industrial refrigeration controllers and raised defrost settings to high temperatures, spoiling food and causing at least 15 fires. Affected chains included Casino, Leclerc, Cooperative U, Metro, Carrefour and Intermarche, plus smaller butcher shops, a slaughterhouse and cooperative food-processing facilities. French authorities estimate tens of millions of euros in food losses, with 250 to 350 supermarkets initially reported affected before the total rose to about 700 of the country's 10,000 supermarkets.

The technical root cause is a predictable authentication weakness in Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC series controllers and Copeland and Dixell xweb consoles, per CERT-FR, with Claroty Team82 documenting an authentication bypass via a code-of-the-day mechanism in Danfoss AK-SM 800A web management. CISA advisory ICSA-25-140-03 covers CVE-2025-41450 in Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA (fixed in R4.2, later R4.3.1). Roughly 2,752 Danfoss systems were exposed online, including 1,590 in France. A follow-on US advisory from CISA, FBI and other agencies urged critical infrastructure operators to secure industrial refrigeration after similar targeting of French blood banks.

France summoned the Iranian ambassador, opened a formal judicial investigation into organized cybercrime serving a foreign power, and said it would seek European sanctions. US officials framed the case as a warning that any organization depending on refrigeration should assume it could be a target.
