France has attributed the October 11-12 cyberattack on supermarket refrigeration to Shahid Kaveh, a cyber-offensive unit tied to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC). Attackers compromised industrial refrigeration controllers at roughly 700 retail locations, remotely raising defrost settings and spoiling food; the incident caused at least 15 fires and tens of millions of euros in losses across Casino, Leclerc, Cooperative U, Metro, Carrefour, Intermarche and smaller food sites.

Paris prosecutors opened a formal investigation into organized cybercrime committed in the interest of a foreign power, and France summoned the Iranian ambassador while pressing for European sanctions against those responsible. Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot called it the first large-scale sabotage cyberattack on French soil. Separately, CISA, the FBI and other US agencies warned critical infrastructure operators to harden industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems after the French supermarket and blood-bank incidents.

What to watch: whether the EU sanctions package materializes and how Iran responds; whether Shahid Kaveh shifts to other European cold-chain or OT targets; and whether operators patch or replace end-of-life refrigeration controllers and isolate remote access. The technical detail (controller manipulation rather than data theft) shows the actor's intent is physical disruption, so food and pharmaceutical cold chains remain the priority.
