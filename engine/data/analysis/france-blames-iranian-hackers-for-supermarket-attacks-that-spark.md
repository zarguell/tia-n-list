France has linked the October 11-12 cyberattacks that disrupted refrigeration at roughly 700 retail locations to Shahid Kaveh, a cyber-offensive unit affiliated with the electronic and cyber center of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Attackers compromised industrial refrigeration controllers and remotely raised defrost settings, spoiling food and causing at least 15 fires, with tens of millions of euros in losses. Affected sites included Casino, Leclerc, Cooperative U, Metro, Carrefour, Intermarche, Monoprix, Super U, plus smaller butcher shops, a slaughterhouse, and cooperative food-processing facilities. The same campaign extended to French blood banks, prompting U.S. agencies to urge critical infrastructure operators to harden cold-chain systems.

The technical picture centers on Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC refrigeration controllers and Copeland and Dixell xweb consoles. Claroty Team82 disclosed multiple flaws in the Danfoss AK-SM 800A web management interface, including an authentication bypass via a predictable code-of-the-day mechanism, and CISA issued ICS advisory ICSA-25-140-03 for CVE-2025-41450, an authentication bypass via datetime-based password generation in Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA versions before R4.2. Danfoss released fixes in R4.2 and later R4.3.1, and CERT-FR urged operators to remove internet exposure and apply firmware updates. Roughly 2,752 Danfoss systems were exposed online, including 1,590 in France.

What to watch is the ongoing global targeting of internet-exposed refrigeration and cold-chain controllers, where attackers can manipulate temperatures to spoil food, blood, and medicines. France's attribution to Iran has drawn some public skepticism, and the advisory coverage from CISA, the FBI, and other agencies signals that operators across healthcare, chemicals, transportation, and food and agriculture sectors should assume they are targets. Patching, removing remote access, and monitoring for anomalous temperature changes remain the immediate priorities.
