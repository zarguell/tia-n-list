On the night of October 11-12, attackers compromised industrial refrigeration controllers at roughly 700 French retail locations, remotely raising defrost temperatures and spoiling food while causing at least 15 fires. French authorities attribute the operation to Shahid Kaveh, a cyber-offensive unit tied to the electronic and cyber center of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Affected chains include Casino, Leclerc, Coopérative U, Metro, Carrefour, and Intermarché, plus smaller butcher shops, a slaughterhouse, and cooperative food-processing facilities, with losses described as tens of millions of euros.

The incident is notable because it bridges IT intrusion and physical damage: the target was operational technology in food retail rather than data, and the outcome included fires and mass food discard. That raises the bar for OT monitoring in supermarkets and for how defenders assess cyber risk when controllers can affect physical safety and inventory.

Watch items include the CERT-FR technical report for controller-level indicators, remediation guidance for the affected refrigeration systems, whether similar targeting spreads to other European retailers, and any further diplomatic or law-enforcement steps tied to the IRGC attribution.
