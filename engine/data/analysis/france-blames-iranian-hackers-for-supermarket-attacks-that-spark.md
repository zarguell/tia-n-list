On the night of October 11-12, 2026, attackers compromised internet-exposed industrial refrigeration controllers at roughly 700 French retail locations, remotely raising defrost settings to high temperatures and spoiling food while causing at least 15 fires. France's Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs attributed the operation to Shahid Kaveh, a cyber-offensive unit tied to the electronic and cyber center of Iran's IRGC, citing shared infrastructure with Schneider Electric reconnaissance, use of the FOFA search platform, Farsi-language traces, and promotion on the CyberbanNews outlet on October 13, 2026. The affected estate spans Casino, Leclerc, Cooperative U, Metro, Carrefour, and Intermarche plus smaller butcher shops, a slaughterhouse, and cooperative food-processing facilities, with losses described as tens of millions of euros. France attributed only the October operation; a later November wave affecting more than 115 stores across six European countries was not assigned to Iran.

The exposure is not a zero-day problem: CVE-2025-41450 and related flaws were published by CISA in May 2025 and detailed by Claroty on August 9, 2026, with Danfoss shipping fixes in R4.2 and R4.3.1. The French sites that burned were running end-of-life or unpatched controllers that remained reachable from the internet, and Claroty had already counted 2,765 publicly reachable Danfoss systems. That makes this a patch-and-exposure failure as much as an intrusion, and it implicates every retailer and cold-chain operator still publishing controller consoles, including the French national blood service sites where temperature and defrost settings were altered at three facilities.

Defenders should inventory every Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC controller, Copeland xweb, and Dixell xweb unit, remove or tightly filter internet exposure, and apply Danfoss firmware R4.2 for CVE-2025-41450 and R4.3.1 for CVE-2025-41451 and CVE-2025-41452, changing default Supervisor and 12345 credentials and enforcing strict security mode. CERT-FR alert CERTFR-2026-ALE-012 (October 12-16, 2026) is the operative reference, and hunts should focus on defrost-setting changes, unexpected controller reboots, new admin accounts, and FOFA, Censys, or Shodan-discoverable management interfaces, plus Schneider Electric reconnaissance in the same window. The FBI and CISA warning on ongoing global attacks against industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems means the same playbook is still in use.

Watch for whether the November European wave and the blood-service incident get attributed to the same Shahid Kaveh infrastructure, whether EU sanctions and the summoned Iranian ambassador produce any change in targeting, and whether additional controllers appear in Shodan counts after the publicity. The assessment changes if investigators tie the later waves to Iran or if a second, distinct actor begins exploiting the same exposed Danfoss estate.
