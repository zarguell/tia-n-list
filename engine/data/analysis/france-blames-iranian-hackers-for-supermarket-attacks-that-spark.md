France attributes the October 11 to 12, 2026 attacks on supermarket refrigeration systems to Shahid Kaveh, a cyber-offensive unit linked to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. CERT-FR counts roughly 700 affected retail locations, with at least 15 fires and tens of millions of euros in food losses across chains including Casino, Leclerc, Cooperativa U, Metro, Carrefour and Intermarche. Attackers remotely hijacked industrial refrigeration controllers and changed defrost settings, pushing some units to about 50 degrees Celsius and forcing stores to discard fresh and frozen stock.

US intelligence has a weaker view of the attribution. A November 18 report cited by Bruce Schneier says three anonymous US officials described a low-confidence assessment that Iran was responsible, pointing to Iran generally rather than a specific group such as the Revolutionary Guards. The officials said Iran was also probably behind similar attacks elsewhere in Europe, with those investigations still at an early stage. Belgium has disputed the French finding, and Iranian officials deny involvement.

The incident shows retail operational technology being used for destructive effect rather than espionage or ransom. Danfoss identified affected legacy controllers including AK-SM800 and AK-SC355, many no longer receiving security updates, and IPinfo scans found 1,331 exposed Danfoss StoreView dashboards in France days before the attack. Follow-on reporting documents tampering in Belgium, Sweden, Estonia and Portugal. Watch for confirmation that the European cases share tooling and access paths, and for asset owners retiring or segmenting the exposed controllers.
