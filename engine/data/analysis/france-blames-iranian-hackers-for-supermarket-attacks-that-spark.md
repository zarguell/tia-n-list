France attributes the October 11-12, 2026 attacks on supermarket refrigeration systems to Shahid Kaveh, a cyber-offensive unit linked to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. CERT-FR counts roughly 700 affected retail locations, with at least 15 fires and tens of millions of euros in food losses across chains including Casino, Leclerc, Coopérative U, Metro, Carrefour, and Intermarché. Attackers remotely hijacked Danfoss refrigeration controllers, extending defrost cycles and pushing some units to about 50°C, which forced stores to discard fresh and frozen stock.

The incident shows operational technology in the food supply chain being used as a destructive weapon rather than for espionage or ransom. Danfoss identified affected legacy systems such as AK-SM800 and AK-SC355, many of which no longer receive security updates, and IPinfo scans found 1,331 exposed Danfoss StoreView dashboards in France days before the attack. Follow-on reporting documents similar refrigeration tampering in Belgium, Sweden, Estonia, and Portugal, plus attacks on blood bank refrigeration, prompting CISA and other U.S. agencies to urge critical infrastructure operators to harden cold-chain systems.

What to watch is whether the European follow-on incidents share the same tooling and access paths, and whether asset owners retire or segment the legacy Danfoss controllers. Danfoss has long advised moving away from obsolete models, so the practical fix is asset inventory, patching where supported, and removing remote access to controllers. The attribution to an IRGC unit also raises the prospect of further state-linked disruption against food and healthcare cold chains.
