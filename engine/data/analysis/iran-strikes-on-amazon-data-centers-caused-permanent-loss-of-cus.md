Ars Technica reported that Iranian military strikes on Amazon data centers caused permanent loss of customer data. The story entered the store on September 16, 2026 and resurfaced on October 2, so it has staying power rather than being a one-cycle item.

This matters because it is a physical-threat outcome most cloud risk models treat as negligible: kinetic damage producing irreversible data loss, not just downtime. It raises hard questions about geographic redundancy, durability guarantees, and whether customers in conflict-exposed regions were adequately protected by replication across sites.

Worth watching: whether Amazon details which regions or facilities were hit, how many customers lost data permanently, and whether the industry revisits multi-region durability assumptions for infrastructure inside countries adjacent to active conflicts.
