Japanese police detained a 28-year-old Russian national in Osaka, believed to be a core Qilin ransomware member, and extradited him to Germany on October 2. The suspect was wanted for a September 2024 ransomware incident at a German logistics company that caused over $160,000 in cryptocurrency extortion. Qilin, also known as Agenda, has operated since August 2022 as a ransomware-as-a-service group, claiming responsibility for attacks on Nissan, Asahi, Lee Enterprises, and Synnovis, and listing more than 400 victims in 2025. Recent statistics cite over 2,350 organizations across 62 countries. The group was also exploiting Check Point CVE-2026-50751 in June 2026. The extradition is rare and reflects coordinated action between Japan, Germany, and international investigators. Watch for further indictments, personnel changes within Qilin, and confirmation of the suspect's role in programming the group's ransomware payloads.
