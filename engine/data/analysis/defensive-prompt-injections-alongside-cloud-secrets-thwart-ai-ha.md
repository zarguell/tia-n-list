Tracebit researchers reported that placing prompt injections alongside passwords, cryptographic keys, and other secrets stored in Amazon Web Services was often sufficient to stop AI hacking agents. The injected prompts direct an attacking LLM to perform an action forbidden by its guardrails, and the model responds by shutting down the operation. Examples include a prompt ordering the LLM to produce steps for developing inhalable anthrax spores, or, for LLMs from Chinese developers, instructions to reference the Tank Man image from the 1989 Tiananmen Square massacre.

The technique reframes prompt injection from an attack to a defense: secrets become honeytokens that trip AI agents at scale, with no human interaction required. It is early-stage and the researchers themselves note it is not a complete solution, but it is a cheap, deployable layer against credential-stealing agents. Watch for follow-on work on placement strategies and bypasses, since agents that filter instructions before acting would defeat the approach.
