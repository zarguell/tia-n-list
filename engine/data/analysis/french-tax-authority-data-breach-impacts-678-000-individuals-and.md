France's Directorate General of Public Finances (DGFiP) disclosed a data breach affecting roughly 678,000 individuals and businesses after attackers accessed internal systems using impersonated credentials. The intrusions occurred in June and July 2026, and a threat actor using the handle "ZeroBytes" claimed responsibility on August 12, listing a stolen database for sale on the PwnForums hacking forum.

The attackers compromised accounts by impersonating both a DGFiP employee and an authorized third party. Data extracted included reference tax income, family quotient, withholding tax rates for individuals, and company turnover figures for businesses. DGFiP said it immediately disabled all associated accounts and launched an investigation. The French Ministry of Economy and Finance issued a formal press release on August 14 confirming the scope.

The breach is significant for its breadth of financial data exposed across nearly 700,000 taxpayers and businesses. Credential impersonation of this kind, targeting both internal staff and third-party access, suggests the attacker had prior knowledge of DGFiP's access architecture. Watch for follow-on phishing campaigns leveraging the stolen tax data, and whether French authorities identify the intrusion vector as a compromised vendor or direct credential theft.
