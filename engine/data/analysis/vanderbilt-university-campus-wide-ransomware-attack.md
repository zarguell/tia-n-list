Vanderbilt University confirmed it is responding to an active cybersecurity incident claimed by the BYOD ransomware gang, which claims to have encrypted the university's databases and stolen sensitive student, employee, and research data. The university warned that university devices, systems, and cloud-based services may be affected, and instructed students, faculty, and staff to shut down university devices, disconnect from university Wi-Fi, and watch for suspicious activity such as unexpected pop-ups, unusual changes to files or permissions in cloud storage, missing or modified files, unfamiliar applications, and unexpected authentication requests. The incident began spreading Monday morning through computers, files, and digital infrastructure, and follows similar hacks at universities across the country (reported November 10, 2026).

The attack appears to be double extortion: encryption of databases plus theft of sensitive data, with the gang claiming responsibility. The university advised those who notice suspicious activity not to investigate or troubleshoot themselves, but to shut down and report. The campus-wide device and Wi-Fi shutdown signals significant operational disruption across teaching, research, and administration.

Watch for confirmation of the data exfiltration scope, whether the BYOD gang publishes stolen data, the recovery timeline, and any disclosure of the initial access vector. Higher-education institutions should review network segmentation and incident response plans in light of repeated university-targeting ransomware campaigns.
