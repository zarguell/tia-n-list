A ransomware attack discovered Monday at Vanderbilt University in Nashville, Tennessee prompted the university to order students, faculty, and staff to shut down university devices and disconnect from campus Wi-Fi as malicious software spread through computers, files, and digital infrastructure. The BYOD ransomware group claimed responsibility via its leak blog, asserting it encrypted the university's databases and stole sensitive student, employee, and research data. As of Tuesday morning the university reported that select campus-wide systems including WiFi, Okta, Outlook, Brightspace, Zoom, and YES were safe to use again, while CampusPrint and other services remained restricted; device use was permitted to resume. Vanderbilt Health, a legally and financially separate entity, was not affected.

BYOD emerged in September, with its first known victim appearing September 29 according to ransomware expert Allan Liska, and has since targeted academic and health care institutions, including the University of New Mexico Health Sciences Center last month where it caused system outages, disrupted patient care, and impacted academics. The group claims to hold more than one million Social Security numbers from the Vanderbilt incident, along with full names, email addresses, phone numbers, student and employee records, and domain controller access, against a community of roughly 14,000 students and more than 6,000 staff. The leak-site post and an audio message played from a compromised Vanderbilt machine are consistent with double-extortion tactics.

Vanderbilt has not publicly confirmed whether data was stolen or systems were encrypted, and says its IT team is working with government agencies and other partners. The claims rest on the attacker's leak-site post and secondary reporting from outlets including BleepingComputer, The Record, and the Nashville Banner. Watch for a formal breach notification, confirmation of the scope of exposed records, whether the university engages with the group, and further targeting of academic or health care institutions by BYOD. Recovery details and a timeline for restoring CampusPrint and other restricted services remain pending.
