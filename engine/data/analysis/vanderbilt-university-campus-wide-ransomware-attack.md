Vanderbilt University in Nashville is responding to a campus-wide ransomware incident first reported Monday, November 9, 2026. The BYOD ransomware gang claims it encrypted the university's databases and stole sensitive student, employee, and research data. Vanderbilt confirmed the incident in a statement on X, warning that university devices, systems, and cloud-based services may be affected, and said its IT team is investigating and working to contain the situation. The university has not disclosed the nature of the attack or confirmed whether data was actually stolen, and BleepingComputer's questions about encryption and scope remain unanswered.

As part of containment, Vanderbilt told students, faculty, and staff to shut down university devices, disconnect from campus Wi-Fi, and monitor for suspicious activity. Indicators of compromise listed by the university include unexpected pop-ups or security notifications, unusual changes to file permissions in cloud storage platforms, missing or modified files, unfamiliar applications, and unexpected authentication requests. Affected users were instructed not to investigate or troubleshoot on their own but to power off their devices and contact the IT service desk.

With four outlets (Vanderbilt Hustler, Dysruption Hub, BleepingComputer, and the Washington Post) now covering the incident and no confirmation of encryption or data exfiltration from the university, the picture remains incomplete. Watch for a formal incident report, any ransom demand or data leak posting by the BYOD gang, and follow-on disclosures affecting research data or student records.
