Vanderbilt University is responding to a campus-wide ransomware attack claimed by the BYOD gang, which listed the university on its leak site and claims to have encrypted databases and stolen student, employee and research data. The university confirmed an active cybersecurity incident on November 9 and told students, faculty and staff to shut down university devices, disconnect from campus Wi-Fi, and watch for signs of compromise such as unexpected pop-ups, changed file permissions, missing files and unfamiliar applications. A broad announcement went out Monday morning as malicious software spread through computers, files and digital infrastructure, and the incident follows similar hacks at universities across the country.

The BYOD leak-site message claims more than one million SSNs along with full names, email addresses, phone numbers, student and employee records, and domain controller access. Vanderbilt serves roughly 13,500 to 14,000 students and more than 6,000 staff. VUIT staff went into classrooms Monday morning to warn faculty that school-issued computers were not safe to use, and the AlertVU campus warning went out at 11:45 a.m. The university hospital was not affected, as Vanderbilt University and Vanderbilt Health are legally and financially separate entities. Vanderbilt has not publicly confirmed whether systems were encrypted or whether any data was actually stolen.

What to watch: whether Vanderbilt confirms encryption and the scope of the data theft, the recovery timeline for devices, systems and cloud services, and whether the claimed SSNs and research data surface publicly. Ransomware expert Allan Liska noted that BYOD's first known victim appeared on September 29, 2026, so the gang's activity and any follow-on targeting of other higher-education institutions bear watching.
