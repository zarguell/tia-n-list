Vanderbilt University in Nashville confirmed an active ransomware attack on Monday that compromised large volumes of student and employee personal data and forced several campus systems offline. The university told students, faculty, and staff to shut down university devices, disconnect from campus Wi-Fi, and watch for suspicious activity, with warning signs including unexpected pop-ups, unusual file or permission changes in cloud storage, and missing or modified files. By Tuesday morning some services were restored, including campus Wi-Fi, Okta, Outlook, Brightspace, Zoom, and YES, while others such as CampusPrint stayed restricted.

The BYOD ransomware group, which emerged in September, claimed the attack on its leak blog and says it encrypted Vanderbilt's databases and stole sensitive student, employee, and research data. BYOD claims to hold more than one million Social Security numbers from the breach, and posted a YouTube video in which a Vanderbilt computer plays an audio message. The group has previously targeted academic and healthcare institutions, including the University of New Mexico Health Sciences Center last month, where it caused outages that disrupted patient care.

A claimed million-plus SSNs from a single university makes this a major identity-theft exposure, not just an availability incident, and the plaintext-ish ransom proof BYOD released raises the odds the data is real. Watch for a Vanderbilt data-breach notification to affected individuals and regulators, for confirmation of how the intruders entered, and for whether BYOD publishes more samples or negotiates publicly. The restoration of Okta and Outlook while other systems stay down suggests the response is still active, so track further outages.
