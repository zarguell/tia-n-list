Vanderbilt University in Nashville is responding to a campus-wide ransomware incident claimed by the BYOD ransomware gang, which added the university to its leak site and said it encrypted university databases and stole sensitive student, employee, and research data. The university confirmed the attack Monday and told students, faculty, and staff to shut down university devices, disconnect from campus Wi-Fi, and watch for signs of compromise such as unexpected pop-ups, changed cloud-storage permissions, missing files, unfamiliar applications, and unexpected authentication requests. The Vanderbilt University Medical Center was not affected, because the university and Vanderbilt Health are legally and financially separate entities.

BYOD's leak-site message claimed it held more than 1 million Social Security numbers, full names, email addresses, phone numbers, student and employee records, and domain controller access, though Vanderbilt has not confirmed any data theft. The gang's first known victim appeared on September 29, per ransomware expert Allan Liska, making this a relatively new operation; the university serves roughly 14,000 students and more than 6,000 staff. VUIT staff went into classrooms Monday morning to warn faculty that school-issued computers were not safe to use, and the AlertVU campus warning went out at 11:45 a.m.

For defenders, the immediate priorities are device isolation, credential rotation, and checking for BYOD's known web-shell and lateral-movement artifacts on any exposed systems. Watch for Vanderbilt's eventual disclosure on whether data was exfiltrated, and for whether BYOD follows through with publication; the gap between claimed and confirmed theft is the key open question. Education-sector incident responders should treat the gang's domain-controller-access claim as a worst-case scenario until the university rules it out.
