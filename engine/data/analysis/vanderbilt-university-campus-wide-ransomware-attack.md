Vanderbilt University in Nashville confirmed an active cybersecurity incident on Monday, November 9, after the BYOD ransomware group listed the university on its leak site. The group claimed the databases were locked and that it held more than 1 million Social Security numbers alongside names, email addresses, phone numbers, student and employee records, and domain controller access. Vanderbilt serves roughly 14,000 students and more than 6,000 staff.

The university told students, faculty, and staff to shut down university devices and disconnect from campus Wi-Fi while it investigated. VUIT staff went into classrooms Monday morning to warn faculty that school-issued computers were not safe to use, and the AlertVU campus warning went out at 11:45 a.m. The Nashville Banner reported that Vanderbilt University and Vanderbilt Health are legally and financially separate, so the hospital was not affected.

Vanderbilt has not publicly confirmed whether data was stolen or systems were encrypted. Ransomware expert Allan Liska noted that BYOD's first victim appeared on September 29, which makes the Vanderbilt listing consistent with a short, active campaign rather than an isolated claim. Watch for a university confirmation of exfiltration and for BYOD to name additional victims from the same period.
