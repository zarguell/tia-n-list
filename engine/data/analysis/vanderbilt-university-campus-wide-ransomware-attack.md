Vanderbilt University confirmed it is responding to a campus-wide cyberattack claimed by the BYOD ransomware gang, which added the university to its leak site and claims to have encrypted databases and stolen sensitive student, employee, and research data. The university warned that devices, systems, and cloud-based services may be affected and told students, faculty, and staff to shut down university devices, disconnect from university Wi-Fi, and watch for suspicious activity such as unexpected pop-ups, changed file permissions, missing files, unfamiliar applications, or unexpected authentication requests. The university hospital was not affected, as Vanderbilt University and Vanderbilt Health are legally and financially separate entities.

The BYOD gang's leak-site message claims to hold over one million SSNs along with full names, email addresses, phone numbers, student and employee records, and domain controller access. Vanderbilt serves roughly 13,500 to 14,000 students and more than 6,000 staff, and ransomware expert Allan Liska noted BYOD's first known victim appeared on September 29, 2026. VUIT staff went into classrooms Monday morning to warn faculty that school-issued computers were not safe to use, and the AlertVU campus warning went out at 11:45 a.m. Vanderbilt has not publicly confirmed whether systems were encrypted or whether any data was actually stolen.

What to watch: whether Vanderbilt confirms encryption and the scope of data theft, the recovery timeline for affected systems and cloud services, and whether the claimed SSNs and research data surface publicly. The incident also bears watching for follow-on targeting of other higher-education institutions, given BYOD's recent activity.
