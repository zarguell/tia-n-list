Cisco's Firewall Management Center (FMC) zero-day, CVE-2026-20079, has been actively exploited by at least three threat clusters, including Sandworm and Qilin. The vulnerability allows unauthenticated remote attackers to execute arbitrary commands as root on affected FMC appliances. Cisco confirmed exploitation on September 12 and added the CVE to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog.

The flaw exists in the management plane's handling of external database access features. Attackers can reach the vulnerable endpoint without authentication, making internet-facing FMC instances immediate targets. The management interface, by design, carries deep trust within the network: it holds credentials for managed firewalls, can push policy changes, and has visibility into traffic logs. Compromising FMC is effectively compromising the entire firewall estate it manages.

Cisco patched the issue in Secure FMC Software releases 7.1.0.2, 7.2.3.1, and 7.4.2. Organizations running older, end-of-life versions have no upstream fix and should isolate FMC from untrusted networks immediately. The broader lesson is that administrative interfaces for security infrastructure are high-value targets. Exposing them to the internet, even behind VPNs, creates a single point of failure that threat actors are actively chaining with other CVEs in the same advisory batch, including CVE-2026-48710 and CVE-2026-49869. Patch or contain; there is no middle ground.
