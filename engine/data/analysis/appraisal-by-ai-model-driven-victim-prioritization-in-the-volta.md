VOLTA is a mature infostealer identified by Cisco Talos that integrates an AI layer into its post-theft workflow. After harvesting browser passwords, cookies, payment cards, cryptocurrency wallet seed phrases, Discord, Telegram, Steam, and Roblox sessions, Microsoft OAuth tokens, and SSH/cloud credentials, the malware sends a structured evidence bundle to an LLM to rank victims by monetization value. If the model refuses the assessment, VOLTA falls back to a three-model chain, indicating direct engineering against guardrails.

The malware is self-deleting with no attacker-owned infrastructure, distinguishing it from traditional stealer operations that rely on persistent C2. The AI integration functions as an intelligence analyst rather than a supporting capability, representing a new idea in cybercrime: cast a wide net, steal everything, and let AI sort the specifics. The story includes events from malware.news and madrobot.blog and carries a score of 5.0.

What to watch: broader adoption of LLM-augmented victim triage across other stealer families, defensive responses from AI providers, and any detection signatures for VOLTA-AUTODOXER. Organizations should prioritize credential rotation and session revocation given the broad harvest scope.
