VOLTA-AUTODOXER is a self-deleting Windows infostealer that sends stolen evidence bundles to an LLM to rank victims by monetization value. After harvesting browser passwords, cookies, payment cards, cryptocurrency seed phrases, Discord, Telegram, Steam, and Roblox sessions, Microsoft OAuth tokens, SSH and cloud keys, Wi-Fi profiles, and screenshots, the malware builds a structured profile with geolocation, employer, inferred birth year, and card details. If the LLM refuses the assessment, VOLTA falls back to a three-model chain, indicating the operators engineered around model guardrails rather than treating AI as an optional accessory.

The significance is structural: the AI layer does not support a traditional malware capability but acts as an intelligence analyst for the operator. The malware is self-deleting with no attacker-owned infrastructure, which reduces forensic footprint and shifts risk to rapid detection. Because VOLTA casts a wide net and lets AI sort victims, defenders should treat infostealer alerts as potential precursors to targeted fraud or account takeover rather than generic commodity infections.

Concrete artifacts from the events include the malware name VOLTA-AUTODOXER, the CAIRN research disclosure, the three-model fallback chain, the full list of harvested session types (Discord, Telegram, Steam, Roblox, Microsoft OAuth, SSH, cloud keys), and the absence of persistent attacker infrastructure due to self-deletion.
