Karen Vardanyan, a 35-year-old Armenian national, has been sentenced to 24 months in federal prison followed by 3 years of supervised release for his role in Ryuk ransomware attacks between March 2019 and June 2020. The Justice Department charged him in February 2024 with conspiracy, fraud, and extortion; he was arrested in Ukraine in April 2025, extradited in June 2025, and pleaded guilty to conspiracy and computer fraud in July 2026. Authorities describe him as likely a ransomware affiliate or initial access provider rather than a core developer or infrastructure operator. He extorted more than $1 million from victims and has been ordered to pay over $1.2 million in restitution.

Time served in pretrial detention is credited, meaning he has already served a substantial portion of the 24-month term. This sentencing adds to a growing list of Ryuk-related convictions, including a Ukrainian Conti developer sentenced to 4 years recently. The case highlights continued international cooperation on ransomware prosecutions. Watch for further DOJ announcements on Ryuk affiliates and any links to current ransomware groups reusing the same access broker networks.
