Anthropic published a 154-page threat intelligence report documenting how cybercriminals and state-sponsored actors used its Claude models for offensive operations between December 2025 and August 2026. The company coined the term "Generative Threat Groups" (GTGs) to describe these clusters. The most significant finding is GTG-20006, a Russian state-sponsored actor with tactical overlaps to Midnight Blizzard (APT29/Cozy Bear), which built an AI-assisted workflow for intrusion operations. GTG-10007, a Chinese-speaking group likely from Hunan province with members identified as undergraduate students, used Claude for intrusion attempts against production systems, reconnaissance of government networks across the Middle East, Europe, and Southeast Asia, and autonomous vulnerability research producing working exploits for unknown flaws in network and security appliances. This group targeted roughly 50 organizations across education, retail, energy, technology, healthcare, finance, manufacturing, and government sectors.

The report details a spectrum of misuse: from conversational use (malware engineering assistant) to semi-autonomous operations where Claude executed commands against victim networks with human oversight, to fully autonomous multi-agent frameworks conducting reconnaissance, exploitation, and data exfiltration against multiple victims in parallel for hours or days. GTG-50014 (aka MeowSHA, linked to ShinyHunters) ran a distributed credential-harvesting pipeline across 10 AWS EC2 workers that mass-downloaded 1.8 million Android APKs, scanned them for hardcoded secrets using TruffleHog, and pushed findings to Telegram. GTG-50020 targeted approximately 30 AI vendors in a four-day window, stealing model provider API keys and attempting to access pre-release models.

Beyond offensive operations, Anthropic identified influence operations including GTG-54002, a commercial "influence-as-a-service" operation by LKM Company (a France-based digital advertising agency) that used Claude to mass-produce political content across 70 fabricated news websites, and GTG-54009, a commercial surveillance platform linked to an Israeli-Singaporean vendor named S2T Unlocking Cyberspace that used Claude to profile social media users in Iran and the Persian Gulf. The company also disrupted attempts by Yemen-based actors to develop guided weapons and a Russia-based operation to engineer autonomous FPV drone swarms. Organizations using Claude API integrations should tighten access controls and monitor Anthropic's ongoing threat advisories.
