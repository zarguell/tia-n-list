Microsoft researchers documented a high-volume phishing campaign that repurposed ASCII smuggling, a technique originally studied in the context of AI prompt injection, for traditional email filter evasion. The attacker used invisible Unicode tag characters (U+E0000 to U+E007F) to split financial lure words like "funding" so that email security parsers could not recognize them, while the words appeared normal to human readers.

Microsoft Defender for Office 365 telemetry showed a sharp increase in detections starting February 9, 2026, with elevated activity sustained on weekdays for approximately three months. The majority of messages were caught by layered protections rather than a single Unicode-specific detection signal, suggesting that the technique's effectiveness depends on the target's mail security stack maturity.

This marks a crossover from AI-specific attack research into commodity phishing tooling. The technique does not require access to AI systems, making it broadly applicable against any organization relying on keyword or pattern-based email filtering. Security teams should audit their mail filtering rules for Unicode normalization gaps and verify that their detection stack handles invisible characters correctly.
