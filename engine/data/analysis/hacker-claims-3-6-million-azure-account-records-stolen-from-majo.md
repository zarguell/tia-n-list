A threat actor using the alias "TheHatman" has been advertising employee databases on underground forums since July 31, 2026, claiming they were exfiltrated directly from Microsoft Azure tenants using compromised credentials. The largest dump is allegedly 1.7 million McDonald's employee records, including names, employee IDs, email addresses, job titles, phone numbers, postal addresses, and service accounts. Other targets include Gap Inc., Vodafone, Tata Consultancy Services, HCL Technologies, InterContinental Hotels, and Kyndryl, totaling an estimated 3.64 million records.

Tata Consultancy Services pushed back, telling India's National Stock Exchange it found no "credible evidence of a breach of TCS systems or customer environments" after investigation. The pattern of claims across multiple Fortune 500 Azure tenants suggests either a shared compromised identity provider, a supply-chain compromise in Azure management tooling, or a credential-stuffing campaign targeting Azure administrative accounts. No CVE or specific vulnerability is cited.

What to watch: The credential-based access vector means this may not involve any software vulnerability at all. Organizations using Azure should audit administrative credential exposure, review sign-in logs for anomalous activity from the July 31 timeframe, and verify whether their Azure AD/Entra ID tenants appear in the claimed dumps. The Tata denial suggests some claims may be inflated, but the volume and specificity of the McDonald's dump warrants investigation.
