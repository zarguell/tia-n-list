Arara Message is a bulk email delivery platform operated by arara inc., a subsidiary of PayCloud Holdings. The compromise began with reconnaissance on October 27, 2026 and unauthorized access on October 28, with attackers pivoting across 97 servers and exfiltrating database contents. PayCloud's disclosures attribute the initial foothold to a web server vulnerability plus broader system-environment issues, and the wider PHP2Shell pair CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 (unauthenticated RCE in PHP, fixed in releases 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35 on October 21) is the runtime bug behind the intrusion. The exposed set reached roughly 77 million email addresses across about 380 client companies, plus 2,196,044 SEGA newsletter addresses and 92,273 records with delivery metadata.

The supply-chain dimension is the real story. Every downstream brand trusted Arara with its subscriber list, so a single vendor breach cascaded into exposure for roughly 380 companies without any of them controlling the platform's security posture. The attackers then weaponized that trust directly: about 7.13 million phishing emails impersonating a credit card company were relayed through Arara's own infrastructure between October 28 and October 30, using stolen addresses as both targets and delivery channel. That volume is why this story matters more than the routine breach of the day. A defender skimming events sees 77 million records; the operational reality is that recipients were primed to expect a security notice and the attackers arrived first.

Defenders should treat this as both a vendor-risk and a runtime-patching incident. Patch PHP to the fixed releases above, or apply vendor mitigations where a direct upgrade is not possible, and verify no PHP-FPM workers are spawning shells or serving webshells. For Arara customers, rotate all API keys and authentication tokens issued to the platform, review management-console audit logs for the October 27 to October 31 window, and confirm with Arara in writing which of your records were in the exfiltrated set. Expect and pre-brief users on phishing that names Arara customers and references credit-card statements or delivery notices, and route unexpected mail through out-of-band channels rather than links.

The assessment could change quickly. PayCloud's third report on November 6 raised the deduplicated count to 81,329,467, so the final scope is still moving. Watch for confirmation of whether PHP2Shell was the actual initial vector, since Arara's disclosures only cite a web server vulnerability and system-environment flaws. Additional downstream brands publishing breach notices, confirmed misuse of the leaked address sets, and any enforcement action by Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission would all push the severity up.
