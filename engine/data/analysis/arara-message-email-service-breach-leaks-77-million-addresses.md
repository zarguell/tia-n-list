Arara Message, a Japanese bulk email distribution service owned by PayCloud Holdings, suffered a breach that exposed roughly 77 million email addresses across about 380 client companies and 97 servers. PayCloud first disclosed about 3.01 million addresses taken from one shared system, attributed to a web server vulnerability with reconnaissance beginning October 27 and unauthorized logins on October 28, then revised the impact to about 77 million after the intrusion spread to additional servers. Between October 28 and 30, attackers sent about 7.13 million phishing emails impersonating a credit card company from compromised Arara infrastructure. Arara stores no credit card numbers and invalidated stolen API keys and administrative passwords; the service was suspended on October 31.

The downstream impact is already visible: SEGA confirmed on November 9 that 2,196,044 customer email addresses, including 92,273 with delivery-linked information, were stolen through Arara, its newsletter provider, affecting 11 newsletters for SEGA and SEGA Fave products. SEGA says no names, dates of birth, addresses, phone numbers, credit card details, or passwords were exposed, and SEGA Account emails handled by a separate system were unaffected. SEGA was assured on October 29 that no data had been compromised, then learned the full extent on November 3. The web server vulnerability cited by Arara may connect to PHP2Shell, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, two critical PHP remote code execution flaws added to the CISA KEV catalog on October 26, though Arara attributes the incident to a web server vulnerability and system environment problems and says the exact intrusion method remains under investigation.

What to watch: further disclosures from the roughly 380 affected clients beyond SEGA, since the full client list is not public. If the PHP2Shell link is confirmed, this becomes a supply-chain exploitation of a known KEV flaw, and 15 of 55 tracked vendors still had not fully patched all affected product lines at last count. Also watch for follow-on phishing leveraging the 77 million addresses and whether Arara's October 27 to 28 reconnaissance timeline holds up under investigation.
