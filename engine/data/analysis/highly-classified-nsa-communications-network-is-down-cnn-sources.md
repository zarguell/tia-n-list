CNN reported on 17 November 2026 that NSANet, the classified internal communications network used by the National Security Agency, was down. Six people familiar with the matter confirmed the outage, and the agency's preliminary statement said there was no evidence of a cyberattack. Workers were told to arrive at staggered times, and sources said backup networks kept urgent intelligence delivery to the president traveling in Asia intact. White House spokesperson Lauren Bis said there was no crisis. The NSA told the Associated Press it had seen temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance and that mission-critical capabilities remained operational.

By the second day the network was still intermittent. Staff at Fort Meade received a Code Gray notification sending non-essential personnel in at 10 a.m. Wednesday, then had that moved to the afternoon. The cafeteria and the campus Starbucks were closed, and a source said the login process was the most taxing part of the system. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said the cause was maintenance affecting PHP; the same source said it was unknown whether the underlying flaw was found by AI or by a person. Officials testifying to the House Homeland Security Committee pointed to routine technical issues behind the recent run of outages, which also included grounded flights, Microsoft service disruption and banking problems.

Two later posts from thecybersecguru claim the disruption lasted nearly four days and that a PHP2Shell security patch was the cause. That site also published the yellow-database joke about the FBI breach, so treat the duration and the specific patch attribution as unverified beyond the earlier reporting that named PHP maintenance. Watch for an NSA after-action statement and for independent confirmation of whether a rushed patch, rather than maintenance itself, took NSANet down.
