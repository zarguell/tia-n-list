CNN reported at 15:31 UTC on November 17 that NSANet, the classified internal communications network used by the National Security Agency, was down. Six people familiar with the matter confirmed the outage. CNN updated the story at 17:59 UTC. Agencies including the Guardian, ABC and Mathrubhumi carried the report; the detail originates with CNN's sources rather than an NSA publication.

The NSA's preliminary statement said there was no evidence the outage was related to a cyberattack, and that remained the published position at the time of the reporting. An Associated Press account published on November 18 quotes the agency calling it "temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance" and saying some internal systems were affected while mission-critical capabilities remained fully operational. The agency ordered staggered arrivals at Fort Meade while technicians assessed and restored the network. Sources said backup networks preserved urgent intelligence delivery to the president traveling in Asia, and White House spokesperson Lauren Bis told Newsmax there was no crisis. Those facts argue against treating this as a confirmed intrusion, despite the timing amid other high-profile outages.

What is established is an availability failure on the highly classified NSA communications network used to share classified material, with a temporary change to personnel movements and reliance on backup paths. The AP account places the outage alongside the air traffic ground stop, the JPMorgan Chase technical issue and the United Airlines system outage, but offers no cause or scope detail for NSANet itself. Watch for a follow-up from the NSA or CNN that attributes the failure, and for any indication that access or traffic was affected rather than service availability alone.
