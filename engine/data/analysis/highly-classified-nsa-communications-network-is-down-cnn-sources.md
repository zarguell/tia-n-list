CNN reported on November 17 that NSANet, the classified internal communications network used by the National Security Agency, was down. Six sources confirmed the outage; at publication there was no evidence of a cyberattack. The agency staggered arrivals at Fort Meade and closed the campus to most employees by evening while backup networks kept urgent intelligence flowing to the president traveling in Asia. The Associated Press later quoted an NSA statement calling it temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance, with mission-critical capabilities fully operational.

Disruption entered a second day on November 18. Non-essential staff received a Code Gray notice directing a 10 a.m. arrival that was pushed to the afternoon, and the cafeteria and on-campus Starbucks stayed closed. A source said the network was working but login was the heaviest load on the system and full restoration could take another day or two. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton attributed the problem to maintenance affecting PHP; whether the underlying flaw was AI-derived or human-driven was not established.

New reporting from heise links the outage to a broader pattern: bank executives and the NSA told Congress that rushed patches for AI-discovered flaws overwhelmed systems behind this week's US outages. That account fits the multi-day login trouble but still conflicts with the routine-maintenance framing, and no root-cause evidence has been published. Watch for an after-action statement, further changes to the restoration estimate, and any reporting that ties this disruption to the air traffic ground stop, JPMorgan Chase and United Airlines incidents named in the coverage.
