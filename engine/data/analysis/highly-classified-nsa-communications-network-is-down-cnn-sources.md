CNN reported at 15:31 UTC on 17 November that NSANet, the classified internal communications network used by the National Security Agency, was down, citing six people familiar with the matter. The agency's preliminary statement said there was no evidence the outage was related to a cyberattack. CNN updated the story at 17:59 UTC. Staff were told to arrive at Fort Meade in staggered groups while technicians assessed the scope, and sources said backup networks kept urgent intelligence delivery to the president traveling in Asia working. White House spokesperson Lauren Bis said there was no crisis. A later social repost at 20:15 UTC repeated the outage and the six-source reporting without adding confirmation of a cause.

The operational detail is that a classified network used to share information across the intelligence community can fail without an intrusion and still disrupt work. The staggered arrivals and reliance on backup channels show the agency treating the network as unavailable for an extended period rather than a brief glitch. No actor, malware, CVE or data access has been tied to the event in the available reporting.

Watch for NSA or CNN follow-up on restoration time and root cause. Until that appears, the defensible reading is an unexplained outage at a high-value communications network, with an explicit early statement ruling out evidence of a cyberattack rather than confirming one.
