CNN reported on 17 November 2026 that NSANet, the classified internal communications network used by the National Security Agency, was down. Six sources confirmed the disruption; at publication there was no evidence of a cyberattack. The agency ordered staggered arrivals at Fort Meade and had closed the campus to most employees by that evening. Backup networks preserved urgent intelligence delivery to the president traveling in Asia, and a White House spokesperson said there was no crisis. The Associated Press later quoted a defense official and an NSA statement describing temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance, with mission-critical capabilities fully operational.

Disruptions entered a second day on 18 November. The Record reported that non-essential staff received a Code Gray notification late Tuesday directing a 10 a.m. Wednesday arrival, which was then pushed to the afternoon. The cafeteria and an on-campus Starbucks remained closed. A source said the network was working but that login was the most taxing process on the system, and that another day or two might be needed for full restoration. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton attributed the outage to maintenance affecting PHP; whether the underlying problem was AI-derived or human-driven remained unknown, and the NSA did not immediately answer follow-up questions.

The key open question is whether a routine maintenance explanation survives the multi-day recovery details. Login pressure, staggered access and the second-day timeline fit a degraded service trying to re-establish normal load, but the available reporting gives no root-cause evidence beyond the PHP maintenance statement. Watch for an NSA after-action note, further changes to the restoration estimate, and any reporting that ties the disruption to the string of recent government and private-sector outages, including the air traffic ground stop, JPMorgan Chase and United Airlines incidents named in the coverage.
