CNN reported on November 17 that NSANet, the classified internal communications network used by the National Security Agency, was down. Six people familiar with the matter confirmed the outage. At publication there was no evidence of a cyberattack, and the agency had ordered staggered arrivals at Fort Meade while technicians assessed the scope. Backup networks preserved urgent intelligence delivery to the president traveling in Asia, and White House spokesperson Lauren Bis said there was no crisis when asked on Newsmax.

The NSA later described the event as temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance. Its statement said some internal systems were affected, mission-critical capabilities remained fully operational, and the infrastructure has multiple layers of redundancy for planned maintenance or unexpected events. The Associated Press obtained a similar account from a defense official. This is an availability incident on a sensitive network, but the agency and CNN both stopped short of attributing it to hostile action.

The outage landed in a week of other disruptions across government and private services, including an FAA ground stop, a JPMorgan Chase technical issue, a United Airlines systems outage, and an Apple problem reported the same day. That cluster explains the concern without proving a common cause. Watch for a restored-service confirmation from the NSA and any later disclosure of the maintenance action that triggered the degradation.
