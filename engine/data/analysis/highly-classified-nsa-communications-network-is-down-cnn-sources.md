NSANet, the classified internal communications network used by the National Security Agency, went offline on 17 November 2026. CNN published the first report at 15:31 UTC, citing six people familiar with the matter, and said the agency had no evidence at that point of a cyberattack. Employees at Fort Meade were told to arrive in staggered groups while technicians assessed the scope. A White House spokesperson, Lauren Bis, said there was no crisis, and sources said backup networks kept urgent intelligence delivery running for the president traveling in Asia.

The NSA told the Associated Press the disruption was a "temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance" and that mission-critical capabilities stayed fully operational through layered redundancy, without detailing the cause or scope. Disruptions continued into a second day. Non-essential staff got a "Code Gray" notice for a 10 a.m. Wednesday arrival that was pushed to the afternoon, the cafeteria and a campus Starbucks stayed closed, and one source said logins were the most taxing part of the system even as the network itself worked. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton attributed the problem to maintenance affecting PHP; whether the underlying fault came from an AI-derived or human-written change remains unknown, and a source estimated another day or two for full restoration.

On Wednesday, DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin, Clayton and FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia told the House Homeland Security Committee that the wave of recent outages, including flight groundings, Microsoft service failures and banking disruptions such as the JPMorgan Chase issue, appeared to be routine technical problems rather than adversarial action. NSANet was still having issues that day. Reporting from Heise suggested rushed, AI-targeted patching may have overwhelmed the NSA and major banks, but that link is not confirmed by the agency statements. What to watch is whether the NSA eventually identifies the specific PHP maintenance change, whether the outage recurs, and whether the early no-cyberattack assessment survives a full review.
