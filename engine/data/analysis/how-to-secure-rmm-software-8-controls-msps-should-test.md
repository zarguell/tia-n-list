The BleepingComputer checklist frames RMM security through two concrete failures rather than abstract best practice. CVE-2026-86218 is a maximum-severity pre-authentication RCE in N-able N-central, patched in September 2026 as an emergency hotfix (the fourth in five weeks) while roughly 1,500 servers remained exposed online. The earlier SharePoint ToolShell chain (CVE-2025-53770 and CVE-2025-53771) exploited in July 2025 compromised at least 85 on-premises servers before a patch existed. Together these show the management plane is the attack surface: RMM grants unattended administrative access across thousands of endpoints, so a single privileged account compromise extends blast radius across an entire MSP customer base, not one device.

This is not another generic hardening guide. The checklist exists because CISA has documented ransomware actors actively abusing legitimate RMM software to pivot downstream, and the N-central incident proves that pre-auth RCE in an RMM platform puts both the provider and every downstream tenant at risk simultaneously. The 1,500 exposed N-central instances and the 85 SharePoint servers are not historical footnotes; they measure how quickly exposure becomes exploitation when patching lags behind disclosure. A defender reading the event list sees CVE numbers; the argument is that RMM is different from endpoint software because the same privileged session that patches endpoints can encrypt them all.

Patch N-central immediately using the September 2026 emergency hotfix for CVE-2026-86218; verify no unpatched instances remain exposed by checking for the four-hotfix sequence N-able released across five weeks. For SharePoint, audit for CVE-2025-53770 and CVE-2025-53771 exploitation indicators from July 2025 onward, since the ToolShell zero-days were exploited before patches existed. Restrict RMM management plane access by network segment, enforce MFA on every privileged RMM account, and hunt for legitimate RMM processes running outside approved maintenance windows, since CISA has specifically flagged ransomware abuse of these tools. Review tenant separation controls directly rather than relying on vendor feature lists.

Watch for a fifth N-able hotfix or new pre-auth RCE disclosure that would confirm the management plane remains under sustained targeting; also watch CISA advisories linking specific ransomware campaigns to RMM tool abuse, since that would shift the threat from theoretical blast radius to named actor behavior.
