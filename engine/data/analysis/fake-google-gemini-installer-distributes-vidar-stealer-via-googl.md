Attackers are distributing the Vidar information stealer through a fake Google Gemini installer hosted on Google Colab, exploiting interest in generative AI tools. Darktrace investigated a July 2026 intrusion in an EMEA customer environment where a user downloaded and executed a malicious file named Download_Google_Gemini_For_Windows.exe. The campaign targeted users actively searching for AI software rather than relying on traditional phishing emails.

The malicious installer launched from a user's Downloads folder and deployed the Vidar stealer, which is designed to harvest browser credentials and other sensitive data. This represents a shift in social engineering tactics, leveraging the popularity of AI tools to lure victims into executing malware. The use of Google Colab as a hosting platform adds legitimacy to the distribution method.

This campaign highlights the growing trend of attackers exploiting AI-related interest for malware distribution. Organizations should educate users about the risks of downloading software from unofficial sources, even when it appears to be from legitimate providers. The Vidar stealer remains a persistent threat, and this distribution method could be adapted for other popular AI tools.
