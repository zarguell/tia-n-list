The FBI is investigating an apparent intrusion involving the National Crime Information Center, the federal criminal database used for arrest warrants and background checks. CNN first reported the breach on 21 November, describing it as another hack of the bureau after earlier incidents, and Yahoo framed it as an apparent hack of the system police use for warrants and background checks. Reuters carried the same report. One earlier post called it the second breach in months.

The collected reports are short link posts and give no intrusion method, actor, timeline or victim count. They focus on the sensitivity of NCIC records and the possibility that arrest warrants and criminal history data were exposed. Commentary from thecybersecguru raised the data-integrity angle, but that post is a joke about database entries being rendered in yellow, not reporting, so it carries no evidentiary weight.

What matters next is official confirmation from the FBI on scope, whether records were read, altered or copied, and which connected systems feed NCIC. Watch for follow-up reporting that names an access path or a suspect, and for any notice to agencies that rely on the database for day-to-day checks.
