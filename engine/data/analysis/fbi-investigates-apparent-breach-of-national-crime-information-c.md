CNN reported, citing two people briefed on the investigation, that the FBI is looking into an apparent breach of the National Crime Information Center. NCIC is the nationwide system law enforcement agencies use to share wanted and missing person records, outstanding arrest warrants, stolen vehicles and firearms, protection orders and other criminal justice data during traffic stops, investigations and background checks. The intrusion was discovered in recent days. The reporting does not establish when the breach occurred, how the attackers got in, or whether information was taken, and the FBI had not publicly confirmed the incident or responded to requests for comment at publication.

A former FBI official quoted in the CNN report framed data integrity as the central concern: if an intruder can alter NCIC records, officers may act on warrants or background-check results that are no longer trustworthy. BleepingComputer and First Post recap the same core facts. NBC News independently cited two people briefed on the matter and noted the bureau had no comment Friday night. No vulnerability, actor, ransom note or intrusion mechanism has been named in the available reporting.

The report lands two months after ShinyHunters claimed it breached FBI systems through an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability on a third-party platform tied to the FBIJobs.gov recruitment site. Earlier coverage also recalls a 2023 breach of an FBI New York field office system and an apparent attack disclosed Thursday on a USDA system used to pay farmers. Whether any of these incidents are connected has not been reported.

Watch for an FBI confirmation, a scope statement covering which NCIC records were accessed or changed, and any technical detail on the entry path. Because the affected system is shared with state and local agencies, a confirmed integrity problem would have operational consequences well beyond the bureau itself.
