CNN reported, citing two people briefed on the investigation, that the FBI is looking into an apparent breach of the National Crime Information Center, or NCIC, discovered in recent days. NCIC is the nationwide system that shares warrant, background-check, missing-person, stolen-property and criminal-history records among federal, state and local law enforcement. One report described it as holding over 19 million records. The Associated Press later cited a person familiar with the matter confirming the investigation, while the FBI declined to comment. Sources told CNN and later the AP that the attack took place on Thursday. Trump told Fox News he had been briefed and said: "I will say that it's not good. I don't like that." In the same interview he speculated that the breach could make investigative files about him public. The reported intrusion is the second against FBI systems in recent months, after ShinyHunters claimed in September to have stolen data through the bureau's FBIJobs.gov platform via an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft flaw.

The scope is unresolved. Reports say it is unclear when the breach occurred, how the attackers gained access, or whether any information was taken. A former FBI official quoted in the early coverage argued that the bigger risk is not only exposure of sensitive records but damage to the accuracy of the data officers rely on during stops and background checks. The Guardian also reported apparent breaches at both the FBI and the Department of Agriculture amid a wave of technology outages, but the AP account said there was no immediate indication that the FBI incident was connected to those outages. President Trump acknowledged the report on Saturday without adding detail.

What to watch is an official FBI confirmation, a timeline for the intrusion, and whether the case involves theft or alteration of NCIC records rather than access alone. Attribution is also open: no group has claimed the NCIC incident, and the ShinyHunters jobs-site claim should not be treated as the same operation without evidence. Any link to the separate USDA incident or to the recent government and private-sector outages remains unconfirmed in the reporting so far.
