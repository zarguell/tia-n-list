The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of the National Crime Information Center, the nationwide system police use for warrants, missing persons, criminal histories, stolen property and background checks. CNN first reported the intrusion on November 20, citing two people briefed on the investigation, and the Associated Press later confirmed it with a person familiar who spoke anonymously. Sources told the AP the attack took place on Thursday and was discovered in recent days. One estimate puts the database at more than 19 million records. The FBI declined to comment, and the intruder, entry path and whether any data was taken remain unknown.

The main concern is not only confidentiality but integrity. A former FBI official quoted in the initial reporting said officers rely on NCIC data during traffic stops and investigations, so the ability to alter records could be as damaging as theft. State, local, tribal and territorial users have not received formal communication from the bureau about the intrusion, according to a law enforcement representative. President Trump acknowledged the incident in a Fox News interview, calling it "not good" and suggesting without evidence that it might reveal information about bureau investigations he has criticized.

The reported incident follows a September breach in which ShinyHunters claimed to have stolen data on agents through a third-party platform tied to FBIJobs.gov and an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability. A USDA system used to pay farmers was reportedly attacked in the same week as the NCIC discovery, and officials have attributed a month of government and private-sector outages to routine upgrades, with no immediate indication those outages are linked. The November 23 securityweek post restates the same unresolved case. Watch for an FBI scope statement, any evidence of record modification, and formal notice to the agencies that contribute to and query NCIC.
