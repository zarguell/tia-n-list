The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of the National Crime Information Center, the nationwide system police use for warrants, missing persons, criminal histories, stolen property and background checks. CNN first reported the intrusion on November 20, citing two people briefed on the investigation, and the Associated Press later confirmed it with a person familiar who spoke anonymously. Sources told the AP the attack took place on Thursday and was discovered in recent days. One estimate puts the database at more than 19 million records. The FBI declined to comment, and the intruder, entry path and whether any data was taken remain unknown.

The main concern is not just confidentiality but integrity. A former FBI official quoted in the initial reporting said officers rely on NCIC data during traffic stops and investigations, so the ability to alter records could be as damaging as theft. State, local, tribal and territorial users have not received formal communication from the bureau about the intrusion, according to a law enforcement representative. This would be the second reported FBI incident in two months: in September, ShinyHunters claimed to have stolen data on agents through a third-party platform tied to the FBIJobs.gov recruitment site, exploiting an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability. A USDA system used to pay farmers was also reportedly attacked in the same week as the NCIC discovery, and officials have attributed a month of government and private-sector outages to routine upgrades, with no immediate indication those outages are linked.

President Trump acknowledged the hack in a Fox News interview on Saturday, calling it "not good" and suggesting the intrusion might reveal information about bureau investigations he has criticized. What to watch is formal confirmation from the FBI, any notice to NCIC contributors, and whether investigators can establish when access began and whether warrant or protection-order records were changed. Until then the scope described in public reporting remains unclear.
