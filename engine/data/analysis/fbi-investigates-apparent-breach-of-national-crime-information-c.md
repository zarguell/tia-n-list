The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of the National Crime Information Center, the nationwide system police use for warrants, missing persons, criminal histories, stolen property and protection orders. CNN first reported the investigation, citing two people briefed on the matter, and the Associated Press later confirmed it with a person familiar. The intrusion was discovered in recent days. The FBI declined to comment, and one report put the database at more than 19 million records.

The scope is still unclear. Reports say it is not known when the attackers got in, how they gained access or whether data was copied. A former FBI official told CNN the greater concern is the integrity of the records officers rely on during stops and background checks, not just disclosure. The reported breach comes about two months after ShinyHunters claimed it stole FBI-related data through the bureau's jobs site, and the Guardian linked it to a wider set of government and private outages that officials have described as upgrade work.

Watch for an FBI confirmation, any indication that warrant or background-check data was altered, and whether this intrusion is tied to the earlier FBIJobs.gov activity or the USDA incident. Until those facts are established, the reporting supports an investigation, not a confirmed theft of specific records.
