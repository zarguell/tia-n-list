CNN reported, citing two people briefed on the investigation, that the FBI is examining an apparent breach of the National Crime Information Center (NCIC), the nationwide system police use for arrest warrants, background checks, missing persons and stolen property records. The intrusion was discovered in recent days, but the reports give no timeline for the initial access, no intrusion method and no confirmation that data was removed. The Associated Press later said a person familiar with the matter confirmed the investigation, while the FBI declined to comment. One account puts NCIC at more than 19 million records.

The concern goes past ordinary data theft. A former FBI official quoted by CNN said the greater risk is whether an intruder could alter the records officers rely on during traffic stops and investigations. That makes integrity, not just confidentiality, part of the incident review. The reported breach follows the September claim by ShinyHunters of stealing data through a third-party platform tied to the FBIJobs.gov recruitment site and an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft flaw. The Guardian also linked reports of the FBI incident with an apparent attack on a US Department of Agriculture system used to pay farmers, though officials described other recent government outages as upgrade work and there was no immediate indication the incidents were connected.

Watch for FBI confirmation of scope, a determination on whether warrants or other records were changed, and any attribution. The reporting so far rests on unnamed sources, and the agency has not publicly confirmed the breach. President Trump told Fox News only that he had just heard about it and that it was not good.
