The FBI is investigating an apparent intrusion into the National Crime Information Center, the nationwide system used for warrants, fugitives, missing persons, criminal histories, stolen property and background checks. CNN first reported the case on 20 November citing two people briefed on the investigation, and the Associated Press later confirmed it with a person familiar. Sources told the AP the attack happened on Thursday and was discovered in recent days. One account puts NCIC at more than 19.3 million records in 2025. No actor, entry path or exfiltration count has been disclosed.

Director Kash Patel said Tuesday that the matter was ongoing and that the bureau was working with other agencies, but he declined to say who was responsible or what information was obtained. A former FBI official quoted in the early reporting said the greater concern is record integrity, since officers rely on NCIC data during traffic stops and investigations. Fox News cited a retired supervisory special agent saying roughly 56 state terminals feed hundreds of thousands of patrol car computers and that NCIC holds names, dates of birth and Social Security numbers, while noting no indication so far that records were altered or the system went down. State and local users have not received formal communication about the intrusion, according to a law enforcement representative.

This would be the second reported FBI breach in recent months. In September, ShinyHunters claimed access through a third-party platform tied to FBIJobs.gov and an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability. A USDA farm payment system was reportedly attacked in the same week as the NCIC discovery, while officials attributed a run of government and private-sector outages to routine upgrades and said there was no immediate link. Watch for an FBI scope statement, evidence of record modification and formal notice to the agencies that contribute to and query NCIC.
