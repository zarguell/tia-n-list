The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of the National Crime Information Center, the nationwide system police use for warrants, missing persons, criminal histories, stolen property and protection orders. CNN first reported the investigation, citing two people briefed on the matter, and the Associated Press later confirmed it with a person familiar who spoke on condition of anonymity. The intrusion was discovered in recent days. The FBI declined to comment, and one report put the database at more than 19 million records. ABC7 New York and Politico later carried Trump's Saturday acknowledgement to Fox News that another cyberattack had hit the federal government, with Trump saying only that it was "not good."

The scope is still unclear. Reports say it is not known when the attackers got in, how they gained access or whether data was copied. A former FBI official told CNN the greater concern is the integrity of the records officers rely on during stops and background checks, not just disclosure. NBC and BleepingComputer noted this is the second reported FBI system intrusion in two months, after ShinyHunters claimed it stole data through the bureau's FBIJobs.gov recruitment platform by exploiting an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft flaw. The Guardian linked the disclosure to a wider set of government and private outages, including a reported hit on the USDA system used to pay farmers, that officials have described as upgrade work.

What to watch is any bureau statement on when access began and whether NCIC records were altered or exfiltrated. Until that lands, the reporting supports an active investigation and a suspected intrusion, not a confirmed data theft. The operational concern stays on data integrity: NCIC is queried at traffic stops, in criminal investigations and during background checks, so even unproven doubts about the records can force verification work across local, state and federal agencies.
