CNN reported, citing two people briefed on the investigation, that the FBI is examining an apparent breach of the National Crime Information Center. NCIC is the FBI-operated system that federal, state and local law enforcement use to share wanted and missing person records, outstanding arrest warrants, stolen vehicles and firearms, protection orders and other criminal justice data during traffic stops, investigations and background checks. The intrusion was discovered in recent days. The reporting does not establish when it occurred, how the attackers got in or whether data was taken, and the FBI had not publicly confirmed the incident or responded to requests for comment.

NBC News, Reuters, the BBC, the Guardian and BleepingComputer picked up the CNN report through the day. BleepingComputer described it as the second FBI breach in two months, after ShinyHunters claimed in September to have exploited an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft flaw on a third-party platform tied to the FBIJobs.gov recruitment site; reporting also recalls a 2023 breach of a New York field office system. An Implicator post put NCIC at more than 19 million records. No source in the public reporting credits an actor or describes an intrusion path for NCIC itself.

A former FBI official quoted in the CNN coverage said the larger risk is not only access to sensitive records but whether an intruder could alter the data officers rely on for warrants and background checks. The Guardian posts tied the disclosure to a wider wave of outages and breaches that also hit the Department of Agriculture, but those posts do not establish a shared actor or technique.

Watch for an FBI confirmation, a timeline of the intrusion and any finding on data theft or modification. Because NCIC feeds routine police decisions, integrity of warrant and protection-order records matters as much as confidentiality. Confirmation of the entry path, affected agencies and whether the earlier PeopleSoft claim is connected would change the scope of this story.
