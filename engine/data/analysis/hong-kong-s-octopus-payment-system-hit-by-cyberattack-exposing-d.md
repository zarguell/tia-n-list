Octopus Cards Limited has confirmed a cyberattack that exfiltrated personal data tied to about 7,500 individual customers and contact records for roughly 22,000 merchants. The Hong Kong smart-card and payments operator detected the intrusion on the morning of November 27 and activated containment procedures. The combined reported scope is nearly 30,000 customer and merchant records, not 30,000 individual customers.

Initial forensic triage identified names among the exfiltrated customer records, while the merchant exposure consisted of contact records. The incident puts both consumer identity data and business contact information held by a major payments operator at risk. The available reports do not establish that payment credentials or transaction data were exposed, so the confirmed impact should not be broadened beyond the stated record types.

Watch for Octopus's final field-level disclosure, any revision to the customer or merchant counts, and findings on whether data beyond the reported personal and contact records was accessed. The separate SmartPLAY and Aeon Credit Service incidents mentioned in follow-up coverage are distinct events and should not be folded into the Octopus impact assessment.
