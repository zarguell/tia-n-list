Octopus Cards Limited confirmed a cyberattack that exfiltrated personal data tied to about 7,500 individual customers and contact records for roughly 22,000 merchants. The Hong Kong smart-card and payments operator detected the intrusion on the morning of November 27 and activated containment procedures. The combined reported scope is nearly 30,000 customer and merchant records, not 30,000 individual customers.

Initial forensic triage identified names among the exfiltrated customer records, while the merchant exposure consisted of contact records. Follow-up reporting says no card or bank account numbers were exposed. That limits the confirmed impact to personal and business contact information, but it does not remove the fraud and phishing risk created by releasing identities and contact details together. The separate SmartPLAY and Aeon Credit Service incidents mentioned in follow-up coverage are distinct events and should not be folded into the Octopus assessment.

Watch for Octopus's final field-level disclosure, any revision to the customer or merchant counts, and findings on the intrusion path and duration. Investigators should also establish whether data beyond the reported records was accessed and whether containment stopped further exfiltration. The priority is to confirm the exact exposed fields and notification scope rather than assume that payment credentials or transaction data were involved.
