The PHP story now has two distinct patch waves. On October 21, PHP fixed the PHP2Shell pair CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, rated 9.2 under CVSS v4. Details leaked before the coordinated release, and Amazon's MadPot honeypot captured exploitation attempts on October 17, four days before public disclosure. CISA later added both to KEV. Fixes shipped in 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35, with source-only backports for older branches and no fix for PHP 5.4 through 7.3. Censys tracked millions of PHP services on affected versions.

On November 18 the team pre-announced another security release for all supported branches on November 19 between 14:00 and 14:30 UTC, with the highest severity rated Critical. Sources disagreed on the final count, reporting 12 or 13 fixes, but named PHP 8.5.13, 8.4.28, 8.3.37 and 8.2.36 as the patched builds and CVE-2026-129289, a PHP-FPM request-smuggling RCE, among the issues. CVE-2026-115560, the config-dependent session upload progress RCE disclosed after PHP2Shell, is also resolved in the November 19 release. It needs session.serialize_handler set to php_binary or php_serialize and then switched to php with ini_set before session_start, with upload progress still enabled; disabling session.upload_progress.enabled was the earlier mitigation.

The recurring pattern is configuration-gated RCE in core PHP plus an exposure window created by early disclosure. Watch for operators who patched PHP2Shell but left session upload progress enabled, for FPM installations handling untrusted request streams, and for confirmation of which November CVEs reach exploitation. Aggregator counts of patched vulnerabilities vary, so track the vendor's advisory and the named CVEs rather than the 12 versus 13 headline.
