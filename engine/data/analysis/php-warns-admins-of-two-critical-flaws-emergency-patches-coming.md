PHP warned administrators of two critical remote code execution flaws, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, each rated 9.2 CVSS v4.0, affecting PHP versions 5 through 8. The vulnerabilities were scheduled for emergency patches on October 21, but details leaked earlier, prompting active exploitation attempts and a rapid response from defenders. The PHP team reserved the right to release fixes earlier if public details or in-the-wild exploitation emerged. Researchers at watchTowr confirmed the RCE nature of both flaws. OffSec released a free hands-on lab for security practitioners to study exploitation and defense of what has been dubbed PHP2Shell.

This story matters because PHP runs a large share of web applications, including CMS platforms and custom services, making widespread RCE a high-impact risk. The rapid appearance of exploitation repositories on GitHub after disclosure, combined with the CISA KEV listing and active probing, indicates immediate, practical danger rather than theoretical exposure. Organizations running PHP should treat patching as urgent and assume attackers are already testing and deploying exploits.

Watch for updated vendor patches, additional CVE details, and further exploitation reports from CISA or threat-intelligence feeds. Monitor for new PHP2Shell-related repositories and detection rules, and verify patch status across PHP 5, 6, 7, and 8 environments.
