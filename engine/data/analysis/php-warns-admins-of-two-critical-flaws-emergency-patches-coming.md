The PHP security team issued an emergency release on October 21, 2026 to patch two critical remote-code-execution vulnerabilities, each rated 9.2 under CVSS v4, after details leaked ahead of the coordinated disclosure date. Tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 and dubbed PHP2Shell, the flaws are believed to affect PHP 5 through 8. PHP moved the release forward from its original schedule and warned it could ship even earlier if active exploitation appeared.

watchTowr researchers, who reproduced at least one of the flaws, reported that no in-the-wild exploitation had been observed yet and that no workaround exists, making patching the only remediation. Cloudflare's CTO separately noted early attack traffic emerging. The emergency patches are provided only for supported PHP 8.x branches, so systems running older, end-of-life PHP versions remain exposed.

Administrators should upgrade to the patched 8.x releases immediately and prioritize internet-facing PHP applications and shared hosting environments. Teams still on PHP 5, 6, or 7 should plan migration, as those branches will not receive fixes for these flaws.
