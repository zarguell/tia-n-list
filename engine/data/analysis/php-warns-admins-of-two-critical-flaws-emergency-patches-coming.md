On October 21, 2026 the PHP development team issued an out-of-cycle security release fixing two critical remote code execution vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, collectively called PHP2Shell. Both carry a CVSS v4.0 score of 9.2 and affect PHP 8.2 through 8.5 across Apache with mod_php, PHP-FPM, PHP-CGI, FrankenPHP, LiteSpeed, and applications embedding vulnerable runtimes. Fixed releases are PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36, and 8.2.35.

CISA added both flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 26, and reporting notes that patching has lagged despite the fixes being available. The exposure extends beyond ordinary web hosts: Eclypsium's InfraTrust Pulse warns that the flaws put web management interfaces of firewalls, VPNs, application delivery controllers, and other appliances at risk. Wordfence reports no confirmed WordPress compromises so far, though it continues to see scanning and exploitation attempts across the millions of sites it protects. A third PHP flaw, CVE-2026-115560, has since emerged.

The scale of the response is visible in government notifications, including roughly 17,416 public institutions alerted in Romania, plus an OffSec hands-on lab and ongoing scanning pressure. Administrators of PHP-based and appliance management interfaces should treat these updates as urgent.
