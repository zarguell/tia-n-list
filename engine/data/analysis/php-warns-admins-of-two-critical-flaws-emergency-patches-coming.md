The PHP security team warned administrators to prepare for emergency security updates to address two critical vulnerabilities, each rated 9.2 under CVSS v4.0, after information about the flaws leaked ahead of scheduled disclosure. The release was planned for October 21, 2026 at 14:00 UTC, with PHP reserving the right to release earlier if details became public or evidence of active exploitation appeared. watchTowr researchers told BleepingComputer the flaws are tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, remote code execution issues believed to affect PHP versions 5 through 8. PHP did not confirm the identifiers or disclose affected versions, fixed versions, or technical details.

PHP powers many websites and web applications, including content management systems, e-commerce platforms, and custom web services, so the exposure is broad. PHP 8.1 and earlier are no longer supported upstream, making those installations a priority for upgrade planning alongside patching. Administrators should monitor PHP's official security announcements and apply updates promptly once available, and inventory PHP installations ahead of the release. No confirmed in-the-wild exploitation had been reported at the time of the advisory, but the leak of details increased the risk of rapid weaponization.
