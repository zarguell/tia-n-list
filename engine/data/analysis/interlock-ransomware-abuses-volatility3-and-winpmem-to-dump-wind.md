Interlock, tracked by Sophos as GOLD EMBRACE, is a small, self-contained crew active since September 2024 that builds its own malware rather than running ransomware-as-a-service, leans on ClickFix lures, the NodeSnake or Interlock RAT, and PHP backdoors, and leaks stolen data through its Worldwide Secrets Blog. In a March 2026 intrusion on a Windows 10 endpoint, the operator turned incident-response tooling into a credential dumper: it ran WinPmem (winpmem_mini_x64.exe acquire mem.raw) to capture a full physical-memory image, then used Volatility3's windows.hashdump.Hashdump module against that image to recover NTLM and LM hashes and cached domain credentials. The same group is tied to CVE-2026-20131, a zero-day in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center that Sophos reports Interlock exploited before the flaw was public.

The durable lesson is that the tools defenders reach for during an incident are now the tools the attacker uses, so the intrusion hides inside expected administrative and IR activity. winpmem and Volatility3 are legitimate, signed utilities; a mem.raw file and a Python process invoking hashdump look like responder work, not malware. The compromised host was described as Defender-managed but was not effectively protected, and that gap proved decisive. One endpoint's dumped cached domain credentials are enough to unlock lateral movement and domain-wide compromise, which is why this matters more than an average ransomware note: a successful memory dump converts a single foothold into a domain credential crisis, and signature-based controls will not see it coming.

Hunt for WinPmem execution and memory-image creation (winpmem_mini_x64.exe, mem.raw), for python or vol.py processes running windows.hashdump.Hashdump against an image file, and for unexpected reads of raw physical memory or crash dumps. Restrict these utilities to the incident-response team with application control and ASR rules, alert on their presence on hosts not under active investigation, and treat any confirmed cached-credential dump as a trigger to rotate domain and privileged credentials, including the krbtgt account. Patch or mitigate CVE-2026-20131 on Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center and review exposure of that management plane.

Two developments would shift the assessment. The first is the real scope of CVE-2026-20131 exploitation: a Cisco advisory or patch with detail would show whether Interlock is the sole operator and how long the zero-day was live before disclosure. The second is adoption, because if other crews copy the Volatility3 and WinPmem credential-theft method, this stops being an Interlock signature and becomes a baseline technique every defender must expect.
