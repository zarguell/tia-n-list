Dragos chief research officer HD Moore led a study that sent approximately two million prompts through AI services from July through November 2026. Each prompt carried canary URLs, hostnames or AWS credentials tied to monitored accounts. The researchers traced about 2,000 request routes and found evidence that prompt data was stolen from at least three AI inference providers, none of which detected the compromise until Dragos notified them.

The study reported that hosting providers were compromised, resellers could read customer prompts through ordinary request logging, and some requests marked Zero Data Retention were still fetched or forwarded to Chinese labs. In the largest documented case, roughly 200 AWS test keys sent to one provider were stolen in late October. Some came from requests rejected before a model ran, which Dragos said showed copying at the provider's front door. Seeded keys were used for tens of thousands of AWS API calls, including S3 listing, Secrets Manager and Parameter Store lookups, Bedrock model enablement and IAM user creation.

OpenRouter said its own systems were not breached, but it removed three inference providers, Together AI, Phala and Novita, revoked Zero Data Retention eligibility for 125 endpoints and notified potentially affected customers. Atlas Cloud said it would withdraw pending review. The incident shows that routing through a broker does not remove provider-level data risk. Watch for provider forensic reports, changes to retention and logging controls, and verification that Zero Data Retention covers rejected as well as processed requests. Teams should inventory AI routes, keep secrets out of prompts and use canaries to test their own data paths.
