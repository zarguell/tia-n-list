Dragos chief research officer HD Moore ran a five-month test from July through November 2026, sending nearly two million prompts with unique URLs, DNS names and canary AWS credentials through AI routers and inference marketplaces. The seeded prompts were processed alongside real customer requests. Dragos saw evidence that data from at least three AI providers was exposed, with some prompts marked Zero Data Retention still fetched or forwarded to Chinese labs, and resellers reading prompts through ordinary request logging.

Seeded keys were used for tens of thousands of AWS API calls, including S3 listing, Secrets Manager and Parameter Store lookups, Bedrock model enablement and IAM user creation. In the largest case, about 200 AWS test keys sent to one provider were stolen in late October. Some of those keys came from requests the provider rejected before a model ran, which Moore read as evidence of copying at the provider front door rather than deeper in the inference stack.

OpenRouter, which routes requests to multiple providers, removed Together AI, Phala and Novita after Dragos notified it. It pulled Zero Data Retention eligibility from 125 endpoints and told affected customers, while saying there was no breach of its own systems. Atlas Cloud said it would withdraw its models from OpenRouter pending review.

A companion honeypot in the same research ran from July 7 to November 7 with more than 500 canary AWS keys. Attackers used 155 of them from 12,353 distinct IPs, 72 percent of used keys went to SES or SNS send-quota checks and 36 percent touched Bedrock. Median time from theft to first use was 37 minutes, and one checker graded stolen keys across five providers from 20 residential connections in 14 countries.
