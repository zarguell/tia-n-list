Dragos researchers sent nearly two million prompts carrying unique URLs, DNS names and canary AWS credentials through AI routers and inference marketplaces between July and November 2026. The report says three hosting providers were compromised and did not know until the researchers told them. Resellers read customer prompts through ordinary request logging, and some requests marked Zero Data Retention were still fetched or forwarded to Chinese labs. OpenRouter dropped three hosts after the findings.

Seeded credentials were not just observed. Attackers used them for tens of thousands of AWS API calls, including S3 listing, Secrets Manager and Parameter Store lookups, Bedrock model enablement and IAM user creation. A separate honeypot run from July 7 to November 7 planted more than 500 canary AWS keys: 155 were used from 12,353 distinct IPs, 72 percent of used keys went to SES or SNS send-quota checks and 36 percent touched Bedrock. The median time from theft to first use was 37 minutes, and one checker graded stolen AI keys across five providers from 20 residential connections in 14 countries.

The finding is about the routing and resale layer, not just model safety. A prompt marked as retained nowhere can still be logged or relayed by an intermediary with access to requests. Watch for provider notifications, changes to OpenRouter's host list and follow-on abuse of the exposed credential types, especially Bedrock enablement and IAM user creation.
