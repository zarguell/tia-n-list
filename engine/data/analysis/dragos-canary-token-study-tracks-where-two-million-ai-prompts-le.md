Dragos ran a five-month test in which researchers sent roughly two million prompts containing canary links and credentials to AI services, then tracked where those markers surfaced. Reporting on the study says the team observed signs that the canaries were taken by users at three providers. The underlying Dragos post frames the work as an AI supply chain and secret leakage experiment rather than a single vendor vulnerability.

The method is the substantive part: planted credentials and links act as traceable markers inside normal-looking prompts, so any later reuse or retrieval can be tied back to the original submission. A separate write-up from the Metasploit creator describes the same two-million-prompt setup and the reported theft at three providers. That gives the study more than one public source, but the provider-level claims still rest on what the canaries showed, not on disclosed breaches from each service.

What to watch is whether the named providers confirm the exposure path and whether the canary credentials were actually used outside the test. Until that confirmation exists, the finding is evidence that prompts can carry tracked secrets into third-party systems and that copies can move further than the submitter expects. It is not evidence of a specific CVE or of a confirmed compromise of every provider named in the reposts.
