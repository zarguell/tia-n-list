Dragos, led by chief research officer HD Moore, sent roughly two million prompts carrying unique URLs, DNS names and canary AWS credentials through AI routers and inference marketplaces between July and November 2026, then watched for those seeds to surface. The study found evidence that data from at least three AI hosting or inference providers was exposed, including requests marked Zero Data Retention. Dragos says resellers could read customer prompts through ordinary request logging, and some requests were fetched or forwarded to Chinese labs. The affected providers did not detect the exposure until researchers notified them.

The abuse was not theoretical. About 200 AWS test keys sent to one provider were stolen in late October, and some came from requests the provider rejected before a model ran, which Moore reads as copying at the front door rather than deeper in the inference stack. Seeded keys were used for tens of thousands of AWS API calls including S3 listing, Secrets Manager and Parameter Store lookups, Bedrock model enablement and IAM user creation. OpenRouter removed three providers, Together AI, Phala and Novita, revoked Zero Data Retention eligibility for 125 endpoints, and notified customers, while saying its own systems were not breached. Atlas Cloud said it would withdraw its models from OpenRouter.

A separate honeypot described in the same coverage ran from 7 July to 7 November 2026 with more than 500 canary AWS keys. Attackers used 155 of them from 12,353 distinct IPs; 72 percent of used keys went to SES or SNS send-quota checks and 36 percent touched Bedrock. The measured median time from theft to first use was 37 minutes. That speed makes credential revocation the first response once a leak is confirmed.

Watch for further provider removals, changes to Zero Data Retention claims, and whether customers receive usable notice about which prompts were exposed. The durable lesson for defenders is to treat anything placed in a prompt as potentially logged: keep real credentials and sensitive customer data out of AI requests, and audit which upstream providers a routing service can select.
