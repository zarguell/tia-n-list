Dragos researchers sent nearly two million prompts carrying unique URLs, DNS names and canary AWS credentials through AI routers and inference marketplaces between July and November 2026. The report says three hosting providers were compromised and did not know until the researchers told them. Resellers read customer prompts through ordinary request logging, and some requests marked Zero Data Retention were still fetched or forwarded to Chinese labs. HD Moore published the study on 19 November and summarized it on Infosec Exchange the same day.

Seeded credentials were not just observed. Attackers used them for tens of thousands of AWS API calls, including S3 listing, Secrets Manager and Parameter Store lookups, Bedrock model enablement and IAM user creation. A separate honeypot run by Lauren Proehl from 7 July to 7 November planted more than 500 canary AWS keys: 155 were used from 12,353 distinct IPs, 72 percent of used keys went to SES or SNS send-quota checks and 36 percent touched Bedrock. The median time from theft to first use was 37 minutes, and one checker graded stolen AI keys across five providers from 20 residential connections in 14 countries.

OpenRouter, the gateway that routes customer requests to inference hosts, dropped three providers after the findings. Its own announcement on 22 November said it off-boarded those providers, removed 125 endpoints from Zero Data Retention eligibility and notified affected customers. That is the concrete remediation so far. What to watch is whether other routers publish provider audits or ZDR changes, and whether the three unnamed hosts confirm the compromise timelines Dragos reported. The canary design measures theft and misuse of seeded secrets, so it does not yield a count of real customer prompts read, which remains the open question for affected users.
