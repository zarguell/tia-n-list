Dragos researchers led by HD Moore sent nearly two million prompts carrying unique URLs, DNS names and canary AWS credentials through AI routers and inference marketplaces between July and November 2026. The five-month test found three hosting providers compromised from inside: resellers read customer prompts through ordinary request logging, and some requests marked Zero Data Retention were still fetched or forwarded to Chinese labs. OpenRouter, one of the gateways in the path, removed three providers after being told.

Separate honeypot work in the same report ran from July 7 to November 7 with a walkable git repository, a fake mail server, fake AI inference endpoints and more than 500 canary AWS keys. Attackers used 155 of those keys from 12,353 distinct IPs; 72 percent of used keys went to SES or SNS send-quota checks and 36 percent touched Bedrock. The median time from theft to first use was 37 minutes. The report also documented a checker that graded stolen AI keys across five providers from 20 residential connections in 14 countries.

Seeded credentials drove tens of thousands of AWS API calls, including S3 listing, Secrets Manager and Parameter Store lookups, Bedrock model enablement and IAM user creation. None of the three compromised providers knew about the theft until the researchers notified them, according to Dragos.

What to watch is whether the affected gateways publish concrete logging and retention controls for reseller traffic, and whether customers can verify that Zero Data Retention flags are enforced downstream. The 37-minute theft-to-use window means leaked keys need automated revocation, not a manual review cycle.
