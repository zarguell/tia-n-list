Dragos researchers led by HD Moore sent nearly two million prompts containing unique URLs, DNS names and canary AWS credentials through AI routers and inference marketplaces between July and November 2026, then watched for the seeds to surface. The study reports three compromised hosting providers, resellers that could read customer prompts through ordinary request logging, and requests marked Zero Data Retention that were still fetched or forwarded to Chinese laboratories. Seeded keys were used for tens of thousands of AWS API calls, including S3 listing, Secrets Manager and Parameter Store lookups, Bedrock model enablement and IAM user creation.

OpenRouter removed three inference providers from its platform after the findings, dropped 125 endpoints from Zero Data Retention eligibility and notified affected customers. A related honeypot run by Lauren Proehl from July 7 to November 7 used more than 500 canary AWS keys and saw 155 of them used from 12,353 distinct IP addresses. The median time from theft to first use was 37 minutes. Seventy-two percent of used keys went to SES or SNS send-quota checks and 36 percent touched Bedrock. One checker graded stolen AI keys across five providers from 20 residential connections in 14 countries.

For defenders the finding is that prompt content and credentials crossing an inference marketplace cannot be assumed private because of a retention flag. Anything placed in a prompt, including a token or a private URL, should be treated as potentially exposed if the provider or a reseller is compromised. Watch for further provider removals, changes to Zero Data Retention claims, and any evidence tying the harvested AWS keys to broader cloud intrusions.
