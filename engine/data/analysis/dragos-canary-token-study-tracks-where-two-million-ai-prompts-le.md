Dragos researcher HD Moore's five-month study put nearly two million prompts carrying unique DNS names, URLs, and permissionless AWS keys through AI routers and inference marketplaces from July through November 2026. The canaries tied each later connection to a prompt, model, router, and privacy flag, and showed attackers inside three hosting providers: in the largest case a couple hundred keys were copied in short late-October windows even from requests the provider rejected before any model ran, then used for tens of thousands of AWS API calls. Marketplace sellers read prompts through ordinary request logging, and URLs from requests marked Zero Data Retention were fetched months later or forwarded to Chinese labs.

The argument is that prompt routing is a data-egress control problem, not a model-selection detail. Every hop in a router chain sees the full prompt and response, so a privacy flag on the first request says nothing about the fourth hop, and a marketplace that auctions capacity to the lowest bidder can move customer text to a reseller with a logging switch and a home broadband line. With three providers unaware of intrusions that began in mid-July, a canary link appearing in a Feishu chat 24 days after a ZDR-marked request, and roughly 2,000 traced routes including several hundred with an unidentified system, the exposure belongs to the routing pattern rather than one vendor. That is why this outranks another single-CVE item: the assets already inside prompts are credentials, source code, and customer data, and no patch restores their confidentiality after a copy has been taken.

Treat any secret that has gone through a router as exposed and rotate it; seed a Thinkst Canarytoken or equivalent in agent context and alert on any hit. Pin upstream providers instead of accepting default marketplace pools, use a direct first-tier contract for sensitive work, and test ZDR with synthetic canaries rather than trusting the flag. Hunt CloudTrail for the user agents Dragos published, artex-agent and artex-judge-r121, plus Boto3 inside OrbStack and aws-cli on Kali, and for the follow-on sequence of S3 listing, Secrets Manager and Parameter Store reads, Bedrock model enablement, API key creation, and IAM user creation.

The assessment changes if the three providers publish breach notices confirming that customer prompt copies were taken, or if Dragos names the compromised services and the canary keys are used outside its controlled accounts. Watch equally for gateways removing reseller marketplaces or enforcing ZDR at every hop. Until then, about a dozen providers under notification and seeded URLs still being accessed months later make the measured leakage a floor, not a closed incident.
