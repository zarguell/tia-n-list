Dragos chief research officer HD Moore published a five-month study, from July through November 2026, that seeded nearly two million prompts sent through AI routers and inference marketplaces with unique URLs, DNS names and canary AWS credentials. Dragos reports three hosting providers compromised, resellers reading customer prompts through ordinary request logging, and some requests marked Zero Data Retention still being fetched or forwarded to Chinese labs. OpenRouter removed three inference providers from its platform, dropped 125 endpoints from ZDR eligibility and notified affected customers.

A related honeypot run by Lauren Proehl from 7 July to 7 November used a walkable git repository, a fake mail server, fake AI inference endpoints and more than 500 canary AWS keys. Attackers used 155 of those keys from 12,353 distinct IP addresses. Seventy-two percent of used keys went to SES or SNS send-quota checks and 36 percent touched Bedrock. The measured median time from theft to first use was 37 minutes, and one checker graded stolen AI keys across five providers from 20 residential connections in 14 countries.

Seeded credentials were used for tens of thousands of AWS API calls, including S3 listing, Secrets Manager and Parameter Store lookups, Bedrock model enablement and IAM user creation. The finding turns a prompt's URL or credential into a tracking signal for the whole inference supply chain, including providers that promise no retention. What to watch: which providers change logging or ZDR enforcement, whether affected customers get usable notification, and whether stolen canaries keep reaching model endpoints outside the three named hosts.
