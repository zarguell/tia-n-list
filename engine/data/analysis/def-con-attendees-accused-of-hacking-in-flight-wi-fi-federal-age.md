A Delta Air Lines flight from Las Vegas to Atlanta on August 10, carrying passengers home from DEF CON, became the scene of a reported in-flight Wi-Fi attack. Roughly an hour into the journey, the crew messaged corporate security over ACARS that a passenger had created a rogue network named Delta WiFi Fast, and that others had jammed the aircraft's official Wi-Fi signal. Federal agents met the aircraft on arrival.

Researchers characterize the described activity as an evil twin attack, with a portable auditing device used to deauthenticate devices from the legitimate access point and steer them toward a fraudulent captive portal. The flight departed just after DEF CON 34, part of the annual Hacker Summer Camp cluster in Las Vegas. In-flight Wi-Fi is a small but real attack surface, and incidents like this one tend to surface quickly because captive-portal networks capture user traffic. Watch for confirmation of whether credentials were harvested, for the FBI or FAA to disclose findings, and for any carrier response around wireless hardening on board.
