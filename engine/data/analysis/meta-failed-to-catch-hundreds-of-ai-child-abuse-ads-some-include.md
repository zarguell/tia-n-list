Researchers at the Tech Transparency Project discovered that Meta published more than 350 paid advertisements containing child sexual abuse material (CSAM) across Facebook, Instagram, Threads, and Meta's advertising network since late 2025. The ads included images of real children, including a member of a European royal family whose official photograph was transformed into a video depicting a sexual act. Three additional minors were identified as US-based, including two social media influencers and a stock photo model labeled as pre-teen.

The ads followed a consistent pattern: an innocuous image of a preteen or teenage girl with overlay text saying "not just a photo" and "there are no restriction" on what can be done with it. When played, the children's faces were morphed into graphic sexual videos. The ads linked to AI nudification or face-swapping apps, nearly 50 across Apple's App Store and Google's Play Store, most linked to Chinese developers. Meta's ad library shows the ads were shown to over 29,000 accounts in the EU, 7,000 in the UK, 250+ in the US, and 120 in Australia.

Multiple lawmakers and regulators announced investigations following the report. Virginia Senator Mark Warner, whose previous letter to Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg went unanswered, called the findings "disappointing." Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier posted on X that his office is investigating. Australia's eSafety regulator said it is "highly concerned" and has requested information from Meta at a senior level. Meta received under $5,000 in ad payments for the abusive content but faces far larger consequences as it simultaneously pays up to $16.7 billion to settle lawsuits over its platforms harming children.
