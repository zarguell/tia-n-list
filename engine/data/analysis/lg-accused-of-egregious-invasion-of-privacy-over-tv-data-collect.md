Researchers from the Gamers Nexus YouTube channel accused LG smart TVs of capturing audio transcripts while the display was in standby, including when voice recognition was supposedly inactive. Their testing found plaintext transcripts from captured audio, along with IP addresses, location data, and the names and signal strengths of nearby Wi-Fi networks. The TVs also enumerated devices on the local network that were not paired with the TV, including smartphones, routers, and servers.

LG previously stated its TVs do not "collect, record, or store ambient conversations" and that voice recognition processes data only when activated by the user. Gamers Nexus claims the TVs can capture and store audio locally even when disconnected from the internet, transmitting it after connectivity is restored. The team says it is working with security researchers to responsibly disclose vulnerabilities, including an alleged remote code execution flaw.

This follows a July report from the same group that LG monitors were installing adware through Windows' device metadata system. LG's advertising division, LG Ads Solutions, deploys automatic content recognition technology across 27 countries and markets "precision targeting at the device level, across households." The broader concern is that LG's privacy disclosures bury data collection details deep in product pages, and consumers are not informed at point of purchase about the extent of data harvesting.
