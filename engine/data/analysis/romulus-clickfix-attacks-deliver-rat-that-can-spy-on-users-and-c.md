Blackpoint's Adversary Pursuit Group has named the previously undocumented RAT in the ROMULUS ClickFix delivery platform as CNCMachineRMS, a family LevelBlue first documented on 2026-08-10. ROMULUS is a shared ClickFix backend with six observed panel servers, not one campaign: Forescout tied it to separate CNCMachineRMS and LegionLoader chains, and Blackpoint reproduced both. In the Stardock variant, a deceptive MSI installs to %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Stardock Smart DeElevation Tool\, replaces DeElevator64.dll so signed DeElevate64.exe loads I++u.dll, then decrypts about 341 KB of shellcode from Build.dat and executes it through EnumSystemCodePagesW. The final implant matches the Huntress sample hash, carries 1,128 archive entries, and supports remote desktop control, screen streaming, microphone and camera capture, file management, system reconnaissance and additional payload execution; Blackpoint reports no observed post-exploitation activity.

The important shift is not that another RAT exists. It is that a months-old implant, with protocol telemetry back to April 2026 and a 600-second beacon, has been folded into a service-style delivery layer that Huntress saw across at least 40 incidents and two confirmed Custom GPT-driven infections. Removing one family's hashes or one lure domain leaves the other payload route intact, because the same panel can serve CNCMachineRMS through Stardock and LegionLoader through a separate PowerShell chain. For businesses and MSPs, the realistic outcome is hands-on access with screen and file control rather than a single deterministic theft event, and DoH resolution through dns.google, cloudflare-dns.com and dns.quad9.net means local DNS logs alone will not show the C2 lookup. That combination makes this story more consequential than the daily volume of commodity ClickFix reports.

Hunt the chain, not just the payload. Look for PowerShell writing a GUID-named MSI under %TEMP%, silent msiexec /qn execution, decimal IP hosts such as 1614733393, ARPSYSTEMCOMPONENT=1 installs, and the paths %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Stardock Smart DeElevation Tool\ and %TEMP%\CNCMachineRMS\tasks\task_payload.bin. Check the Run key and scheduled task both named Stardock DeElevation Tool, with recreation intervals of 150 and 875 seconds, plus unsigned DeElevator64.dll loading I++u.dll from a signed DeElevate64.exe process and non-browser DoH traffic. Block unauthorized MSI installs and DLL loads from user-writable directories, restrict outbound DoH and traffic to ROMULUS C2 IPs including 167.76.44[.]160 and 167.148.201[.]224, and treat execution of Build.dat, senddmp.resources.dll or execute_engine_disconnect.raw as an incident. No vendor patch applies: this is social engineering and sideloading, so allowlisting and command-line monitoring are the controls.

Watch for follow-on payloads or account abuse that would overturn Blackpoint's no post-exploitation finding, and for ROMULUS adding payload families or signed host binaries beyond the six observed servers, the Stardock and Canon chains, and the documented CNCMachineRMS and LegionLoader split. Either change would turn a delivery-platform story into a broader access-broker problem.
