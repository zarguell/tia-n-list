Foreign hackers targeted operational technology systems at two private water utilities in Colorado in late August 2026, according to the governor's office. The attackers changed equipment settings, disabled remote access and alarms, and altered pumping cycles at facilities serving fewer than 200 people. Disruptions were brief and did not affect water services or public safety.

The governor's office described the attackers only as "foreign actors" and noted awareness of ongoing Iranian-linked efforts to access drinking water and wastewater systems across the United States, as flagged by CISA. It has not been confirmed whether the Colorado incidents are connected to the broader campaign that hit water sector facilities in at least a dozen states in July. Confirmed targets in that earlier wave spanned facilities in Minnesota and other states, though federal authorities have not released a complete list.

This incident fits a pattern of nation-state actors probing U.S. water infrastructure. The attackers focused on industrial control systems and OT environments rather than IT networks, using compromised access to manipulate operational parameters. Even though the impact was limited, the targeting of pumping cycles and alarm systems demonstrates intent to cause physical disruption. Defenders should monitor for similar OT-focused intrusions, particularly in small utilities with limited cybersecurity staffing.
