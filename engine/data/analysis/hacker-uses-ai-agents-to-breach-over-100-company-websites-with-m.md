A Chinese-speaking operator ran an autonomous intrusion campaign against e-commerce sites using the open-source Cairn agent harness, attempting to compromise more than 1,200 websites and gaining access to over 100 companies in the United States, Canada, Japan, and Australia. The campaign began around September 25, 2026 and ran through mid-October, with NorForge identifying 14 exposed Cairn panels in the Chinese-language AIA0001 cluster tied to more than 1,100 attack projects hosted on AS56594. Agents worked under a worker named claudecode_deepseek-v4.1-flash, which may indicate DeepSeek V4.1 Flash driven by Anthropic's Claude Code, though neither NorForge nor Reuters confirmed the models. One documented chain used unauthorized write access to a memcached session store, unsafe PHP object deserialization, and a legacy Zend Framework weakness to plant an uploader shell, and NorForge verified a live uploader shell on a large hardware-parts vendor's site.

The economics are the real story: NorForge estimates roughly $5 per task and about $5,000 in total token spend, or about $50 per successful compromise, against a measured success rate near 10 percent and an average time from project start to goal of under 23 hours. That puts mass, hands-off intrusion within reach of a single operator at a cost below a single phishing kit, and it is not theoretical: Gambit Security documented a related July-to-September campaign using Hermes, Strix, and Cairn that compromised at least 27 companies, stole more than 600,000 credit card records from two victims, and injected card skimmers on at least 19 sites. The exposed panels compound the damage because they leak target lists, findings, and harvested credentials: Trufflehog over the panel data found hundreds of secrets, 11 percent still valid, and the panels appear to expose full administrative controls for starting, stopping, and redirecting agents. This story stands out from the daily vulnerability feed because the failure is not a single unpatched CVE but an operating model in which AI agents compress reconnaissance-to-shell timelines to hours while defenders still measure remediation in weeks.

Defenders should treat this as an incident-response and exposure problem first: check e-commerce and payment-adjacent estates for uploader shells and unexpected upload forms such as confirmation.php, review memcached session stores for unauthorized writes, and audit legacy Zend Framework and PHP deserialization paths. Hunt for skimmer loaders referencing static-js.com and cdn.netlfjs.com, for Cairn panels reachable on port 8000, and for the worker name claudecode_deepseek-v4.1-flash in process or container telemetry. Rotate any secrets that may have traversed third-party panels and run Trufflehog-style scanning over code and configuration repositories, since 11 percent of the secrets NorForge recovered were still valid. Named victims Deere, SharkNinja, and Cummins reported limited impact, but two smaller businesses were confirmed compromised and NorForge validated a live shell on a large hardware-parts vendor, so absence of a public breach notice is not evidence of safety.

Watch whether hosting providers take down the exposed panels, since NorForge's takedown effort depends on host response, and whether the operator shifts to secured panels or a different harness mix after this exposure. Also watch for expansion beyond e-commerce and payment targets, for named victims disclosing data loss from the Gambit-style wipe-after-extraction routine, and for any attribution that links AIA0001 to a known group rather than an unaffiliated Chinese-speaking operator.
