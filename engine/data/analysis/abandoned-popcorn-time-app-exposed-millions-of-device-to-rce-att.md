Researchers disabled the update mechanism of Time4Popcorn, an abandoned fork of the Popcorn Time BitTorrent client, closing a flaw that could have let anyone controlling its lapsed update domains execute code with full privileges on millions of forgotten devices. The vulnerability, CVE-2026-30612, affects Windows, macOS, and Android clients whose updater contacts servers over unencrypted HTTP and accepts executables without verifying digital signatures; on Windows updates run as SYSTEM and on macOS as root.

Eye Security registered abandoned domains such as popcorntimeupd.xyz and observed 2.5 to 3 million Windows and 250,000 to 350,000 macOS hosts connecting, plus fewer than 200,000 Android devices, including systems on government and military networks. Between November 2025 and March 2026 the team pushed a harmless update that disabled the updater on more than two million systems under Operation IXIM, then transferred 15 domains to The Shadowserver Foundation for sinkholing. The app was abandoned for years and many users had forgotten it was installed.

Because no patched version exists, the software should be removed entirely; otherwise administrators can disable the updater manually, the Windows "Update service" via sc.exe or the macOS privileged helper PT.updd via launchctl. The case illustrates how abandoned software with privileged updaters becomes a persistent mass-compromise risk.
