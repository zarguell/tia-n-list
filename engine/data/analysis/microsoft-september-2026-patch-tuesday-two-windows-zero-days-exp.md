Microsoft's September 2026 Patch Tuesday includes fixes for two Windows privilege escalation zero-days already exploited in the wild: CVE-2026-85880 and CVE-2026-81963. Both carry a CVSS score of 7.8 and allow an attacker with initial local access to escalate to SYSTEM privileges, potentially turning a limited foothold into full control of a compromised device.

CVE-2026-85880 affects Windows Advanced Local Procedure Call (ALPC), while CVE-2026-81963 resides in the Windows Update Stack. Despite targeting different components and Windows versions, both share the same attack profile: low privileges required, low complexity, no user interaction, and confirmed exploitation before patches shipped. CISA added both to the KEV catalog on September 8 with a September 22 remediation deadline for federal civilian agencies.

The exploitation status before patch release indicates active campaigns are leveraging these flaws. Privilege escalation vulnerabilities are typically chained after initial access, meaning defenders should investigate whether any systems show signs of compromise prior to patch deployment. SOC Prime has published detection rules mapped to MITRE ATT&CK for both CVEs, covering behavioral patterns associated with privilege escalation and post-exploitation activity. Organizations should prioritize patching and hunt for indicators of SYSTEM-level abuse in environments where initial access may have occurred through phishing, stolen credentials, or other vectors.
