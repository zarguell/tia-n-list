A secretive DHS unit focused on predictive policing has been analyzing Americans' financial habits, according to reporting by 404 Media. The program operates under the Department of Homeland Security and appears to use financial data as an input for predictive threat assessments. Details on the scope, data sources, and decision-making process remain limited.

This raises significant civil liberties concerns. Financial data can reveal political donations, religious affiliations, medical spending, and personal relationships. Using it for predictive policing creates a surveillance infrastructure that can target individuals based on behavior that is legal but flagged as anomalous. The lack of transparency around the program's parameters, oversight mechanisms, and accountability structures compounds the risk.

What to watch: Congressional inquiry or GAO investigation into the program's legal authority and data retention policies. Look for FOIA litigation or leaked documents that clarify which financial institutions are cooperating and what analytics are being applied. Monitor whether this intersects with other DHS surveillance programs or intelligence community data-sharing arrangements.
