SOCRadar's Threat Research Unit attributes RedOps to the Vietnam-sponsored WeaveLotus actor and dates the campaign to August-September 2026. It targeted military, government and legislative organizations in Southeast Asia for intelligence on South China Sea defense and military agreements. SOCRadar confirms five intrusions in Thailand and the Philippines, with gigabytes of internal documents, project plans and credentials stolen; activity against more than 30 Indonesian government organizations remained reconnaissance.

Reported initial access came from exposed open-source web applications: GeoServer CVE-2024-36401, JasperReports default credentials and osTicket CVE-2021-42235. GeoServer's flaw allows unauthenticated remote code execution through crafted OGC parameters that cause property names to be evaluated as XPath expressions. CISA has added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on evidence of active exploitation. The combination matters because ordinary web-application weaknesses provided entry to strategic targets, while stolen credentials and documents create risk beyond the initial web tier.

Patch GeoServer to 2.22.6, 2.23.6, 2.24.4 or 2.25.2 as appropriate for the installed branch, and apply osTicket 1.14.8 or 1.15.4. If GeoServer cannot be patched immediately, its advisory describes removing gt-complex-x.y.jar or replacing the gt-app-schema, gt-complex and gt-xsd-core jars, with warnings that functionality may break. Watch for SOCRadar's promised whitepaper and IoCs, evidence that Indonesian reconnaissance became intrusion, and reuse of the same tooling outside Southeast Asia.
