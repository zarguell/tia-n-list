SOCRadar's Threat Research Unit attributes RedOps to the Vietnam-sponsored WeaveLotus actor and dates the espionage campaign to August-September 2026. It targeted military, government and legislative organizations in Southeast Asia for South China Sea defense and military-agreement intelligence, confirming five intrusions in Thailand and the Philippines with gigabytes of documents, project plans and credentials stolen; activity against more than 30 Indonesian government organizations remained reconnaissance. The reported entry paths were GeoServer CVE-2024-36401, JasperReports default credentials and osTicket CVE-2021-42235. GeoServer's flaw is unauthenticated RCE through OGC parameters that unsafely evaluate property names as XPath, rooted in GeoTools CVE-2024-36404, while CVE-2021-42235 is SQL injection in osTicket's login and password-reset flow.

The strategic signal is not a new exploit: it is a state-aligned actor using stale, ordinary web-application weaknesses to reach intelligence targets, so waiting for actor-specific malware indicators leaves the initial-access window open. Credential theft makes each successful intrusion a potential identity compromise beyond the web tier, while document and plan theft directly serves the campaign's stated collection goal. The five confirmed victims and more than 30 reconnaissance targets should not be read as the exposed population; BleepingComputer cited approximately 16,462 internet-facing GeoServer servers in July 2024, showing that one reported entry path already had global scale. That combination of confirmed government theft, regional reconnaissance and commodity access makes this more operationally significant than another scanner-only CVE report.

Patch GeoServer to 2.25.2, 2.24.4, 2.23.6 or 2.22.6 as appropriate for the installed branch, or newer, and move osTicket to 1.15.4 or 1.14.8 or newer; replace JasperReports' `jasperadmin` and `superuser` defaults and remove sample users. If GeoServer cannot be patched immediately, remove `gt-complex-x.y.jar` or use the vendor's patched `gt-app-schema`, `gt-complex` and `gt-xsd-core` jars, but test first because removal can break dependent extensions or stop deployment. Restrict direct internet access to GeoServer, JasperReports and osTicket, especially GeoServer's OGC endpoints and administrative interfaces. Hunt GeoServer access logs for crafted property-name or XPath input against WFS GetFeature, WFS GetPropertyValue, WMS GetMap, WMS GetFeatureInfo, WMS GetLegendGraphic and WPS Execute, and review osTicket authentication and password-reset logs for SQL-injection patterns. Search recovered tooling for the `redops` string as a low-specificity lead, and reset credentials exposed by any confirmed intrusion.

The next assessment turns on SOCRadar's promised whitepaper: usable IoCs and code excerpts would improve victim scoping, while their absence leaves attribution dependent on SOCRadar's reporting. Evidence that Indonesian reconnaissance became compromise, or that the same tooling appeared outside Southeast Asia, would materially widen the exposed victim set.
