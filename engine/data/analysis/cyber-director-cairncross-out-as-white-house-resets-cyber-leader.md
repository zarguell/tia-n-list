Sean Cairncross is leaving the post of U.S. National Cyber Director. Axios reported the departure first, and a White House official confirmed it to Recorded Future News. The report says President Donald Trump's chief of staff Stephen Miller pushed for the removal on the grounds that Cairncross lacked the credentials for the job. CNBC reported the same transition on November 20.

Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies was named acting cyber director, a role that advises the president on cybersecurity and coordinates digital policy across federal agencies. Davies was confirmed by the Senate for her Pentagon post about a year ago, and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Secretary of State Marco Rubio publicly congratulated her.

The change lands during a run of technology failures, including a nationwide aviation ground stop, a global Microsoft Outlook outage and disruption to the National Security Agency's classified communications network. Administration officials have said they see no indications of a coordinated cyberattack behind those outages. What to watch is whether a permanent nominee is put forward, how the acting director handles the cyber policy coordination role, and whether any post-incident findings on the outages change that public assessment.
