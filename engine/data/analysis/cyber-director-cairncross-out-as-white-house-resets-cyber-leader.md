Sean Cairncross is leaving as U.S. National Cyber Director, with Axios first reporting the exit on November 19 and a White House official confirming it to Recorded Future News. The record does not resolve why: CBS, The Independent and the Record say chief of staff Stephen Miller pushed him out over weak cyber credentials, Axios and TechCrunch describe an amicable departure timed for a Senate confirmation before Democrats take Congress in January, and Semafor quotes officials on both sides of a fired-versus-resigned dispute. Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies is acting director, a role that advises the president and coordinates federal cyber policy; Cairncross, confirmed 59-35 with no cyber background, took office in August 2025. No CVE, patch or IoC attaches to this story.

This is a coordination failure risk, not a software incident. The acting director is already the Pentagon's CIO, so the same person now carries DoD IT and the interagency cyber policy brief while a nationwide aviation ground stop, a global Microsoft Outlook outage and disruption to the NSA's classified communications network remain under investigation. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin was quoted at 98 percent confidence the outages were not malicious foreign activity and Marco Rubio said there was zero evidence of a systemic attack, but those are assessments, not post-incident findings. The desk signal is the concentration of authority during unresolved cross-sector failures, not another exploit.

There is no patch version, workaround or network restriction to apply to a staffing change, and no log path, telemetry field or IoC is named in the reporting. Defenders should keep escalation through existing incident-reporting channels, preserve the outage-window records for the aviation ground stop, Microsoft Outlook and NSA classified communications disruption, and correlate timestamps across those three sectors instead of assuming the no-coordinated-attack line closes the case. Verify who speaks for federal cyber coordination before any cross-agency request is accepted, and track the CISA nomination of Michael LoSapio alongside the permanent cyber director process.

The assessment changes if a permanent nominee is put forward before the January Senate turnover, especially one other than Davies, or if the White House issues an official account of whether this was a firing or a resignation. It also changes if post-incident findings attribute any part of the aviation, Outlook or NSA disruption to malicious activity, which would put the acting director in charge of the response to the very failures that triggered the reshuffle.
