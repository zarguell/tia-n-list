Sean Cairncross is out as National Cyber Director after just over a year in the job, with Axios first reporting the departure and a White House official confirming it to several outlets. Accounts split on whether he was fired. CBS News, Semafor sources and a person close to the White House quoted by Japan Times said chief of staff Stephen Miller had long wanted him out over questions about his expertise; other White House officials told Semafor he resigned without being asked. Cairncross took office in August 2025, was confirmed 59 to 35, and was the third Senate-confirmed head of an office created in early 2021.

Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies was named acting director, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth congratulating her publicly. A permanent appointment needs Senate confirmation, and several reports tie the timing to Democrats taking Congress in January. Davies wrote that the country must modernize and build resilience into the systems it runs on. On the same day, Michael LoSapio, Trump's nominee to lead CISA, testified at his confirmation hearing.

The leadership change lands amid a run of disruptive outages: a nationwide aviation ground stop, a global Microsoft Outlook outage and problems in the NSA's classified communications network. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said investigators saw no link between them and was 98 percent confident they were not malicious foreign activity, while Rubio said there was zero evidence of a connection. Watch whether the acting arrangement holds through confirmation and whether congressional scrutiny of the outages turns into hearings on the cyber director role itself.
