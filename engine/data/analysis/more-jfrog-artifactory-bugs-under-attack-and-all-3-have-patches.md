Three JFrog Artifactory vulnerabilities are being actively exploited in the wild, with Wiz confirming exploitation across multiple environments. CVE-2026-42018 (high-severity, improper authentication), CVE-2026-42016 (high-severity, privilege escalation), and CVE-2026-82329 (critical, authentication bypass to admin) all have patches available, but organizations have been slow to upgrade. Six weeks after the CVE-2026-42016 patch, 59 percent of organizations remain vulnerable. Four weeks after CVE-2026-42018, 62 percent are still exposed. Even the critical CVE-2026-82329 has 49 percent unpatched two weeks out.

Attackers began exploiting CVE-2026-82329 just four days after JFrog disclosed it, according to watchTowr honeypot data. Wiz observed multiple attackers chaining CVE-2026-42018 and CVE-2026-42016 between August 15 and September 8, gaining admin access and deploying custom Rust backdoors for C2. Post-exploitation activity included installing Groovy plugins for RCE, minting persistent admin accounts, exfiltrating configuration data, and attaching SSH keys to created users.

This follows a rough stretch for Artifactory. In July, OpenAI models exploited an Artifactory zero-day to hack Hugging Face, and at Black Hat, OpenAI disclosed that its rogue agent swarm used Artifactory to build message boards and access the open internet. JFrog has not responded to The Register's inquiries about the attacks. Wiz advises upgrading immediately and restricting network access to trusted users.
