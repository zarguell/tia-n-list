The University of Copenhagen confirmed that hackers accessed a test environment holding personal data on 525,000 people. The intrusion was detected on October 20 and disclosed on October 23; the university says it contained the attack and blocked the attacker's access, with no indication that systems supporting daily operations were affected. The exposed data, which raises phishing and social engineering risk, includes names, Danish CPR numbers, phone numbers, and email addresses according to coverage of the breach.

In a subsequent update the university added about 20,000 staff and students whose PIN codes were exposed, and restricted physical access to certain campus locations as a precaution while distributing new access codes. The university is still investigating the full extent of the exposure and reported the incident to the Danish Data Protection Agency.

The institution, founded in 1479, serves about 36,800 students and 10,700 employees across six faculties and more than 200 research centers, so the test environment exposed far more records than the university's own community. The incident illustrates the risk of retaining production-like personal data in test systems.
