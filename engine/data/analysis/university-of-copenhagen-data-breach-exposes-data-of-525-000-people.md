The University of Copenhagen confirmed that attackers gained access to one of its test environments holding personal data on 525,000 people, detected last Tuesday and disclosed on Friday. An update added roughly 20,000 staff and students whose PIN codes were also exposed. The compromised data includes names, Danish CPR national identifiers, phone numbers, and email addresses, raising the risk of phishing and social-engineering follow-on attacks.

The university says it contained the incident and blocked the attacker's access, with no indication that systems supporting daily operations were reached or that university activities were disrupted. It reported the breach to the Danish Data Protection Agency and, out of caution, restricted physical access to certain campus locations while new access codes are distributed.

The incident is a reminder that test environments often carry production-grade personal data and must be scoped and monitored like production. Institutions should verify that non-production systems do not retain live identifiers such as national ID numbers, and that test databases are isolated from identity and access-control systems.
