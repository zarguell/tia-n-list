The University of Copenhagen confirmed that attackers gained access to one of its test environments holding personal data belonging to 525,000 people. The breach was detected last Tuesday and disclosed on Friday, with a follow-up update adding another 20,000 staff and students whose PIN codes were exposed. The university says it contained the attack and blocked the attacker's access, and reports no indication that systems supporting daily operations were accessed or that university activities were disrupted.

Founded in 1479, the University of Copenhagen is Denmark's oldest university and one of its leading research institutions, with roughly 36,800 students and 10,700 employees across six faculties and more than 200 research centers. The institution is still investigating the full extent of the incident, but warns that the exposed information raises the risk of phishing and social engineering. Because of the PIN exposure, physical access to certain critical campus locations was restricted as a precaution and is being gradually restored as new access codes are distributed.

The university reported the incident to the Danish Data Protection authority. The available excerpt does not enumerate the specific data fields beyond PIN codes, so defenders should treat the full 525,000-record population as potentially exposed until the university publishes a complete breakdown. Watch for follow-up disclosures listing affected data categories and any regulatory or notification obligations under GDPR.
