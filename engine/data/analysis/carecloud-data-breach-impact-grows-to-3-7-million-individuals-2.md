CareCloud's data breach now affects 3,756,469 individuals, a ten-fold jump from the roughly 350,000 reported in July. The company, which provides cloud-based healthcare solutions, detected the intrusion in mid-March after a disruption to its EHR environment. Threat actors had access to one of CareCloud's AWS environments from March 10 to March 16, exfiltrating databases containing names, addresses, SSNs, driver's license numbers, dates of birth, health insurance info, and medical records. A small subset also had payment card data stolen.

The massive revised count initially looked like a clerical error, but HHS confirmed the figure is accurate. No group has claimed responsibility, and it is unclear whether CareCloud paid a ransom. The breach sits in a growing pattern of healthcare cloud infrastructure becoming the primary attack surface, following similar large-scale incidents at organizations like Amgen and Unlimited Technology Systems in recent months.

Watch for: whether CareCloud discloses the intrusion vector or attacker identity; class-action litigation timelines given the volume of PII and PHI; and any downstream regulatory action from HHS or state AGs. The gap between initial disclosure and revised impact numbers suggests many healthcare breach notifications may still be significantly understated.
