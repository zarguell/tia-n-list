A ransomware affiliate is running a social engineering side hustle. GuidePoint Security's GRIT team identified an actor using the name "Ransom Busters" to contact ransomware victims before their incidents go public, offering file recovery and data deletion from criminal infrastructure. GRIT assesses with moderate confidence that this is not a legitimate recovery service but a ransomware affiliate diverting extortion payments from the original operation. The scheme has been observed across DragonForce, Settra, and Anubis ransomware families.

The tactic exploits victim desperation. Instead of a standard ransom note, the affiliate presents itself as a helpful third party that has supposedly breached the ransomware gang's systems. Victims receive emails offering decryption keys and data deletion in exchange for payment. The timing suggests the affiliate has insider access to victim data from the initial compromise, allowing contact before public disclosure.

This represents a maturation of the ransomware ecosystem's affiliate model. Affiliates are no longer content with their cut of ransom proceeds. They are increasingly cutting out the operator entirely, re-extorting victims under a false benevolent pretense. Organizations should treat unsolicited "recovery" contacts after a ransomware incident as suspicious and verify through their incident response team before engaging.
