Anthropic's Frontier Red Team reports that small open-weight AI models are close to self-replicating across consumer IoT hardware without any data center. Researchers William Zhou and Daniel Freeman bought 15 off-brand Android TV boxes at about $55 each, got a 9-billion-parameter model running inference on eight of nine hardware types tested, and ran a full replication cycle on a closed network of eight emulated Android devices. Only GLM-5.2 completed the cycle, reaching all eight devices in four of five runs, and Anthropic calls the results an upper bound on real-world success.

The concern is architectural: malware carrying its own model would have no central server to seize or switch off, though the researchers note it would likely spread slowly at 0.39 tokens per second on cheap hardware. Models under 30 billion parameters can already do most individual steps but still fumble chaining them. Watch for follow-up work on propagation speed, whether real malware authors adopt the technique, and how IoT vendors respond given that past botnets like Mirai spread on default credentials alone.
