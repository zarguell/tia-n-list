Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange, has denied claims of a new security breach made by a threat actor using the 'ShinyHunters' handle on a newly emerged underground cybercrime forum. The actor, believed to be impersonating the ShinyHunters extortion gang, put up for sale what they claimed were records of 335 million Binance users, including KYC documents, internal infrastructure information, account passwords, crypto wallet private keys, transaction history, emails, and phone numbers, asking $900,000 and threatening to leak the data. Binance said it found no evidence of a breach and called the listing likely a scam, noting that the company does not hold private keys for users' external wallets, does not store passwords in plaintext, and no longer operates in some of the regions referenced.

The claim is unverified and several details conflict with how Binance stores user data, which supports the company's position that the material may come from infostealer malware collected from users' own devices rather than a compromise of Binance systems. Similar past listings have followed that pattern. There is no confirmed breach, no CVE, and no evidence of compromised systems at this point; the incident is a claimed sale plus a denial.

What to watch is whether the actor publishes samples that can be independently verified, whether any regulator or third party corroborates a compromise, and whether Binance's account of the data inconsistencies holds up. Until sample verification or an independent finding emerges, treat the listing as unconfirmed.
