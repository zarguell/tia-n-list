Apple confirmed it sent threat notifications on August 13, 2026 to targeted users in 110 countries, warning them of spyware attacks against their iPhones. The notifications are Apple's mechanism for alerting individuals it believes are targeted by state-sponsored surveillance campaigns, and the breadth of the alert indicates a wide, multi-region targeting effort rather than an isolated incident.

A follow-up report from Germany advised recipients to treat their iPhones as already compromised, reflecting the severity of the surveillance tools involved. The available detail is thin: no specific spyware family, actor, or CVE is named in the coverage, and the only concrete facts are the date, the country count, and the German guidance. The gap between the alert and public technical detail is typical for these cases, where Apple and researchers disclose indicators privately to victims first.

For at-risk users, the practical steps are the standard ones for targeted surveillance: update iOS promptly, enable Lockdown Mode if warranted, and follow Apple's threat-notification guidance. Organizations with high-risk staff should ensure those users know how to recognize and report such alerts, since the first notification may be the only early warning.
