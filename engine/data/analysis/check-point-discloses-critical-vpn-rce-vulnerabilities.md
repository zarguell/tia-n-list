Check Point disclosed two critical CVSS 9.8 vulnerabilities in its VPN gateway technology: CVE-2026-85102, an authentication bypass combined with remote code execution, and CVE-2026-85103, a related flaw in the same VPN stack. Both affect Remote Access VPN and Site-to-Site VPN functionality on Check Point security gateways. Successful exploitation allows an unauthenticated attacker to execute arbitrary code on internet-facing VPN appliances without any credentials, potentially gaining a foothold at the network perimeter for lateral movement, credential theft, or ransomware deployment. Check Point said its internal research team discovered and remediated both issues and currently has no indication of exploitation in the wild.

A third vulnerability, CVE-2026-91843, affects Check Point's management interface. It is a critical stack overflow that permits unauthenticated remote root access to the management plane. Check Point issued a patch alongside the VPN fixes. CERT-In advisory CIVN-2026-045 urges immediate patching, and the vulnerabilities have drawn coverage from over 20 sources including BleepingComputer, SecurityWeek, The Hacker News, and official Check Point support channels.

The combined impact is severe: any organization running internet-facing Check Point VPN or management interfaces faces a credible risk of complete gateway compromise without authentication. The lack of known exploitation in the wild so far does not diminish the urgency, since these are the types of flaws that attract rapid weaponization once technical details circulate. Organizations should prioritize patching and verify that management interfaces are not exposed to untrusted networks.
