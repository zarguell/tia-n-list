Check Point disclosed three critical vulnerabilities in its VPN gateway software: CVE-2026-85102, CVE-2026-85103, and CVE-2026-91843. The flaws allow unauthenticated remote attackers to execute arbitrary commands on VPN gateways without valid credentials. Check Point rated all three as critical and released patches, but exploitation has been reported in the wild since disclosure.

The most severe issue, CVE-2026-91843, is a login flaw that enables remote takeover of the gateway itself. The other two CVEs involve certificate handling weaknesses that can be chained for full pre-authentication code execution. With 18 different sources covering this story within a week, it has clearly attracted widespread attention from both defenders and attackers. Check Point's VPN appliances sit at the network perimeter, meaning a compromised gateway gives attackers direct access to internal resources behind the VPN.

Organizations should patch immediately. Check Point released hotfixes for R81.20 and R81.40 releases. If patching is delayed, disconnecting the VPN management interface from untrusted networks reduces exposure, though it does not eliminate the risk for actively exploited configurations. The breadth of coverage and the pre-authentication nature of these flaws make this one of the more consequential VPN disclosures of 2026.
