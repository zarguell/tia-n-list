Berlin's city administration confirmed that the Rhysida ransomware group exfiltrated data from its network and is now attempting extortion. The attack was discovered in mid-August, and Rhysida listed Berlin on its leak site on August 28. The attackers claim to have stolen 5.79 TB comprising approximately 1.44 million files, including government, legal, financial, and HR records, thousands of personal identifiers, 148 IBANs, plaintext credentials, password vault data, personnel files, and classified government material including Bundesrat committee records. Mayor Kai Wergner stated the city will not pay. The State Criminal Police Office, public prosecutor, and federal security agencies are investigating.

Rhysida has been active since mid-2023, targeting healthcare, education, government, and critical infrastructure. The Berlin breach is notable for its scale and the sensitivity of the claimed data. Government ransomware incidents carry political pressure that private sector breaches do not, and the decision not to pay sets a posture other municipal governments will watch closely.

The key question is whether Rhysida follows through on leaking the data. Previous government targets have seen partial or full dumps after refused payment. If the claimed content is accurate, the exposure of classified government records and individual financial data (including IBANs) creates both national security and identity fraud risks. Monitor the Rhysida leak site for data publication and watch for further government statements on scope and impact.
