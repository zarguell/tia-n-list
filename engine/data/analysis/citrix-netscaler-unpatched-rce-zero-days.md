Two unpatched remote code execution zero-days in Citrix NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway were confirmed by watchTowr on September 26, 2026, and the exploitation evidence came out of incident response forensics on victim appliances, not from scanner noise. The disclosure actually ran ahead of the vendor: a Reddit r/Citrix thread posted at 06:43 UTC that day had sysadmins reporting shutdown advice from their suppliers with nothing published anywhere else, a thread Tenable ties to a Dutch NCSC pre-notification distributed TLP:AMBER+STRICT, and watchTowr was publicly reacting to the rumors on X by 16:57 UTC. As of September 27 there are no CVE IDs, no CVSS scores, no Citrix advisory and no patch, with fixes expected early in the week of September 28. Tenable states the pair is unrelated to the August bugs CVE-2026-19490 and CVE-2026-19489, the former patched August 19 in CTX696939 and added to the CISA KEV catalog September 9.

The shutdown advice that rippled through NetScaler shops on September 26 and 27 is not overreaction, it is the rational response to unauthenticated memory corruption on the one appliance that terminates untrusted traffic and holds decrypted sessions in memory. Tenable counts 13 NetScaler entries in CISA's KEV catalog and 24 for Citrix products overall, and estimates roughly two thirds of threat actor activity against NetScaler over the past seven years came from APT groups with the remaining third from ransomware crews; CitrixBleed (CVE-2023-4966) and CitrixBleed 2 (CVE-2025-5777) both began as pre-auth flaws on this same appliance line and both carry known ransomware association in Tenable's KEV table. That history is the argument: a gateway zero-day with no vendor indicators converts directly into stolen VPN sessions and a foothold behind the perimeter, which is why nine separate sources plus threat intel packages carrying 9 detections and 10 to 14 IoCs picked this up within 19 hours. The leak also broke the quiet period coordinated disclosure depends on, so attackers have had the flaw's existence longer than Citrix has acknowledged it, and any internet-facing NetScaler edge should be treated as targeted until telemetry proves otherwise.

Inventory every internet-facing NetScaler ADC and Gateway now. Where the appliance fronts VPN or AAA traffic, take it offline or restrict inbound TCP/443 to managed source addresses, never 0.0.0.0/0, and where Gateway and AAA features go unused, disable the vpn vserver. Deploy the community exploit-attempt signature built on the watchTowr advisory, the SH3LLC0D3 Suricata rule sid:202608901 mirrored as rulezet rule 770971, and pull the threadlinqs packages TL-2026-2682, TL-2026-2688 and TL-2026-2693 for their detections and IoCs. On appliances that must stay up, hunt from the FreeBSD shell: read /flash/nsconfig/rc.netscaler for appended lines, check /etc/crontab for added jobs, look for files modified in the last few days under /netscaler/ns_gui/ and /var/netscaler/gui/, and inspect /var/crash/ and /var/core/ for unexplained dumps. Any sign of compromise means imaging the box, revoking its certificates and keys, and forcing password and session resets for everyone who authenticated through the gateway in the past 30 days.

Two developments move this assessment. The first is the Citrix bulletin expected early in the week of September 28: whether it covers one flaw or both, and which firmware branches receive builds, decides who can patch immediately and who stays in containment. The second is scale: watchTowr's full technical writeup, publication of the NCSC-NL notification, or the first GreyNoise-class mass scanning against NetScaler edges would convert a targeted incident-response finding into a commodity campaign, at which point shutdown stops being a defensible posture and patch-and-hunt becomes the only option.
