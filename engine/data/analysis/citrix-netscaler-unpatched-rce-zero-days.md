Citrix spent the first week of October handling a chain of actively exploited NetScaler zero-days. The core pair, CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772, are pre-authentication flaws in NetScaler ADC and Gateway; watchTowr verified reports of unpatched remote code execution and many organizations shut down or urgently patched exposed appliances. Exploitation is confirmed beyond doubt: LevelBlue's threat hunt team documented attackers moving from authentication anomalies to reverse shells, privileged account creation and configuration theft, and eSentire separately tracked exploitation activity. Both flaws carry CISA KEV listing with an October 7 remediation deadline for federal agencies.

Mid-remediation, a third flaw landed. CVE-2026-88779 is a memory overflow in the SAML parser affecting customer-managed appliances configured as SAML SP or IdP, rated CVSS 8.7. Citrix describes unauthenticated remote denial of service as the confirmed impact and has not established RCE, but it was exploited as a zero-day in targeted attacks before a fix existed. CISA added it to KEV on October 4 with the same October 7 deadline. watchTowr's Jake Knott told CyberScoop the SAML prerequisite and DoS impact make it far less alarming than the RCE pair, though the timing, while customers were still re-patching, maximized disruption.

The practical exposure is broad because NetScaler sits at the network edge as an application delivery and remote access gateway, and appliance management interfaces have repeatedly proven exploitable in this product line. LevelBlue's observations show intrusion activity, not just scanning.

Watch for exploit chains that escalate the SAML flaw past denial of service, wider reporting on how many appliances remain unpatched after the deadline, and any fourth disclosure, since the cadence of one zero-day every few days has not clearly ended.
