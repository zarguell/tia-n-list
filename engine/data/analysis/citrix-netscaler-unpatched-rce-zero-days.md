Two actively exploited Citrix NetScaler zero-days, CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772, were patched in emergency advisory CTX697096 on September 27, 2026, part of a bulletin covering eight NetScaler ADC and Gateway flaws. CVE-2026-88771 is a pre-authentication command injection via improper input validation, CVSS v4 9.5, affecting every NetScaler ADC and Gateway deployment including default configurations, with low attack complexity. CVE-2026-88772 is a DTLS preauth memory overflow allowing RCE or denial of service, requiring DTLS, which is enabled by default on VPN virtual servers, with high attack complexity. Citrix confirmed both were exploited as zero-days before disclosure and CISA added both to the KEV catalog the same day, ordering federal agencies to patch by Wednesday.

The disclosure process drew criticism. Reports began September 25 on Reddit when IT suppliers, CERTs, and law enforcement told administrators to shut NetScalers down, tracing to a TLP:AMBER pre-notification from the Dutch NCSC-NL citing a European partner CERT. watchTowr verified the flaws, published analyses of both, and released a PoC for CVE-2026-88771 on GitHub; a crash PoC also appeared. Citrix confirmed exploitation publicly roughly two days later, a gap watchTowr's CEO called a harmful information vacuum. GreyNoise observed the IP 149.104.78.141 attempting zero-day exploitation against a NetScaler Gateway on September 24, before the rumors surfaced. IOCs are available from Citrix.

Given exposed NetScaler VPN infrastructure and public exploit code, assume unpatched appliances are being hit now. Watch for confirmed compromises and data theft follow-on, additional CVEs in the bulletin getting exploit traffic, and regulators adding requirements. The story compounds an August problem: the CVE-2026-19490 authentication bypass, patched August 19, began drawing real exploitation attempts on September 3 per Previdian sensors.
