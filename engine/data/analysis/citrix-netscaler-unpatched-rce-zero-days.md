Citrix closed out its third NetScaler emergency of 2026 after confirming two pre-auth RCE zero-days were exploited before a patch existed. CVE-2026-88771 (CVSS 9.5) is an unauthenticated command injection affecting all NetScaler ADC and Gateway deployments in default configurations; CVE-2026-88772 (CVSS 9.5) is a DTLS pre-auth memory overflow giving remote code execution or denial of service where DTLS is enabled, which it is by default on VPN virtual servers. Fixed builds are 14.1-73.37 and 13.1-64.23. The disclosure ran backwards: shutdown advice surfaced on r/Citrix on September 25, watchTowr verified the exploitation on September 26 while the flaws still had no CVE IDs, and Citrix's advisory (CTX697096, covering both plus six additional defects) landed September 27. WatchTowr's CEO publicly criticized the information vacuum, since CERTs, insurers, and the Dutch NCSC warned customers privately for nearly two days while Citrix stayed silent. Researchers dubbed the pair PitScaler and watchTowr published a working exploit.

Exploitation is confirmed and predates disclosure. GreyNoise observed exploitation attempts on September 24 from 149.104.78[.]141, three days before the advisory. Mandiant and Google Threat Intelligence Group place the campaign since at least early September, running undetected for at least three weeks in some environments and hitting government, financial services, education, legal, and professional services organizations across North America and Europe. Post-exploitation goes straight to root: attackers modify the appliance web server configuration to plant a PHP web shell named WHIPSHOT running as root, paired with a Python tunneling tool named SLAPSHOT. Truesec listed C2 infrastructure including 104.248.244[.]66 and 139.180.152[.]138. CISA added both CVEs to the KEV catalog on September 27 and ordered federal agencies to patch by Wednesday; hospitals in the Netherlands closed patient portals preventively during the uncertainty window.

NetScaler terminates authentication for huge chunks of enterprise and government traffic, so root on the appliance means session hijacking, MFA bypass, and a stepping stone into the internal network. Any appliance that was internet-facing before patching must be treated as compromised: hunt for WHIPSHOT and SLAPSHOT, unexplained web server configuration changes, and the listed C2 addresses, and rotate credentials and secrets that transited the device. Context matters for prioritization: this follows the exploited CVE-2026-19490 authentication bypass in August, meaning sustained adversary interest in the platform rather than a one-off. Watch for victim disclosures, exploitation detail on the additional six defects (CVE-2026-88773 through 88778), and fallout from the disclosure-delay criticism.
