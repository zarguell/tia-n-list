Citrix confirmed and patched two actively exploited NetScaler zero-days, disclosed Sunday September 27-28 as CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772, with six additional defects fixed in the same advisory. Researchers have dubbed the pair PitScaler. CVE-2026-88771 is an unauthenticated remote code execution flaw affecting all NetScaler ADC and Gateway deployments, reported around CVSS 9.5. CVE-2026-88772 is a memory overflow that can lead to remote code execution or denial of service when DTLS is enabled. Citrix confirmed both were exploited on unmitigated deployments, and watchTowr verified the exploitation reports before the vendor said anything publicly.

The exploitation is serious and broad. Mandiant and Google Threat Intelligence Group published their own report on September 29 on defending against the active exploitation of NetScaler ADC and Gateway appliances, and it says attacks began at least in early September and hit organizations in North America and Europe across government, financial services, education, legal, and professional services. Observed tradecraft includes custom web shells, tunneling malware, root access, credential theft, and lateral movement into internal networks. The disclosure process failed defenders: over the preceding weekend, IT suppliers, CERTs, and national cyber agencies privately warned customers, some advising them to shut appliances down entirely, after an NCSC-NL pre-notification circulated TLP:AMBER+STRICT, while Citrix stayed publicly silent for nearly two days. watchTowr's CEO called the information vacuum striking and noted hours matter during active exploitation.

This story also carries the August wave. Cloud Software Group's August 19 bulletin fixed CVE-2026-19490, a CVSS v4 9.3 authentication bypass (CWE-288) affecting Gateway configurations (SSL VPN, ICA Proxy, Clientless VPN, RDP Proxy) and AAA virtual servers, and CVE-2026-19489, a denial-of-service flaw. A credible PoC for CVE-2026-19490 appeared, and on September 3 Previdian sensors saw matching requests from three source IPs in Australia, the US, and Germany.

Apply the September patches immediately and treat internet-facing NetScaler appliances as compromised if they ran unpatched builds since early September: hunt for web shells, unexpected tunnels, and credential reuse. The repeated pattern with NetScaler (Citrix Bleed, CVE-2025-7775, now PitScaler) justifies assuming edge appliances are attacker-adjacent by default and isolating management planes accordingly.
