Two unpatched remote code execution zero-days in Citrix NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway were found exploited during incident response forensics, per watchTowr reporting relayed by CyberKendra, The Hacker News and others. With no bulletin and no CVEs, the only guidance in circulation for most of September 27 was to pull internet-facing appliances offline or restrict management access hard, and multiple outlets documented administrators doing exactly that. Citrix broke its silence the same evening with security bulletin CTX697096, assigning eight CVEs, CVE-2026-88771 through CVE-2026-88778, and confirming that exploits of CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 have been observed on unmitigated NetScaler deployments.

The confirmed-exploitation pairing matters: the two observed CVEs are the operational priority, while the remaining six need triage on exposure grounds alone. These flaws are distinct from the patched August pair (CVE-2026-19490 authentication bypass, CVE-2026-19489 denial of service, tracked in a separate story), and pre-existing exploitation noise against NetScaler edges since the September 3 auth-bypass attempts makes it easy to conflate old scanning with the new zero-day signals. Patch to the builds specified in CTX697096, verify every internet-facing ADC and Gateway appliance, and treat pre-patch web and management logs as potentially compromised.

What to watch: the mapping between the eight CVEs and the two watchTowr-reported RCEs, and which carries CVSS 10-level severity; watchTowr's full technical writeup and published indicators; mass-scanning or payload telemetry that would move this from targeted incident response to broad campaign; and whether CISA adds the actively exploited pair to the KEV catalog.
