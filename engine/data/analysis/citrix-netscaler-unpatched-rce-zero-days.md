Citrix confirmed on September 27, 2026 that two NetScaler zero-days were exploited in the wild before patches existed: CVE-2026-88771, an unauthenticated RCE from improper input validation (CVSS v4.0 9.5) affecting all NetScaler ADC and Gateway deployments in default configuration, and CVE-2026-88772, a memory overflow allowing RCE or denial of service when DTLS is enabled, which is on by default for VPN virtual servers. Both were added to CISA's KEV catalog the same day, with a deadline for federal agencies the following Wednesday. They sit inside bulletin CTX697096 covering eight issues (CVE-2026-88771 through 88778) that also include HTTP request smuggling, denial of service, policy bypass and TCP sequence prediction flaws, fixed in builds 14.1-73.37 and the 13.1-63.23/13.1-37.279 FIPS lines.

The disclosure was unusual. On September 25, NetScaler administrators reported on Reddit that IT suppliers, CERTs and national cyber agencies were privately telling them to shut appliances down, advice traced to a TLP:AMBER+STRICT pre-notification from the Dutch NCSC-NL citing a European partner CERT. watchTowr publicly validated the reports on September 26 before CVE IDs existed and has since published technical analysis plus a PoC repository for CVE-2026-88771, with a separate crash PoC for CVE-2026-88772 appearing on GitHub. This is the third NetScaler emergency since June, following the August CVE-2026-19490 and CVE-2026-19489 disclosures, which Citrix says are unrelated.

Impact has been concrete: hospitals in the Dutch Brabant and Friesland regions preemptively took patient portals offline, and Truesec listed suspected C2 addresses (104.248.244.66, 139.180.152.138, 77.83.199.39). Citrix's advisory states exploitation of both CVEs on unmitigated deployments has been observed, and Unit 42 is tracking zero-day activity against NetScaler devices.

Patch to 14.1-73.37 (or the current 13.1/FIPS equivalents) is the priority, but CISA and Citrix both urge checking for compromise before remediating, since exploitation predates the fix. Any internet-facing NetScaler should be treated as potentially breached: audit for unexpected processes and persistence, review the Citrix-published IOCs, and watch for reuse of the Truesec C2 infrastructure.
