OpenAI has announced a "Defense Factory" initiative designed to continuously discover, validate, remediate, and verify vulnerabilities using agent-driven cybersecurity operations. The announcement acknowledges that increasingly capable open-weight models are enabling attackers to automate reconnaissance, chain exploits across sessions, and pursue complex attack paths at machine speed. OpenAI frames this as a closing "defender's window": defenders currently have an advantage because their AI agents can access proprietary source code, asset inventories, and internal security findings, while attackers must rely on external information. But as agents become capable of running long-lived, multi-step operations, that asymmetry erodes.

The announcement comes as multiple research groups have demonstrated AI agents finding real vulnerabilities. Google Mandiant reported its AI agents identified over 100 critical vulns, and Proofpoint's BlueMoon exploit kit analysis showed state-aligned actors rapidly chaining Chrome V8 flaws with Windows privilege escalations. OpenAI's pitch is that defenders should adopt the same persistent, agent-driven approach before attackers do.

The practical implication is that the vulnerability management lifecycle is compressing. A flaw that once required days of manual research could eventually be discovered, chained, and exploited before a security team finishes triage. Organizations should evaluate whether their patch management and incident response processes can operate at agent speed, because the adversaries are already building toward that cadence.
