The FBI issued FLASH-20261113-001 on November 13 warning that multiple threat actors have weaponized ARTEX, an open-source autonomous penetration testing framework written in Go, since at least August 2026. ARTEX integrates external large language models, including Anthropic Claude, OpenAI GPT, DeepSeek, GLM, Grok, and Kimi, and uses AI agents to run reconnaissance, discover vulnerabilities, exploit systems, escalate privileges, and steal data with little human intervention. The advisory says some intrusions progressed from initial targeting to privileged access in under three hours, and victims span the financial, government, technology, defense, and healthcare sectors, including a recent ConnectWise supply-chain breach that compromised Microsoft 365 customer environments.

The ecosystem around ARTEX is broad. Cisco Talos says these frameworks compress work that took skilled teams hours or days and points to Hephaestus, PentAGI, and HexStrike AI as peers. Wiz reported Storm-0940 using ARTEX against IT and software vendors. Amazon Threat Intelligence says Chinese-nexus actors used ARTEX to steal LLM API credentials, with MadPot decoy captures between October 3 and November 5, 2026. GMO Cybersecurity found ARTEX on attack servers targeting about 20 Japanese companies, and South Korean officials have linked AI agents to bank breaches.

The risk is speed and accessibility: the FBI says the framework lets actors with minimal technical expertise run complex, multi-target operations at once. GBHackers controlled testing found ARTEX-derived agents chaining Nmap, PsExec, and Windows Remote Management, switching techniques after a step failed, and generating detectable network activity even when they did not reach their objective, which means the framework accelerates familiar techniques rather than inventing new ones. Defenders should look for ARTEX artifacts, exposed instances, and stolen LLM API keys, and should review exposure through ConnectWise and Microsoft 365 supply chains. Watch the FBI advisory for IOCs and for further attribution as campaigns are uncovered.
