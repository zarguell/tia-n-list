Cisco Talos reports growing in-the-wild use of AI-enabled penetration-testing frameworks that automate and chain reconnaissance, vulnerability testing, and follow-on actions, lowering the skill bar for attackers and speeding up intrusions. The frameworks stitch together common open-source tools such as nmap and nuclei with internet-mapping services like Censys and Shodan, plus CAPTCHA bypass, credential-leak handling, residential proxies, and hash cracking. Talos observed full automation of the attack chain in cases such as Hephaestus, a command-line tool deployable from a single script on a VPS and controllable over Telegram, and PentAGI, which exposes a web interface with an attack-chain graph; Talos found more than 700 PentAGI instances reachable on the internet. HexStrike AI adds MCP integration with clients such as Claude Code and VS Code.

ARTEX is the framework drawing the most attention. An FBI flash alert says it significantly reduces the time needed to chain attack techniques, with some intrusions moving from initial targeting to privileged access in under three hours. Talos notes ARTEX has let threat actors scale offensive operations, compressing work that previously took skilled teams hours or days into far shorter timelines.

What to watch: wider adoption of these frameworks by lower-skill actors, the exposure of self-hosted instances such as PentAGI, and whether defenders can match the automation pace with equally fast detection and response. Organizations should treat exposed AI pentest consoles and MCP-connected tooling as high-risk assets and monitor for the reconnaissance and exploitation patterns these frameworks generate.
