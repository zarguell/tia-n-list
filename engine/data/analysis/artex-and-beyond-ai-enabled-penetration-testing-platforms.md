Cisco Talos is tracking a wave of AI-enabled penetration-testing frameworks that automate and chain reconnaissance, exploitation, and follow-on action, and that are being used in real intrusions rather than only in red-team engagements. These frameworks wrap free and open-source tools such as nmap and nuclei and bolt on internet mapping services including Censys and Shodan, CAPTCHA bypass services, credential-leak management, residential proxies, and hash cracking. They differ mainly in packaging: Hephaestus deploys with a single script on a VPS and is driven entirely through Telegram, PentAGI exposes a web interface with a full attack-chain graph, and Talos found more than 700 reachable PentAGI instances on the internet. HexStrike AI plugs into MCP-capable clients such as Claude Code and VS Code. ARTEX is the one with confirmed criminal and espionage use, compressing work that took skilled teams hours or days into intrusions that reach privileged access in under three hours, a figure the FBI echoed in FLASH-20261113-001 on November 13, 2026, describing attacks at machine speed since August 2026 across reconnaissance, exploitation, privilege escalation, and data exfiltration.

The ARTEX reporting accumulated across vendors points the same direction. The Hacker News tied ARTEX to data theft at South Korean financial firms in late September to early October 2026, Wiz tracked Storm-0940 using it against IT and software vendors including a ConnectWise ScreenConnect supply-chain compromise throughout October, GitGuardian catalogued 45 comparable tools, Unit 42 wrote on what defenders need to know, TechCrunch reported hundreds of ARTEX instances left publicly exposed via Censys, and Obsidian Security framed the shift as agentic exploitation measured in hours rather than days. SentinelOne Labs added its own analysis on November 14. Separately, Amazon Threat Intelligence uncovered financially motivated Chinese-nexus campaigns using ARTEX to steal large language model API credentials and feed them back into attack infrastructure, capturing the agents' operational memory through its MadPot decoy network between October 3 and November 5, 2026.

Defenders should treat exposed framework consoles as attack surface in their own right: remove PentAGI, HexStrike, and similar management interfaces from the public internet, require authentication and network allowlisting, and ingest published ARTEX-associated command-and-control address lists as network indicators. Because the intrusion timeline from first probe to privileged access can close inside three hours, detection engineering matters more than signature coverage, and alert triage should prioritize chained reconnaissance, credential access, and lateral movement completing in a single session. Two things would change the picture: a vulnerability or default credential in a framework console turning the 700-odd exposed PentAGI instances into a mass-compromise vector, and adoption by additional state-aligned actors beyond Storm-0940.
