ARTEX is an open-source autonomous penetration testing framework written in Go, released in July 2026, that wraps external large language models including Anthropic Claude, OpenAI GPT, DeepSeek, GLM, Grok and Kimi, and drives reconnaissance, vulnerability discovery, exploitation, privilege escalation and data theft with little human input. The FBI described criminal use of it in FLASH-20261113-001 on 13 November, saying multiple threat actors have weaponized ARTEX since at least August 2026, that some intrusions progressed from initial targeting to privileged access in under three hours, and that victims span the financial, government, technology, defense and healthcare sectors, including a ConnectWise supply-chain breach that reached Microsoft 365 customer environments.

The vendor research is consistent. Cisco Talos places ARTEX alongside Hephaestus, PentAGI and HexStrike AI as frameworks that compress work skilled teams used to take hours or days over. Wiz reported Storm-0940 using ARTEX against IT and software vendors. Amazon Threat Intelligence attributes ARTEX use to Chinese-nexus actors stealing LLM API credentials, and researchers and Amazon have separately flagged an automated campaign against AI services built on the same tooling. GMO Cybersecurity found ARTEX on attack servers aimed at about 20 Japanese companies, and South Korean officials have linked AI agents to bank breaches.

Controlled testing by GBHackers found ARTEX-derived agents chaining Nmap, PsExec and Windows Remote Management and switching techniques after a step failed, generating detectable network activity even when they did not reach the objective. Qualys makes the operational point plainly: ARTEX does not exploit new classes of vulnerability, it automates the discovery, chaining and retrying of existing weaknesses across many targets at once, and observed campaigns reach privileged access faster than the median 7 days most customers take to fix a critical flaw.

Defenders should identify and approve these tools before a penetration testing provider runs one against their estate, look for ARTEX artifacts and internet-exposed instances, and treat stolen LLM API keys as a first-class secret to rotate. The detection opportunity is behavioral: bursts of enumeration followed by technique switching across many hosts, and tool-generated traffic that continues after an objective fails. Watch the FBI advisory for indicators and for further attribution as campaigns are uncovered.
