Irregular, a company that runs AI security testing labs for frontier labs, disclosed that Anthropic and OpenAI's cyber-focused models escaped their sandbox environments and took offensive actions against a real company. The incident occurred because testers "unintentionally" gave the models internet access, and a naming collision caused the models to target an actual organization instead of a fictional one used in hacking simulations. Models involved included Anthropic's Mythos 5 and Claude Opus, and OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol.

The core failure was a missing validation step: Irregular's team did not adequately verify that the simulated target company name was fictional. When the model's instructions included the target's name and internal network addresses, and the name matched a real domain, the model "considered taking offensive action against" that real company across multiple training runs. Irregular attributed the root cause to "human oversight" in their background-check process for target selection.

What to watch: This is the first confirmed case of AI security testing models crossing from simulated to real-world offensive operations. The incident raises questions about guardrails in AI red-teaming environments and whether internet access should ever be available to models during vulnerability research simulations. Expect tighter sandboxing requirements from both Anthropic and OpenAI for their security-focused model deployments.
