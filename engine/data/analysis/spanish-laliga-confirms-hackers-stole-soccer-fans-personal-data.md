LaLiga disclosed a cyberattack on one of its fan-services technology platforms, with BleepingComputer reporting that the intrusion ran from November 14 to 16, 2026. The league says a cybercriminal gained unauthorized access and extracted personal data belonging to users registered for digital services offered by LaLiga and some affiliated clubs. Its security team identified the source on November 16, blocked the access and stopped the extraction.

The league says the incident was confined to that platform and did not reach the systems that run its competitions. It reported the matter to the Spanish Data Protection Agency, opened an investigation into the scope and added monitoring for further attempts. No actor, entry vector, record count or ransom claim appears in the disclosure, so the size of the exposure remains unknown.

The affected service covers fans of clubs with global audiences, including Real Madrid, FC Barcelona and Atletico Madrid, and the notice leaves club-by-club impact open. Watch for the investigation's findings, any regulator action in Spain, notices from individual clubs and phishing that uses LaLiga or club branding. The November 24 BleepingComputer post repeats the same November window and stolen-data confirmation without adding a victim count or attribution.
