LaLiga disclosed a cyberattack on one of its fan-services technology platforms. The league says a cybercriminal had unauthorized access between November 14 and 16, 2026, and extracted personal data belonging to users registered for digital services offered by LaLiga and some affiliated clubs. Its security team identified the source of the access on November 16, blocked the attack and stopped the data extraction.

The league says the incident was confined to that platform and did not spread to the systems that run its competitions. It reported the matter to the Spanish Data Protection Agency, opened an investigation into the scope and added monitoring for further attempts. No actor, entry vector, record count or ransom claim appears in the disclosure, so the size of the exposure is still unknown.

This matters because the affected service covers fans of clubs with global audiences, including Real Madrid, FC Barcelona and Atlético Madrid, and the notification leaves club-by-club impact open. Watch for the investigation's scope findings, any regulator action in Spain, notices from individual clubs and phishing that uses LaLiga or club branding. The league's statement does not name a CVE or a second affected system, so claims of wider compromise are unsupported so far.
