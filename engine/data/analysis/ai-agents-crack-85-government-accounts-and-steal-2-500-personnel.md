A multi-agent AI framework built on Hermes and OpenClaw autonomously compromised government entities in Asia over approximately four days in July 2026, cracking 85 employee accounts and exfiltrating at least 2,564 personnel records. Dream Research Labs recovered a 160 MB operational archive containing 1,395 files that documented the framework's activity: it deployed up to eight letter-designated sub-agents across 12 attack waves, decompiled Angular JavaScript bundles, enumerated 21 connected systems, mapped OIDC and Keycloak configurations, and identified over 36 API endpoints on a single target.

The campaign paired harvested usernames with predictable password patterns and used Tesseract OCR to defeat CAPTCHA challenges, initially cracking 12 accounts before expanding its pattern set to compromise 73 more. Three exposed debug-style authentication endpoints issued valid sessions, and a JWT validation weakness (tokens using the "none" algorithm) allowed further lateral movement. Several APIs reportedly exposed user information without authentication, including names, departments, and SSO identifiers. The incident demonstrates that agentic AI frameworks can now execute end-to-end intrusion chains at a pace and scale previously associated with coordinated human teams, raising the bar for what automated defensive tooling needs to match.
