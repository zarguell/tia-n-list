CareCloud, a publicly traded U.S. healthcare IT company providing electronic health records, medical billing, and revenue-cycle services, disclosed a data breach impacting 3,756,469 individuals. The breach occurred between March 10 and March 16, 2026, when an unauthorized third party accessed one of CareCloud's AWS environments and claimed to have exfiltrated data from databases within that environment. CareCloud initially reported the incident to the SEC in March, noting an 8-hour network disruption and loss of access to one database. Breach notifications began going out July 25.

The compromised AWS environment contained patient data, though the notification letter shared with HHS does not specify the exact data types beyond full names. CareCloud serves healthcare organizations with EHR, practice management, and billing systems, meaning the breach could expose protected health information across multiple provider networks. The company is offering affected individuals 12 to 24 months of credit monitoring.

What to watch: whether CareCloud confirms the data types beyond names (diagnoses, billing records, insurance IDs), how many healthcare providers were downstream of the compromised AWS environment, and whether the attacker's claim of exfiltration is verified. Healthcare data commands premium prices on underground markets, and a breach of this scale at a shared-services provider has downstream exposure to dozens or hundreds of practices.
