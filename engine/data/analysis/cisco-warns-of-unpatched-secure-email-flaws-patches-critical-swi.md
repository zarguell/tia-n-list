Cisco has patched a critical remote code execution vulnerability in Nexus 9000 Series Switches running Silicon One ASICs, while simultaneously warning that several vulnerabilities in Cisco Secure Email and other products remain unpatched. The Nexus 9000 flaw allows unauthenticated attackers to execute code as root on affected switches, which are widely deployed in enterprise data centers and service provider networks. Cisco also disclosed vulnerabilities in IOS XR Software, Cisco Desk Phone 9800 Series, IP Phone 7800/8800 Series, Video Phone 8875, and Wireless IP Phone 8821.

The Nexus 9000 RCE is the highest priority. These switches form the backbone of many large networks, and unauthenticated RCE at the root level means an attacker could intercept traffic, modify routing, or pivot to other network segments. Cisco's advisory (AV26-876) was published September 3, 2026. Network operators running Nexus 9000 with Silicon One should apply the patch immediately.

The unpatched Secure Email vulnerabilities are concerning because Cisco Secure Email (formerly Email Security Appliance) sits at the network perimeter handling inbound email for many enterprises. Without patches, organizations relying on Cisco email security may be exposed to attacks targeting the appliance itself. Cisco has not disclosed whether these vulnerabilities are being exploited, but perimeter email gateways are high-value targets. Administrators should monitor for follow-up advisories and consider compensating controls.
