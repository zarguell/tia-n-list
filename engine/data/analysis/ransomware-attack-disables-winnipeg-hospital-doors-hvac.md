Ransomware hit the facility management systems of a large Winnipeg hospital in Manitoba, affecting the building's doors and its heating, ventilation, and air conditioning equipment. The incident, reported the week of August 12, was described as an attack on the hospital's facility management systems rather than its clinical IT. Experts cited the case as evidence of growing cyberthreats against operational technology in healthcare.

The significance is that a ransomware operator reached building automation infrastructure, which in hospitals controls physical security and environmental systems. Door and HVAC disruption creates operational and patient-safety risk even when clinical systems are untouched, and it complicates response because facility management gear is often on flat or poorly segmented networks. Whether the attackers moved laterally from IT into building controls or hit the BAS directly is not yet public.

Watch for the hospital's disclosure of scope, particularly whether clinical systems, patient data, or other OT domains were affected, and whether any stolen data is posted. The incident should push hospitals to inventory building management connections, segment them from IT, and plan for ransomware scenarios where physical systems, not just servers, are the target.
