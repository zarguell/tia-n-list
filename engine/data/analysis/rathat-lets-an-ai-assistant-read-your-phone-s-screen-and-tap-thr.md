Zimperium zLabs disclosed RatHat, an Android banking trojan that uses a generative AI assistant to interpret the device's live Accessibility node tree and autonomously navigate banking apps. The malware serializes the screen to XML, sends it to the AI, and receives back tap/scroll/type instructions, which it executes via Android's accessibility input-injection APIs. Secondary reporting identifies the AI as Google Gemini, though Zimperium's original report does not name it. Attribution points to China-based operators based on LLM prompts found in Chinese and C2 infrastructure patterns.

The attack chain starts with smishing and malvertising lures pushing sideloaded APKs disguised as legitimate apps (Disney+, Chrome, a fake "Nexus One"). After install, RatHat exploits Accessibility permissions to silently enable Wireless Debugging, self-pairs with the local ADB daemon, and obtains a shell outside the app sandbox without exploiting any software vulnerability. From that shell it drops two Go-based native binaries: liblocal-service.so (a local command server on 127.0.0.1:7910 that manages Doze exemptions and reinstall persistence) and libmedia_codec.so (a reverse-proxy client built on fatedier/frp that tunnels traffic over HTTPS/WSS). The Go agent survives app uninstallation by intercepting the uninstall flow with a fake Google Play failure overlay and silently reinstalling from a cached APK.

Credential theft spans banking apps (Bank of America, Russian-language banks), payment apps (WeChat Pay, Alipay), and lock-screen PINs reconstructed from raw touch-input coordinates using a bundled locateValues.json keypad geometry file. The malware also intercepts SMS and notifications for MFA codes, records screens via MediaProjection, and captures browser history across Chrome, Brave, Opera, Edge, and Samsung Internet. Zimperium's IOC set documents 256 malicious APK hashes and 16 live C2 domains; a follow-up report from SSBCrack counts at least 162 distinct apps in active contact with the infrastructure.

No CVE applies. RatHat achieves shell-level compromise entirely through social engineering and abuse of legitimate Android features (Accessibility Service, Wireless Debugging). Android 11 through 16 are affected. Detection should focus on anomalous Accessibility service grants, unauthorized ADB pairing events, and the published 256 SHA-256 hashes.
