The ransomware remediation company MonsterCloud and its owner Zohar Pinhasi have been indicted. Authorities accuse Pinhasi of charging clients over $19 million over five years, using more than $8 million of that to pay ransoms while telling victims he used proprietary decryption tools. The Justice Department says no such tool existed. This case highlights the opaque ransomware recovery economy and how some operators profit by hiding ransom payments from clients.

Why it matters: Victims of ransomware already face extortion; relying on unverified remediation firms adds a second layer of fraud. The indictment, reported by CyberScoop and others, confirms that at least some recovery services act as intermediaries without client consent. Watch for further sentencing details and whether other former negotiators face additional charges.
