The record covers two separate federal ransomware recovery-fraud cases, not one company. Zohar Pinhasi, owner and operator of MonsterCloud, was accused of charging hundreds of clients more than $19 million while paying more than $8 million in ransom during a five-year period ending in 2023. The Justice Department said his claimed proprietary decryption tools did not exist and that client fees secretly funded ransom payments. He faced two wire fraud counts and one conspiracy count.

A federal grand jury also indicted Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, 52, a Colombian national and co-founder of a Florida recovery company. Reporting by 404 Media and Brian Krebs identified the operation as Ransomwarehelp.com, whose website was placed under maintenance. Prosecutors said Escobar claimed to have decryption tools, secretly negotiated with ransomware gangs, paid ransoms and charged customers enough to profit. He faces three wire fraud counts carrying a maximum of 60 years in prison, and the available reporting describes an FBI sting.

Both cases expose a weak point in incident response: victims may hand money and trust to intermediaries whose recovery claims cannot be independently verified. The earlier sentencing of three former ransomware negotiators for deceiving clients and conspiring with affiliates shows the problem is not confined to one firm. Watch for restitution orders, trial disclosures and any regulatory guidance on due diligence for recovery vendors, especially proof that a proposed decryption method works before payment.
