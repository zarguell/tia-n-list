The September 14 threat intelligence bulletin covers three notable incidents. IDScan.net, a US identity verification provider, disclosed a breach after detecting unauthorized access on September 1. Exposed data included names and government identification numbers, and a criminal marketplace was found advertising millions of identity documents including driver's licenses tied to the company's verification services.

Mathspace, an education platform serving Australia and New Zealand, suffered a breach affecting over 1 million people. Attackers exploited CVE-2026-72898, a SQL injection vulnerability in self-hosted Metabase instances, to access an internal reporting database. Exposed data included names, email addresses, usernames, and locations. Passwords and academic records were not compromised. Check Point IPS provides detection for this CVE.

Revolut disclosed that employees fulfilled fraudulent information requests sent from an email account within a legitimate government agency domain. The exposed records included identity documents. The bulletin also flags CVE-2026-72898 as a KEV-listed vulnerability, and the Mathspace exploitation demonstrates real-world impact of the Metabase SQL injection flaw at scale.
