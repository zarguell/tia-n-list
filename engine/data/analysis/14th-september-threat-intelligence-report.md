This Check Point weekly bulletin, mirrored on malware.news on 14 September 2026, anchors on the Mathspace breach: attackers exploited CVE-2026-72898, a SQL injection in self-hosted Metabase, to reach an internal reporting database at the education platform used across Australia and New Zealand. More than 1 million people are affected, with names, email addresses, usernames, and locations exposed, while passwords and academic records were not. Check Point ships a dedicated IPS signature, Metabase SQL Injection (CVE-2026-72898), which puts a named detection on the table instead of a generic advisory. The same issue records a 1 September unauthorized access at IDScan.net, a US identity verification provider, whose stolen data surfaced as a criminal marketplace collection of millions of identity documents including driver's licenses.

The structural point the event list does not spell out is that a BI tool is a consolidation point, and its reporting database is by design a wide join across upstream systems. Compromise the analytics tier and you inherit the cross-section of customer data rather than one application's slice; the exposed-field list, absent passwords and academic records, shows the attackers read the reporting copy, and the true scope was bounded by what the Metabase service account could query, not by what any single application holds. Metabase typically listens on its default port 3000 and is operated as internal tooling, outside patch cadence and external-exposure reviews, which is how a routine injection bug becomes a seven-figure-record breach. A million affected people across Australia and New Zealand makes this one of the region's larger disclosed breaches and triggers Notifiable Data Breaches obligations, while the IDScan.net entry in the same bulletin shows the monetization path for exactly this kind of identity data: bulk document collections sold to fraud operations.

Defenders should inventory self-hosted Metabase instances and check running builds against the vendor advisory for CVE-2026-72898, since the bulletin carries no fixed version, then upgrade as soon as a patched release is identified. Where Check Point is inline, set the Metabase SQL Injection (CVE-2026-72898) signature to block; otherwise apply equivalent SQLi WAF rules and restrict TCP 3000 to administrative networks or VPN. Audit the database account Metabase uses, because anything that service account can read must be treated as potentially exfiltrated until log review says otherwise. Hunt in reverse proxy and Metabase application logs for injection-shaped requests referencing information_schema or UNION SELECT and for result volumes inconsistent with dashboard traffic, correlating timestamps against early-September windows given the IDScan.net detection date of 1 September. Expect downstream identity fraud from name plus government identification number pairings and monitor marketplace chatter for exposed customers' document images.

Two developments would change this assessment. A vendor advisory with concrete fixed versions for CVE-2026-72898, or a KEV listing and mass-scanning telemetry, would move the flaw from targeted abuse to commodity exploitation and compress remediation timelines. Confirmation that the advertised collection of millions of identity documents is sourced from IDScan.net and extends beyond its disclosed record set would expand the blast radius and argue for document reissue rather than credit monitoring alone.
