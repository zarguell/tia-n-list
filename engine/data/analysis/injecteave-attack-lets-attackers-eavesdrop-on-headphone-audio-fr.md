Researchers from Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) and Hong Kong Polytechnic University have demonstrated InjectEave, an electromagnetic side-channel attack that recovers audio from consumer headphones at distances up to 30 meters. The paper, accepted at USENIX Security 26, breaks past the roughly 1.5-meter range ceiling of prior passive EM eavesdropping techniques like MagEar and Periscope.

The core innovation is active RF injection rather than passive emission collection. Traditional passive attacks fail because audio signals occupy 20 Hz to 20 kHz while device wiring radiates efficiently at megahertz-to-gigahertz frequencies. InjectEave transmits a tuned carrier signal into the target device, where nonlinear components such as audio amplifiers, ADCs, power converters, and switching MOSFETs unintentionally mix the audio with the carrier. The combined signal re-radiates through attached cables and can be intercepted and demodulated.

In testing, the team recovered audible speech from wireless headphones at range using amplified equipment. The attack requires line of sight or close proximity to the target's cables and is limited to devices with insufficient EM shielding. It does not require physical access or software exploitation. The practical risk is concentrated in targeted surveillance scenarios rather than mass collection, but it materially extends the distance envelope for EM-based audio exfiltration beyond what was previously considered feasible.
