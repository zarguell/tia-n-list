Ellis County, Kansas discovered a ransomware attack on its government IT systems on September 17, 2026. Officials said they isolated affected systems and brought in cybersecurity specialists, but some services could remain disrupted for days or weeks. The county's statement confirmed 911 and emergency public-safety response remained operational, while other county services would be unavailable or delayed. As of September 20, no restoration notice had been issued and no timetable was set for full service recovery.

The incident is part of a cluster of Kansas local government attacks in recent months. Fort Scott restored municipal systems after a ransomware attack in September, and Douglas County Sheriff's Office experienced suspicious activity that interrupted online inmate reports and fingerprint scheduling in August. Ellis County said it was working with local law enforcement, the Kansas Highway Patrol, and the Kansas Bureau of Investigation to determine scope and cause.

No ransomware family, threat actor, or ransom demand has been publicly identified. The county stated it had no evidence that personal or sensitive information had been accessed, but said it would notify affected people if that determination changes. Officials warned residents to use caution with messages from ellisco.net during the investigation.
