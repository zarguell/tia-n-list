The US Treasury cut MEXC Global off from the US financial system through a FinCEN order issued under section 9714(a) of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act. The Seychelles-based exchange, founded in 2018, serves more than 40 million users across more than 170 countries with access to 3,000 cryptocurrencies and tokenized assets. FinCEN alleges at least $16 billion in illicit transactions tied to Iranian, North Korean, and Russian sanctions evasion and cybercrime between May 2021 and May 2026. Treasury states that as recently as June 2026 MEXC still processed transactions with sanctioned Russian entities Garantex and Grinex, and handled at least $226 million for the Russia-based OFAC-sanctioned A7 sanctions evasion network.

This is a transmittal ban, not an SDN designation: MEXC is not added to the sanctions list, no property is blocked, and US institutions simply must stop sending or receiving funds. The claim the event list does not spell out is that the practical effect is a banking severance rather than an asset seizure, so MEXC's customers face disrupted deposits and withdrawals while the exchange's funds remain in place. The order matters because the alleged activity was current, continuing to June 2026, and because it applies a rarely used money-laundering statute to one of the world's largest exchanges, which handled $1 billion in direct transactions with more than 40 identified Iranian CVC exchangers over the same period.

Defenders should treat Federal Register publication as the start of a 30-day window after which covered institutions must stop processing any funds transfers to or from MEXC, including cryptocurrency. Compliance teams should add MEXC, Garantex, Grinex, and the A7 and A7A5 networks to sanctions screening and block rules immediately, and expect counterparties to wind down relationships on the same timeline. Hunting should look for wire activity mentioning Garantex or Grinex, deposits to or withdrawals from MEXC-linked addresses, and customer onboarding tied to the more than 40 identified Iranian CVC exchangers cited in the FinCEN order. No patch exists because this is not a software vulnerability; remediation is screening and process changes.

Watch for Federal Register publication, which starts the 30-day enforcement clock, and for MEXC's eventual addition to the SDN list, which would convert the transmittal ban into a full asset freeze. Also watch whether Treasury applies section 9714(a) to other exchanges, which would signal a broader campaign against crypto venues used for sanctions evasion.
