Microsoft and Health-ISAC seized and redirected domains powering YAM Store, CoGUI, and Iron Man, a Chinese-language cybercrime ecosystem tracked as Storm-2990. Since January 2026 the operators delivered over one billion phishing emails and SMS messages to more than 75,000 organizations across 150 countries, with over 750 million messages targeting Microsoft customers. The action was authorized by the US District Court for the Southern District of Florida; investigators disabled over 20,000 YAM Store accounts, blocked over 100,000 impersonation domains, and redirected the underlying infrastructure.

This is not a vulnerability advisory but a supply-chain disruption of the phishing economy itself, which makes it more consequential than a single patch: the kits targeted healthcare, financial services, logistics, and IT predominantly in the United States and Japan, and operators made tens of millions of dollars. The investigation began when Japan's National Police Agency alerted Microsoft in late 2025; Resecurity, Cloudflare, Oracle, Proofpoint, and TRM Labs later joined. Because the marketplace served many actors, residual campaigns will outlive the seizure, and defenders should treat this as a shift in attacker sourcing rather than a clean termination.

Concrete defense: hunt for residual YAM Store, CoGUI, or Iron Man infrastructure in email headers and proxy logs, block the 100,000 impersonation domains Microsoft has identified, and monitor for messages mimicking the kits previously sold through the market. Check for accounts disabled in the 20,000-account sweep and verify no organizational mail gateways reference redirected domains. Report any matching phishing messages to security teams and coordinate with Health-ISAC if healthcare-targeted.

Watch for follow-on reporting from Resecurity or Microsoft on replacement marketplaces, any court filings that name additional operators, and telemetry showing Storm-2990 kits re-hosted on alternate infrastructure. A significant rebound in Chinese-language phishing-kit sales within 30 days would indicate the disruption only displaced, rather than eliminated, the supply chain.
