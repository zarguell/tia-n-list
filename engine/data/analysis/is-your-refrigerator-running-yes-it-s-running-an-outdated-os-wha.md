Overnight on November 2, supermarket freezers across Sweden, Belgium, Luxembourg and Ireland climbed to around 50 degrees Celsius, spoiling stock. This was the second wave within a month, after a similar attack on hundreds of French stores in October, and UK food suppliers were also affected. Hackers altered refrigeration settings, forcing stores to discard food, demonstrating physical consequences from a cyber intrusion into retail operational technology.

Pro-Russian groups NoName057(16) and Z-Pentest later claimed responsibility for the supermarket refrigeration attacks, according to a French cyber outlet. Earlier reports had not publicly attributed either wave, and the initial attack vector remains unconfirmed. Coverage from Dutch security researchers frames the incidents as illustrating invisible vulnerabilities in cyber-physical systems.

Defenders should treat refrigeration and other embedded control systems as part of the attack surface: segment OT networks, restrict remote access, monitor configuration changes and maintain manual override procedures. The key watch item is confirmation of the intrusion vector and whether the same actors target additional retail or cold-chain operators.
