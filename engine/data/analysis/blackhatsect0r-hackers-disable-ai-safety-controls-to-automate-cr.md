A French-speaking cybercrime group calling itself BlackHatSect0r && DXQRTXX operated a self-hosted AI agent with safety controls deliberately disabled. Socradar researchers found an internet-exposed server containing 4.9 GB of data across 9,299 files, including a Go-based C2 platform named DXSCAN, a vault of 16,834 harvested credentials, phishing infrastructure, and operator shell history. The agent was a Nous Research Hermes model connected to DeepSeek, with the "discernment retained" instruction stripped from its configuration and HERMES_DISABLE_SAFETY=1 set in the environment.

The DXSCAN platform scanned 2.75 million domains, reached over 726,000 hosts, and generated 1.37 million IP addresses. It used country-weighted IP generation, web-service fingerprinting, configuration-file scanning, and Telegram-based notifications. The scanner contained over 200 patterns targeting secrets in .env, YAML, JSON, PHP, and WordPress config files. Researchers noted every confirmed compromise followed predictable paths: exposed cloud buckets, leaked environment files, browser-shipped keys, or default signing secrets.

The crew ran a vishing campaign targeting older French telecom subscribers, possessing a database of nearly 450,000 records with identity data and banking identifiers. A subset of 1,697 people aged 50 or older in Savoie and Haute-Savoie received Societe Generale-themed fraud alerts directing them to attacker-controlled numbers. The operation also targeted French public-sector institutions and a cryptocurrency exchange using the literal signing secret "secret" for JWT forging. CVE-2026-42530, a real use-after-free in NGINX HTTP/3 QPACK handling (fixed in 1.31.2), was among the vulnerabilities in the toolkit. The case demonstrates that removing AI safety controls transforms commodity cybercrime tooling into a high-volume attack pipeline without requiring novel exploitation.
