The White House is putting Kirsten Davies, currently the Pentagon's chief information officer, in line to be its nominee for national cyber director. While she seeks Senate confirmation, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton will run the Office of the National Cyber Director on an interim basis, and Davies will serve as a senior adviser in that office. A White House official said broad support for Davies drove the choice. The arrangement follows the removal of Sean Cairncross as cyber chief on the preceding Thursday.

Dark Reading frames the pick as a move toward more industry experience in the role, against a backdrop of recent outages and geopolitical tension. Clayton already carries two other major portfolios, intelligence coordination and the administration's AI work, so the temporary cyber post adds a third. The reporting is about personnel and process, not a new vulnerability or incident, but the turnover affects who owns federal cyber policy during confirmation.

Watch how long Clayton holds the acting job, whether Davies wins confirmation without delay, and whether the office keeps its current staff and priorities through the handoff. Any change in the way the White House coordinates cyber and AI policy would be the substantive follow-up to this personnel story.
