Check Point Research discovered a campaign it tracks as "Yoba," in which attackers abuse the popularity of AI tools by creating fake GitHub projects posing as desktop applications such as GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6 Astra, and Claude Opus. Malware is placed alongside legitimate, signed executables from OpenAI and Anthropic and loaded indirectly via DLL sideloading, a technique often used to bypass security protections. The campaign avoids dedicated command-and-control infrastructure entirely, instead relying on Telegram, Tor, Catbox, and DNS over HTTPS, demonstrating strong operational security.

The actor shows indicators suggesting Russian origins and appears to operate primarily in the UTC-4 time zone, with activity spanning roughly two months. Defenders should scrutinize AI-tool downloads sourced from GitHub and watch for DLL sideloading next to signed binaries, since the signed hosts help the malware evade trust-based controls.
