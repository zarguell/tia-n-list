Cyberdione Labs' Tailworm audit of Tailscale v1.104.2 and headscale v0.29.5 identified 11 vulnerabilities, including SSH Unix-socket forwarding paths that allow a compromised device on a tailnet to escalate privileges to root on reachable servers. The testing window ran October 22 to October 24, 2026. The disclosure was made without advance vendor notice, leaving the flaws unpatched at the time of reporting. The Mastodon update reinforces the severity: attackers on authorized nodes can exploit Unix socket forwarding to gain root access, undermining the zero-trust isolation that Tailscale is designed to provide.

Organizations using Tailscale or Headscale should review ACL configurations and restrict Unix-socket forwarding paths until vendor patches are released. Because no fixes were available as of October 26, this is an active exposure rather than a theoretical risk. Monitor vendor channels for emergency releases and consider temporary network segmentation for critical servers reachable via Tailscale connections.
