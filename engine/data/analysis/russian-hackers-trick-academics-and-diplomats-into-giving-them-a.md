Google Threat Intelligence Group identified three suspected Russian cyber-espionage clusters (UNC6293, UNC7005, UNC5976) running coordinated social-engineering campaigns against academics, diplomats, defense staff, and think-tank researchers across Europe and the United States. Rather than deploying malware or harvesting credentials through fake login pages, the groups manipulate targets into completing legitimate authentication workflows that hand the attackers persistent access to email, Microsoft 365, Google, and WhatsApp accounts.

UNC6293, assessed with moderate confidence as connected to the Russia-linked ICE RELIC cluster, has used application-specific password phishing since 2025. Targets receive State Department-themed correspondence urging them to create app passwords with names like "ms.state.gov" and submit the generated passcode, granting mailbox access without needing the victim's normal password or 2FA code. UNC7005 (STORM-2945) expanded this with Microsoft device-code phishing and WhatsApp device-linking scams, while UNC5976 leveraged similar OAuth and device-code flows.

The attack chains are notable because they abuse features designed for legitimate convenience. Victims complete real authentication steps, so traditional indicators of compromise like malicious attachments or phishing domains are absent. The campaigns represent a shift toward trust-based interception of identity infrastructure rather than credential theft, and the scope of targets (academic critics of Russia, European diplomats, defense personnel) aligns with known Russian intelligence collection priorities.
