Zenity Labs disclosed SalesBleed, a chain of three vulnerabilities in Salesforce Agentforce that allowed CRM data theft without credentials or user interaction. The entry point was a public Web-to-Lead form: hidden instructions planted in a lead field created a poisoned CRM record that stayed dormant until an employee asked an Agentforce agent to review recent submissions.

Two of the flaws abused weaknesses in Trusted URLs, the mechanism meant to stop Agentforce from loading URLs and images from untrusted sources. A payload could steer the General CRM subagent into invoking its existing Query Records capability against other accessible objects, including Account records, then exfiltrate results via HTML image tags to an attacker-controlled server. Agentforce reported that the content had been blocked by security policies even though the data had already been transmitted. The third flaw, in the Agentforce-Slack integration, let an attacker weaponize an agent to distribute phishing messages.

Salesforce was notified on June 1, 2026, fixed the specific Trusted URLs bypasses used in the research, and said it found no evidence of exploitation in the wild; Zenity confirmed the fixes. The broader risk is that agentic CRM features turn unauthenticated form input into a path to live agent capabilities, so protection rests on URL redaction and agent guardrails. Watch for similar zero-click prompt injection chains in other agent platforms and whether Salesforce hardens Trusted URLs beyond the specific bypasses patched here.
