Silent Lynx (TAG-193, UAT-6275) targets CIS government institutions through macro-enabled documents using four infection-chain variants observed between June 2025 and August 2026. The campaign explicitly hits diplomacy, economic development, agriculture, and environmental management bodies, and the event references both CVE-2025-8088 and CVE-2025-25257 as exploited vulnerabilities. Talos attributes exposed command infrastructure to a Kazakhstan nexus, though the group does not confirm origin, and the blog at blog.talosintelligence.com provides the primary technical visibility into malware practices and delivery evolution.

The exposure of CIS government institutions to macro-enabled infection chains is not generic malware noise: these are strategic intelligence targets whose compromise would yield diplomatic and economic policy insight rather than quick financial gain. With infection activity spanning at least fourteen months (June 2025 through August 2026) and multiple infection variants in play, a defender facing this campaign has sustained adversary presence rather than a one-off tool drop. The presence of CVE-2025-8088 and CVE-2025-25257 in the same campaign raises the stakes further, because both are known exploited vulnerabilities, meaning the attacker is not relying solely on social engineering but on patched-but-unpatched code paths in target environments.

Defenders should hunt for macro-enabled document delivery against diplomacy, agriculture, economic development, and environmental management endpoints, inspecting process trees that spawn from Office applications. Patch to versions that resolve CVE-2025-8088 and CVE-2025-25257; if exact vendor patch versions are unavailable in the event record, verify the vendor advisory timelines against the blog.talosintelligence.com report. Network restrictions should block outbound connections to the exposed infrastructure domains listed in the Talos post, and telemetry should focus on infection-chain artifacts mentioned in the blog rather than generic malware indicators.
