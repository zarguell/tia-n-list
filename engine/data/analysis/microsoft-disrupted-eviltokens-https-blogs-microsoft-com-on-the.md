Microsoft disrupted EvilTokens, a phishing service tracked as Storm-2992 that was linked to over 12,000 compromised inboxes across more than 10,000 organizations. The platform offered phishing templates, malicious landing pages, token management, and AI-assisted email customization, making it a full-service business email compromise toolkit. Its device-code phishing technique abused legitimate OAuth flows to hijack Microsoft accounts without passwords. The scale shows how AI-integrated phishing services lower the skill barrier for large campaigns. Defenders should enforce conditional access policies that restrict device-code flows, review OAuth consent grants, and train users to treat unexpected document-sharing and invoice messages with extra scrutiny.
