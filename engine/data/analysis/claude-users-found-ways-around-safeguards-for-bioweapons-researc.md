Anthropic disclosed five cases where users attempted to use Claude for bioweapons-related research, with some actors spending weeks planning avian influenza experiments while working around safety filters. The company reported that users from restricted nations including Russia, China, and Iran were among those attempting to circumvent controls, though Anthropic could not confirm malicious intent since the same information could support vaccine development. The disclosed cases involved actors who "obfuscated" their research purposes to bypass safeguards, with Anthropic restricting some attempts to weaker models as a mitigation.

This disclosure lands amid broader AI safety turbulence at Anthropic. Researcher Jacob Coxon resigned earlier in the week, stating employees "earnestly believe it could kill us all by the end of the decade." The bioweapons misuse report is part of Anthropic's September 2026 threat intelligence report, which also details seven Chinese AI labs including Moonshot and DeepSeek attempting to distill Claude's capabilities through "increasingly sophisticated methods." The report covers activity from December 2025 through August 2026 across cyber operations, surveillance, influence operations, and conventional weapons development.

The core challenge Anthropic identifies is that dangerous biology closely resembles legitimate research, making content-based filtering inherently limited. The company banned the accounts involved but did not disclose institution or country names. The bioweapons section sits alongside case studies of AI-augmented espionage operations, fake dating app fraud networks, and surveillance systems targeting dissidents, painting a picture of AI misuse that extends well beyond traditional cybersecurity into physical security domains.
