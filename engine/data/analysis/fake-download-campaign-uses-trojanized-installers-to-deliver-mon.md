Microsoft Threat Intelligence documented an evolving fake-download campaign that distributes trojanized Inno Setup-style installers to users searching for cracked software and free utilities. The campaign uses a per-offer callback and status-tracking model to manage payload delivery during a single installation session. Downstream branches turn affected devices into monetized assets by routing third-party traffic through the device, enrolling it in distributed-computing platforms, or silently installing additional software without informed consent. The campaign introduces security and privacy risks through deceptive software bundles rather than traditional malware execution.

Defenders should treat this as a supply-chain hygiene and potentially-unwanted-application issue rather than a classic exploit chain. Mitigation includes preventing use of untrusted software sources, enabling PUA protection, and monitoring for unexpected installations and persistence mechanisms. No CVE or fixed version applies; the exposure is behavioral, driven by users running trojanized installers.
