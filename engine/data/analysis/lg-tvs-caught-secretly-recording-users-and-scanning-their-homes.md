Researchers from Gamers Nexus, working with Level1Techs and independent security researchers, found that LG smart TVs perform extensive local network discovery, collecting device names, Wi-Fi SSIDs, signal strength, and hardware identifiers from phones, PCs, printers, switches, and smart-home gear on the same network. The TVs also run Automated Content Recognition to fingerprint what is on screen or audible in the room, building viewing profiles tied to advertising IDs.

The most alarming finding is that a compromised LG TV can record room audio through its built-in microphone even when the screen appears off. The researchers demonstrated that audio could be captured and stored locally, then transmitted once a network connection was restored, including after the TV had been physically unplugged from the internet. The team also reported remote-code-execution vulnerabilities to LG that remain under responsible disclosure.

LG has disputed the broader privacy implications, saying voice data processes only after a wake word or manual button press and that ACR requires user consent. The company confirmed local network scanning is standard for device connectivity. Regardless, the core lesson is that smart TVs are full networked computers with microphones, cameras, and persistent connections, and they deserve the same security scrutiny as any other endpoint. Defenders should segment IoT devices on separate VLANs, disable UPnP, and audit which devices hold always-on microphones.
