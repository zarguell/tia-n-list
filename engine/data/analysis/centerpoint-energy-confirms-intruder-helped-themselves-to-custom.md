CenterPoint Energy confirmed a data breach in an SEC Form 8-K filing after a cybercrime forum post claimed to offer 7.49 million customer files stolen from a poorly secured API. The Houston-based utility serves approximately 7 million electricity and gas customers across Texas. The company said power and gas services remain operational and unaffected.

The allegedly stolen data includes customer names, contact details, billing data, move-in dates, driver's license information, and the last four digits of Social Security numbers. The Register has not independently verified the data dump. CenterPoint told The Register its "filing speaks for itself" and said it does not believe the incident will materially affect its financial condition. The company has engaged third-party investigators, notified law enforcement, and begun regulatory notifications.

This breach fits a pattern of attacks targeting US energy utilities. Recent disruptive incidents hit facilities in Poland and the UK, and over 100 US water systems were affected by attacks in July 2026. While this incident appears to be data theft rather than operational disruption, the exposure of driver's license and partial SSN data for millions of utility customers creates significant identity fraud risk. The "poorly secured API" detail suggests an application-layer misconfiguration rather than a sophisticated intrusion.
