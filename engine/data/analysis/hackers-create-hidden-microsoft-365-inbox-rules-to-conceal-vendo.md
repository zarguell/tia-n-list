A cloud-only business email compromise campaign is targeting finance employees at organizations using Microsoft 365. The attackers use an Adversary-in-the-Middle (AiTM) phishing kit to capture authenticated MFA session tokens, bypassing multi-factor authentication entirely. The initial lure is an HR-themed email claiming a PTO request was denied, with a "View PTO Conflicting Dates" button routed through SendGrid tracking links to a fake M365 sign-in page. Once the attacker holds a valid session token, they create hidden inbox rules that auto-delete or redirect emails related to vendor payments, making the fraud invisible to the victim.

The campaign leaves no malware on the endpoint and no endpoint compromise to detect. The entire attack lives in the cloud: session token theft, inbox rule manipulation, and wire fraud redirection to attacker-controlled bank accounts. This makes traditional EDR and endpoint-based detection irrelevant. Gbhackers and Cyberpress both reported on this activity, and the technique of hiding inbox rules to conceal payment fraud is a known but underdetected BEC tactic.

What to watch: the specific AiTM kit infrastructure being used (likely EvilGinx2 or a derivative), whether M365 Conditional Access policies or token protection features could have caught this, and whether the hidden inbox rules leave forensic artifacts in the Unified Audit Log that defenders can hunt on retroactively. Organizations should audit M365 inbox rules regularly and consider FIDO2/passkey-based authentication to defeat AiTM token theft.
