Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) detailed attacks showing how AI is accelerating credential theft operations. In one campaign disclosed September 8, a threat actor compromised an organization's cloud infrastructure and deployed a multi-agent attack framework that completed in under six hours, compromising thousands of third-party credentials. The AI managed parts of the vulnerability-scanning pipeline autonomously, troubleshooting issues and rotating IP addresses with minimal human input.

Microsoft reported in April that AI-assisted phishing campaigns achieved click-through rates as high as 54%, compared with roughly 12% for traditional campaigns. The economic implication is clear: AI lowers the marginal cost of convincing phishing at scale. Credential harvesting, already routine via infostealers, becomes faster and requires less operator skill when augmented by language models that can tailor lures and adapt in real time.

The core defensive implication is that authentication systems designed for a world where phishing required human craft no longer hold. Phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2, passkeys) becomes more urgent as AI makes credential phishing both cheaper and more effective. Security teams should assume that any credential-only authentication is increasingly brittle and prioritize phishing-resistant methods for high-value accounts.
