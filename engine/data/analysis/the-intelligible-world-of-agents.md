Recorded Future published a piece arguing that the performance gap between cybersecurity AI agents comes down to knowledge representation, not model intelligence. The core claim: an agent operating over a structured knowledge graph that captures relationships among assets, vulnerabilities, threat actors, and detections will outperform one fed fragmented alerts and disconnected data sources, even when both use the same underlying LLM.

The argument has practical implications for how organizations deploy AI in their SOC. Most current agent implementations ingest raw alert streams or unstructured logs. Recorded Future contends that building a "living enterprise model" where entities and their relationships are pre-linked gives the agent a decisive advantage in reasoning speed and accuracy. The detective analogy is apt: same investigator, dramatically different outcomes based on how the evidence is organized.

This is vendor content from a threat intelligence company with a commercial interest in knowledge graphs, so weigh accordingly. But the underlying point about structured context improving agent performance is well-supported by the broader AI research on retrieval-augmented generation and knowledge-grounded reasoning. Organizations evaluating AI security tools should ask vendors specifically how their agents represent and query organizational context.
