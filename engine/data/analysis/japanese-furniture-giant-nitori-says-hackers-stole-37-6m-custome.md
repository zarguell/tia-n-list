Nitori Holdings said on November 9, 2026 that a third party reached a system it manages and that up to 37.62 million records held on its servers may have been taken. The company confirmed the intrusion on November 6 and cut the access, then moved the earliest intrusion back to mid to late October through log analysis. The exposed set runs wider than a member database: member numbers, names with kana readings, email addresses, postal addresses, phone numbers, birth dates and gender for individuals, corporate contact records for business members, and order data generated by Nitori Net and the company's storefronts on third-party online malls. Nitori says member passwords and credit card numbers are outside the set, has reported the incident to the Personal Information Protection Commission, and is consulting police.

The count is the headline; the composition is the risk. With no passwords and no card data, account takeover and card fraud are not supported by what was disclosed. What is supported is targeted impersonation: a caller or sender who quotes a real Nitori order, a real delivery address and a real birth date is hard to separate from the company, and the people most exposed are the one-off purchasers who never created an account and have no other Nitori relationship to check the contact against. The figure is also a count of records rather than a headcount, and Nitori has not said how many individuals are affected. The surrounding year sharpens rather than dilutes the point: Bookoff disclosed up to 6.43 million member records on October 9, about 6.38 million individuals, with password hashes in scope; JR East reported roughly 6.09 million records and Yamato Transport about 5.31 million; and a TBS NEWS DIG explainer on October 16 put Japanese disclosures for the year above 115 million records. Japan Cyber Watch ranks this as the fourth-largest Japanese breach disclosed in 2026, and a footprint of 879 stores in Japan plus 219 overseas turns one notification into a multi-jurisdiction problem.

For defenders, the window Nitori defined is the search space. Between mid-October and November 6, look for bulk reads against member, order and address tables, for query or export volumes far outside the norm for a retail member system, and for staged customer files leaving the environment by file transfer, email or cloud storage. Nitori says the vulnerable point has been fixed and monitoring strengthened, but it has not named the entry method, so a peer retailer should treat its own internet-facing storefront and order estate as unexamined until proven otherwise. Because no password material was taken, credential stuffing against Nitori accounts is a low-value lead; verification questions built on static profile fields, including company name, company address and contact person for corporate members, should be treated as compromised for anyone whose data sits in this set.

Two developments would change the assessment. Nitori said on November 11 that the cause is still under investigation, including whether ransomware was involved, and no group has claimed the intrusion; a named actor or an extortion deadline moves this from data exposure to a live extortion case with a publication clock. The second is a revision of the count or of the excluded fields, since the claim that passwords and card data are absent is the load-bearing part of this disclosure and Bookoff showed that hashes can sit inside a system described as member data. A named initial access vector would also tell other Japanese retailers whether they run the same stack.
