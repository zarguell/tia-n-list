SecurityWeek's weekly roundup covers several developments worth tracking, led by Black Lotus Labs' disclosure of PoeLLM malware. Active since April 2026, it targets exposed AI and open-source services including LiteLLM, Ollama, Gotenberg and Gitea to mine cryptocurrency and expand a botnet. The malware extracts four keywords from a GitHub-hosted poem and converts them into the current C&C server IP, giving the operator an easy rotation mechanism; the poem has been updated 11 times and the operator is assessed Italian-speaking.

Also notable: GitGuardian reports the GhostAction supply chain campaign pushed its secret-stealing GitHub Actions workflow to 772 additional public repositories between August 31 and September 30, targeting 2,577 secrets including SSH keys and cloud credentials. The attacker added a new exfiltration server but reused the 2025 playbook. The Korean bank breach and Empire admin sentencing are mentioned briefly but lack detail in this summary.

Why it matters: PoeLLM shows how malware operators are adapting to AI infrastructure as targets, using creative C&C techniques. The scale of GhostAction reinforces that supply-chain exposure through GitHub Actions remains a high-volume threat. Monitor Black Lotus Labs updates for PoeLLM indicators and GitGuardian feeds for GhostAction expansions.
