Fortinet's September 8 batch patched ten vulnerabilities, and the two criticals are not equal threats. CVE-2026-84390 (CVSS 9.6, CWE-540) is an inclusion of sensitive information in source code in the FortiMonitorOnSight web portal: the advisory title states the JWT used for GUI authentication is signed with a static key, so a remote unauthenticated attacker can bypass authentication with a forged or reused token. Fortinet TAC's Cameron Milstead found it internally, and FortiMonitorOnSight 7.2.0 through 7.2.2 and 7.2.4 through 7.2.7 need 7.2.8 or above. CVE-2026-84388 (CVSS 9.1, CWE-287) is improper authentication in the Fortinet Privileged Access Agent Chrome extension, where one visit to a malicious website lets an attacker steer the browser's proxy, open tabs, and capture the extension's privileged-session screen recordings. That fix requires two components moved together: FortiPAM at 1.9.1 or 1.8.4 and the extension at 8.0.1.123 or above. Researcher James Arnott of Bay Area Labs reported the extension chain to Fortinet on July 17 and says the extension fix shipped August 1, six weeks before the coordinated advisory; Fortinet credits four external reporters and marks both issues Known Exploited: No.

The CVSS ranking inverts the operational risk. The OnSight portal bug needs no user interaction, and CISA's SSVC scoring marks it automatable with total technical impact, while the advisory's own framing, a static signing key instead of a per-installation secret, implies one forgery recipe works against every unpatched portal rather than an attack that must be rebuilt per target. The extension bug demands a user misstep, but on an admin workstation it hands over exactly what FortiPAM exists to protect: credential injection into sessions, proxy policy control, and audit recordings of privileged access, against an install base the disclosure puts at more than one million users. Neither CVE is in KEV, EPSS sits below one percent for both (0.00377 and 0.00522), and CISA rates exploitation as none, so this is a patch-now story rather than an incident response. The combination of an automatable unauthenticated server bypass and a client-side PAM hijack in the same vendor drop, plus a public proof-of-concept video for the extension chain, is why this batch outranks routine Fortinet Tuesday noise.

Inventory both products before touching anything else. FortiMonitorOnSight 7.2.0 through 7.2.2 and 7.2.4 through 7.2.7 upgrade to 7.2.8 or above, and the portal should sit behind management-network restrictions until that lands; check its access logs for repeated or anomalous JWT acceptance while scoping, since forged or reused tokens were the stated vector. For FortiPAM, verify the appliance is at 1.9.1 or 1.8.4 and separately confirm the Chrome extension fleet-wide is at 8.0.1.123 or above, because appliance version alone proves nothing; extension 7.4 all versions has no in-place fix and must migrate. In proxy and egress logs, hunt HTTPS requests to /api/v2/monitor/web-ui/state aimed at hosts that are not legitimate FortiPAM servers, since the disclosure shows any hostname receiving that path was silently added to the extension's trusted-server list, and the consent dialog selectors named in the write-up (fortinet-sv-modal-overlay, .sv-btn-allow) give browser forensics a concrete artifact to look for. The same drop carried high-severity fixes for FortiSandbox (CVE-2026-26084) and the FortiOS and FortiProxy agentless ZTNA portal (CVE-2026-84393, man-in-the-middle), with FortiOS 7.6.1 through 7.6.6 and FortiProxy 7.6.2 through 7.6.6 on the affected list, so sweep those in the same maintenance window.

Two developments would change this assessment. Any exploitation signal against either CVE, a KEV listing, or vendor confirmation would move the OnSight flaw from patch-now to incident triage given its unattended and automatable profile, and Fortinet's Known Exploited flags and CISA's none ratings are the current baseline to measure against. The other is NVD's pending analysis, which has already scored CVE-2026-84390 at 9.8 against the vendor's 9.6, a signal the severity consensus is still moving; combined with the six-week gap between the August 1 extension fix and the September 8 advisory, any widening of that gap in public reporting would justify assuming the PoC has circulated longer than the disclosure timeline suggests.
