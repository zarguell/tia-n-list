N-able disclosed a security incident affecting one server in its Mail Assure and SpamExperts Hosted Cloud environment after a disruption that began at 10:00 p.m. ET on November 16. The company first described the outage as a combination of platform configuration and service dependencies, then reclassified it as a security incident at 10:05 p.m. ET on November 17. Management interfaces and APIs were disabled across the Americas, Asia-Pacific and Europe, while inbound and outbound mail flow and spam filtering kept running. At the time N-able said it had no evidence of customer data compromise or access to tenants' mailboxes.

The affected host was a single web server, and N-able told SecurityWeek that an unauthorized party may have been able to access certain credentials stored on the platform. Response steps included taking that server offline, rotating internal platform credentials, revoking existing customer API tokens, invalidating OAuth tokens and proactively resetting passwords for local users. As of the November 18 update quoted by Brotherly Technology, the dashboard, quarantine, logging and mailbox synchronization were still being restored, and customers were advised to check quarantined mail and rotate credentials. Later coverage on November 22 still described the portal and API as disabled while restoration and remediation continued.

The incident matters because the compromised system was the management plane for hosted email filtering used by MSPs, not the mail delivery path itself. Loss of portal, quarantine and logging access leaves operators unable to review blocked messages or verify filtering during an outage, and the possible exposure of stored platform credentials raises the question of what an attacker could reach beyond that one server. Watch for N-able's final forensic statement on credential exposure, completion of dashboard and API restoration, and any change to the early finding that customer data and tenant mailboxes were untouched. N-able also stated this incident was unrelated to the earlier N-central incident.
