N-able confirmed on 18 November that unauthorized access to one server in its Mail Assure and SpamExperts Hosted Cloud environment caused a multi-region outage that began at 10:00 p.m. ET on 16 November. The company first described the disruption as a combination of platform configuration and service dependencies, then reclassified it as a security incident at 10:05 p.m. ET on 17 November. It disabled management UI and API access while inbound and outbound mail flow and spam filtering kept running. Dashboard, quarantine, logging and mailbox synchronization were among the services taken offline across the Americas, Asia-Pacific and Europe, and were still being restored on the morning of 18 November.

SecurityWeek reported N-able's statement that an unauthorized party may have accessed certain credentials stored on the affected web server. The response included taking that server offline, rotating internal platform credentials, revoking existing customer API tokens, invalidating OAuth tokens and proactively resetting passwords for local users. N-able said it had no evidence that customer data or tenant mailboxes were compromised, and said this incident was unrelated to a separate N-central issue. BleepingComputer's 20 November report is the most detailed account of the outage and remediation, and its syndication on 22 November added no new technical findings.

The operational risk sits in the affected credentials and the management plane rather than in mail delivery. MSP customers were advised to ask about quarantined mail and credential rotation while the portal and API remained disabled, which limits their ability to inspect filtering decisions during the incident. What to watch is whether N-able publishes a final scope statement on the credentials stored on that web server, and when portal, API and synchronization access return to normal in all three regions.
