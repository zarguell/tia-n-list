N-able disclosed unauthorized access to one server in the environment hosting Mail Assure and SpamExperts Hosted Cloud. The disruption was first reported on 16 November at 10:00 p.m. ET and was initially described as a platform configuration and service dependency issue. N-able reclassified it as a security incident on 17 November at 10:05 p.m. ET. The company disabled management UI and API access while inbound and outbound mail delivery and spam filtering kept running, and said at the time that it had no evidence of customer data or tenant mailbox compromise. The incident is separate from the earlier N-central incident.

The impact fell on the administrative plane across the Americas, Asia-Pacific and Europe. GBHackers reported that credentials could potentially have been exposed. Brotherly Technology recorded that as of 07:56 a.m. ET on 18 November the dashboard, quarantine, logging and mailbox synchronization were still being restored, and advised MSP customers to check quarantined mail and rotate credentials. N-able's own status notice confirms the security incident and the service scope.

This matters because Mail Assure and SpamExperts sit in front of customer mail for managed service providers, so loss of management visibility does not mean mail flow was normal in every respect. Quarantine and logging gaps can hide delivery issues during the window. Watch for updated forensic findings, any change to the no-evidence statement, and whether N-able publishes concrete credential-rotation instructions for affected tenants.
