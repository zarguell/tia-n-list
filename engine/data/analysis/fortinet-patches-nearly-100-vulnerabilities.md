Fortinet released 89 security advisories addressing nearly 100 vulnerabilities across its product line, roughly three weeks after announcing it had integrated frontier AI models into product security testing to find defects more efficiently. Approximately 17 of the newly resolved flaws are critical, several allowing arbitrary code execution; others enable authentication bypass, account takeover, access to other VDOMs, secure email account access, IBE webmail session compromise, server-side request execution, and command execution as super admin. FortiMail received the largest batch of critical fixes, followed by FortiAuthenticator, FortiOS, FortiManager, and FortiAnalyzer, with new FortiSASE and FortiClient updates also resolving critical issues.

The headline flaw is CVE-2026-121529 in FortiClient, nicknamed Fortinet2Shell by researcher Kevin Joensen of Baldur Security, who reported it. A malicious website can trigger remote code execution on a victim's computer through the vulnerable FortiClient; the bug is exploitable when FortiPAM is enabled. Attackers have targeted Fortinet endpoint tools before, and Fortinet frames the accelerated disclosure cycle as giving customers time to prioritize upgrades before technical details become public.

Organizations running Fortinet products should prioritize FortiClient endpoints with FortiPAM enabled, then FortiMail and the other critical fixes, since AI-assisted discovery is shortening the window between disclosure and exploitation. Watch for follow-up technical details on the 17 critical issues and any observed exploitation reports, especially against FortiClient and FortiMail.
