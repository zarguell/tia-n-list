Fortinet's PSIRT published the batch on November 10, 2026: 89 advisories running from FG-IR-26-190 through FG-IR-26-277 and covering roughly 100 vulnerabilities, with 20 of them carrying a critical rating on the individual advisory pages. FortiMail takes the largest share. CVE-2026-126147 is an unauthenticated path traversal rated 9.8 that ends in code execution, CVE-2026-126148 is a 9.8 command injection reached through crafted high-availability join-request packets, and the IBE webmail cluster (CVE-2026-126150, CVE-2026-126155, CVE-2026-126156, CVE-2026-126157) lets a low-privilege or unauthenticated attacker reset an arbitrary recipient's password, take an authenticated IBE session, and read or send that user's encrypted mail. FortiAnalyzer and FortiManager carry CVE-2026-126170, a 9.8 output-encoding flaw in the management GUI that needs no login, plus the internal-protocol set: CVE-2026-126259 hands super-admin command execution to anyone able to send crafted FGSP HA packets to a cluster running standalone-config-sync or autoscale, CVE-2026-126261 is unauthenticated code execution on the FortiManager FGFM port, and CVE-2026-126260 and CVE-2026-126262 sit on the oftp port. The only externally reported bug is FortiClient CVE-2026-121529, FG-IR-26-194, CVSS 9.1, an origin validation error in the client's local API that Kevin Joensen of Baldur Security traced to a CORS misconfiguration and nicknamed Fortinet2Shell; Fortinet rates it unauthenticated, requiring victim interaction, Known Exploited: No.

The composition of this batch matters more than its size. At least 20 of the advisories credit internal discovery by Fortinet Product Security staff "with the use of the frontier AI model: Claude Mythos", and those credits cluster on FortiAuthenticator, where FG-IR-26-252 through FG-IR-26-266 all resolve to the same two fixed releases, 8.0.4 and 6.6.11. The flaws defenders are triaging tonight were found by an automated audit inside the vendor rather than by an outside researcher, which cuts two ways: there is likely no public proof of concept yet, and the same tooling has almost certainly not finished sweeping the rest of the product line, so the 89 advisories are a floor rather than a census. The identity consequence is also different in kind from a mail-server RCE. An authentication bypass in FortiAuthenticator (CVE-2026-126251) yields the credential store that unlocks every FortiGate and FortiManager managed by it, so a 9.1 there outranks a 9.8 elsewhere on a blast-radius basis, not a CVSS basis. The second structural detail is branch arithmetic: FortiMail 7.2 has no fixed release at all and the advisory says migrate, so exposure is decided by which branch an operator chose, and FortiMail Cloud was already remediated on October 5, 2026 by moving cloud firmwares to v7.6.7 GA and v8.0.2 GA. The cloud fleet is the canary that shows this work was finished weeks earlier; the on-premises fleet is where the unpatched window actually is.

Patch by exposure rather than score. FortiMail 8.0.0 through 8.0.1 moves to 8.0.2, 7.6.0 through 7.6.6 to 7.6.7, 7.4.0 through 7.4.8 to 7.4.9, and any 7.2 install must migrate because no fix exists. FortiClient Mac, Windows and Windows PAM move from 7.4.0 through 7.4.7 to 7.4.8, and from 7.2.0 through 7.2.14 to 7.2.15. FortiAuthenticator 8.0.0 through 8.0.3 goes to 8.0.4 and 6.6.0 through 6.6.10 to 6.6.11. FortiAnalyzer 8.0.0 through 8.0.2 waits on the upcoming 8.0.3 or takes the documented workaround, 7.6.0 through 7.6.7 goes to 7.6.8, 7.4.0 through 7.4.11 to 7.4.12, and 7.2.0 through 7.2.12 to 7.2.13. FortiManager 8.0.0 goes to 8.0.1, 7.6.0 through 7.6.7 to 7.6.8, 7.4.0 through 7.4.5 to 7.4.6, and 7.2.0 through 7.2.8 to 7.2.9. FortiOS 7.2.0 through 7.2.13 moves to 7.2.14, 7.4.0 through 7.4.12 to 7.4.13, 7.6.0 through 7.6.7 to 7.6.8, and 8.0.0 to 8.0.1. Restrict the FGFM port and the oftp port so only known managed FortiGates and FortiAnalyzer peers can reach them; the FGFM deny-unknown-peer control Fortinet pushed during the FortiJump CVE-2024-47575 response is the precedent, and the FGSP flaws only matter where standalone-config-sync or autoscale is enabled, so disable those where the cluster does not need them. For hunting, inventory FortiClient versions against the two affected branches and watch for browser origins reaching the client's local API, review FortiMail HTTP request logs for crafted requests against the IBE endpoints, and read FortiAuthenticator authentication events for unexpected successful administrative logins. Every advisory in the batch answers Known Exploited: No, so treat the near-term telemetry as scanning against FortiMail and management GUI TLS endpoints rather than as evidence of a campaign.

Two things would move this assessment. The first is the FortiAnalyzer 8.0.3 release date: the 9.8 unauthenticated GUI RCE currently has a workaround and no shipping fix, which makes the FortiAnalyzer and FortiManager web tier the widest open exposure in the batch. The second is any of these CVE ids appearing in the CISA KEV catalog or in published exploit code, particularly CVE-2026-126147, since the FortiMail path traversal is the one flaw here with both a 9.8 rating and an unauthenticated path from the internet. A third signal worth tracking is whether the next monthly batch repeats the Claude Mythos acknowledgement pattern in a different product family, which would confirm that the vendor's automated audit is finding bugs faster than its advisory cadence can absorb them.
