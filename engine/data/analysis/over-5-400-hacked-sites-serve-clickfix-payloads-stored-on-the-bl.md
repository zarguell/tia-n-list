A Netskope research team identified a large ClickFix campaign that has compromised more than 5,400 small-business websites, mostly WordPress and PrestaShop installations. The operators injected each site with a script that fetches its next-stage payload from a smart contract on the BNB Smart Chain Testnet, a technique the industry calls EtherHiding. Because the BSC Testnet is free and functionally equivalent to mainnet, the attackers get a resilient, censorship-resistant delivery channel at zero cost. The initial compromise vector for these sites has not been identified.

The delivery chain is straightforward. The injected script presents a fake CAPTCHA page that instructs the visitor to open the Windows Run dialog and paste a PowerShell command. That command downloads and executes the final payload. Because the payload lives inside a smart contract, the attackers can swap it at will. Netskope observed the campaign evolve in the field: the ClickFix payload was replaced with a WebRTC data-channel stager, allowing the operators to adapt without touching the compromised sites again.

This matters because it demonstrates how threat actors are moving beyond traditional C2 infrastructure. Blockchain-based payload storage makes takedowns far harder than taking down a single VPS or domain. The scale is significant for a campaign targeting small businesses that typically lack security monitoring. Defenders should watch for additional EtherHiding campaigns and consider smart contract analysis as part of their threat intelligence workflow.
