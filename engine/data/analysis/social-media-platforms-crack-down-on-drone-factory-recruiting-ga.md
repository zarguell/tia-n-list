A video game about drone warfare has become a recruiting vector for a Russian drone factory. Investigators tie the online game Drone Battle: Ukraine to the Alabuga Special Economic Zone in Tatarstan, a site associated with production of attack drones used in the war against Ukraine. The game is part of a broader influence campaign that has run across YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, and X to reach young people, and social media platforms are now cracking down on the content.

The story is less about malware than about platform-based influence. The campaign uses game mechanics and social media presence to build trust with minors, then moves them toward the drone program, a recruitment pattern that mirrors the one used to staff the Iranian drone industry. Platform takedowns respond to the visible layer, but the operators can re-post under new accounts, which is the standard arms race for this kind of content.

For defenders the lesson is the same one that applies to corporate social engineering: trust-building is the delivery mechanism, and the platform content is only the front end. Watch for follow-on takedowns and for reporting on how far the pipeline extends, particularly whether the game is a funnel for technical training or for personnel generally.
