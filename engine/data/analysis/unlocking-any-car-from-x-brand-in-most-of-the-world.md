Shubham Shah and Sam Curry disclosed a vulnerability chain that gave remote code execution on the production ForgeRock identity servers handling customer logins for two unnamed automakers, then used it to take over customer accounts and their connected vehicles. Published November 7, 2026, the research chains a WAF path-confusion bypass, using encoded slashes and semicolon path parameters, past the edge to reach a hidden Apache Felix console on OpenIDM protected only by default admin:admin credentials. It then abuses OpenIDM's scheduler to run Groovy and install an OSGi servlet that provides a shell on the identity server. From that shell the researchers recovered idmadmin credentials, queried and patched managed-user records over localhost to change a victim's email and password, completed the normal mobile-app login and OTP flow, exchanged the session for OAuth tokens, retrieved the garage and VINs, and sent remote commands such as door unlock and engine start through the same telematics APIs the owner's app uses.

Why it matters: the same chain worked against both automakers' identity stacks across multiple regions, and the production access extended to customer records and vehicle commands, including location, telemetry, lock and unlock, and remote engine start and stop. The findings also highlight systemic issues: default Felix console credentials, an exposed ConfigMgr that can schedule arbitrary Groovy, and a WAF whose URL normalization disagreed with Jetty, Felix, and ForgeRock CREST. Both automakers remediated within roughly two hours of disclosure and rotated affected credentials, and testing was limited to US accounts and vehicles.

What to watch: whether other ForgeRock deployments share the same WAF path-confusion and default-console exposure, and any follow-up advisories or hardening guidance. Defenders should verify that OpenIDM administrative endpoints and the Felix console are not reachable through path-encoding tricks and that default credentials are changed.
