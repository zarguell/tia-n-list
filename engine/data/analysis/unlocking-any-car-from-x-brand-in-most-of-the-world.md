Shubham Shah and Sam Curry disclosed a chain against two unnamed automakers' ForgeRock-based identity platforms: default `admin:admin` credentials on the Apache Felix (OpenIDM) admin console, leaked in a vendor JavaScript bundle, combined with a WAF path-confusion bypass (`/static%2f..%2fopenidm/`) and an MFA bypass via the resource-owner password credentials (ROPC) flow, yielding remote code execution on production identity servers and the ability to mint consumer app tokens for any customer. The research, published 2026-11-07 on samcurry.net and shubs.io, covers vehicles in Australia, the United States, and Canada; the Australian chain ran from license plate to VIN to subscriber GUID to OpenIDM account takeover. No CVE is assigned because the defect is deployment and configuration, not a product flaw, and Ping Identity's hardening guide already calls for removing or protecting the Felix console and changing default credentials.

The impact is not "car hacking" novelty but identity-provider failure at vehicle scale: any account holder of the two brands could have had their vehicle located, unlocked, and engine-started by an attacker who knew only a plate number, and the same token path exposed customer PII (password hashes, PIN hashes, push tokens, names, phone numbers) from the `getUser` endpoint. Exposure is bounded to the two unnamed brands' connected-car populations in three regions, but the blast radius is account-takeover of a safety-critical asset class, not a data leak, and the same default-credential and client-side-secret patterns recur across other ForgeRock deployments. The reason this story matters more than the day's other credential findings is that the control plane and the vehicle command plane share one identity token, so a single leaked `admin:admin` pair collapses authentication, authorization, and physical actuation into one hop.

Defenders should treat this as an identity-provider and edge-gateway review, not a patch event: rotate any default or shared IDM/Felix console credentials, remove or IP-restrict the Felix console, audit JavaScript bundles and mobile apps for embedded secrets, and verify WAF rules normalize and block encoded path traversal (`%2f`, `..`) consistently across the WAF, origin, and application routing layers. Hunt in access logs for requests to `/openidm/` and `/static%2f..%2fopenidm/`, unexpected `X-OpenIDM-Username: idmadmin` usage, password-reset or PATCH operations against `managed/user`, and ROPC `grant_type=password` token issuance outside normal client flows; the `getUser` endpoint returning password and PIN hashes is a second detection point. Owners of the two brands should assume connected-app credentials may be exposed until the automakers confirm remediation and re-enroll MFA.

What would change the assessment is whether either automaker confirms region-by-region remediation and credential rotation, whether the same chain surfaces against other ForgeRock or PingIDM customers whose consoles are internet-reachable, and whether Ping Identity tightens defaults or makes the Felix console non-default in future releases.
