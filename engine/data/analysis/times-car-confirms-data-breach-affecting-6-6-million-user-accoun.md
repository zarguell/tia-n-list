Times Car, the Japanese car-sharing and rental service operated by Times Mobility under the Park24 Group, has confirmed that roughly 6.6 million accounts were compromised. The company disclosed the intrusion on September 25, saying a third party got into its systems at the beginning of September and was blocked on September 26. A September 28 update confirmed data theft affecting current and former Times Car members plus current and former members of the Times Business Service corporate account program.

The exposed set is broad: full name, physical address, date of birth, phone number, email, driver's license information including images of identity verification documents, account passwords, linked service IDs, and department names for corporate members. Passwords were reportedly stored in a form that cannot be restored, which suggests hashing or encryption, and credit card data was confirmed unaffected. The company says there is no evidence yet that the stolen data has been distributed online.

The driver's license images are the sensitive item here. Combined with names, birth dates and addresses, they are enough for convincing identity fraud and for passing weak identity checks on other Japanese services. This is the second large Japanese consumer breach confirmed this month alongside the Keio ransomware incident, and the pattern is consistent: long dwell time early in the month, containment within days, confirmation of theft only after forensics.

Worth watching: whether the data surfaces on leak sites or in Japanese-language fraud channels, whether Times Car details how passwords were protected and forces resets, and any regulatory response under Japan's APPI. Six million identity-document images would be a durable fraud resource even if never leaked publicly.
