Park24 confirmed a third party obtained data on about 6.6 million accounts from the Times Car web system, the car-sharing service run by Times Mobility in the Park24 Group, whose membership passed 4 million in August 2026. The company detected unauthorized access at 09:07 JST on September 25, cut the intrusion path and blocked attack-source traffic with post-block verification complete by 07:25 JST on September 26, then confirmed data theft in its second report published September 28 at 13:30 JST, about 16 hours before the first English-language coverage. The stolen set spans names, addresses, dates of birth, phone numbers, email addresses, driver's license information, identity verification document images, passwords, and linked service IDs covering nine external services, including JR West's WESTER ID. Park24 says passwords were stored in a form that cannot be restored, credit card data was untouched, and the case has been reported to Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission and the police.

The password field is the least of it. Park24's third report, published September 29, put the number of accounts whose verification images leaked at about 1.6 million: driver's licenses, address proofs such as utility bills, student IDs on the student plan, and family-verification documents on the family plan. A license image plus name, address, and date of birth is a working identity kit for help-desk impersonation and for photo-ID checks at unrelated services, so the realistic harm lands on victims as account takeover and fraud well outside Times Car. The 6.6 million figure also counts people who applied but never finished enrolling and long-gone former members, meaning many affected people do not think of themselves as Times Car customers and may dismiss the notification as spam. The nine linked service IDs hand attackers valid identifiers for partner programs that no Times Car breach notice will reach.

For members, change the Times Car password and any account that shared it, treat inbound mail, SMS, and calls claiming to be from Times Car as hostile, since Park24 states it never asks for passwords or card details through those channels, and use the 24-hour hotline 0120-25-8924 or the form at share.timescar.jp/inquiry.html to verify contact. Email notices to the 1.6 million document-exposure accounts began September 29, with per-account detail promised within about two weeks. Defenders at Japanese-facing organizations should brief help desks for verification social engineering built on real license data, tune mail filters for Times Car impersonation lures, and expect fraud attempts against KYC flows that accept driver's license images. Any organization storing ID document images should re-examine its retention window against this incident.

Two developments would change the assessment. Publication of the stolen set on a leak site or broker market would contradict Park24's current position that no distribution is confirmed and shift this from exposure to active fraud preparation. The follow-up reports and the per-account notices due within two weeks could grow the 1.6 million document figure or add data categories, which would widen the fraud exposure and likely draw formal action from the Personal Information Protection Commission.
