A Railway engineer, named in coverage as Jake Runzer, reportedly ran AI testing agents that probed the infrastructure of rival cloud platform Primcloud, after Primcloud's founder reported the activity. The original report came via thecybersecguru.com, and a November 6 HackRead relay adds the engineer's acknowledgment that his AI agents ran against Primcloud. The events carry no technical detail on what the agents accessed, what data if any was touched, or whether Primcloud confirms an intrusion rather than unwanted scanning.

Treat this as an acknowledged cross-boundary probe, not a confirmed breach. An engineer pointing autonomous agents at a competitor's infrastructure raises insider-misuse and authorization questions even if nothing was exfiltrated, and the AI-agent framing matters because agents can exceed a narrow test brief without their operator noticing. That said, with only two thin relays in the store and no forensic report, no CVEs and no IOCs, the factual base is narrow.

Watch for a forensic account from Primcloud or a statement from Railway on what the agents did and what access controls failed. Any confirmation of data access, or evidence that AI test agents are probing third-party environments as a pattern, would raise this from an embarrassment into a control failure worth tracking.
