A reported intrusion at Wellington IT, a Belfast-based supplier used by about 50 credit unions including 43 in Ireland, exposed member data linked to about 40 Irish credit unions. Minister of State Robert Troy confirmed to RTÉ that Wellington IT was the third-party provider involved. The Irish Times reported that more than 40 credit unions were contacted earlier this week, and Tánaiste and Finance Minister Simon Harris called the potential breach deeply concerning.

Notices seen by the Irish Examiner describe an automated script that made five million requests against an authorization flaw between October 2 and 4, 2026, using valid credentials to reach private records. Wellington IT said it lacks activity logs detailed enough to say which member data was accessed, so the impact on individual payees cannot be verified. The disclosures warn of financial fraud, impersonation and targeted phishing risk; the total number of affected people remains unknown, and at least six credit unions have disclosed impact on their own.

Troy called the delay in notifying the credit unions not acceptable. The next developments to watch are a fuller Wellington IT forensic statement, the total affected population, and whether the five-million-request script can be tied to a specific actor or to broader credential misuse at other customers of the provider.
