Wellington IT, the Belfast provider of banking software to about 50 credit unions including 43 in Ireland, is the supplier Minister of State Robert Troy linked to a breach affecting roughly 40 Irish credit unions. Notices seen by the Irish Examiner say an automated script made five million requests against an authorization flaw between October 2 and 4, 2026, then used valid credentials to reach private member records. Wellington IT has not disclosed the incident publicly and says it lacks the activity logs needed to identify which member data was accessed. The Irish Times reported on November 17 that the Irish League of Credit Unions was told the vulnerability was closed and that accounts and balances were not compromised.

The scale argument is about concentration, not malware: one supplier spans 43 of Ireland's credit unions, and the Irish Times put the potential population at 1.8 million members, more than half of Irish credit union members. Payment-out details and saved payee information give an attacker enough context for credible impersonation even when balances stay intact, and absent activity logs leave each credit union unable to scope its own notifications. That combination matters more than the story's 5.6 score: a closed vulnerability does not undo the exposure from October, and Troy's public criticism of the notification delay is the real indicator of unresolved risk.

Defenders should press Wellington IT for written confirmation of the fix and a product-level scope, then hunt their own access logs from October 2 to 4 for the automated request pattern and for successful access from legitimate credentialed sessions. Where those logs do not exist, treat the member population as unknown and tighten transaction monitoring for new payees, unexpected payment requests and outbound payment changes. Use the notices' own warnings to brief members on fraud, impersonation and targeted phishing that cites account or payment details, and track the breach notifications the Data Protection Commission said it had already received.

Watch for a formal Wellington IT disclosure that names the affected platform, the field-level data and a confirmed victim count, and for DPC or Central Bank findings that replace the 1.8 million ceiling with a verified number. A claim of actual financial loss or an actor attribution would also change the assessment, since the reporting so far describes risk and notification failure rather than confirmed theft.
