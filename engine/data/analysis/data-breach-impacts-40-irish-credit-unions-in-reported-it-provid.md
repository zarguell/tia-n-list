A breach at Wellington IT, a Belfast based supplier used by about 50 credit unions including 43 in Ireland, has exposed member data from roughly 40 Irish credit unions. Minister of State Robert Troy confirmed the provider's role to RTÉ, and Tánaiste Simon Harris called the suspected breach deeply concerning. The Data Protection Commission has received notifications from affected credit unions, and at least six credit unions have disclosed impact on their own.

According to notices seen by the Irish Examiner, an automated script made five million requests against an authorization flaw between October 2 and 4, 2026, and the intruder used valid credentials to reach private records. Wellington IT said it lacks activity logs detailed enough to say which member data was taken, so the number of affected individuals is still unknown. Reporting put potential exposure at 1.8 million members, with payment details leaving accounts in scope while balances were not compromised.

The stated risk is financial fraud, impersonation and targeted phishing against credit union members. Troy has called the delay in warning the credit unions not acceptable and now wants to meet the provider. Watch for per-credit-union notifications, any DPC finding on notification timing, and whether the five million request script and missing logs can be tied to specific records.
