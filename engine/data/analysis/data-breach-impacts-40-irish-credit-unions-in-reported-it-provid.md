A cyberattack on Wellington IT, a Belfast supplier used by about 50 credit unions including 43 in Ireland, exposed data connected to roughly 40 Irish credit unions. Minister of State Robert Troy confirmed to RTÉ that Wellington IT was the third-party provider behind the incident. The company reportedly notified affected organizations that an automated script made five million requests against an authorization flaw in its infrastructure between October 2 and 4, 2026, and that the attacker used valid credentials to reach private records. Wellington IT has not publicly disclosed the incident itself.

The notices seen by the Irish Examiner warn of financial fraud, impersonation and targeted phishing against members. Wellington IT told the credit unions it lacks the activity logs needed to say which member data was accessed, so it cannot verify the impact on individual payees. Troy called the delay in notifying the credit unions unacceptable, and Tánaiste Simon Harris described the possible breach as deeply concerning. The total number of affected individuals was still unknown in the reporting reviewed here.

For defenders, the useful detail is the combination of an authorization flaw, credential-backed access and missing logs. That leaves member notifications without a reliable scope, which raises the value of monitoring for impersonation and phishing that references credit union accounts. Watch for a formal Wellington IT disclosure, a statement from Ireland's data protection authority, and any revised victim count as the affected credit unions complete their reviews.
