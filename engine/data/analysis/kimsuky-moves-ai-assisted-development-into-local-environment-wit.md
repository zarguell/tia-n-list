North Korean threat actor Kimsuky has expanded its espionage toolkit with locally hosted AI development environments as part of Operation GitPower. Researchers at Genians Security Center found that Kimsuky is building local systems capable of running large language models, processing private documents, supporting code development, and converting speech to text without sending data to external providers. The campaign retains the group's familiar spear-phishing methods using malicious Windows shortcut files, PowerShell loaders, and GitHub-based C2 infrastructure.

The shift to local AI tooling represents a significant operational evolution for Kimsuky. By hosting AI capabilities locally, the group can process stolen documents, develop malware, and automate social engineering without relying on public AI services that might log or monitor their activity. This approach also allows Kimsuky to fine-tune models on classified or sensitive documents harvested from previous intrusions.

The campaign demonstrates how state-backed threat actors are investing in AI infrastructure to scale their operations. Organizations in government, defense, and critical infrastructure sectors should expect Kimsuky to leverage these capabilities for more sophisticated phishing campaigns, faster malware development, and improved data exfiltration. Monitor for GitHub-based C2 activity and unusual PowerShell execution patterns consistent with Kimsuky's established TTPs.
