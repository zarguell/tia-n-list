The FBI seized domains associated with QScan and QTRouter, two hacking platforms operated by a Chinese state-sponsored group the DOJ calls QTFY. Court records from the Southern District of California identified the operator as Nanjing Xinjiuwei Network Technology Company, a private Chinese firm that sold hacking services to China's Ministry of State Security and military. The group has targeted NASA, the Federal Reserve, the National Institutes of Health, the US Senate, and the Departments of Energy, Justice, and Health and Human Services since 2018. Hospitals, telecoms, power companies, banks, and defense contractors were also targeted.

The infrastructure included four components: QScan for reconnaissance and target profiling, Fast Labyrinth as an encrypted relay network, QTRouter as a preconfigured access device, and QTProxy for relay management. Black Lotus Labs (Lumen Technologies) tracked the infrastructure for a year before the takedown. Court filings indicate the group also attempted, unsuccessfully, to access a US election system in June. The operation was described as a "quartermaster" service providing reusable capabilities to Chinese cyber espionage campaigns, and the seizure represents a disruption of the supply chain enabling those operations.
