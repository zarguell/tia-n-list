The FBI seized three domains used by Chinese state-sponsored hackers to operate QScan and QTRouter, offensive tools deployed against U.S. federal agencies since 2018. The operation was announced Wednesday by the Justice Department. The tools were built and maintained by Nanjing Xinjiuwei Network Technology Company, a private firm that sold hacking services to China's Ministry of State Security and military.

According to an FBI affidavit, the group known as QTFY targeted networks belonging to NASA, the Federal Reserve, the National Institutes of Health, the U.S. Senate, and the departments of Energy, Justice, and Health and Human Services. Hospitals, telecommunications providers, power companies, banks, and defense contractors were also in scope. The filing notes that not every attack succeeded; a 2019 attempt against NASA was blocked because the agency had already patched the targeted vulnerability.

This takedown removes operational infrastructure rather than disrupting a live campaign. Organizations in the listed sectors should verify network logs for indicators of QScan or QTRouter activity, particularly those with internet-facing services exposed since 2018. The breadth of targets, spanning federal agencies and critical infrastructure, suggests the operators had access to high-privilege access brokers or exploited widely deployed software.
