Prophet Security's Q2 2026 quarterly threat report (May-July) found that identity was the target in roughly half of all confirmed malicious activity across customer environments. Their AI SOC analyst investigated every alert across endpoint, identity, email, cloud, and network telemetry. Of investigated alerts, 93% were benign and 7% confirmed malicious. The findings are based on 4.7 million questions asked of customer environments, with a median of 35 per investigation.

The four main attack patterns: (1) Session hijacking was the top initial access method (18% of malicious activity). Stolen authenticated sessions bypassed conditional access policies entirely, and password-based attempts were usually blocked by conditional access and phishing-resistant MFA. Individual accounts had dozens of malicious sign-ins over three weeks in some cases. (2) Infostealers delivered via browsers (23% of activity) were the primary source of stolen sessions. ClearFake, SocGholish, and ClickFix lures delivered Lumma Stealer, Vidar, HijackLoader, and AsyncRAT. Trojanized software installers were found at roughly a quarter of organizations. (3) Phishing (28%) was highly targeted, hitting finance executives repeatedly over months. Emails came from legitimate collaboration platforms and bypassed SPF/DKIM/DMARC in some cases. (4) Pre-ransomware activity (9%) mostly occurred on unmonitored assets without endpoint agents.

The key finding is that stolen session cookies, hidden inbox rules, and persistent remote-support tools all survive password resets. Two organizations discovered their incident response stopped at credential reset while the attacker maintained access. The strongest predictor of successful account takeover was whether the attacker used a password or an already-authenticated session.
