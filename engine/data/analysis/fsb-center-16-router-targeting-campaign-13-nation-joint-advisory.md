The NSA, FBI, and cyber authorities from twelve partner nations, including Canada, Australia, the UK, and ten other allies, have reissued a joint advisory on FSB Center 16's campaign against edge network devices, and the update is technical detail, not a new bug. Center 16 operators scan the internet for routers and switches running default or weak passwords, then abuse Cisco Smart Install and two named vulnerabilities: CVE-2018-0171, an unauthenticated remote code execution and denial of service flaw in the Smart Install feature of Cisco IOS and IOS XE that Cisco disclosed on March 28, 2018, and CVE-2008-4128, a cross-site request forgery in the HTTP administration component of IOS 12.4 on 871 series routers, published September 18, 2008. The targeting set has not moved in years: defense industrial base, energy, financial services, and healthcare, the same sectors this desk covered on July 10 with the Berserk Bear attribution and July 11 with the LapDogs campaign.

Nothing in this advisory is new except the date, and that is the argument. The same Smart Install flaw was weaponized within ten days of its 2018 disclosure: a group signing itself JHT rewrote configurations on roughly 200,000 routers worldwide, 3,500 of them in Iran, per Iran's ICT ministry, and TA18-106A warned about Russian state actors on routers a week after that. Center 16 still working CVE-2018-0171 and CVE-2008-4128 in 2026 means eight years of monthly patch cycles have not cleared these flaws out of critical infrastructure, and an eighteen-year-old CSRF bug still pays rent on forgotten edge boxes. Any device that still answers on Smart Install has effectively never been patched, so its compromise is an inventory and lifecycle failure, not an intelligence gap. The thirteen-signature list is also the message: allied governments are telling their own operators they share this victim base, and they expect it to persist.

Kill Smart Install outright rather than patching around it: remove the feature with "no vstack", confirm with "show vstack summary", and upgrade IOS and IOS XE to the fixed releases listed in cisco-sa-20180328-smi2. The TA18-106A hunt still works eight years later: alert on any TCP traffic to port 4786, the Smart Install port, that does not originate from a trusted management host, and hold SNMP on 161/162 and HTTP on 80 to the same standard. Purge default and weak credentials on every edge device, rotate local account passwords, and per the advisory watch for unusual credential use and logins through local accounts. Pull current configurations from exposed devices and look for the harvest pattern TA18-106A documented: password hash values and SNMP community strings lifted from stolen config files are what let Center 16 derive legitimate credentials and come back later.

Two developments would change this assessment. A 2026-vintage edge device CVE folding into the same Center 16 toolset would signal active exploitation of current bugs instead of stale ones, which would raise severity sharply. Named victims, sector counts, or exploitation telemetry tied to this advisory would convert a reiteration into an incident and justify emergency exposure sweeps.
