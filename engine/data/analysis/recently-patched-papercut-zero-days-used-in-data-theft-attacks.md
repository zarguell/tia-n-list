Two PaperCut NG/MF zero-days, CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078, were chained to bypass authentication and achieve remote code execution on print management servers used by 100 million users across 70,000 organizations. PaperCut released emergency patches on August 28 and 29, but by the weekend Defused confirmed active data-theft exploitation in its honeypots. Attackers are abusing the auth bypass to hijack PaperCut's external user-lookup mechanism and dump database tables via Derby, rather than taking the RCE path documented in public writeups.

Shadowserver tracks over 800 exposed PaperCut MF and NG servers that remain vulnerable. PaperCut has not attributed the attacks or described the post-compromise objectives. The divergence between the publicly documented RCE path and the observed data-theft technique suggests at least one actor adapted the exploit chain for information theft rather than persistent access.

Given the installed base and the speed of exploitation after disclosure, this is a patch-now situation. Organizations running PaperCut NG or MF should verify they have applied both emergency patches, check for the IOCs PaperCut published, and review logs for unusual external user-lookup activity or Derby database access.
