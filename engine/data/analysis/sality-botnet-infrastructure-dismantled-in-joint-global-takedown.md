On 31 August 2026, an international law enforcement operation seized infrastructure belonging to the Sality P2P botnet, which had been active since at least 2003. The U.S. DOJ, FBI, and DCIS seized Sality-linked domains in the United States, while partners in Bulgaria, Hungary, and Romania took down European-hosted domains. CrowdStrike's Counter Adversary Operations team executed a P2P sinkhole that isolated infected machines from the botnet's control channels. Europol confirmed the operation targeted a network linked to over 11 million infected IP addresses worldwide over its lifespan.

CrowdStrike tracks the operator as SALTY SPIDER, believed to be based in the Republic of Bashkortostan, Russia. At the time of the takedown, two active Sality sub-networks were pushing EggJagger malware payloads through clipjacking attacks. Historically, Sality distributed a wide variety of malware including banking trojans and information stealers. The botnet had infected over 15,000 active devices when it was disrupted. This is a significant law enforcement success against one of the longest-running criminal botnets, though the 23-year operational lifespan underscores how difficult sustained P2P infrastructure is to fully eradicate.
