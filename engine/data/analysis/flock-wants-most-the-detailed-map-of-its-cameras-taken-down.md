Flock Safety has asked security researchers to take down a map documenting its surveillance camera network. Per Tom's Hardware, the map charted 335 to 701 camera locations nationwide and drew on an unauthenticated flaw that exposed Flock camera data. Flock wants the map removed rather than leaving the exposure question to a patch-and-disclose cycle, and community discussion of the request continues on Mastodon.

The significance is the collision between vendor takedown pressure and public-interest research on surveillance infrastructure. An unauthenticated flaw in a camera network of this scale is a security problem, but the map is also a record of surveillance scope that Flock would prefer not to exist. Watch whether the researchers comply, whether Flock pursues legal action, and whether the underlying unauthenticated access flaw gets a proper advisory, since the takedown request does not fix the flaw itself.
