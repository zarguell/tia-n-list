Federal agents arrested OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan, 35, a Saudi national employed since July 2025, on October 9 for allegedly planting a hidden recording device in an executive conference room at OpenAI's San Francisco offices. The Justice Department charged him with illegally intercepting oral communications. OpenAI security found the device on September 30 plugged into a USB port behind a television, concealed in toilet paper and installed around September 28, containing encrypted data.

The room was routinely used for meetings covering proprietary research, product plans, technical capabilities, business relationships, and government engagements. Security camera footage allegedly showed Alfozan near the room repeatedly despite lacking authorization for those meetings, and the complaint says he used a productivity tool to search for information about OpenAI's internal matters. The FBI made the arrest after DOJ prosecutors announced the case Tuesday.

The insider-threat angle is the substance here: a recording device in a room handling frontier model research and government work raises counterintelligence questions beyond ordinary workplace misconduct. Watch for court filings detailing a motive and whether the encrypted contents or any exfiltration are disclosed.
