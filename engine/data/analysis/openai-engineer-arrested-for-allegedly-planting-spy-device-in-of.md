The US Department of Justice charged Abdulrahman Alfozan, a 35-year-old Saudi national who worked at OpenAI since July 2025, with illegally intercepting oral communications after he allegedly planted a hidden audio recording device in an executive conference room at the company's San Francisco offices. OpenAI security discovered the device on September 30, 2026, plugged into a USB port behind a television and concealed in toilet paper; it had been installed around September 28 and contained encrypted data. Alfozan was arrested on October 9 and made his first court appearance on October 13. The room was used for meetings involving confidential research, product plans, technical capabilities, business relationships, and government engagements. Security camera footage allegedly showed Alfozan near the room on multiple occasions despite not being authorized to attend.

OpenAI stated it used its own models to understand the device and trace its origin, identified the specific employee, referred the matter to the FBI, and terminated him. The company said it has no indications that the individual inappropriately accessed or exfiltrated model weights, source code, or customer data. FBI Director Kash Patel and DNI Jay Clayton publicly framed the case as a counterintelligence matter tied to protecting American superintelligence technology.

What to watch: the complaint does not detail what Alfozan intended to do with any captured communications, and the FBI investigation is ongoing. The case underscores insider-threat risk in AI labs handling sensitive research and government engagements, even where no data exfiltration is confirmed.
