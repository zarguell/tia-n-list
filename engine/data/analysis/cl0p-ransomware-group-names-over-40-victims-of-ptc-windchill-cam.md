The Cl0p ransomware group has named over 40 victims from its campaign targeting PTC Windchill, an industrial product lifecycle management platform used heavily in manufacturing and engineering. The mass-naming follows Cl0p's established pattern of exploiting a single vulnerability in a widely deployed enterprise product, harvesting data from all reachable instances, then pressuring victims individually with leak threats.

PTC Windchill manages CAD files, BOMs, and engineering change orders, meaning compromised instances expose proprietary product designs, manufacturing specifications, and internal communications. The 40+ named victims likely represent a fraction of the total compromise, as Cl0p typically exfiltrates from many more organizations than it publicly names. The campaign echoes Cl0p's earlier MOVEit and GoAnywhere mass-exploitation plays, where a single zero-day or misconfiguration yielded hundreds of victims.

Organizations running PTC Windchill should verify they are on the patched version and audit logs for unauthorized data export. The naming cadence suggests Cl0p is now in the extortion phase for this first batch of victims, with more names likely to follow. Watch for PTC's advisory updates and any CISA involvement if the vulnerability qualifies for the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog.
