Researchers at Hunt.io documented a campaign they dubbed CameraSwarm that compromised over 14,500 Dahua IP cameras across Ukraine and Russia between June 17 and July 22, 2026. The operator exploited CVE-2021-33044 and CVE-2021-33045 — authentication bypass flaws in Dahua firmware that have been known since 2021 — alongside brute-force attacks and offline recovery codes for cloud-registered devices. Hunt.io discovered the campaign after finding an unprotected working directory on the attacker's HTTP server, which contained 407 MB of data including source code, credentials, captured camera images, and exploitation results.

The scale is striking: 14,500 cameras in 35 days suggests automated tooling operating at high throughput. The geographic concentration in Ukraine and Russia points to either a state-sponsored collection operation or a botnet builder targeting a specific region. The fact that the operator left their infrastructure exposed indicates operational security failures, which is common in large-scale automated campaigns where speed outpaces discipline.

These CVEs are five years old and have known patches. The persistence of vulnerable Dahua cameras in the wild — enough to yield a 14,500-victim campaign — highlights the gap between patch availability and deployment in IoT devices. Organizations running Dahua hardware should verify firmware versions and disable cloud recovery features where not needed. Watch for follow-on analysis of the captured data to determine whether this was espionage, botnet recruitment, or both.
