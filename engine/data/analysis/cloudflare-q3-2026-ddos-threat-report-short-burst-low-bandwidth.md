Cloudflare's Q3 2026 DDoS threat report, first published on 2026-10-29 via blog.cloudflare.com, documents 18.2 million network-layer attacks mitigated during the quarter. Short-burst, low-bandwidth floods lasting under 10 minutes and below 1 Gbps surged by 208 percent compared with Q3 2025, while hyper-volumetric attacks declined in share. Event records show a concurrent shift toward DNS and UDP protocols and coverage from securityonline.info and gbhackers.com on 2026-10-30.

This shift matters because short, frequent disruptions bypass traditional bandwidth-based alerting faster than sustained volumetric floods, allowing attackers to exhaust rate-limit policies before defenders can respond. The 18.2 million figure and the 208 percent increase are not abstract trends; they represent a measurable change in attacker economics favoring low-cost, high-frequency campaigns over large-scale infrastructure investment. Without a CVE or named actor, the exposure is still concrete: any organization relying solely on peak-bandwidth thresholds is under-matched for sub-minute events.

Defenders should review rate-limit tuning for sub-minute events, audit DNS and UDP telemetry specifically, and check log paths for sudden spikes in small-packet volume rather than only throughput peaks. No patch or hotfix applies because this is a statistical threat report rather than a product vulnerability; network restrictions and telemetry adjustments are the direct mitigation. Watch for follow-on reports that attribute the 208 percent surge to a specific botnet or campaign, or that release updated Cloudflare mitigation guidance for low-bandwidth burst patterns.
