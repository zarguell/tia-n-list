Security company Gambit reports that a Chinese-speaking financially motivated actor used three open-source AI agents to automate vulnerability discovery and exploitation, breaching 27 companies and stealing more than 600,000 credit card records. The reporting describes low-cost retail intrusions turned into a payment card theft operation, with the agents handling vulnerability discovery and exploitation end to end rather than assisting a human operator.

This is one of the clearest documented cases of agentic AI running the attack loop itself, and the economics are the point: open-source agents and modest infrastructure replaced the manual skill that used to gate card-breaching campaigns. If the workflow generalizes, expect higher attack volume against mid-size retail and e-commerce targets that cannot patch fast, with stolen card data monetized as before.

Watch for Gambit or affected acquirers to name victims or publish indicators, for the specific open-source agent frameworks to be identified and hardened, and for copycat campaigns reusing the same tooling. The 27-company count and 600,000-record figure are Gambit's reporting, so treat precise numbers as provisional until corroborated by payment processors or law enforcement.
