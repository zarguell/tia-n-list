Federal agents searched more than 40 Texas residences and seized 187 laptops and other devices, dismantling a network of US-based laptop farms that put North Korean operatives into remote jobs at American companies under false identities. The Justice Department branded the action Operation Lonestar Roundup, CrowdStrike's Counter Adversary Operations supplied the intelligence and technical analysis, and the broader campaign touched more than 70 US companies and produced two arrests in Ohio. The mechanics are unglamorous: US-based facilitators hosted employer-issued laptops, workers abroad reached them remotely, and facilitators moved salaries into North Korea-controlled accounts. Same-day reporting put the charge count at 14 and the fraud value at roughly $3 million.

The shift that matters is that the laptop farm is a business process rather than an intrusion technique, so the point of compromise is the offer letter. A placed worker arrives with a provisioned endpoint, enrolled credentials, VPN access and a payroll line, meaning the employer absorbs network risk and wage leakage from day one of employment. The scale is not anecdotal: the 2025 enforcement wave raided 29 laptop farms across 16 states, the Christina Chapman case ended in an 8.5-year sentence for a scheme that moved about $17.1 million through 136 US companies, and the December 2024 indictment of 14 North Koreans priced six years of the trade at $88 million. Against that baseline this campaign is small, and the arrest-to-sentencing pipeline now running means victim companies get named in charging documents, which converts a bad hire into follow-on identity theft, the data extortion the FBI warned about in January 2025, and a public record of exactly who was fooled.

Hiring hygiene is the control that pays: Microsoft's Jasper Sleet tracking documents deepfake aids in interviews used by these crews, so live interviews, out-of-band identity verification, and checks on the legal entity behind the paycheck do more than endpoint hardening at the offer stage. If a hire looks wrong, treat the laptop as already compromised: preserve and image the device, revoke sessions and tokens rather than only wiping it, rotate credentials and MFA enrollments, and audit payroll for salaries routed through third-party accounts, the facilitator signature this case makes explicit. Report suspicions to the FBI, where Rewards for Justice has carried a $5 million offer since December 2024, and assume a terminated operative may pivot to extorting whatever data the role touched.

Watch two things: the charging documents, which should name the 70-plus victim companies and the facilitator identities, and whether the Ohio arrests connect to already-sentenced facilitators or expose a new node. Platform behavior is the other tell: Amazon said in December 2025 it had blocked more than 1,800 applications from suspected DPRK agents, and if large employers keep industrializing their screening, expect operatives to concentrate on smaller firms with weaker identity verification, the exact population the eleven-nation joint alert of August 2026 was written for.
