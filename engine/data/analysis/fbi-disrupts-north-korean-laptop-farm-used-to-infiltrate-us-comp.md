The FBI's Operation Lonestar Roundup dismantled a network of US-based "laptop farms" that let North Korean operatives hold remote jobs at American companies under false identities. Federal prosecutors announced Tuesday that agents searched more than 40 Texas residences and seized 187 laptops and other devices. The farm model is simple: US-based facilitators hosted employer-issued computers, DPRK workers reached them remotely, and facilitators moved salaries to North Korea-controlled accounts.

The Justice Department's announcement credits CrowdStrike's Counter Adversary Operations with intelligence and technical analysis. CrowdStrike says the broader campaign targeted more than 70 US companies and included two arrests in Ohio, though neither the arrestees nor the charges were named, and the Texas release does not mention the Ohio arrests. Authorities framed the scheme as both a corporate infiltration problem and a revenue stream for the regime's sanctioned weapons programs.

This is the operational arm of the DPRK IT-worker problem that advisories have warned about for two years: deepfake-aided identities, complicit or duped facilitators, and credential access inside victim networks from day one of employment. Companies that employed any of these workers should assume the laptop farm also meant network access, and should watch for follow-on indictments naming the facilitators and the victim companies.
