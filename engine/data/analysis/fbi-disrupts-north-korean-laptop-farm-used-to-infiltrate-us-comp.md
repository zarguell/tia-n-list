Federal authorities in Texas have charged 14 people and seized 187 laptops in Operation Lonestar Roundup, the latest strike against North Korea's remote IT worker scheme. More than 40 Texas residences suspected of operating as laptop farms were searched. The farms housed employer-issued computers so North Korean engineers working abroad could access U.S. corporate networks under false identities, with U.S.-based facilitators configuring the devices and routing pay to regime-controlled accounts.

An indictment unsealed in the Northern District of Ohio charges two U.S. citizens, Michael James Bostelle and Juan Cecilio Pena Carrillo, alongside 10 others located in China. Prosecutors say the group held remote jobs at more than 70 companies from 2019 through 2025 using stolen or fabricated identities and collected about $3 million. CrowdStrike provided intelligence and technical analysis supporting the FBI's work. Prosecutors framed the case as counterproliferation as much as fraud, saying the revenue funds sanctioned weapons programs.
