FinCEN confirmed unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems and took some systems offline while investigating, with the notice posted on the Bank Secrecy Act E-Filing portal. The affected filing paths are narrower than the banner language suggests: order FIN-2026-R002, issued November 24, names SDTM batch submissions of BSA reports and Beneficial Ownership Information Reports or FinCEN ID updates under 31 C.F.R. 1010.380, with due dates from November 18 through December 29 moved to December 30. The filing website remained up, and no actor has claimed the intrusion. There is no CVE, patch identifier, data-exfiltration statement, or attribution in the agency notice.

Calling this a limited outage understates the blast radius. The intrusion surfaced through a reporting failure: Hummingbird notice #515050 records an SDTM connection failure beginning about 17:45 ET on November 18, with FinCEN posting a service disruption notice only on November 19, while CU*SOUTH was rejecting CTR batches and tracking them as Ready to Transmit. No count of affected institutions or records has been published, but the exposure is the AML reporting chain itself, which carries suspicious activity reports and currency transaction reports from banks, credit unions, brokerages and casinos. FinCEN's silence on whether data was accessed matters more than the website staying online, and the deadline relief is the closest thing to an impact statement the agency has published.

Defenders have no patch to apply and no published indicators to block, so the immediate work is filing continuity and queue hygiene. Use the BSA E-Filing website for individual and batch reports instead of SDTM, route time-sensitive suspicious activity information through the Financial Institutions Hotline at 1-866-556-3974, and reconcile every CTR transmitted from November 18 onward against the portal, including the status in CU*SOUTH's tool #991 Work with CTR Forms, where a Saved state prevents a duplicate filing. Hunt SDTM connection and batch submission logs from November 18 forward for failed or queued transmissions and check whether filers shifted to the website path during the outage. The prior Treasury intrusions in 2024 and 2025, including the case with twelve Chinese nationals charged and two Ministry of Public Security officers, are context only and offer no grounds to attribute this event.

Watch for FinCEN saying whether data was accessed or taken and for any extension or revision of FIN-2026-R002 beyond the December 30 deadline. Either would change the assessment from a disruption with a known workaround to a data incident with a longer compliance tail.
