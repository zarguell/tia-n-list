FinCEN disclosed on Tuesday evening that it is investigating a cybersecurity incident that resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems. The notice sits on the Bank Secrecy Act E-Filing System website. Some systems were taken offline as a precaution; the E-Filing website stayed operational, with some services temporarily unavailable, and the Financial Institutions Hotline was unaffected.

The disruption reaches filers through Secure Data Transfer Mode. Hummingbird reported failed batch submissions of BSA reports from around 17:45 ET on November 18, and CU*SOUTH said credit unions using CBX had CTRs rejected and should file directly through the portal. FinCEN order FIN-2026-R002, dated November 24, moved deadlines for certain reports filed through that mode and for Beneficial Ownership Information Reports or FinCEN ID updates from November 18 through December 29 to December 30.

FinCEN has not said which systems were accessed, how the intrusion happened, how long it lasted, who was behind it, or whether data were stolen, and no group has claimed credit. The incident was disclosed hours after reports of the breach of an unclassified CIA and ODNI intelligence analysis system, though no link between them is reported. Watch for a restored SDTM service, details of the accessed subset, and any attribution; earlier Treasury breaches in 2024 and 2025 were tied to Chinese state-backed groups, with 12 Chinese nationals charged over the 2024 intrusion.
