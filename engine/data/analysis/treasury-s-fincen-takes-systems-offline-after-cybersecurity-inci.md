The Treasury Department posted a notice on the Bank Secrecy Act E-Filing System website saying FinCEN is investigating a cybersecurity incident that resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems. As a proactive measure, FinCEN took some systems offline. The filing website remained operational, but some services were temporarily unavailable. The agency has not said which systems were affected, how the intrusion happened, how long it lasted, whether data was taken, or who was responsible, and no actor has claimed credit.

FinCEN is the US financial intelligence unit, and the BSA E-Filing System carries suspicious activity reports and currency transaction reports from banks, credit unions, brokerages, casinos and other institutions. FinCEN said it would extend deadlines for affected filers through a November 24 order granting temporary, limited exceptive relief, and that its Financial Institutions Hotline was unaffected for institutions needing to expedite suspicious activity information to law enforcement. Reporting across The Record, ABC News, CyberScoop, SecurityWeek and The Hacker News is based on the same agency notice rather than independent detail on the intrusion.

The incident lands amid other US government intrusions, including a breach of an unclassified CIA system. It follows earlier Treasury compromises attributed to Chinese state-backed groups in 2024 and 2025; twelve Chinese nationals, including two Ministry of Public Security officers, were charged over the 2024 breach that reached computers used by then-Secretary Janet Yellen and her deputy. There is no evidence tying those operations to this one. Watch for restoration of the offline services, any filing backlog, and a FinCEN statement on data access or attribution.
