FinCEN disclosed on Tuesday evening that a cybersecurity incident gave an unauthorized party access to a limited subset of its systems and that some systems were taken offline as a proactive measure. The notice sits on the Bank Secrecy Act E-Filing portal. The agency said the BSA E-Filing website remained operational but some services could be unavailable, and it did not say what data was accessed, how the intrusion happened or who was responsible. No group has claimed the attack.

The disruption is visible in filer operations. Hummingbird reported that batch submissions through Secure Direct Transfer Mode stopped around 17:45 ET on November 18, with filings retained locally rather than lost. CU*SOUTH told credit unions that CTRs sent through CBX were rejected and advised direct portal filing, then warned on November 23 about changing filing type from Amended to Initial to avoid duplicates. FinCEN order FIN-2026-R002, issued November 24, moved deadlines for certain SDTM BSA reports and for Beneficial Ownership Information Reports or FinCEN ID updates from the November 18 to December 29 window to December 30. The Financial Institutions Hotline was unaffected.

FinCEN handles suspicious activity reports and other confidential Bank Secrecy Act filings, so questions about access to those records matter more than the brief public notice admits. Earlier Treasury intrusions in 2024 and 2025 were attributed to Chinese state-backed groups, and twelve Chinese nationals including two Ministry of Public Security officers were charged over the 2024 breach, but no similar attribution has been made here. Watch for a revised incident notice, restoration of SDTM batch service and any confirmation of data access beyond the unnamed subset of systems.
