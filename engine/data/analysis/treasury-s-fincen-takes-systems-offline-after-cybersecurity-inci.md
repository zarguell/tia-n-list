The Treasury Department posted a notice on the Bank Secrecy Act E-Filing System website saying its Financial Crimes Enforcement Network is investigating a cybersecurity incident that resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems. FinCEN took some systems offline as a precaution. The BSA E-Filing website remained operational, but the agency warned that some services could be temporarily unavailable and said it was extending deadlines for affected filers. Financial institutions that need to send suspicious activity reports were told to use a hotline. The incident was first reported by The Verge, and ABC News carried the same agency statement on 25 November.

No attacker has claimed responsibility, and the notice gives no timeline for restoring normal operations. The disclosure follows earlier Treasury intrusions attributed to Chinese state-backed groups in 2024 and 2025. Twelve Chinese nationals, including two officers with China's Ministry of Public Security, were charged over the 2024 breach, which reached computers used by then-Secretary Janet Yellen and the then-Deputy Secretary. There is no evidence in the event reporting that the current incident is linked to those actors.

What to watch is the scope of the accessed subset, whether FinCEN identifies what data was read, and how long the disrupted services stay down. Filing deadlines and SAR delivery are the practical impact for banks while systems remain offline. Any attribution or victim detail should wait for Treasury or FinCEN to publish it.
