FinCEN disclosed on Tuesday evening that it is investigating a cybersecurity incident that resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems. The notice sits on the Bank Secrecy Act E-Filing System website. Some systems were taken offline as a precaution; the E-Filing website stayed operational with some services temporarily unavailable, and the Financial Institutions Hotline was unaffected. A further outlet pickup on 26 November restates the confirmation without adding technical detail.

The disruption is visible in filing operations. Hummingbird reported failed batch submissions of BSA reports from around 17:45 ET on 18 November and said prepared filings were retained rather than lost. CU*SOUTH told credit unions that CTRs filed through CBX were rejected and should be sent directly through the portal, with guidance to change filing type from Amended to Initial to avoid duplicates. JBizNews reported that the outage affected only Secure Data Transfer Mode batch filers while the website remained up.

FinCEN issued order FIN-2026-R002 on 24 November, moving deadlines for certain SDTM reports and for Beneficial Ownership Information Reports or FinCEN ID updates from 18 November through 29 December to 30 December. The agency has not said which systems were accessed, how the intrusion happened, how long it lasted, who was behind it or whether data were taken, and no group has claimed credit. The disclosure came in the same period as reports of a breach of an unclassified CIA and ODNI analysis system, but the available material reports no link.

The affected bureau receives suspicious activity reports from banks, credit unions, brokerages and casinos, which makes access to even a limited subset sensitive. Earlier Treasury intrusions in 2024 and 2025 were attributed to Chinese state-backed groups, and 12 Chinese nationals including two Ministry of Public Security officers were charged over the 2024 breach; that history is context, not attribution for this incident. Watch for restoration of SDTM, specifics on the accessed systems and any data-theft finding.
