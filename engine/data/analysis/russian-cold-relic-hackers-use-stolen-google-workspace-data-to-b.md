Google's Mandiant and Threat Intelligence Group published a case study on Cold Relic, the Russia-linked espionage group formerly tracked as COLDRIVER and UNC4057. In a 2026 compromise of a US organization, the attackers took control of an administrator account, downloaded the organization's entire cloud drive and employee directories, and searched email, passwords and personnel records. They stayed inside for several months, continued reading mail through third-party applications, and found ways back in after password changes and session resets. The Record dates the initial data theft to late 2025.

The stolen material became the input for a second intrusion. Cold Relic impersonated an employee using contact details and correspondence from the first victim to approach a second US organization, turning one cloud compromise into a launchpad for a linked breach. US authorities have tied the group to Russia's Federal Security Service, and earlier reporting lists government officials, defense contractors and nonprofit organizations in Europe and North America among its targets. The case shows that containment at the first victim does not end the risk when staff directories and mail history are already in attacker hands.

Watch for third-party applications with mail access, anomalous administrator downloads of drive content, and logins that persist through credential and session resets. Partner and contact notification deserves as much weight as endpoint cleanup when a Cold Relic incident is declared, because the second intrusion depended on trusted identity rather than a new exploit. The published material gives no victim names or counts for the second breach, so any scope judgment should wait for further Mandiant detail or disclosure from the affected organizations.
