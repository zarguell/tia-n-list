Google's Mandiant and Threat Intelligence Group investigated a 2026 compromise at a US-based organization and attributed a linked intrusion at a second US organization to Cold Relic, a Russia-linked espionage group also tracked as COLDRIVER and UNC4057. The attackers used information stolen in the first cloud compromise, including staff directories and correspondence, to impersonate an employee and gain access at the second organization. The Hacker News describes the operation as a single cloud compromise turned into a launchpad for third-party compromise.

GBHackers frames the same two intrusions as a warning that a cloud breach can keep putting business partners at risk after the original victim contains the incident. That is the practical consequence here: identity data taken from one tenant can be reused to target another organization that trusts the impersonated employee. This is an identity and owned-tenant problem, not a vulnerability with a patch number attached.

Watch for identity-centric follow-on activity after a Workspace compromise, especially unexpected logins that rely on harvested staff names and internal correspondence. Both articles tie the campaign to Google's investigation, but the carried text gives no named victim, CVE or IOC, so the claim rests on GTIG's attribution rather than independently enumerated artifacts. Defense therefore starts with auditing third-party access and Workspace identity signals rather than hunting a specific malware family.
