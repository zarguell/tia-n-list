Mandiant and Google Threat Intelligence Group investigated a 2026 cloud compromise at a U.S. organization and tied a linked intrusion at a second U.S. organization to Cold Relic, a Russia-linked espionage group also tracked as COLDRIVER and UNC4057. The operators used staff directories and correspondence stolen from the first tenant to impersonate an employee and gain access at the other. The reports carried on November 18, 2026 attach no CVE, patch version, victim name, log path or indicator to either intrusion.

The distinction that matters is that containment at the first victim did not end the operation: the stolen directory and mail corpus stayed useful as an identity kit after access was cut off, turning a closed incident into a partner's entry problem. A defender working this as a vulnerability queue will look for a patch that does not exist. The transferable asset was internal names, relationships and conversation context, and the trust being abused was the identity of an employee at a counterparty. Two U.S. organizations are named, and that count is a floor rather than a ceiling because the same material can be replayed against any partner the correspondence identifies. The alias change from COLDRIVER and UNC4057 to Cold Relic also means detections and older reporting keyed only to the former names can fail to link into this cluster.

With no CVE or hotfix version in the record, the response is identity-side: revoke standing third-party and partner access, reset credentials for affected accounts, and audit Workspace directory and correspondence access around the 2026 compromise rather than waiting for an indicator feed. The hunt starts where the events do, with logins or access requests that present an employee identity from the first tenant at partner tenants, reads or exports of staff directories and mailbox content, and any access path that survived initial containment. Since the carried text names no indicators, log paths or telemetry fields, staff directories and correspondence are the search pivot; building detections around invented addresses or filenames would only create false confidence.

Reassess if GTIG publishes indicators, a named victim or more than the two U.S. organizations described here, or if the chain is tied to a specific Workspace misconfiguration or product flaw that would make this a patchable, larger-scale exposure. Until then this is a targeted espionage chain with partner blast radius, not a broad cloud vulnerability event.
