Kaspersky GReAT researchers identified BlackX, a UEFI bootkit that exploits CVE-2022-21894 (Baton Drop) to bypass Secure Boot on fully patched systems that lack revocation updates. The malware targets diplomatic agencies in Central Asia and resembles BlackLotus, reusing an unrevoked vulnerable boot manager binary to maintain persistence below the operating system. Because Secure Boot is meant to block malicious bootloaders, a working bypass at this layer undermines a core platform integrity control.

The bootkit's reliance on a 2022 vulnerability highlights a revocation-gap risk: systems that never received the updated UEFI revocation list remain vulnerable years after the patch was released. Diplomatic targeting in Central Asia suggests state-aligned or espionage-focused threat actors rather than commodity cybercrime. The similarity to BlackLotus implies either shared tooling or deliberate reuse of proven boot-level techniques.

What to watch: whether Microsoft or OEMs push refreshed revocation lists to close the Baton Drop gap, whether Kaspersky publishes IOCs or boot-manager hashes for detection, and whether other bootkits adopt the same unrevoked binary. Organizations should verify their UEFI Secure Boot revocation databases include the 2022 updates rather than relying solely on OS-level patch status.
