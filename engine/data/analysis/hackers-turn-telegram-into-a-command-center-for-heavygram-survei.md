Group-IB has published a detailed analysis of HEAVYGRAM, a Windows backdoor that uses Telegram's bot API as its command-and-control channel. The malware is attributed with moderate confidence to Handala Hack, an Iran-linked threat actor assessed to be a front for Void Manticore (also tracked as Storm-0842), operating on behalf of Iran's Ministry of Intelligence and Security (MOIS). Active since Fall 2023, HEAVYGRAM has been used to surveil Iranian dissidents, journalists, and government opponents.

The infection chain begins with social engineering: victims are contacted via Telegram or messaging apps and tricked into running a first-stage loader disguised as legitimate software (KeePass, Telegram, or Pictory). The loader drops a Python-compiled persistent implant that communicates with Telegram bots for command execution, screenshot capture, audio recording, file exfiltration, and DLL side-loading. Group-IB identified 29 new samples, including a companion malware family called CRUDEEXCLUDE that adds Windows Defender exclusions to protect the implant. The C2 infrastructure uses single-bot and dual-bot configurations, with Telegram groups using Persian female names and photos of Iranian dissidents as cover.

The technical tradecraft is notable for its simplicity and resilience. Telegram provides an encrypted C2 channel with low setup cost and no need for dedicated infrastructure. Group-IB found that much of the identified Telegram infrastructure remains active as of 2026, with some accounts repurposed by unrelated actors. The U.S. DOJ seized four MOIS-linked domains in March 2026, including Handala Hack leak sites, and the FBI issued a FLASH report on the malware. Organizations with ties to Iranian dissident communities should audit for the IOCs listed in Group-IB's report, particularly the spoofed directory pattern "C:\Windows " (with trailing space) and anomalous outbound calls to api.telegram.org.
