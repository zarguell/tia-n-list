Dark web monitoring service DarkWebSonar flagged a data breach claim by the threat actor DBHunter, listing Nepal's Ministry of Finance as a target with access to a hacked database. A second claim from the same actor covered Xinjiang Changyuan Water Affairs Group and affiliated regional water supply and heating operators in China. DBHunter has not released sample data or provided proof of access beyond the listing, so the claims remain unverified.

Both claims follow the familiar pattern of a broker posting government and utility-sector databases for sale or extortion. Nepal's Ministry of Finance is a high-value target for espionage and financial fraud, while Chinese regional water infrastructure represents critical services. If either claim proves legitimate, the exposure could range from citizen financial records to operational data for water and heating utilities serving large populations.

The connection between a Nepali government ministry and a Chinese water utility appearing in the same actor's portfolio is notable but not necessarily coordinated. DBHunter may simply be a data broker aggregating breaches from multiple intrusion operations. Watch for verification signals: sample data releases, corroboration from the affected organizations, or overlapping indicators with known campaigns.
