SBC Medical Group disclosed two separate intrusions discovered on November 16, 2026. In its Form 8-K exhibit to the SEC, the company said an unauthorized third party accessed and extracted data from databases in a website system run by its Japanese subsidiary, with basic personal data for 1,624,000 customers from 2010 through 2023. The categories include names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, dates of birth, gender, patient card numbers and member IDs. SBC also counted 255,416 reservation requests, 217,769 web inquiries, 4,513 treatment-monitor records with image file names, 15,007 reviews and 14 expired card records. About 20,000 employee account records and bank or pension details for 459 employees were in the same disclosure.

The second incident hit Tribeau, a booking platform used by 56 clinics. Tribeau detected unauthorized logins on November 16 into clinic administrator accounts for Shonan Beauty Clinic and Ritz Beauty Surgery Tokyo and disabled the accounts that day. Reservation data for 12,641 people was exposed, with message content, date of birth and sex for three, and treatment name, date and fee for 6,804. The operator said the stolen records were not linked to case or review data with photographs and that it had not confirmed external publication or misuse.

The Japanese notice described roughly 770,000 consecutive attacks from overseas and passwords from the affected period stored unencrypted. SBC isolated the server, preserved logs, tightened access, invalidated Tribeau clinic passwords and blocked attacker communications. It reported the database incident to Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission on November 16 and told the SEC it could not reasonably estimate costs for notification, call center operations, forensics, legal work and compensation.

The exposure combines patient identifiers with reservation, treatment and fee detail, which is enough for targeted phishing that references real clinic visits. Watch for confirmation of the intrusion path, any evidence of data posting or misuse, and whether the unencrypted passwords lead to credential abuse at clinics or patients. The 1,624,000 and Tribeau counts overlap across categories, so they cannot be added into a single unique victim total.
