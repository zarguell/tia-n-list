Two concurrent incidents, not one breach, sit behind this story. On November 16, 2026 an unauthorized third party accessed databases in a website system managed by SBC Medical Group's Japanese subsidiary and extracted customer data; SBC's first report logged 770,000 consecutive attacks from overseas and said passwords from that era were stored unencrypted. Separately, the Tribeau operator detected unauthorized logins that day into clinic administrator accounts for Shonan Beauty Clinic and Ritz Beauty Surgery Tokyo, exposing reservation records for 12,641 people, treatment name, date and fee for 6,804, and message content, date of birth and sex for three. No CVE, version, named actor or intrusion route has been published; SBC said its investigation continues, and Tribeau said it is still determining how the credentials were obtained.

The headline count is the basic-information category of 1,624,000 people, not a de-duplicated victim total: reservation requests for 255,416, web inquiries for 217,769, treatment-monitor records for 4,513, reviews for 15,007 and card data for 14 sit in overlapping buckets, so SecurityWeek's over two million framing should not be read as two million distinct patients. What makes this story stand out is the combination of clinical context and reusable credentials. Names, addresses, birth dates, patient card numbers, treatment descriptions and stored image file names give an attacker enough detail to impersonate a clinic with a real appointment, while roughly 20,000 employee account records and bank or pension data for 459 staff create a second, internal phishing path; none of those secondary uses has been confirmed yet.

Defenders should treat the preserved system logs and November 16 monitoring alerts as the primary evidence, since no source has published a log path, port or IoC; the hunt is the overnight window in the SBC-managed database system, database egress to outside destinations, and Tribeau admin logins from the multiple domestic and overseas sources the operator described. SBC isolated the affected server, blocked access, tightened access restrictions and monitoring, reported to Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission, and commissioned external investigators; verify those controls also cover mySBC before assuming containment. Tribeau invalidated the affected clinic passwords and blocked attacker communications, so force resets for any reused credentials on connected systems, review the 56 clinics on the platform, and prepare customer messaging that does not ask for passwords or card numbers by link.

The assessment changes if an amended 8-K or follow-up notice separates the two populations, de-duplicates the category counts, or names the intrusion route and the way Tribeau administrator credentials were obtained. Confirmed extortion, fraudulent use of the treatment or reservation details, or evidence that other clinics on Tribeau were hit would move this from a disclosure story to an active campaign.
