Salesforce has stated that no platform vulnerability has been identified amid an AU Small Finance Bank data leak investigation. Hackers claim access to customer data including Aadhaar identifiers, and the bank is investigating the claims. Salesforce is cooperating with the investigation but maintains its platform security is intact. The incident raises questions about whether the breach occurred through a third-party integration, compromised credentials, or another vector unrelated to Salesforce's core infrastructure. The bank's customers are being notified as the investigation proceeds.

Data leaks involving financial services and identity documents such as Aadhaar numbers are high-risk events. Even if Salesforce's platform is not at fault, the incident highlights the complexity of supply-chain and integration security in modern banking. Attackers may target smaller financial institutions through third-party vendors, integration partners, or compromised employee credentials rather than direct platform exploitation. Banks should audit all third-party integrations, enforce strong authentication for service accounts, and monitor for unusual data-access patterns. The claim of access to identity data also increases regulatory scrutiny and potential penalties.

What to watch: whether the investigation identifies the actual breach vector, whether Salesforce issues a joint statement with the bank, and whether Indian regulators impose new requirements for financial data protection. Organizations using Salesforce integrations should review their own access logs for similar indicators.
