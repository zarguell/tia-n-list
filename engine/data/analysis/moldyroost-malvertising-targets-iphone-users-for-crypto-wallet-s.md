MoldyRoost is a malvertising campaign that uses fake advertisements to push iOS exploit kits at iPhone users and steal cryptocurrency wallet recovery phrases and passwords. Security firm Confiant linked more than 480 advertising domains to the operation. Security Online describes the kits as Coruna and DarkSword. GBHackers reports that the operators place malicious Instagram advertisements, abandon analytics tags and compromise websites to reach victims during ordinary browsing, then deliver spyware through the advertising chain.

iVerify's 9 November report calls these half-click attacks: the exploit can trigger without a tap, so a victim only has to encounter an infected page for a vulnerable device to be at risk. That fits the distribution model described in the later coverage, where the scale comes from ad infrastructure rather than from convincing a user to click or install anything. No CVEs are named in the reporting collected here.

The main things to watch are new ad domains and exploit kit versions, further reports of wallet seed phrase theft from iPhone users, and whether the Instagram and abandoned-tag delivery routes persist after the 480 domains are identified.
