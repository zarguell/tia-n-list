UAT-11985 runs an AI-assisted spear-phishing campaign against Taiwanese research organizations, using institutional event invitations with altered QR codes and adversary-in-the-middle phishing to steal Google credentials and bypass MFA. The operation repurposes real public event details to make fraudulent invitations credible, then intercepts authentication in real time. No CVEs are involved; the threat is purely social-engineering and session-theft infrastructure. A related post on GBHackers documents the same activity with additional technical detail on the phishing kit.

This matters because it couples AI-generated lure content with live AitM interception, making it harder to detect than static credential-harvesting pages. Organizations that rely on Google authentication and event-based collaboration should watch for unexpected event invitations that redirect through QR-based login flows. The campaign targets a specific vertical (research), which implies focused reconnaissance rather than broad spray.

What to watch: additional reporting on UAT-11985 infrastructure, any Google or Taiwanese CERT advisories, and whether the phishing framework is reused against other sectors. The GBHackers and malware.news sources cover the event details; verify any new IOCs against primary vendor or government feeds before acting.
