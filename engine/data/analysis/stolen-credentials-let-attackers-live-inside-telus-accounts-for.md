TELUS disclosed that attackers gained sustained access to customer accounts using stolen credentials and used that access to impersonate TELUS in scams targeting other customers. The attackers maintained persistent access for months, suggesting the credentials were harvested through a prior breach or credential-stuffing campaign and used repeatedly without triggering account-lockout controls. TELUS detected nine distinct attack patterns and identified eight indicators of compromise associated with the campaign.

The account takeover model here is notable because the attackers did not exploit a software vulnerability. They used legitimate credentials to log in as real customers, then leveraged the trust associated with TELUS-branded communications to defraud additional victims. This social-engineering layer multiplies the impact beyond the initial credential theft, turning each compromised account into a launchpad for further scams.

Organizations should review their detection rules for long-lived sessions from unusual locations, especially when the compromised accounts are used to contact other customers. TELUS's disclosure is a reminder that credential reuse and weak post-authentication monitoring remain among the most common and least defended attack vectors in telecommunications.
