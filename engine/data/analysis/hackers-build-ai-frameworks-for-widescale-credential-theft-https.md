Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) documented threat actors moving beyond simple AI-powered coding assistants to autonomous multi-agent frameworks that coordinate entire attack lifecycles. Drawing on Mandiant incident response telemetry and live platform defenses, GTIG observed financially motivated attackers deploying these frameworks with minimal human oversight.

In one incident, a threat actor compromised an organization's cloud infrastructure and, in under six hours, used an AI coding chatbot plus markdown agent instructions to plan, build, and deploy a mass credential-harvesting campaign. The AI agents managed the vulnerability-scanning pipeline, harvested thousands of third-party credentials, troubleshot failures in real time, rotated IP addresses, and routed attack traffic through legitimate services to blend in with normal traffic.

This represents a shift from single-shot AI abuse (code generation, phishing drafts) to end-to-end autonomous attack chains. The speed and low barrier to deployment make these frameworks particularly dangerous: a single operator can orchestrate campaigns that previously required multiple specialists. Defenders should expect this pattern to accelerate as open-source multi-agent tooling matures.
