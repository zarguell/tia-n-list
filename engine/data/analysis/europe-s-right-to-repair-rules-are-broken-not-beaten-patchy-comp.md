This Register column argues that Europe's right-to-repair regulations, while patchy in compliance, represent a policy framework that should be strengthened through enforcement rather than abandoned. The piece examines manufacturer compliance failures and the regulatory response. It is a technology policy article, not a cybersecurity event.

From a security standpoint, right-to-repair intersects with cybersecurity when device firmware updates, parts pairing restrictions, and vendor lock-in affect the ability to maintain and secure hardware. However, this article focuses on general compliance issues rather than specific security implications.

No actionable security intelligence in this story. It is a policy commentary on consumer electronics regulation.
