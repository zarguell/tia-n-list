Keio Corporation, a major Tokyo commuter rail operator, confirmed a ransomware attack on a group server on September 26. The incident disrupted retail payments and hotel services linked to the rail network. The attack targets critical transport infrastructure with direct impact on commuter-facing operations.

This matters because rail operators are part of national transport infrastructure; ransomware disrupting payments shows the attack reached service-facing systems, not just back-office IT. Tokyo's commuter network carries millions daily, so any service disruption has broad public impact. The disclosure comes from the company itself, not an external researcher, giving it direct credibility.

Watch for updates on recovery timelines, whether Keio names the ransomware group responsible, and if other Tokyo rail operators or transport entities report similar incidents. No CVE is assigned and the event does not claim data theft, so the scope remains operational disruption for now.
