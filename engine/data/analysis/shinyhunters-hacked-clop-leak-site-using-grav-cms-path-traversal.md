The ShinyHunters extortion group breached the Clop ransomware gang's Tor leak site by exploiting an unpatched Grav CMS path traversal flaw, defacing the site and stealing source code, plugins, server logs, and private Tor onion keys. Clop moved to a new onion address and denied any relationship with ShinyHunters. A separate development is the arrest of Edward Dubrovsky, a Canadian ransomware-response specialist, by the FBI in Philadelphia; authorities suspect he participated in ShinyHunters attacks including a recent intrusion into FBI systems targeting agent and applicant data. The FBI director confirmed multiple recent arrests tied to the network.

This story matters because it shows a rare inter-gang conflict: one extortion group (ShinyHunters) directly compromising a rival's infrastructure rather than targeting enterprises. The exposure of Clop's private Tor keys and source material weakens that operation's operational security. The arrest of Dubrovsky, reported by The New York Times and Brian Krebs and confirmed by the RCMP, links the group to the FBI breach and indicates active law enforcement dismantling efforts rather than just online posturing.

Watch whether Clop's new onion domain sustains operations or if further ShinyHunters leaks expose victim data. Confirm whether Dubrovsky's charges specify technical roles versus coordination, and whether additional co-conspirators are named. Monitor CISA or vendor advisories for any Grav CMS patch guidance tied to the path traversal vulnerability used in the breach.
