AOMEI Backupper, a Windows backup and disk cloning tool used by more than 186 million people, was repeatedly compromised between April and July 2026 in a short-burst supply chain campaign attributed to threat cluster UNC7017. Attackers briefly tampered with configuration files on AOMEI's update servers, down_html.ini and Notifycfg.ini, for five to ten minutes at a time, redirecting clients to a malicious upgrade-now.zip carrying the CrossRoad downloader and ShinyRoad backdoor before reverting to authentic content. Researchers confirmed three tampering windows on May 28, July 16, and July 20, and the campaign was opportunistic, hitting any system that fetched updates during those windows.

The client does not cryptographically verify the configuration files, and the checksum meant to validate the archive sits in the same unauthenticated file that names it, so tampering went undetected by users. ManageEngine's METAL lab first detected the activity on May 28 when AOMEI Backupper suddenly installed the legitimate Radmin remote administration tool on computers at two unrelated companies in different countries, and notified AOMEI in early June. Higher education, IT services, and telecommunications entities in eleven countries were confirmed compromised, and the same infrastructure also serves AnyViewer customers.

What to watch: whether AOMEI confirms the scope and ships a verified update mechanism, any further tampering windows beyond the three confirmed, and whether the AnyViewer channel was similarly abused. Users who updated during the affected period should inspect for CrossRoad and ShinyRoad artifacts and rotate credentials.
