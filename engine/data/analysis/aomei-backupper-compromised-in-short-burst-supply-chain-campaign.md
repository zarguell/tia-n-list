AOMEI Backupper, Windows backup, sync, and disk-cloning software that AOMEI says is used by more than 186 million people worldwide, was compromised in an ephemeral supply chain campaign between April and July 2026 attributed to threat cluster UNC7017. There was no vulnerability in the software and no user action beyond having it installed and running: the attacker modified two unauthenticated remote configuration files, down_html.ini and Notifycfg.ini, on AOMEI's origin servers during windows of only five to ten minutes, redirecting clients to a malicious upgrade-now.zip that carried the CROSSROAD downloader and the SHINYROAD backdoor. Three windows are confirmed, May 28 05:40 to 05:50 UTC, July 16 08:33 to 08:39 UTC, and July 20 10:35 to 10:40 UTC, and the same flow also affected AnyViewer customers. On July 20 the attacker added a second vector, replacing a legitimate 400x380 promotional banner with an invisible one-by-one pixel window at maximum priority that forced the client to request a remote file over a WebDAV UNC path and harvest NetNTLMv2 hashes from 8.210.84.56. Later stages deployed a modified Radmin Server backdoor and the Adaptix C2 framework masquerading as Nitro PDF tools.

The risk is structural rather than a single bad download, and that is why this story matters more than the day's other supply chain items. Because Notifycfg.ini was bloated with over 35,000 lines of dummy sections that overrode client version checks, any installation that polled during an active window pulled and executed the payload regardless of its installed version, so exposure is defined by polling timing rather than by a vulnerable version range that can be patched. Confirmed victims span higher education, IT services, and telecommunications across at least twelve countries, but the campaign was opportunistic and likely broader than identified. Worse, the five to ten minute tampering windows and the loader's own reversion of local configuration files mean the vendor's servers return clean files during almost any later check, so a server-side fix by AOMEI, completed late July after Mandiant's July 17 notification, does not disinfect endpoints and the initial vector is easy to dismiss as a false positive during triage.

Defenders should inventory any host where AOMEI Backupper or AnyViewer polled for updates on May 28, July 16, or July 20 and inspect the AOMEI Tmphtml and html directories for upgrade-now.zip and upgrade-now.exe, plus scheduled tasks named RadminComponentRuntime and NitroRuntime. On disk, hunt %LOCALAPPDATA%\Radmin for BlkScrDl.dll, csvc\RuntimeComponent.dll, config.zip, and metadata.dat, and %LOCALAPPDATA%\Nitro Pro for Eztwain4.dll and NitroPromotion.exe. Network controls should block outbound connections to promotion.storage-data.com, storage-data.com, aomei.storage-data.com, 8.210.84.56, 47.239.60.117, and 8.218.65.184, block external SMB on TCP 445 and WebDAV, and alert on the User-Agent strings Anyviewer-loader/1.0 and Anyviewer-ipcheck/1.0 and on requests to serv.oss-cn-hongkong.aliyuncs.com. Because no affected version list was published, verify installed binaries against an internal integrity baseline and preserve cached configuration files in %PROGRAMFILES(X86)%\AOMEI\*\Tmphtml\ for retrospective analysis rather than relying on live web checks. Full indicators of compromise and YARA rules are in Google's GTI collection.

The assessment changes if telemetry reveals additional tampering windows between April and July beyond the three confirmed, since GTIG considers that probable, or if Google and METAL publish detection rules that let organizations confirm or rule out compromise. Also watch whether UNC7017 applies the same minutes-long tampering pattern to other vendors that share AOMEI's promotional update channel design, and whether affected organizations report follow-on intrusions through the SHINYROAD or Adaptix footholds already established.
