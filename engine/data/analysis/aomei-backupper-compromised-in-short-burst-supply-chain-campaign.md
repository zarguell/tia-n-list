AOMEI Backupper was compromised in a short-burst supply chain campaign between April and July 2026. The attackers did not maintain persistent access; instead they replaced legitimate download files with malware for only five to ten minutes before reverting to authentic versions. Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant, working with ManageEngine Threat Analysis Lab, linked the campaign to the CrossRoad downloader and ShinyRoad backdoor. The tampered packages hit anyone who downloaded during the brief windows, making the campaign opportunistic rather than targeted, though compromised entities span higher education, IT services, and telecommunications in at least eleven countries including Australia, Brazil, India, and the United States. AOMEI claims its products are used by more than 186 million people worldwide, which amplifies the potential exposure.

The key takeaway is that the short duration of the tampering windows limits forensics and makes detection harder. Organizations relying on AOMEI Backupper should verify installed versions and check for indicators of CrossRoad or ShinyRoad activity. Because the attack targeted software updates rather than the build pipeline, it resembles a download server compromise rather than a classic source-code supply chain breach.

Watch for follow-up reporting that links specific victim sectors or provides IOCs tied to the observed malware families. If additional tampering windows are confirmed for 2026, treat them as part of the same campaign rather than isolated incidents.
