SecurityWeek columnist and ISF CEO Steve Durbin argues that AI governance is a leadership problem that will not resolve itself by waiting for regulation to settle. He cites Grant Thornton's 2026 survey finding that 46% of organizations say AI governance and compliance issues are the reason their AI underperforms, and notes that more than 1,100 AI bills were introduced in US state legislatures last year, 130 of which became law.

The concrete risk he highlights is legal exposure: information entered into general-purpose AI tools is not covered by legal privilege, so anything fed into an assistant for legal or sensitive guidance becomes discoverable in a dispute. His recommendation is to stop waiting for regulatory clarity and build three capabilities instead: visibility into where AI touches data, a governance framework that can adapt as rules change, and rehearsed incident response for AI-related crises. For security teams the message is operational: AI adoption is outpacing policy, and the gap is already showing up as data handling decisions made without a control framework.
