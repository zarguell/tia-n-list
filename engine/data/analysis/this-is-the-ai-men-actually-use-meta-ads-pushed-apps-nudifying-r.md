The Tech Transparency Project (TTP) identified 332 ads on Facebook and Instagram promoting AI "nudify" apps that contained child sexual abuse material (CSAM), including AI-generated content derived from real photos of minors. The ads were placed primarily through Chinese ad resellers and promoted apps that digitally alter images to produce fake explicit content of children. Among the victims depicted were a young member of a European royal family, whose photo was animated into a graphic video, and a 14-year-old Instagram influencer whose sports uniform photo was morphed into explicit content. A third victim, a preteen shown in stock photos, appeared in an ad that accumulated 330 impressions before Meta removed it.

Meta's ad review system failed to detect the vast majority of these ads despite claiming to have deployed new detection systems after an earlier batch of 53 ads was flagged in August. TTP found hundreds of new CSAM ads ran after Meta publicly announced its improved technology. Meta's removal was inconsistent: identical ads received different enforcement decisions, with one copycat ad being allowed to remain after an identical version was taken down. Meta also flagged removals without properly marking violations in its archived ad library, suggesting potential undercounting of CSAM enforcement actions.

Three Meta ad reseller partners in China—GIMC, Meetsocial, and BlueFocus—were linked to CSAM ad distribution without detection. The ads collectively reached over 29,000 people in the EU and 6,800 in the UK, with approximately 80 percent of impressions served in the US. Attorneys general in Michigan and Florida have opened investigations, Senator Mark Warner has demanded Meta respond, and Australia's eSafety regulator has requested information at a senior level. The Internet Watch Foundation warned that AI apps are making it "far too easy to create realistic criminal imagery of child sexual abuse."
