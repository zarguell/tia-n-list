CenterPoint Energy, a Houston-based utility serving roughly 7 million customers across Texas, Indiana, Minnesota, and Ohio, confirmed a data breach after a threat actor leaked what they claimed were 7.49 million customer records. The company disclosed the incident in an SEC filing but did not name the attacker or specify the number of affected customers. BleepingComputer reported that the attacker, operating under the alias "4d722e4d656f77," exfiltrated the data by iterating through millions of IDs on CenterPoint's public API, which reportedly lacked rate limiting, a web application firewall, and other protections against automated scraping.

The stolen dataset includes customer names, phone numbers, service and billing addresses, account numbers, billing amounts, and partial Social Security numbers. The attacker leaked the data after claiming CenterPoint ignored their messages. SecurityWeek independently confirmed the breach through the SEC filing. CenterPoint said it has engaged in remediation and recovery operations but has not disclosed the scope of compromise beyond confirming data was stolen.

The attack vector is notable for its simplicity. An unauthenticated API endpoint that allowed sequential ID enumeration without rate limiting is a basic access control failure. Utility companies handle sensitive customer data including partial SSNs and billing information, making them attractive targets for both financial fraud and extortion. Organizations with public APIs should audit for enumeration vulnerabilities and implement rate limiting, WAF rules, and authentication requirements on endpoints that expose customer records.
