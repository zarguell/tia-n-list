Research published by Wired reveals that at least 160 deepfake websites are targeting more than 100 politicians across 22 European countries with sexually explicit synthetic content. The sites compile and publish fabricated intimate images of public figures, combining AI-generated imagery with personal information scraped from public sources. The scale of the operation indicates organized, purpose-built infrastructure rather than individual harassment.

The targeting pattern shows both gender bias and political motivation, with female politicians disproportionately affected. The synthetic content is often combined with real personal details like office addresses and family information, increasing the harassment impact. Site operators appear to use automated tools to generate and publish content at scale, with some sites hosting hundreds of fabricated images.

This represents an evolution in political harassment and disinformation tactics. Unlike previous deepfake incidents that targeted individual celebrities, these sites represent systematic, infrastructure-backed campaigns against democratic officials. European politicians are calling for stronger legal frameworks to address synthetic media abuse, though cross-border enforcement remains challenging.
