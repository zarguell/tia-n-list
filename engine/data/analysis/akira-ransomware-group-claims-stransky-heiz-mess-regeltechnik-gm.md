Akira's leak blog keeps a high cadence, and this story tracks three of its claims. The oldest is Stransky Heiz-Mess-Regeltechnik GmbH, a German heating, measurement, and control engineering firm, now corroborated by an independent German incident listing that records a ransomware attack with data theft and a data leak. Manders Companies, a family-owned contractor in the Washington metropolitan area, was claimed on September 16 with a threat to publish 70GB including employee SSNs, passports, driver's licenses, financials, client files, and NDAs. Krycler, Ervin, Taubman and Kaminsky, an accounting and consulting firm in Sherman Oaks, California, was claimed on October 1 with 88GB threatened, including passports, SSNs, birth and death certificates, insurance and credit card data, and client information.

The sourcing pattern matters when weighing these: all three claims are the group's own leak-site statements relayed through monitoring aggregators, and the aggregator posts carry vendor marketing boilerplate. The German listing is the only third-party corroboration in the captured material. Treat the claims as leads with named organizations worth checking, not as confirmed breaches, and do not repeat the threatened data volumes as fact until a victim confirms.

Akira remains one of the highest-volume ransomware brands, and the claimed loot is the identity-theft bundle: SSNs, passports, and credit card data expose employees to fraud long after any negotiation ends. Named organizations should assume employee identity protection obligations now. Watch for victim confirmation or leak publication for the three names, additional Akira claims in the same sectors, and any law-enforcement action against Akira's infrastructure following the pattern of recent takedowns against other RaaS brands.
