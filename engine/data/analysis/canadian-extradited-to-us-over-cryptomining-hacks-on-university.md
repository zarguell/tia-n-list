Two Canadian men, Ryan James Roach, 35, of Saskatchewan, and Mathiew James Stubbings, 41, of Ontario, have been indicted by a federal grand jury in Rochester, New York for a multi-year campaign that hijacked university computing resources to mine cryptocurrency. The indictment, returned in April 2022 and unsealed November 3, 2026, charges conspiracy to obtain information from and cause damage to protected computers, obtaining information from a protected computer, and intentional computer damage, each carrying up to ten years in prison and a $250,000 fine. Roach was extradited from Canada on October 28, 2026 and arraigned on November 3, while extradition proceedings against Stubbings continue before Canadian courts.

According to the Justice Department, the pair compromised networks at universities in New York, Tennessee, North Carolina, and Massachusetts between June 2016 and December 2019, targeting campuses with supercomputers. They allegedly rented virtual private servers in Europe and the United States under false identities to deploy rootkits, steal credentials and data, and transmit cryptocurrency mining software. A Saskatchewan court filing said university hardware was repurposed to mine Electroneum, and one U.S. institution suffered about $337,000 in damages from shutdowns, forensics, and rebuilding. A Saskatchewan King's Bench judge found the evidence met Canada's extradition threshold, though the Court of Appeal granted Roach interim release pending a decision by the federal justice minister.

The case shows law enforcement continuing to act on older intrusions, with this indictment covering activity from 2016 to 2019 and unsealed years later. Defenders should treat compromise of research infrastructure as a persistent threat, since stolen credentials and pivot access were reused across institutions. Watch for Stubbings' extradition decision and any further proceedings in the Western District of New York.
