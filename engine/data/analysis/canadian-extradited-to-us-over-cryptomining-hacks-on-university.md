Two Canadian men, Ryan James Roach of Saskatchewan and Mathiew James Stubbings of Ontario, face federal charges in New York over a multi-year campaign that compromised university networks across four US states to mine cryptocurrency. A grand jury indicted both on conspiracy and computer intrusion charges carrying up to 10 years in prison and $250,000 in fines per defendant. The intrusions ran from June 2016 through December 2019 and targeted campuses in New York, Massachusetts, North Carolina, and Tennessee, particularly those hosting supercomputers.

The operators built multi-country virtual private server infrastructure under fraudulent identities to manage the campaign, transmitted malware and mining software to victims, and stored stolen login credentials. Once inside, they allegedly stole data and credentials, deployed crypto-mining malware, and in some cases used compromised supercomputers for vulnerability research. Canadian court filings identify the mined cryptocurrency as Electroneum. Roach was extradited from Canada on October 28, while Stubbings remains subject to ongoing extradition proceedings.

The case illustrates the persistent risk of exposed academic supercomputing resources being repurposed for illicit mining and reconnaissance, and the cross-border enforcement effort now underway to bring both defendants to trial.
