A federal grand jury in Rochester, New York indicted Ryan James Roach, 35, of Saskatchewan, and Mathiew James Stubbings, 41, of Ontario, for a campaign that ran from June 2016 through December 2019. The pair allegedly rented virtual private servers in multiple countries under fraudulent identities, used them to deliver malware and cryptocurrency mining software to university networks, and stored stolen login credentials on those servers. They specifically targeted campuses running supercomputers, pivoted through already-compromised machines to hide their origins, mined Electroneum on hijacked hardware, and in some cases used access to victim supercomputers for vulnerability research. Roach was extradited from Canada on October 28, 2026 and arraigned on November 3; extradition proceedings against Stubbings remain underway. Each defendant faces up to ten years in prison and a $250,000 fine.

The exposure is not a product vulnerability but a class of target: academic high-performance computing clusters on research networks with broad access and often loose egress controls. One undisclosed U.S. educational institution suffered roughly $337,000 in incident costs, covering shutdowns, forensic analysis, and rebuilding compromised infrastructure. A three-and-a-half-year dwell time shows how long mining activity can persist inside a university network when nobody watches compute usage. This story differs from the other intrusion reports because the compute itself was the prize, and because it is a rare cross-border enforcement case rather than a vendor patch cycle.

Defenders cannot patch this campaign; there is no CVE and no vendor fix. Treat HPC and research networks as high-value assets: require MFA, restrict SSH to jump hosts, and monitor authentication and job scheduler logs for logins from foreign virtual private server ranges. Check /var/log/auth.log and equivalent Windows event channels, Slurm or equivalent scheduler accounting records, and netflow for long-lived connections to overseas VPS infrastructure. Hunts should target Electroneum miner binaries, credential-dumping tooling, and reused administrative credentials across research and general-purpose networks.

Watch for the conclusion of Stubbings' extradition proceedings and any sentencing that reveals additional victim institutions or wider losses. Renewed Electroneum mining activity tied to the same multi-country virtual private server infrastructure would indicate the campaign outlived the defendants and would raise the assessment.
