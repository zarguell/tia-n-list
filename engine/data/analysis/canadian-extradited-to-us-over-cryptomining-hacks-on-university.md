A federal grand jury in Rochester, New York indicted two Canadian men, Ryan James Roach, 35, of Saskatchewan, and Mathiew James Stubbings, 41, of Ontario, for a multi-year campaign that hijacked university computing resources to mine cryptocurrency. Roach was extradited from Canada on October 28 and arraigned in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of New York; Stubbings remains in Canada with extradition proceedings underway. The DOJ announced the indictment on November 3, 2026.

According to the indictment, the intrusions ran from June 2016 through December 2019 against academic institutions in New York, Massachusetts, North Carolina, and Tennessee, with a focus on campuses running supercomputers. The pair allegedly rented virtual private servers in multiple countries under fraudulent identities, used previously compromised machines as pivot points, deployed rootkits and malware, stole login credentials, and transmitted mining software that commandeered processing power to mine Electroneum. Each faces up to ten years in prison and a $250,000 fine on the conspiracy and protected-computer charges; a grand jury had previously indicted them in April 2022.

The case is notable for the length of the campaign and the target profile: university supercomputers offer far more processing power than typical endpoints, which is what made them attractive for a mining operation rather than data theft. Defenders in research and education networks should watch for unauthorized mining pools, supercomputing job abuse, and credential use from unfamiliar VPS infrastructure. Defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty.
