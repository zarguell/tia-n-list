Revolut disclosed a significant data breach after employees accepted fraudulent government data requests that turned out to be from threat actors. The attacker impersonated a government agency using a legitimate email domain, bypassing authentication checks. Revolut's systems processed the requests as authentic, exposing customer PII including full names, dates of birth, addresses, phone numbers, passport copies, driver's licenses, facial verification images, and financial statements with IBAN numbers.

The breach affected an undisclosed number of Revolut's 80 million customers across 160+ countries. The company detected the activity, blocked the sending address, and notified relevant government agencies, law enforcement, and data protection regulators. Revolut characterized this as external impersonation rather than a system intrusion, and stated customer funds were not affected. However, the exposed data represents exactly what identity thieves need for account takeover and fraud.

Multiple outlets including BleepingComputer, The Register, and malware.news covered the incident, with French security community also discussing the implications. The breach highlights how social engineering targeting corporate data request processes can bypass technical controls. Financial institutions increasingly face these impersonation attacks as threat actors recognize that vendor and government impersonation channels often have weaker verification than direct system access.
