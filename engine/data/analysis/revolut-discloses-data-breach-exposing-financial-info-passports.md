Revolut disclosed a data breach after fulfilling what appeared to be a government data request but was actually sent from an unauthorized email account spoofing a legitimate government domain. Because the email carried valid domain authentication credentials, Revolut treated it as genuine and handed over customer data. The exposed information includes full names, dates of birth, occupation, postal addresses, email addresses, phone numbers, copies of identity documents (passports and driver's licenses), facial verification selfies from KYC onboarding, and account statements with IBAN numbers.

The breach affects an undisclosed number of Revolut's 80 million-plus customers across 160 countries. The combination of passport scans, selfies, and financial records creates a high-risk package for identity theft and targeted fraud. Reports indicate the stolen data has been distributed on Telegram, where broadcast channels make it widely accessible rather than confined to a single buyer. Security researchers have flagged the elevated risk of real-world "wrench attacks" against high-net-worth users whose crypto holdings and home addresses may now be exposed.

The core failure here is process, not code. Revolut's intake pipeline for government requests did not adequately verify the sender's legitimacy beyond email authentication headers, which can be spoofed with sufficient knowledge of a target agency's mail infrastructure. Organizations that handle bulk PII and fulfill legal or regulatory data requests should treat this as a case study in supply-chain-style social engineering of trust workflows.
