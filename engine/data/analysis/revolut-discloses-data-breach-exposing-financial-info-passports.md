Revolut disclosed a data breach in which a threat actor obtained customer personally identifiable information by impersonating a government agency. The attacker sent an email from an unauthorized account that spoofed a legitimate government domain, and because the message carried valid domain authentication credentials (likely SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass), Revolut fulfilled the request believing it was authentic.

The data shared with the attacker includes full names, dates of birth, occupation, postal addresses, email addresses, phone numbers, copies of identity documents (passports and/or driver's licenses), facial verification images from KYC onboarding, and account statements with IBAN numbers and withdrawal details. Revolut serves over 80 million customers across 160+ countries, including 800,000 business accounts. The company did not disclose how many customers were affected.

This is a social engineering compromise of a data disclosure process, not a systems breach. The attack vector is straightforward: domain-spoofed email with passing authentication results. Financial institutions that fulfill government data requests via email should review whether their verification procedures include out-of-band confirmation (e.g., callback to a known government contact number) rather than relying solely on email domain checks. The exposed KYC documents and facial biometrics are particularly high-value for identity fraud and cannot be rotated like passwords.

Watch for follow-up credential stuffing or phishing campaigns targeting Revolut users using the leaked PII, and for Revolut's regulatory notifications under GDPR given its European customer base.
