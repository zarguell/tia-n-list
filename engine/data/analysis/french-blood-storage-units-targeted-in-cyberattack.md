Attackers breached Coolworld, the maintenance provider for refrigeration at France's Etablissement francais du sang (EFS), and reprogrammed cold-storage units from their normal minus 30 degrees Celsius to 110 degrees Celsius overnight November 1-2, 2026. The affected units held blood samples and frozen plasma at three facilities, and the temperature swing was caught by an on-call technician before the stock was destroyed. France's Health Ministry stated there was zero operational impact on the blood supply. The attack was first reported by Le Canard enchaine and OMM, with Anadolu, GBHackers, and others relaying it.

The striking element is the target choice rather than the technique. Blood storage is a classic cyber-physical target: the equipment is internet-reachable, the consequence of a missed temperature rise is destroyed medical stock, and the blast radius is measured in patients who cannot be transfused. The attacker never had to touch EFS's own network; the maintenance supplier was the weaker link, which is the same pattern behind the supermarket refrigeration attacks across Europe this autumn. That the sabotage was detected by a human watching a thermometer, not by an automated alarm, is the uncomfortable detail.

Defenders in healthcare supply chains should verify that cold-storage controllers are segmented from general IT, that temperature alarms fire out-of-band and cannot be silenced from the same console that sets the setpoint, and that maintenance vendors carry the same access scrutiny as clinical systems. Watch for a follow-up from EFS or French authorities naming the intrusion vector and whether any plasma units were discarded despite the Ministry's zero-impact statement, since the two claims sit uneasily together.
