Attackers breached Coolworld, a refrigeration maintenance provider for France's Etablissement francais du sang (EFS), overnight November 1-2, 2026, and reprogrammed cold-storage units at three facilities from minus 30 degrees Celsius to 110 degrees Celsius, threatening stored blood samples and frozen plasma. The incident was detected by an on-duty technician, and France's Health Ministry reported zero operational impact. EFS was not directly targeted; the attacker exploited a vulnerability in a third-party component of Coolworld's remote monitoring software, outside Coolworld's direct control.

The attack is part of a wider European wave against refrigeration control systems that also hit supermarket cold chains. Coolworld confirmed only a limited number of units were affected and said it responded promptly. Reports from Le Canard enchaine, OMM, and multiple European outlets converged on the same timeline and temperature manipulation, with the Guardian noting remote access to control systems.

Watch for Coolworld's full incident report and whether the third-party component flaw is named, plus any follow-on attempts against other European cold-chain operators using the same remote monitoring software. Stored blood product integrity checks and supplier access reviews are the immediate priorities.
