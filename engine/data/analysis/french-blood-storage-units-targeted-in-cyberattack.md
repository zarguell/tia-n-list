Overnight November 1-2, 2026, attackers breached Coolworld, a maintenance provider for France's Etablissement francais du sang (EFS), and reprogrammed cold-storage units at three facilities from roughly -30 degrees Celsius to 110 degrees Celsius, threatening blood samples and frozen plasma. The incident was detected by an on-duty technician, and France's Ministry of Health stated there was zero operational impact. Reporting by Le Canard enchaine and OMM, relayed through DataBreaches.Net, described the targeted equipment as Coolworld-maintained refrigeration used by the national blood service.

The significance is the targeting of medical cold-chain infrastructure through a service provider rather than the blood service itself. Raising storage temperatures to 110 degrees Celsius would destroy affected plasma and blood products, and the compromise of a maintenance contractor's remote access raises questions about how internet-connected medical equipment is segmented and monitored. Coverage also noted the incident followed a pattern of cyberattacks on healthcare and European-scale targeting of EFS refrigeration systems.

What to watch: whether any plasma was actually lost despite the ministry's zero-impact statement, the scope of Coolworld's access across other EFS sites and customers, and whether investigators identify the intruders or a known ransomware or extortion group. Blood services and their cold-chain vendors should review remote-access controls and temperature-alert monitoring.
