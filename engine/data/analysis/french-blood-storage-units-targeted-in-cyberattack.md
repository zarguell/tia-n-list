Attackers breached the refrigeration supplier Coolworld and reprogrammed cold-storage units at three facilities of France's national blood service, the Etablissement francais du sang (EFS), raising temperature and defrost thresholds from roughly minus 30 degrees Celsius to 110 degrees Celsius overnight November 1-2, 2026. The incident was detected by an on-duty technician, and the Ministry of Health stated there was no operational impact. Coolworld confirmed a cybersecurity incident involving remote monitoring software and said the exploited vulnerability sat in a third-party component outside its direct control, affecting only a limited number of units.

The concrete artifact is the temperature manipulation itself: a shift from minus 30 to 110 degrees Celsius on blood and frozen plasma storage, which could have destroyed inventory had the technician not caught it. The attack reached EFS indirectly through its supplier rather than the blood service's own systems, which is the recurring pattern in this cluster of refrigeration incidents.

Defenders should watch for remote monitoring and control interfaces on cold-chain equipment, require supplier-side segmentation and patching of third-party components, and treat refrigeration control systems as critical infrastructure rather than facilities IT. The EFS case shows that even a supplier-side compromise can threaten medical inventory, and that detection depended on a human noticing anomalous temperatures.
