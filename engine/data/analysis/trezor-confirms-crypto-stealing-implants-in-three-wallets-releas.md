Trezor has confirmed that three Safe 5 hardware wallets sold by Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis between August 20 and September 23, 2026 contained malicious hardware implants. The implants capture wallet recovery phrases and exfiltrate them over cellular networks. The investigation began in October when a customer found an unauthorized circuit board inside a device bought from the Indonesian reseller; Trezor has since examined that unit in its lab and identified two more compromised wallets from owner-submitted photographs. So far 48 CryptoBilis customers have contacted the company, with devices under examination in the lab or via photographs, and inspections of devices from other regional resellers found no tampering. No affected customer has reported losing funds, and the confirmed cases are limited to Safe 5 units sold through CryptoBilis.

The incident is a supply-chain attack on the distribution channel rather than on Trezor's manufacturing: the implant firmware was analyzed and confirmed capable of capturing sensitive information displayed on the device. Owners of Safe 5 units purchased through CryptoBilis in that window should inspect the device internals or contact Trezor before use, and anyone who has already entered a recovery phrase on an affected unit should move funds to a new wallet. Watch for additional compromised units as the remaining customer devices are examined, and for any reports of fund theft that would change the impact assessment.
