The US State Department's Rewards for Justice program is offering up to $10 million for information on Zhang Yu, a Chinese national charged for his role in the theft of American COVID-19 research and the Hafnium campaign against Microsoft Exchange servers. Per the reward announcement, Zhang is a director at Shanghai Firetech Information Science and Technology and allegedly worked on behalf of the Shanghai State Security Bureau, part of China's Ministry of State Security.

The charges tie back to a nine-count indictment unsealed in July 2025, days after Italian police arrested his codefendant Xu Zewei in Milan at Washington's request. Xu was extradited to the US in April 2026 and has appeared in federal court in Houston; Zhang remains at large. Prosecutors allege that starting in early 2020 the two gained unauthorized access to COVID-19 research at US universities and to leading immunologists and virologists, then moved to exploiting Microsoft Exchange vulnerabilities in 2021 as part of Hafnium, the campaign Microsoft disclosed in March 2021 and now tracks as Silk Typhoon. Thousands of computers worldwide were compromised, with victims including a US university and a US law firm.

The reward signals continued pressure on the MSS contractor ecosystem rather than a new intrusion. Naming a company director at Shanghai Firetech puts a commercial outfit on the map as state-adjacent infrastructure, the same contractor model seen across Chinese espionage reporting. Watch for further designations against Shanghai Firetech or affiliated firms, additional indictments from the 2025 case, and whether Chinese retaliation follows the pattern seen after prior RFJ rewards for PLA and MSS personnel.
