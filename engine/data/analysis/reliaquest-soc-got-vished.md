ReliaQuest, a major SOC-as-a-service and managed detection provider, was targeted in a social engineering attack using voice phishing (vishing). The incident was reported on Reddit's r/cybersecurity and picked up by SecurityWeek, which confirmed the company acknowledged the attack but characterized the impact as limited.

The attack is notable because ReliaQuest operates security operations centers for enterprise clients, making it a high-value target. A successful compromise of a managed security provider could cascade into access to downstream customer environments. The vishing technique, where attackers use phone calls to manipulate employees into granting access or revealing credentials, has seen increased adoption against security teams who are difficult to phish via email.

The incident underscores that even organizations whose core business is security operations are vulnerable to social engineering. Vishing bypasses technical controls like email filtering and MFA by targeting human judgment directly. The security community has noted that SOC analysts and security engineers often receive elevated access privileges that make them attractive targets for voice-based attacks.
