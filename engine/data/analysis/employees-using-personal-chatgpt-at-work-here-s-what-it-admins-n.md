A NordLayer-authored piece argues that personal ChatGPT accounts used on managed devices let staff submit prompts containing customer data or sensitive documents while bypassing corporate logging and controls. It walks two scenarios: one where security tools flag personal account use but do not inspect prompt contents, and one where browser-level tooling classifies conversations as personal or corporate and blocks on data patterns such as customer account numbers.

The practical takeaway is identity and data-in-transit governance rather than a CVE. Administrators should distinguish the signed-in user's corporate identity from a personal ChatGPT login, monitor which accounts carry conversation data, and put controls on the browser path where prompts leave the endpoint. The piece references an acting CISA director's use of ChatGPT with sensitive material in 2025 as a cautionary example.

Because the article is vendor marketing for NordLayer AI Scout, treat the product framing skeptically and validate controls independently. The underlying risk, employees pasting internal data into personal AI accounts, is real and worth addressing through existing DLP and SaaS governance rather than a single vendor tool.
