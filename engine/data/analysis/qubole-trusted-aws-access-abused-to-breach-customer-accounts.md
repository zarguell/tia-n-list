Between October 14 and October 26, 2026 a threat actor used compromised IAM credentials for the AWS account of big data platform Qubole to assume cross-account roles in customer environments and pull data out of them. Wiz investigated after Sentra reported suspicious activity in its customers' accounts, with Mandiant also involved, and concluded the attacker had compromised two Qubole IAM users. More than a dozen organizations were affected, with Sentra describing the broader impact as dozens of Qubole customers.

The access path is the familiar SaaS trust pattern. Customers create an IAM role that Qubole's AWS account can assume, normally gated by an External ID, and vendors call the STS AssumeRole API for temporary credentials. The failure here is stale trust, not a bypass: Qubole roles stayed in place long after organizations stopped using Qubole, and those roles retained read access to nearly all the data the actor targeted. Dormant roles were then activated for enumeration, secret and configuration collection, and data access. Infrastructure details reported include a rented OVH server and Mullvad VPN, with no observed activity originating from Qubole's own network, and role session names following a consistent quiam- prefix followed by an action descriptor. Stolen S3 data is the reported exfiltration target.

That session-name pattern is the most actionable artifact in the incident. It gives defenders a concrete CloudTrail signature to hunt, and the quiam- prefix is unusual enough to survive in logs long after the October 26 cutoff.

What to watch is scope: Sentra's dozen-plus figure and its dozens-of-customers characterization leave the tail undefined, and named-victim disclosure will indicate whether this was opportunistic credential resale or targeting of a specific data set. The structural lesson is that every third-party integration role is a standing credential. Organizations should audit unused cross-account roles, enforce External ID conditions, scope trust policies to specific accounts, and alert on AssumeRole calls from vendor accounts that have had no legitimate activity for months.
