Westfield Public Schools in New Jersey kept classrooms open through a districtwide network outage in the first week of school, with communications and digital instruction disrupted across all district schools and offices. The district initially said a networking hardware failure caused the disruption. A later report recorded a ransomware group posting samples, and a security-incident listing described the event as a ransomware attack, data breach, and outage. The gap between the hardware-failure statement and the ransomware claims is the unresolved part of the Westfield record.

This story also carries a separate November report on Richmond Public Schools, where four weeks after an Oct. 21 cyberattack staff were calling parents to confirm who could collect children, students were huddling around small computers for lessons, and teachers were relying on personal devices. Richmond is a different district from Westfield, so the two reports should not be read as one incident.

Watch for a formal Westfield confirmation of the ransomware claim, a sample set that can be tied to a named group, and any notice of what data was taken. For Richmond, the recovery signal is whether normal pickup verification and classroom devices return, and whether the district publishes the scope of the Oct. 21 attack. Neither event text gives a victim count, ransom demand, or attribution.
