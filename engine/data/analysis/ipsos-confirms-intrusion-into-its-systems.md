Ipsos confirmed an intrusion into one internal information system on November 16, five days after filing a complaint with authorities on November 11. The French market research firm told SecurityWeek the incident did not affect its operations or ability to deliver for clients and that outside experts were brought in to contain and investigate it. At the time the company would not say when the breach happened, which system was hit or whether client, project or business partner data was accessed. Ipsos employs nearly 20,000 people in 90 markets, serves about 5,000 clients and holds survey and social media monitoring data, which makes any confirmed access to client material significant.

On November 25 InfonewsCyber reported that the Qilin ransomware group claims to have stolen sensitive data from Ipsos. That claim is the first named actor in the coverage of this incident, and none of the available reports independently confirms the theft, publishes a sample of the material or states a ransom demand.

What to watch: whether Ipsos confirms data access and starts direct notifications to clients, projects or business partners, whether Qilin posts proof on its leak site, and whether the intrusion is linked to other recent attacks on market research and survey data. Until then the confirmed facts are the single affected internal system, the November 11 complaint and the November 16 public disclosure.
