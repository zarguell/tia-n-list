Ipsos, the French market research firm, has confirmed an intrusion into one of its internal information systems. The company made the incident public on Monday, November 16, five days after filing a complaint with authorities. Securityweek reports that Ipsos said the incident had no impact on its operations or ability to deliver for clients, and that outside security experts were brought in to contain and investigate it.

The scope is unresolved. Ipsos has not said whether data was accessed or stolen, which system was affected, when the breach was detected, or how the attackers got in. Its statement says clients, projects or business partners would be contacted directly if the investigation establishes their information was affected. A later report framed the intrusion as targeting internal systems and client data, but that goes beyond what Ipsos has confirmed.

Ipsos has nearly 20,000 employees in 90 markets and works with 5,000 clients, so a confirmed data-access finding could affect a wide range of organizations. What to watch is the completion of the investigation and any direct notifications to clients or partners. Until then, the confirmed facts are the complaint, the internal-system breach, the five-day gap before public disclosure, and the absence of operational disruption.
