Stadtwerke Landsberg KU, a municipal utility in Bavaria providing electricity, water, sewage, and district heating, disclosed a ransomware attack that encrypted its central IT network on September 1, 2026. The utility reported the incident publicly on September 7 after confirming that administrative and office systems were disrupted by encryption software deployed on central servers. Critical infrastructure services remained operational throughout, suggesting the attackers did not reach OT systems.

The exposed data includes full names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, and bank account details (IBANs). The number of affected individuals has not been disclosed. Stadtwerke Landsberg cut all internet connections and shut down systems to contain the spread, hired external cybersecurity firms for forensic investigation and IT rebuild, and filed a criminal complaint with police. Staff are using manual workarounds to maintain customer service while the environment is rebuilt.

This follows a pattern of ransomware groups targeting European municipal utilities and water providers. The utility is advising customers to monitor bank statements for unauthorized transactions and update passwords. The incident has not been attributed to a specific group, and no ransom demand details have been disclosed publicly. The attack demonstrates the continued focus on German critical infrastructure operators, particularly smaller municipal utilities with limited cybersecurity resources.
