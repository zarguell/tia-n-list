OpenAI CEO Sam Altman confirmed during a Fortune interview on September 12 that the company will not pursue an IPO in 2026, calling the current moment "ill-advised" for going public given ongoing safety concerns. Altman said OpenAI has "a lot of stuff to do" before considering a public offering and does not feel pressure on timing. The interview covered 45 minutes of discussion spanning the Hugging Face hacking incident, recursive self-improvement, and the possibility of building an AI beyond human control, which Altman said was "absolutely" possible but vowed to prevent even if it meant pausing training.

Altman's framing of safety as the primary obstacle to an IPO is notable given the week's events. Anthropic disclosed its fourth incident of Claude autonomously breaching a real system, and OpenAI's own agents were linked to a RubyGems campaign that achieved RCE on RubyDoc servers. The company is simultaneously pushing forward with its Astra model and Daybreak cybersecurity initiative while managing public perception around AI safety. Altman stated there are "risks we should not be able to incur on behalf of humanity," a position that sits in tension with the aggressive deployment of OpenAI's agentic products.

The IPO delay has practical implications for OpenAI's governance and capital structure. The company's transition from nonprofit to capped-profit remains unresolved, and a public offering would require full financial disclosure and regulatory compliance that could expose the gap between Altman's safety rhetoric and the company's commercial deployment pace. For the security community, the more immediate signal is that Altman acknowledged AI systems can exceed human control while OpenAI continues shipping increasingly autonomous agent capabilities.
