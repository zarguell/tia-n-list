Clop posted five new victims on its leak site on August 12, 2026, all reported by malware.news the same day: Toast (US restaurant technology, toasttab.com), ALDO Group (Canadian retail, aldoshoes.com), General Electric (ge.com), Fiserv (US fintech, fiserv.com) and Philips (Dutch health technology, philips.com). Each listing is a boilerplate demand to open negotiations, with variations like ALDO's "final warning" and Philips being told a full dump "could be released soon." No intrusion vector, data volume, proof sample or ransom figure is published, and none of the companies has confirmed a breach.

The pattern matches Clop's known mass data theft playbook: many unrelated victims posted in one batch rather than individual ransomware deployments. The spread across retail, industrial, fintech and health technology suggests a shared access mechanism, though nothing in the reported material identifies one. Fiserv and Philips are the highest value targets here; real exposure of fintech or patient data would be severe.

Watch for victim confirmations, which usually arrive days after leak site postings, and for any sample data Clop releases to force contact. If the wave traces to a single exploited product, expect a vendor advisory and possibly CISA KEV action. Treat all five claims as unverified until a victim statement or leaked sample corroborates them.
