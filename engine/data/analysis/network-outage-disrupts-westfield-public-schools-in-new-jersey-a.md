Westfield Public Schools in New Jersey experienced a districtwide network outage during the first week of school that disrupted communications and digital instruction across all schools and offices. The district initially attributed the outage to "networking hardware failure," but a ransomware group subsequently posted data samples claiming responsibility for the incident. The outage persisted through multiple school days, with the district keeping classrooms open while network services remained down.

The incident highlights the vulnerability of school districts at the start of academic years, when IT staff are focused on onboarding and network systems are under peak load. The initial misattribution to hardware failure is a common pattern in school district breaches, where incident identification and response capabilities are constrained by resource limitations.

This case illustrates the operational impact of ransomware beyond data theft: a districtwide outage during the first week of school affects thousands of students, teachers, and parents. Districts should ensure they have incident response plans tested before the school year begins, including offline communication channels and alternative instructional methods that do not depend on network connectivity. The posting of data samples suggests exfiltration has occurred, meaning the district faces both operational recovery and potential data breach notification obligations.
