CERT-AGID reported on September 24, 2026 that it identified and disrupted a MintsLoader campaign the same day, in which compromised PEC mailboxes, Italy's legally binding certified email system, were used to send malicious messages to other certified email addresses. The lure is a fake payment demand, a solicitation, which fits the mailbox: recipients of PEC traffic expect formal payment requests and deadlines, so an attachment or link demanding settlement arrives inside a channel most users treat as trusted infrastructure. CERT-AGID's operational note is the substance of the advisory: a PEC header certifies transmission and delivery of a message, not that the sender's mailbox is uncompromised and not that the attachment is safe.

The mechanics matter because PEC is used for invoices, contracts, and official communications between Italian businesses and public administration, and trust in the channel is the product. A loader family like MintsLoader arriving through compromised PEC boxes means sender-side trust signals, a real counterpart's address and a plausible payment thread, are being weaponized, and the victims are accountants, procurement staff, and back-office workers whose entire job is processing such mail. CERT-AGID does not name the final payload in the advisory summary, so the downstream behavior beyond loader delivery is unconfirmed.

Defensive guidance follows from the advisory's own caveat. Treat PEC attachments with the same screening as ordinary email: verify payment requests out of band with the sender before opening anything, and flag PEC threads that pressure immediate settlement. For defenders, a compromised PEC mailbox in the supply chain is an account-takeover incident, so compromised senders should be reported and credentials rotated, and CERT-AGID's IOC feed is the place to pick up the campaign's indicators.

Worth watching is whether the campaign recurs, since CERT-AGID's wording, identified and countered today, describes a live operation rather than a historical case. MintsLoader has historically served second-stage payloads including malware families deployed against energy and government targets, so confirmation of what this campaign delivered would sharpen the risk picture for Italian PEC users.
