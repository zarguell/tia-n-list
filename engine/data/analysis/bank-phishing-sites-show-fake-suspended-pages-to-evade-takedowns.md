Kela reports a phishing campaign active since May that hides bank login pages behind fake suspended-account notices, tracked as the Suspended infrastructure. More than 300 fake domains were involved, including 248 domains impersonating 189 US financial institutions. The first domains were registered in late 2025, but most appeared between July and September 2026, and 49 more went live in the week to October 25. As of October 25, 183 targeted organizations still had live look-alikes, mainly mid-sized regional banks.

The kit remembers each visitor's IP address, so a first visit shows a bank login page while a return visit shows what looks like a closed hosting account, which helps the campaign evade takedowns and analysis. Domain names use keywords such as secure, disable, protect, cancel, authorize, terminate, credentials, and commercial followed by a hyphen and the bank's name, with a smaller group using help-desk, live-support, treasury-portal, and primary-device names. Look-alikes cited by Kela include JPMorgan, Citi, BMO, and Santander. Users should treat unexpected account-suspension messages as suspicious even on repeat visits, and banks should monitor for newly registered look-alike domains.
