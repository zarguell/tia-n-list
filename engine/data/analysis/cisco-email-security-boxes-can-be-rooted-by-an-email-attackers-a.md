A critical flaw in Cisco Secure Email Gateway allows a remote attacker to gain root-level access on the appliance simply by sending a crafted email. Cisco confirmed active exploitation and warned that attackers who have already exploited the flaw may have retained persistent access even after patching. The vulnerability enables full device compromise through the email parsing pipeline, meaning no prior authentication or user interaction is required beyond delivering the malicious message.

Cisco's advisory urges immediate patching and warns that a successful exploit grants the attacker the same privileges as the device's root account, including the ability to read all email traffic, modify configurations, and pivot to internal networks. Organizations running Cisco email security appliances should treat this as emergency-level: scan logs for anomalous mailbox processing activity, verify patch status, and consider rotating any credentials or certificates stored on affected appliances. The Register reported that exploitation was already underway before the advisory was published.
