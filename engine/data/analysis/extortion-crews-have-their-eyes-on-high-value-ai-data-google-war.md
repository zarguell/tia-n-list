Google's Threat Intelligence Group warned that extortion crews are increasingly targeting organizations holding high-value AI intellectual property. According to Google's research, attackers recognize that companies are unwilling to have their proprietary AI models, training data, or research exposed publicly, making them more likely to pay extortion demands. The dynamic mirrors the shift ransomware groups made from encrypting data to exfiltrating it and threatening publication.

The core problem is that AI models and training datasets represent enormous R&D investments that cannot be easily rebuilt. Unlike traditional ransomware where backups can restore operations, a leaked proprietary model or dataset can permanently destroy competitive advantage. Companies that have spent millions on custom model training or curated datasets face a calculated decision: pay the extortionists or accept that their intellectual property will be freely available to competitors.

This trend is likely to accelerate as AI becomes more central to business strategy. Organizations developing proprietary AI should treat their model weights, training pipelines, and datasets with the same security posture as trade secrets. This means implementing data loss prevention controls, monitoring for unauthorized access to model repositories, and ensuring that extortion scenarios are included in incident response planning. The shift from operational disruption to intellectual property theft represents a fundamental change in how AI-native organizations must approach cybersecurity.
