BAE Systems restored the federal judiciary's Bankruptcy Noticing Center (BNC) on October 26, 2026 after a cybersecurity incident knocked it offline for more than two weeks, and says no data was extracted: "No other systems were affected. No data was extracted. There was no breach of any PII." The BNC, which BAE operates under contract with the judiciary, is the channel through which all federal bankruptcy courts in the US send their notices; notices stopped flowing around October 9 and the center cleared delayed notices oldest-first on return. In response to the outage, the Judicial Conference declared a "Bankruptcy Rules emergency" on October 22, letting bankruptcy judges push back deadlines and pause time periods. BAE's statement still omits what caused the incident and whether it was a cyberattack, and neither BAE nor the judiciary addressed data compromise when the incident first came to light.

The incident exposes the federal judiciary's dependence on a single $106 million contractor for a core notice-delivery function, with no disclosed root cause or attacker. Watch for whether BAE or the Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts identifies the intrusion vector, whether any ransomware or other malware is confirmed, and whether the judiciary changes its vendor or resilience posture for the BNC.
