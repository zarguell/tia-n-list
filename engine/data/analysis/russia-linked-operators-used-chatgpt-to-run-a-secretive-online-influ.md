OpenAI disclosed and disrupted a covert influence operation that used ChatGPT to generate content for a Russia-linked network operating across major social platforms. The operation promoted a purported Israeli think tank, spread Russia-favorable narratives, and manufactured the appearance of academic legitimacy by having ChatGPT produce articles and social media posts mimicking scholarly analysis. OpenAI traced the activity to a cluster of accounts assessed as very likely originating in Russia and banned them.

The operation is part of a broader trend of state-backed actors using commercial AI tools to scale influence operations. Using ChatGPT lets operators produce higher-volume, more linguistically natural content than older generation approaches, lowering the cost and raising the quality of information operations. OpenAI's disclosure follows similar actions against Chinese, Iranian, and Russian influence operations using its models over the past year.

This matters because it demonstrates that generative AI platforms are now a standard tool in state-sponsored influence campaigns, not just a theoretical risk. The link to a fabricated Israeli think tank suggests the operation was designed to launder pro-Russia narratives through a perceived neutral or even adversarial-to-Russia source, adding a layer of attribution misdirection. Watch for follow-on disclosures from other AI providers and for the social platforms where the content was seeded to publish their own enforcement actions.
