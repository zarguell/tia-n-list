The Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure (DIVD), a nonprofit of volunteer researchers that scans for known vulnerabilities and notifies owners, confirmed it was breached in what it calls an agentic AI-powered attack. The organization described the intrusion as "loud and very, very messy" and said the attacker ran autonomously, deciding each next step itself, mixing a MITM approach with password spraying, and over-explaining its actions, which made reverse engineering easier. DIVD blocked access, engaged a third-party incident response team, and reported to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, the National Cyber Security Centre, and police, with vulnerability details withheld while the investigation runs.

The entry point is now identified. DIVD says the agent got in through two zero-days in Zammad, the open-source ticketing system, chaining CVE-2026-102489 and CVE-2026-102490 to hijack sessions, execute code, and escalate to root on DIVD's network. That detail matters beyond one nonprofit: Zammad runs customer-facing ticket portals at many organizations, session hijacking plus root on the ticketing host exposes every conversation, attachment, and credential reset thread stored there, and a publicly disclosed AI agent that can work through that chain unattended is a reusable attack pattern, not a bespoke hack. DIVD had no evidence linking the attack to known public threat actors or to a former volunteer.

Watch for Zammad's patches and mitigation guidance for both CVEs, and for whether other Zammad operators report intrusions with the same fingerprints. DIVD promised fuller findings on Thursday 1 October, which should confirm the chain details, whether any external organizations or data subjects were affected, and what the session-hijack stage actually reached. Any indication the agent scanned beyond the ticketing system would widen the incident from a DIVD breach to a template others can copy.
