The Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure, the volunteer nonprofit that scans the internet for vulnerable systems and notifies their owners, was breached after almost seven years without incident, and its own incident team classifies the intrusion as an agentic AI powered attack. Entry came through exploitation of a technical vulnerability in an undisclosed system, with no CVE or product named, and DIVD explicitly rules out Citrix NetScaler as the flaw. After the exploit, an autonomous agent picked each next step itself: DIVD watched it operate "loud and very very messy," pollute its own adversary-in-the-middle position with password spraying, and over-explain its decisions in comments, leaving enough material to reverse engineer the whole incident. DIVD reported the case to police, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens and NCSC-NL, runs forensics with Merlon Security and NVISO, disclosed on 25 September, updated on 28 September, and promised a detailed public update on 1 October.

The important part is not that a nonprofit got breached, it is who acted inside the network. This is the first publicly documented intrusion where post-exploitation decisions were made by the agent itself rather than a human operator, which breaks the dwell-time and rhythm assumptions most detection baselines are tuned for, and DIVD's incident transcript is the first public case study of what that tradecraft actually looks like in logs: fast, sloppy, verbose. The victim's role also makes the blast radius asymmetric in a way the event list does not spell out. DIVD's notification pipeline is a trusted channel into thousands of Dutch organizations, so an actor holding it could send convincing fake vulnerability notices at scale, which is exactly why DIVD is withholding the vulnerability details to avoid putting more victims at risk and treating the case as worst-case-scenario until proven otherwise. Exposure is narrow today, one unknown flaw in one undisclosed system with no victim count, so this story matters as a leading indicator of agentic intrusion tradecraft, not as a mass event.

There is no patch to point at yet, so the concrete work is behavioral. Hunt identity telemetry for the signature DIVD actually observed: one principal interleaving password spraying across accounts with on-path session interception, a combination few human actors produce by accident. Hunt shell, audit and proxy logs for action sequences where each command is followed by a written rationale, the agent's over-explaining habit, and for chains of actions spaced far below human cadence. NetScaler coverage can be deprioritized for correlation with this incident since DIVD excluded it. If DIVD's promised scan of other victims of this vulnerability produces a notification to your organization, treat it as credible and act on it the same day.

Two developments would change the assessment. The 1 October update is the hinge: if DIVD names the product and the flaw, this turns from a single-organization incident into an exposure story for everyone running that system, and its victim-notification scan will produce the first real exposure count. The other is scenario resolution between DIVD's stated worst case, a targeted attempt on its scanning infrastructure and notification pipeline, and opportunistic smash and grab; today there is no link to any known public threat actor, and any attribution or count of additional victims would move this from curiosity to campaign.
