This story aggregates several recent security research posts covering diverse threat actor activity and vulnerability research. The most notable items include WaterPlum, a North Korean campaign that infected approximately 30,000 devices through fake interview tasks, and Rapuncel, which abuses fake GitHub repositories to distribute a signed kernel driver capable of disabling EDR solutions. Both represent supply chain and social engineering approaches that bypass traditional security controls.

The research also covers KREMLIN, a technique for forging Chromium integrity checks to steal banking sessions via browser extensions, and Chosen Brick, Iranian surveillance malware targeting dissidents that uses Telegram for command-and-control. These demonstrate continued APT focus on custom tooling and platform-specific exploitation rather than off-the-shelf malware.

From a defensive perspective, the North Korean device infection scale and the signed kernel driver EDR bypass are the highest-signal items. The signed driver technique in particular suggests compromise of a legitimate code signing certificate or a certificate authority, which would have broader implications for trust in signed binaries. The Iranian Telegram C2 infrastructure provides actionable indicators for organizations monitoring Middle Eastern threat actor activity.
