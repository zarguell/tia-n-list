In July 2026, Iran-linked hackers forced a small UK power-generation facility offline for four consecutive days. The Telegraph broke the story on August 22. The Department for Energy Security and Net Zero confirmed the target was a "small-scale energy generator" below the statutory reporting threshold for major incidents. The UK grid was not affected. Security analysts characterize the facility as "less than a rounding error compared to grid capacity."

Four days to recover from an attack on critical infrastructure is the real concern. Huntress vCISO Muhammad Yahya Patel flagged the recovery time as indicative of poor incident response preparedness among smaller operators. Dragos field CTO Phil Tonkin noted these are "repeatable attacks that could be deployed at scale." The UK has thousands of distributed energy assets contributing to grid operations; individually insignificant, collectively their resilience matters.

The attack fits a broader pattern of Iranian cyber operations since the US-Iran conflict escalated. Iranian-affiliated groups have hit targets across the US (water, critical infrastructure, military-linked), Israel, Gulf states, and now Europe (Cyprus, Romania, UK). FBI warnings about Iranian attacks on Rockwell Automation Allen-Bradley MicroLogix 1100/1400 PLCs suggest opportunistic OT targeting of exposed devices. The UK's position as a major US ally makes it an expected target; the surprise is this appears to be the only confirmed successful Iranian attack on UK infrastructure to date.

Watch for: whether the attack vector was internet-exposed PLCs or a different entry point, official NCSC guidance on OT hardening, and whether other UK energy facilities have experienced similar incidents that haven't been disclosed.
