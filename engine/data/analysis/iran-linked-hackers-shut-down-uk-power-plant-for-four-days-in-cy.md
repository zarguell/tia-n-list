A cyberattack attributed to Iran-linked hackers forced a small UK power generation facility offline for four consecutive days in July 2026, making it the first publicly known Iranian cyber operation to disrupt a UK energy site. The facility, described as a 15 MW generator, was below the statutory reporting threshold for major electricity generators. The UK government and NCSC have not formally attributed the incident to Iran or any named threat group, though multiple outlets cite Iranian affiliation. No consumer power outages resulted; the wider grid operated normally.

The attackers targeted operational technology, specifically programmable logic controllers. An FBI warning highlighted Rockwell Automation Allen-Bradley MicroLogix 1100 and 1400 series PLCs, though other brands were also flagged. Security firm Truesec assessed the attacks as mostly unsophisticated and opportunistic, targeting small plants with internet-exposed OT devices lacking proper protection. The primary aim appeared to be psychological impact rather than physical damage, consistent with Iran's broader pattern of retaliatory cyber operations against US and allied infrastructure since the 2026 US-Iran conflict.

The four-day recovery time is the most concerning detail. As Dragos field CTO Phil Tonkin noted, these are repeatable attacks that could be deployed at scale. With thousands of distributed energy assets across the UK, the collective resilience of small operators matters enormously even if individual facilities seem insignificant. The incident also expands the known geographic scope of Iranian cyber activity beyond the US, Israel, and Gulf states into Europe, raising questions about whether the UK is adequately prepared for coordinated attacks on its energy sector.
