Researchers from Gamers Nexus working with Level1Techs and independent security analysts found significant privacy and security issues across multiple LG smart TV models. The investigation uncovered extensive device and network discovery capabilities, Automated Content Recognition tracking that samples audio and visual content to fingerprint viewing habits, and several security weaknesses that could escalate the impact of a compromised television. Some of the findings relate to LG's designed product behavior, while others involve vulnerabilities the researchers say are still under responsible disclosure.

The core risk is that smart TVs function as full internet-connected computers with microphones, app stores, and access to home networks alongside phones, laptops, and smart home devices. If an attacker compromises a TV through the identified weaknesses, they could potentially leverage the microphone for surveillance, even when the device appears to be in standby mode. The ACR tracking alone raises privacy concerns similar to those that led Samsung to settle with the Texas Attorney General over viewing data collection practices.

This matters because smart TVs are ubiquitous in homes and often receive less security scrutiny than other connected devices. The research highlights that TVs deserve the same privacy and security consideration as any other networked computer. Watch for LG's response to the disclosure, patches for the vulnerabilities still being reported responsibly, and further technical details as the researchers publish their full findings.
