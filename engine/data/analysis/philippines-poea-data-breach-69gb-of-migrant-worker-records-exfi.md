An unknown operator exfiltrated 27 Microsoft SQL Server backup files totaling 68.837 GiB from systems tied to the Philippine Overseas Employment Administration and Department of Migrant Workers on September 6, 2026, completing the copy in 23 minutes and 46 seconds. Hunt.io recovered transfer logs showing the backups were staged on an open working folder at 213.163.205.35 (UpCloud, Singapore) on port 80, which served 543 requests from 76 addresses over 16 hours. The directory also contained a rebranded GodPotato privilege-escalation tool, a PowerShell stager, two payloads, and a full Impacket environment.

The National Privacy Commission is investigating the suspected theft, and Philippine authorities are probing the incident. Hunt.io notified the relevant national computer emergency response team before publishing. The firm did not examine the contents of the backups, so the exact scope of exposed data remains unconfirmed, though filenames point to migrant-worker administration systems, contracts, cashier, and LandBank records.

Because the backups likely contain personal data on overseas Filipino workers, the breach carries significant privacy risk even though content confirmation is pending. Organizations handling migrant-worker data should verify backup storage and transfer controls, monitor for the disclosed staging infrastructure and tools, and prepare notification obligations while the investigation continues.
