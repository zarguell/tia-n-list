Harvard University disclosed in November 2025 that an unauthorized party accessed its Alumni Affairs and Development systems via a phone-based phishing (vishing) attack discovered November 18, 2025. The breached systems held email addresses, telephone numbers, home and business addresses, event attendance records, donation details, and biographical information tied to fundraising and alumni engagement for students, alumni, donors, parents, faculty, and staff. Harvard's CIO Klara Jelinkova stated the systems did not generally store Social Security numbers, passwords, payment card information, or financial account numbers. Breach notification service Have I Been Pwned later tied the incident to more than 850,000 unique email addresses, while the attackers claimed over one million records per university.

The ShinyHunters extortion gang, operating within the Scattered LAPSUS$ Hunters collective, claimed responsibility and said it stole identifiable information and donation data. After Harvard refused to pay a ransom, the group leaked a 1.1GB archive on its dark web leak site in early February 2026. TechCrunch verified portions of the published dataset against alumni and public records, and reported the intruders described the attacks as part of a phishing campaign targeting Okta and single sign-on providers. Similar alumni and donor record breaches were reported at Princeton University and the University of Pennsylvania around the same period, suggesting a coordinated campaign against university advancement systems.

What to watch: whether additional institutions disclose related SSO-focused intrusions, how Harvard notifies and supports affected affiliates, and whether leaked donor and biographical data fuels targeted fraud or social engineering against alumni. Even without financial account numbers or passwords, the combination of contact details, donation history, and biographical notes gives attackers material for convincing impersonation and fundraising scams.
