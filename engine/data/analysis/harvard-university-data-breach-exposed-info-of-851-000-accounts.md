Hackers breached Harvard University's Alumni Affairs and Development systems in November 2025, with the intrusion discovered on November 18, 2025. According to breach notification service Have I Been Pwned, data linked to more than 850,000 unique email addresses was stolen. Exposed fields include email addresses, telephone numbers, home and business addresses, event attendance records, donation details, and biographical information tied to fundraising and alumni engagement. Harvard Vice President and CIO Klara Jelinkova stated the breached systems did not store Social Security numbers, passwords, payment card information, or financial account data.

The ShinyHunters extortion gang claimed responsibility, asserting it took over one million records containing identifiable information and donation data. After Harvard refused to pay a ransom, the group leaked a 1.1GB archive of documents on its dark web leak site in early February. The haul targets a community of more than 400,000 alumni plus current students, faculty, and staff, making the donated-data and biographical details valuable for follow-on social engineering.

Watch for phishing and impersonation attempts aimed at alumni and donors using the leaked contact and donation context, since no credentials were exposed and password reuse is less likely. Track whether the leak archive contains additional records beyond the 850,000 HIBP count and whether Harvard's investigation identifies further affected systems or a second access path.
