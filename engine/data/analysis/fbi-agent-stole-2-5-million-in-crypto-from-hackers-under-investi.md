A former FBI supervisor, Patrick Steven Yaroch, 37, pleaded guilty on November 5, 2026 to wire fraud and money laundering after stealing roughly $2.47 million in cryptocurrency from wallets tied to FBI investigations. Prosecutors say the funds came from crypto wallets connected to 35 FBI cases and that Yaroch planned to use the proceeds to finance a new life in Portugal. He turned himself in after confessing to a former colleague. Fuller reporting revises the earlier headline figure of about $2.5 million down to $2.47 million.

The theft is a chain-of-custody and case-integrity problem, not just an embezzlement. The wallets held evidence and victim funds from active investigations, including fraud and nation-state hacker cases, so draining them compromises the underlying cases and any later forfeiture or restitution tied to that evidence. Yaroch has pleaded guilty, but the available reporting does not yet name the specific investigations affected or set a sentencing date.

Watch for the sentencing and for disclosure of which of the 35 cases had their evidence or victim wallets drained, since that determines which prosecutions and restitution orders are now exposed.
