The FBI's CJIS Security Policy v6.1, published June 25, 2026, tightens two concrete requirements for agencies handling Criminal Justice Information. Under SC-13 (cryptographic protection for CJI in transit), the minimum symmetric cipher key strength jumps from 128-bit to 256-bit. SC-28 (protection of CJI at rest) now also specifies 256-bit minimum encryption. These are not aspirational guidelines: Priority 1 controls have been sanctionable since October 1, 2024, and audit programs are already enforcing them.

The other significant change is in vulnerability management. Previous versions required quarterly vulnerability scanning. v6.1 moves that to monthly, reflecting the faster pace of exploitation in the wild. Michigan State Police, presenting at the October 2025 CJIS Board meeting, flagged MFA gaps as a top audit finding and outlined a shift toward continuous assessment rather than triennial visits.

Organizations subject to CJIS should verify their current encryption implementations meet the 256-bit floor and accelerate their scanning cadence to monthly. Password and MFA requirements (IA-2, IA-5) remain materially unchanged from v6.0 but are under increasing audit scrutiny. State CSAs may have their own transition timelines; Texas, for example, is still auditing against v5.9.5 through March 2027. The broader trajectory aligns with zero-trust principles: stronger identity verification, device assurance, and least-privilege access rather than relying on network location as a trust signal.
