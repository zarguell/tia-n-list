IDScan.net, an ID fraud protection platform, confirmed a data security incident that may be connected to a dark web marketplace called Nexus. On September 1, Brian Krebs reported that Nexus was selling scans of over 153 million US and Canadian driver's licenses and millions of other ID documents. The marketplace shut down ahead of an FBI investigation, and IDScan was cited as a possible source for the leaked documents.

IDScan is now offering free credit monitoring and identity protection services to affected customers. The company says an unauthorized third party may have accessed customer information including full names and driver's license or government-issued ID numbers. Full access to the data reportedly "required payment." IDScan says it is cooperating with federal law enforcement.

The incident matters because it combines a massive identity document leak with law enforcement activity. The 153 million figure covers a significant portion of driving-age adults in North America. Exposed driver's license scans can be used for identity theft, account openings, and synthetic identity fraud. The FBI's involvement suggests the scope is serious enough for federal investigation. Organizations using IDScan's services should review their exposure and consider whether the breach extends beyond what has been publicly disclosed.
