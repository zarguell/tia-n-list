The Anubis ransomware group hit Fairlife, the Coca-Cola-owned dairy company, gaining initial access by exploiting CVE-2025-5777 (CitrixBleed 2) on a network edge device. The group says it encrypted Fairlife's Nutanix systems and stole roughly 1 TB of data. The intrusion forced Fairlife to suspend production across its U.S. facilities; Coca-Cola said most production had resumed eleven days after the initial disclosure, and has confirmed data was taken without publicly validating all of Anubis's claims.

This is a useful case study in why patching alone is not a complete defense: the entry point was an edge product, and the blast radius reached production infrastructure. The CVE is on CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list, which is why this story scores high despite being a single-source account from malware.news. The 1 TB theft claim, if accurate, makes data extortion a live second phase even after recovery.

Watch for three things: whether Anubis publishes or sells the stolen data, whether Coca-Cola or Fairlife disclose additional compromised systems, and whether CVE-2025-5777 exploitation is observed at other organizations, which would suggest the CitrixBleed 2 pattern is broader than this one incident.
