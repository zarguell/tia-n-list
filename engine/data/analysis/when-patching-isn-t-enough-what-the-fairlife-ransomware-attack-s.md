The Fairlife incident began with CVE-2025-5777, the NetScaler memory-disclosure flaw dubbed CitrixBleed 2, which leaks uninitialized memory from appliances configured as a Gateway or AAA virtual server and yields session tokens that work past MFA. Citrix rated it 9.3 CVSS and disclosed it on June 17, 2025 with fixed builds 14.1-43.56, 13.1-58.32, 13.1-FIPS 13.1-37.235 and 12.1-FIPS 12.1-55.328. The Anubis group claims it used the bug for initial access, encrypted Fairlife's Nutanix systems and stole about 1 TB of data; Coca-Cola confirmed data was taken, suspended production across Fairlife's US facilities, and restored the majority of output 11 days after its initial disclosure.

The date arithmetic is the story. CISA put the bug in the KEV catalog on July 10, 2025 with a one-day federal patch deadline, GreyNoise honeypot telemetry showed exploitation attempts from June 23, 2025, and two working public exploits were published by July 7, 2025. A Coca-Cola-owned dairy producer was still breached by a flaw whose public fix predates the compromise by more than a year, so the real exposure window for edge devices runs from exploit availability to the last straggler patching, not from vendor disclosure to first patch. Any organization that ran a vulnerable NetScaler Gateway during the mid-2025 mass scanning and caught up later must assume session tokens and credentials were harvested and can sit dormant until ransomware day; Fairlife is what that assumption looks like when it matures into an encrypted Nutanix cluster and a 1 TB extortion claim.

Inventory every NetScaler ADC and Gateway build and upgrade anything below 14.1-43.56, 13.1-58.32, 13.1-FIPS/NDcPP 13.1-37.235 or 12.1-FIPS 12.1-55.328, then terminate all active and persistent ICA and PCoIP sessions and force reauthentication, since tokens issued before the patch stay valid after it. Hunt on the appliance: POSTs to /p/u/doAuthentication.do carrying a bare login parameter, authentication responses whose InitialValue element contains non-printable bytes, and ns.log entries with non-printable characters. Audit NetScaler Gateway sessions for one account used from multiple client IPs at once, and diff show ns runningConfig -withDefaults against known-good backups for added admin accounts, the persistence pattern researchers documented for this bug class.

Two developments would change this assessment. Confirmation from Fairlife, Coca-Cola or incident responders that initial access actually traces to CVE-2025-5777, rather than resting on the group's assertion, would validate the patch-lag narrative; publication of the claimed 1 TB on Anubis's leak site would turn an unverified extortion claim into confirmed exposure and show what the stolen Nutanix data contained.
