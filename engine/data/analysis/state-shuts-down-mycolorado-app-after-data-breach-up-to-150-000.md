Colorado's Governor's Office of Information Technology discovered on the evening of November 18 that an unauthorized user had accessed data on the myColorado app platform. The state shut the app down and said on November 23 that it was investigating a cybersecurity incident and would notify affected people once the review was complete. The app supplies digital ID, document storage and access to state services.

KKTV put the possible impact at 50,000 to 150,000 of the app's roughly 2.1 million users. FOX31 had reported the app going down just before noon on November 19, when a state spokesperson described an outage of unspecified cause, four days before the security incident was acknowledged. The state told users to carry physical licenses and state IDs, use agency websites and online portals, and consider a DMV-approved mobile wallet for flights; TSA ConfirmID was cited at a 45 dollar fee for travelers without acceptable ID.

The availability impact landed just before Thanksgiving travel, and the state gave no restoration date while the investigation continued. Investigators declined to say what data may have been taken. Watch for a confirmed record count and data categories, a restoration timeline, and whether the November 18 discovery date is followed by a public attribution or a notification campaign.
