Colorado's Governor's Office of Information Technology found an unauthorized user in data on the myColorado platform on the evening of November 18 and took the app offline. The app provides digital identification, document storage and access to state services for about 2.1 million users. OIT estimated on November 23, as carried by KKTV, that 50,000 to 150,000 people may have had data affected, while The Record's November 24 report used nearly 150,000. No field list, actor, CVE or ransomware claim has been published.

The defender-relevant detail is the gap between discovery and disclosure. On November 19, a state spokesperson told FOX31 only that the app was down for an outage and that a team was restoring service; the cybersecurity incident did not appear on the state's outage page until November 23. Travelers had no reason to change behavior during Thanksgiving week while the credential app was already pulled. This is not a generic state record breach: Colorado's mobile driver license is accepted at Denver International Airport and more than 250 participating airports under ISO/IEC 18013-5, so the uncertainty reaches a travel credential as well as a data set.

For immediate response, carry a physical driver license or state ID, add the mDL to a DMV-approved wallet, and use agency websites or the DMV portal instead of the app; TSA ConfirmID is a $45 fallback for travelers without acceptable ID. Watch the mycolorado.gov daily update page for a restoration date and the notification OIT promised for anyone whose data was affected. OIT has published no log paths, telemetry fields, hashes or actor names, so there is no defensible indicator-led hunt yet; the concrete work is to inventory app use between November 18 and November 23 and map any later record-type definition against your own integrations.

What changes the assessment is an OIT scope statement naming the fields, especially whether credential material or only contact and service data was exposed, and a resolved count inside the 50,000 to 150,000 range. A restoration date plus the promised notices would close the November 19 to November 23 gap; an attribution or evidence that the exposed data was used would raise the severity.
