Colorado's Governor's Office of Information Technology found on the evening of November 18 that an unauthorized user had reached data on the platform behind myColorado, the state app used for digital identification, document storage and access to state services. OIT took the app offline and opened an investigation. The Record reported on November 24 that the app was still unavailable, in an incident involving data tied to nearly 150,000 people out of a user base of 2.1 million. Investigators have not said which fields were taken.

The outage has practical consequences beyond the notification process. Colorado told residents to carry physical identification when traveling for Thanksgiving, while state services remain reachable through agency websites. OIT director Sarah Tuneberg said systems would return once security is restored. The state has previously dealt with cyberattacks on its Department of Higher Education and ransomware incidents in smaller municipalities, so this adds to a run of pressure on local government services.

What to watch is the scope statement from OIT: whether the affected records are identity documents, contact data or service histories, and whether the intrusion touched systems outside the app platform. A confirmed restoration date and any credit or identity-protection offer will indicate how the state classifies the exposure. The 150,000 figure is an early estimate, not a final count.
