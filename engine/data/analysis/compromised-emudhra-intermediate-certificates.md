Eight intermediate certificates issued by eMudhra had private keys with severely insufficient entropy, in some cases single or double digit integers instead of the required 384 bits. Cisco Talos reported the issue, and Mozilla received the initial report on 17 October 2026 at 20:06 UTC. Cloudflare became aware on 19 October 2026 at approximately 17:00 UTC. eMudhra acknowledged the compromise on 20 October 2026, revoked the affected intermediates, and stopped issuing under its G3 root. The weak keys allow an attacker holding any compromised intermediate private key to impersonate arbitrary websites or services.

The incident triggered emergency blocks across trust stores: six TLS intermediates trusted by Chrome, Mozilla, and Apple were blocked, and two code-signing intermediates used by Microsoft were blocked as well. Browser and operating system vendors shipped updates, including Debian DSA-6572-1 for ca-certificates and a Firefox ESR update. The Register noted the entropy reduction to roughly 3 to 8 bits.

Operators should ensure trust stores and browsers are updated to the patched versions, verify no fraudulent certificates were issued under the compromised intermediates, and review code-signing trust chains that depended on the affected eMudhra intermediates. Additional remediation patches across applications are expected as the exposure is addressed.
