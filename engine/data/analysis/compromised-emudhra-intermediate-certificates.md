Eight eMudhra intermediate CA certificates had their private keys compromised because a flaw during an April 30, 2025 key generation ceremony for 24 subordinate CAs created a second set of weak P-384 keys during a wrap/unwrap step. Those keys carried only 3 to 8 bits of entropy instead of 384, making recovery trivial; six of the intermediates were TLS CAs trusted by Chrome, Mozilla, and Apple, and two were Microsoft code-signing CAs. eMudhra revoked the compromised certificates and stopped issuing under its G3 root, while Mozilla, Google, Apple, and Microsoft blocked them in their trust stores.

A successful attacker could have impersonated any website or service chaining to those intermediates, and the affected roots were widely trusted, so the blast radius is not limited to eMudhra customers. The more important point is that the weakness sat in a signing path for months after the April 2025 ceremony and was only surfaced when a researcher, Wayne, signed the message "We have a problem here - Wayne" with each of the eight keys and reported it on October 17, 2026 at 20:06 UTC. That means trust stores had to be updated out of band rather than through normal revocation, which is why Debian shipped DSA-6572-1 and Firefox ESR updates removed the intermediates from ca-certificates.

Defenders should confirm Firefox ESR and ca-certificates are updated to the versions that remove the eMudhra intermediates, then inventory any internal or third-party services still presenting chains rooted at emSign Root TLS CA - G3 or the cross-signed Comodo AAA Certificate Services. Hunt TLS logs and certificate transparency for leaf certificates issued by the eight named intermediates after April 30, 2025, and validate code-signing trust chains on Windows hosts for binaries signed by the two blocked code-signing intermediates. Where patching is not possible, restrict trust to known-good roots and monitor for unexpected issuers.

The assessment changes if evidence emerges that any leaf certificates were actually issued from the compromised intermediates, since eMudhra states none were, or if additional weak intermediates surface from the same ceremony. Watch also for downstream breakage as more distributions and applications ship trust-store updates, and for any disclosure of the affected key values beyond the already published single-digit and double-digit integers.
