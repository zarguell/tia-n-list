Eight intermediate CA certificates issued by Indian certificate authority eMudhra carry private keys that were generated with only 3 to 8 bits of entropy instead of the required 384 bits, making them trivially recoverable. One key was the integer 4. The incident, tracked as CVE-2026-111943 and CVE-2026-111944, was first reported to Mozilla on October 17, 2026 at 20:06 UTC. Chrome, Firefox, Apple, Microsoft, Go, and Debian have all blocked or revoked the intermediates, but command-line stacks including default curl, Python, and Node may still trust them until CA bundles update. The root cause was an April 30, 2025 key generation ceremony error in which a wrap/unwrap step produced a second set of weak P-384 keys that were mistakenly selected for signing.

eMudhra revoked the certificates and stopped issuing new ones under its G3 root. The company has since announced it will exit the global SSL/TLS certificate business. Defenders should audit their CA bundles, verify that Go 1.26.10/1.27.3 or equivalent updates are applied, and use the published disclosure-site probes to confirm that clients no longer trust the compromised chain.
