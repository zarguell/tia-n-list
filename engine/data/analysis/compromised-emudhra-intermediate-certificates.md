Eight eMudhra intermediate certificate private keys were compromised, with some keys as weak as single-digit integers, collapsing 384-bit entropy to 3-8 bits. Cloudflare became aware on October 19, 2026 around 17:00 UTC, triggering emergency blocks across browsers, operating systems, and trust stores including Debian DSA-6572-1. The incident spans CVE-2026-111943, CVE-2026-111944, CVE-2026-113470, and CVE-2026-115929. Coverage has been broad (40 sources, score 7.3, peak 7.3 on October 22) with sustained updates through October 25. Indian government sites have been observed serving revoked certificates, indicating real-world persistence of the revoked intermediates. No exploitation campaigns tied to the weak keys have been reported, but the browser-level blocks confirm this is an active infrastructure threat, not a theoretical advisory.

What to watch: whether additional government or enterprise endpoints continue serving revoked certificates, and whether eMudhra issues full revocation timelines or replacement procedures for the compromised intermediates.
