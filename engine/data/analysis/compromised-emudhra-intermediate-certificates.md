Cisco Talos identified eight compromised intermediate certificates for eMudhra, where private keys contained only 3 to 8 bits of entropy instead of the required 384 bits. The weak P-384 keys were generated during an April 30, 2025 key-generation ceremony under the emSign Root TLS CA G3; a wrap/unwrap flaw produced a second, trivially recoverable set. Six certificates trusted by Chrome, Mozilla, and Apple have been blocked, and two Microsoft codesigning certificates were also blocked.

Following disclosure, eMudhra revoked the certificates, halted new issuance under its G3 root, and announced it will exit the SSL/TLS certificate business entirely. Security researcher Scott Helme reconstructed all eight private keys in under a second, demonstrating the flaw's severity. Major vendors have already pushed blocks, reducing immediate impersonation risk.

Monitor vendor patch releases and software updates that embed or validate these certificates. Additional remediation is expected across browsers and operating systems as the revoked intermediates propagate. The incident also raises questions about certificate-authority key-generation audits for other providers.
