eMudhra's intermediate certificates were compromised because a key-generation flaw reduced P-384 private keys from 384 bits of entropy to between 3 and 8 bits, making recovery trivial. Eight intermediate CAs were affected; an attacker holding any compromised intermediate's private key could impersonate any website or service. Chrome, Mozilla, and Apple have blocked six TLS certificates, and Microsoft has blocked two codesigning certificates.

The weakness originated during an April 30, 2025 key-generation ceremony for 24 subordinate CAs, when a wrap/unwrap step created a second set of weak keys that were mistakenly used for the eight intermediates. eMudhra has revoked the compromised certificates and stopped issuing under its G3 root. Go, among others, has added explicit distrust for the affected intermediates.

Administrators should inventory certificates chained to eMudhra intermediates, replace them, and apply vendor trust-store updates. Watch for additional patches across browsers, operating systems, and language runtimes as distrust propagates.
