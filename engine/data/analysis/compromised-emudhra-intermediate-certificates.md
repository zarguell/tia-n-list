The eMudhra certificate authority compromise involves eight intermediate CA certificates whose P-384 private keys carried only 3 to 8 bits of entropy instead of the expected 384. One key was the integer 4; others were 56, 66, 115, 155, 156, 230, and 237. Because the intermediates were trusted by major browsers and operating systems, anyone who recovered these keys could forge trusted TLS certificates for arbitrary domains or sign code. Chrome and Firefox blocked the certificates within hours of the October 17, 2026 disclosure; Apple and Microsoft followed on October 19 and 20. Go assigned CVE-2026-111943 for its denial list update (Go 1.26.10 and 1.27.3), and Microsoft released out-of-band updates KB5134125 (Windows 10) and KB5134133 (Windows 11) on October 26 to remove the intermediates from trust lists. eMudhra revoked all eight certificates and announced it will exit the global SSL/TLS business.

The incident exposes a structural weakness in PKI: intermediate CA keys are high-value targets, and weak entropy generation is catastrophic. The disclosure site and researcher Wayne demonstrated possession by signing messages with each compromised key. Remediation extends beyond browsers to command-line stacks: default curl, Python, Node, and Go environments that rely on pinned or outdated CA bundles may still trust the revoked chain until updates propagate. Security teams should audit TLS certificate chains in their environments and verify that software bundles include the denylist changes.
