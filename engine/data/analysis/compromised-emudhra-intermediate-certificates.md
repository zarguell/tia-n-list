Eight intermediate certificates issued by Indian certificate authority eMudhra in an April 30, 2025 key ceremony carried P-384 private keys with only 3 to 8 bits of entropy, small enough to recover by testing a few hundred values; one key had the value 4. The weakness, discovered October 17, 2026 by researcher Wayne, stemmed from a script error that created a second set of weak keys during a wrap/unwrap step and mistakenly selected them. Six of the intermediates issued TLS certificates and two signed code, so possession of a key could let an attacker forge trusted certificates for any domain or lend malware a verified-publisher signature.

Major browsers and trust stores blocked the eight intermediates within days: Chrome and Firefox on October 17, Apple on October 19, Microsoft on October 20. eMudhra revoked the certificates, stopped issuing under its G3 root, and later announced it would exit the SSL/TLS certificate business. Vendors shipped remediation including Go 1.26.10 and 1.27.3, Microsoft out-of-band Windows updates KB5134133 and KB5134125, and Debian DSA-6572-1. The issue is tracked as CVE-2026-111943 and CVE-2026-111944 for affected Go certificate verification.

The broader risk is long-tail remediation: browsers are now protected, but software with bundled CA stores, IoT devices, containers, and legacy Linux systems that skip revocation checks may keep trusting the compromised chain until updated. Organizations should inventory certificates issued under eMudhra intermediates and apply vendor patches and trust-store updates.
