Cisco Talos found that eight intermediate certificate authority certificates issued by eMudhra were generated with near-zero entropy, making their private keys trivially recoverable. During an April 30, 2025 key-generation ceremony for 24 subordinate CAs under the emSign Root TLS CA G3 and cross-signed Comodo AAA hierarchy, a flaw in eMudhra's key generation process produced a second set of weak P-384 keys during a wrap/unwrap step, dropping entropy from 384 bits to between 3 and 8 bits. An attacker holding any compromised intermediate's private key could impersonate any website or service. Six of the TLS certificates, trusted by Chrome, Mozilla, and Apple, have been blocked by those vendors, and two code-signing certificates used by Microsoft have also been blocked. Microsoft shipped out-of-band Windows updates (KB5134133 for Windows 11 24H2/25H2/26H2 and KB5134125 for Windows 10 21H2/22H2) on October 26, 2026 to remove trust in the affected certificates, with the same removals pushed to Microsoft Edge on October 27.

Security researcher Scott Helme recovered all eight private keys on his laptop in less than a second, and said an AI agent later repeated the feat from a single prompt. Researcher Wayne discovered the weakness on October 17 while scanning public certificate data, and the incident was publicly disclosed on October 20, 2026. Another researcher, Fluepke, published one of the recovered private keys on GitHub, showing that the key for eMudhra's EM OV TLS CA G3C certificate had a value of just 4; BleepingComputer independently verified that the published key matched one of the revoked certificates. eMudhra blamed an error in a custom Python script written to move private keys between hardware security modules across vendors after hardware-procurement delays. The script reduced eight ECDSA private keys to a single byte, and it also processed eight RSA private keys in the memory of the host machine rather than inside an HSM, so eMudhra could not demonstrate those RSA keys were handled under its certificate security policies.

eMudhra has revoked the compromised certificates, stopped issuing new certificates under its G3 root, and on November 11, 2026 revoked eight additional intermediate certificates, bringing the total affected to 16. That second wave traces to the same root cause: a custom Python script eMudhra wrote to move private keys between hardware security modules during the April 2025 key ceremony, and coverage of the new revocations describes the additional material as RSA intermediate keys, so the cleanup is no longer confined to the original P-384 set. Days later the company announced its board had decided to exit the SSL/TLS certificate business entirely. Chairman V. Srinivasan cited rising costs from data sovereignty and compliance requirements, competition from providers that issue certificates free of charge, and social media amplification that he said brought scrutiny out of proportion to the unit's place in the company. The certificates are covered by CVE-2026-111943, CVE-2026-111944, CVE-2026-113470, and CVE-2026-115929.

Organizations should verify their trust stores and certificate chains reflect the revocations and vendor blocks, confirm Windows systems received the out-of-band trust-list updates or automatic CTL refreshes, check that browsers and operating systems block all 16 intermediates, and reissue any certificates chained to the affected intermediates. Watch for further vendor advisories extending the block list and for public tooling that automates recovery of the weak keys, which would make exploitation trivial against any system still trusting the affected intermediates.
