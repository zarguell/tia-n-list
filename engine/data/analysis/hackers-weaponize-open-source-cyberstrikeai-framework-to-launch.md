Kaspersky published on November 2 that attackers are abusing CyberStrikeAI, an open-source AI-powered penetration-testing framework, to run network intrusions against educational organizations across Africa and Latin America. An LLM orchestrator on the command-and-control server selects tools, generates commands, interprets results, and decides subsequent actions, while the implant itself is a conventional C2 beacon without AI logic. Kaspersky describes it as one of the first public cases of an LLM running an intrusion in the wild.

The intrusions moved from reconnaissance through persistence, credential dumping, lateral movement, and data exfiltration within three to four days. Earlier telemetry shows the framework is not new: Team Cymru tracked 21 CyberStrikeAI servers between January and February 2026 linked to more than 600 FortiGate compromises across 55 countries, and Trend Micro on October 23 found 44 AI agents automating reconnaissance through data theft at a Japanese target with a simplified-Chinese management console. Seven outlets carry the story, including GBHackers, BleepingComputer, The Hacker News, and Securelist.

Watch for the LLM-orchestrated technique spreading beyond education targets and for defender indicators such as CyberStrikeAI banners on port 8080 or simplified-Chinese consoles on attacker infrastructure.
