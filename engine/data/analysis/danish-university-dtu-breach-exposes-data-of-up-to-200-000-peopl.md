Technical University of Denmark (DTU) confirmed that hackers accessed its identity and access management system DTUBasen and downloaded a large volume of data. The attacker used compromised credentials to log in, allowing access to over two decades of user data. DTU stores information for nearly 40,000 active users and around 160,000 former users, meaning up to 200,000 people may be affected. Potentially exposed information includes Danish civil registration numbers (CPR), full names, and other user details.

DTU cannot determine precisely what was downloaded or exactly how many users are affected. The university disclosed that it cannot confirm whether all user categories were impacted equally. Organizations with similar IAM architectures should rotate credentials, enforce multi-factor authentication, and review access logs for unauthorized DTUBasen queries. The breach highlights universities as rich targets due to long data retention and extensive user bases.
