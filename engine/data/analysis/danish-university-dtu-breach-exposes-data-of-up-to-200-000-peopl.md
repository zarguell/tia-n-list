The Technical University of Denmark disclosed that attackers used compromised credentials to log into DTUBasen, the university's identity and access management system, and downloaded a large volume of data covering more than two decades of user records. DTU says up to 200,000 people may be affected: roughly 40,000 active users and about 160,000 former users.

Potentially exposed data on active users includes Danish civil registration numbers (CPR), full names, home addresses, profile pictures, work email addresses, job titles, office locations, and other employment details, plus names, relationships, and phone numbers of next of kin where provided. For former users, addresses, profile pictures, and next-of-kin details are automatically deleted after six months, which limits but does not close that exposure window.

DTU states it cannot determine precisely what was downloaded or how many people are affected, so the real scope remains open. University Director Bjarke Bak Christensen called it a serious attack and apologized for the uncertainty. Watch for Danish data protection follow-up, for the dataset surfacing on leak forums, and for phishing against affected current and former staff now that attackers hold CPR numbers and contact data.
