A Zurich District Court sentenced a 52-year-old Ukrainian national to 12 years and 9 months in prison for developing the LockerGoga, MegaCortex, and Nefilim ransomware families. The defendant was held in pretrial detention since October 2021. The court found he played a key role in attacks on Stadler Rail (May 2020, $6 million Nefilim ransom demand), HVAC company Meier Tobler, and software company Crealogix. The attacker consistently denied knowledge that his code was used for criminal purposes, claiming the source code found at his home in Basel-Landschaft came from legitimate consulting work. The court rejected this because extortion messages were also found among his data.

The sentence is notable for targeting the developer rather than the operator or affiliate. The same 2021 law enforcement action identified other alleged members of all three ransomware crews. Volodymyr Tymoshchuk was formally indicted in the US and placed on the FBI's most wanted list with an $11 million bounty. Tymoshchuk allegedly masterminded attacks on at least 250 companies, including the Norsk Hydro attack in 2019. The three ransomware families together caused estimated losses of several hundred million Swiss francs across 1,800 victims in 71 countries.

The judgment is not final and can be appealed. A 10-year ban from Switzerland was also imposed. This case reinforces the legal precedent that ransomware development, even without direct operational involvement, carries significant criminal liability. It also demonstrates international cooperation in prosecuting ransomware supply chains, with Swiss, US, and Europol coordination.
