Check Point Research disclosed a cross-account data leakage vulnerability in ChatGPT's sandbox architecture. The flaw exploits a shared internal package-delivery service that spans different ChatGPT account containers. Although the containers cannot access the public internet or communicate directly, they can all reach this shared service. An attacker could plant a hidden instruction through a malicious prompt, shared conversation, or custom GPT, then trigger it with an ordinary message. The victim receives a normal response while the attacker's task executes separately, with results relayed cross-account.

In the proof of concept, Check Point demonstrated that ChatGPT retrieved email data from a victim's connected Gmail account and returned it to the attacker. The channel could also exfiltrate conversation history and files available in the affected chat. The researchers characterized the channel as "covert" because the victim sees only their own visible interaction.

This is a significant supply-chain-style vulnerability in a widely deployed AI platform. The ability to exfiltrate data from connected services (Gmail, in the PoC) without the victim noticing means the attack surface extends far beyond ChatGPT itself. Organizations that have integrated ChatGPT with business data or connected third-party services should evaluate the risk of cross-account exploitation.
