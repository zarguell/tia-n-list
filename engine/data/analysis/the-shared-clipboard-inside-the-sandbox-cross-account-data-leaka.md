Check Point Research discovered a cross-account data leakage vulnerability in ChatGPT that allows attackers to execute hidden tasks using a victim's connected apps and data. The attack works through code-execution environments that share access to an internal package delivery service, creating a covert channel between different ChatGPT accounts. An attacker can inject hidden instructions through a malicious prompt, shared conversation, or custom GPT, then trigger task execution without the victim seeing it in their visible response.

In their proof of concept, researchers demonstrated exfiltrating email data from a victim's connected Gmail account and relaying it to the attacker. The same channel could also be used to steal conversation history and files from affected chats. This vulnerability is particularly concerning because ChatGPT's connected apps feature is designed to give the AI access to external services for productivity purposes. Organizations deploying ChatGPT with connected apps should treat this as a data exfiltration vector and evaluate whether the convenience justifies the risk of cross-account data leakage.
