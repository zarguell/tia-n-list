1Password began investigating on 19 November 2026 after signs that an employee's corporate GitHub account had been compromised. The intruder used that account to read private repositories and push malicious code into backend services, which then downloaded administrative customer records. The company also said the attacker reached its CI/CD environment, potentially exposing internal infrastructure credentials. The disclosure was posted on 25 November at 17:33 UTC, with a small number of customers individually notified.

The copied data included customer names, email addresses, account and user identifiers, account creation and last sign-in times, public keys, SRP parameters, and tokens embedded in invitation, provisioning and recovery links. 1Password said vault contents and the keys protecting them were not accessed and that services kept running normally. Tokens from those emails could not be used alone because account provisioning or recovery still required single sign-on or a six-digit code sent to the registered email address; the company lengthened those codes to 10 alphanumeric characters and added detection for similar attacks.

1Password disabled the employee account, revoked the stolen token, removed attacker-created access and rotated potentially exposed internal credentials, with independent experts engaged to validate containment. It told customers no account action was required but warned about phishing that references invitations or recovery. Watch for the missing victim count, any evidence that CI/CD secrets were abused, and phishing campaigns using stolen invitation or recovery tokens.
