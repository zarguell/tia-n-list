1Password disclosed on November 25 that an attacker used an employee's corporate GitHub account to read private source code, push malicious code into backend services and download administrative customer records. The investigation began on November 19. The intruder also accessed the CI/CD environment, potentially exposing internal infrastructure credentials, and copied company source code.

The customer subset included names, email addresses, account and user identifiers, creation and last sign-in times, public keys, Secure Remote Password parameters, and tokens in invitation, provisioning and recovery emails. 1Password said vault contents and the keys protecting them were not accessed. The stolen workflow tokens could not be used alone: completion required SSO or an emailed verification code. The company lengthened those codes from six digits to 10 alphanumeric characters, notified a small number of affected users and did not disclose a total victim count.

The incident matters because the attacker obtained both internal development material and valid account-workflow tokens, useful inputs for follow-on intrusions, while the exposed customer list creates a targeted phishing risk. Watch for further detail on CI/CD credential exposure, downstream use of the stolen tokens, and additional notifications. Customers should treat unexpected invitation or recovery messages as suspect even though 1Password said no account action was required.
