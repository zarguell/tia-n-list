1Password disclosed on November 25, 2026 that an attacker used an employee's corporate GitHub account to read private source code, push malicious code into backend services and download administrative customer records. The company began investigating on November 19. The stolen subset included names, email addresses, account and user identifiers, creation and last sign-in times, public keys, Secure Remote Password parameters, and tokens in invitation, provisioning and recovery emails. The attacker also entered the CI/CD environment, creating potential exposure of internal infrastructure credentials.

1Password said customer vault contents and the keys protecting them were not accessed, and services continued normally. It described the affected group as a small number of users and said they were notified individually. The invitation and recovery tokens were not sufficient alone: redemption required SSO or a six-digit code sent to the registered email. The company lengthened those codes to 10 alphanumeric characters and added detection for similar attacks. Its containment included disabling the account, revoking the stolen token, removing attacker-created access and rotating potentially exposed credentials.

The intrusion shows how a developer-account compromise can become a backend data theft without breaking vault encryption. The copied source code and CI/CD access may matter beyond the disclosed customer fields, while the list of 1Password users and genuine invitation or recovery messages give phishing operators credible lures. Watch for further detail on the number of affected users, the precise CI/CD exposure and whether any internal secrets were used. Customers were told no account action was required, but should treat unsolicited invitation or recovery mail with suspicion.
