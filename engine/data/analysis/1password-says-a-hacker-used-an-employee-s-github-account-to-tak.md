1Password disclosed on November 25, 2026 that an attacker gained access to an employee's corporate GitHub account. The company began investigating on November 19 after detecting signs of compromise. The attacker read source code from private repositories, pushed malicious code into backend services and used that code to download administrative customer records. The intruder also accessed the CI/CD environment, which holds infrastructure secrets.

The stolen customer records included names, email addresses, account and user identifiers, creation and last sign-in times, public keys, Secure Remote Password parameters and tokens contained in invitation, provisioning and recovery emails. 1Password said vault contents, vault items and the keys protecting them were not accessed. It described the affected group as a small number of customers who were individually notified, but did not disclose a total. The company also said the stolen email tokens could not be used alone: completing invitation or recovery required SSO or a six-digit code sent to the user's email address. Reporting on the response says those codes were increased to 10 alphanumeric characters and new detection was added.

The incident matters because the attacker reached both customer account data and the software delivery environment of a password manager, even though the company says the encrypted vaults remained out of reach. 1Password revoked the stolen token, disabled the account, removed attacker-created access, rotated potentially exposed internal credentials and engaged independent experts to validate containment. Customers should watch for phishing that references invitations or account recovery, the warning included in 1Password's own community notice. The main open questions are the final affected population, the independent validation results and whether any stolen token was used in a follow-on attempt.
