1Password said it began investigating on November 19, 2026 after an attacker gained access to an employee's corporate GitHub account. The attacker read private repositories, pushed malicious code into backend services, and used that code to download administrative records for a subset of customers. The records included names, email addresses, account and user identifiers, creation and last sign-in times, public keys, SRP parameters, and tokens from invitation, provisioning, and recovery emails. The company described the number affected as small and said those users were notified.

1Password said customer vault contents and the keys protecting them were not accessed, and it reported no evidence of account takeover. The stolen email-link tokens could not be used alone because completing an invitation or recovery required SSO or a code sent to the registered email address. The company lengthened those codes from six digits to 10 alphanumeric characters and added detection for similar attacks. It also warned customers about phishing that references invitations or account recovery.

The attacker copied internal source code and accessed the CI/CD system, potentially exposing infrastructure credentials. 1Password said it disabled the compromised account, revoked the stolen token, removed attacker-created access, rotated potentially exposed internal credentials, and engaged outside experts. The immediate risk is follow-on phishing against a verified set of password-manager users, while the longer-term concern is the scope of source-code and pipeline access. Watch for clarification on the affected customer count and whether any CI/CD secrets were used.
