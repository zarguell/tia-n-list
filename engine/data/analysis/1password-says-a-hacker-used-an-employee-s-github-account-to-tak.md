1Password began investigating on 19 November 2026 after signs that an employee's corporate GitHub account had been compromised. The intruder used that account to read private repositories and push malicious code into backend services, which then downloaded administrative customer records. 1Password also said the attacker reached its CI/CD environment, where internal infrastructure credentials could be exposed. The disclosure went up on 25 November at 17:33 UTC, with a small number of customers notified individually.

The copied fields included customer names, email addresses, account and user identifiers, account creation and last sign-in times, public keys, SRP parameters, and tokens embedded in invitation, provisioning and recovery links. 1Password said vault contents and the keys protecting them were not accessed and that services kept running normally. Tokens from those emails could not be used on their own because provisioning or recovery still required single sign-on or a six-digit code sent to the registered email address. The company lengthened those codes to 10 alphanumeric characters against brute force and added detection for similar attacks.

1Password disabled the employee account, revoked the stolen token, removed attacker-created access and rotated potentially exposed internal credentials, with independent experts engaged to validate containment. Coverage on 26 November from Taiwan's TechNews repeated the company's position that customer password storage was unaffected. The customer count remains undisclosed, and there is no public evidence yet that CI/CD secrets were abused. Watch for phishing that cites invitations or recovery, and for any later finding on the exposed internal credentials.
