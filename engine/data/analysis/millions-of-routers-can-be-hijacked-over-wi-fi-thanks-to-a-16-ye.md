A 16-year-old bug in Wi-Fi Protected Setup (WPS) lets attackers hijack millions of routers over Wi-Fi, as reported by TechSpot under the name WPSplinter. The vulnerability has apparently lingered in router firmware for over a decade and a half, meaning a large installed base of consumer and small-office devices may still be exposed. The social posts circulating the story point to the same TechSpot article, so the underlying reporting comes from that single outlet.

The impact is broad because affected routers may never receive firmware updates, and WPS is often enabled by default. Defenders should disable WPS on any router that supports it, verify firmware is current, and consider replacing hardware that no longer gets updates. Watch for a CVE assignment, vendor advisories, and a proof-of-concept or scanner that can identify vulnerable devices.
