Large-scale DDoS attacks hit the Swiss secure messaging service Threema earlier this week, severely disrupting communications. Interruptions began Tuesday around 6 PM UTC, and the company first attributed them to a network outage at its colocation partner before the situation was disclosed as an attack. Users in Switzerland, India, and China were still reporting the service down the next day, after an initial window in which Threema said it was restoring services once the partner reported the network issue resolved.

The campaign targeted Threema's hosted infrastructure rather than exposing a flaw in the product. Organizations on Threema On-Prem were unaffected because they run their own servers, and the end-to-end encrypted messaging layer was not compromised. The Friday post-mortem said the threat actor constantly changed attack patterns, which Threema described as making the assaults difficult to defend against.

The episode matters because Threema is a paid, privacy-focused messenger with its own server infrastructure across multiple Swiss locations, marketed on a no ads, no profiling, no hidden data analyses model and used by security-conscious individuals and organizations. DDoS disruption of such a service is a trust and availability event even when message confidentiality holds, and it shows that even hardened messaging providers remain exposed to brute-force volumetric pressure. The ingested report ends before covering attack scale, attribution, or what mitigations Threema plans; those details are worth watching for in follow-up coverage.
