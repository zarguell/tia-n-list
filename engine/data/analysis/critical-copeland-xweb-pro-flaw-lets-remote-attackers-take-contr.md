Copeland XWEB controllers have become a multi-branch exposure. Claroty's Team82 disclosed 23 vulnerabilities in XWEB Pro supervisory controllers, 21 rated high severity, affecting XWEB300D PRO and XWEB500D PRO. The set includes an OS command injection in XWEB Pro 1.12.1 and earlier that gives an unauthenticated attacker root-level remote code execution, plus authentication bypass and predictable administrator credential generation. Firmware 1.13 is the fix. These devices sit between enterprise networks and RS485/Modbus field controllers that run compressors, fans, defrost heaters and sensors, so one compromised supervisory device can reach many refrigeration units.

The newer XWEB EVO line adds a separate cluster of problems. CVE-2026-124010 and CVE-2026-124014 were reported with public proof-of-concept detail and in-the-wild exploitation; CVE-2026-124002 is a critical pre-authentication OS command injection. CVE-2026-124015 affects XWEB EVO 5.6.2 through 5.8.8 and lets an unauthenticated attacker set the password of any account flagged to reset at next login through missing session validation in the users.lua update_password action, clearing the expiration flag and gaining an authenticated foothold.

CVE-2026-124015 was assigned to a product line that reached end of service in March 2026, so a routine firmware upgrade may not be available for affected EVO installations. That raises the value of network controls: keep management interfaces off the internet, segment controller networks from enterprise systems, and check for newly reset operator accounts and unexpected authenticated sessions.

Watch for further EVO CVEs landing in this same cluster and for signs that the published PoCs are being used against exposed devices. The older Pro disclosure and the EVO exploitation reports describe different firmware families, but they share the same operational target: remote control of commercial refrigeration equipment through a single supervisory controller.
