Copeland XWEB refrigeration controllers have a second, separate set of critical flaws after the August Claroty Team82 disclosure. The original report covered 23 vulnerabilities in XWEB Pro units, 21 rated high severity, including an authentication bypass and predictable credential generation that together allow unauthenticated remote attackers to reach root-level code execution. Team82 found the most severe issue, CVE-2026-25085, in the Lua-based web authentication logic. Affected products include the XWEB300D PRO and XWEB500D PRO supervisory controllers, which sit on enterprise networks and front Modbus/RS485 field controllers running compressors, cooling fans, defrost heaters and temperature sensors across supermarket and distribution-center refrigeration units. Copeland shipped firmware version 1.13 for the Team82 issues.

A newer wave targets the XWEB EVO line with a different CVE pair. CVE-2026-124010 gives unauthenticated root on XWEB EVO devices, and securityonline.info reports it is already exploited in the wild alongside CVE-2026-124014, with proof-of-concept details published. That moves XWEB from a research finding to an actively used attack surface, and it means firmware 1.13, which addressed the Pro-family Team82 bugs, does not cover EVO units exposed to this chain.

The impact of either chain is physical-world control. A compromised supervisory controller bridges enterprise IT to every field device below it, so one foothold can manipulate cooling equipment across many stores at once. Watch for a Copeland advisory naming fixed EVO firmware versions, for the CVE-2026-124010 PoC circulating publicly, and for confirmed victim reports. Operators should inventory exposed XWEB interfaces, verify the EVO firmware level, and restrict the controllers' network reach.
