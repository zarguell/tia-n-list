Pwn2Own Ireland 2026 opened in Cork on October 6 with 21 of more than 60 registered entries taking the stage, and day one closed with 32 zero-days demonstrated and $388,500 paid across seven categories: mobile phones, printers, smart home, messaging, AI infrastructure, AI coding agents, and a new wellness healthcare category. Interrupt Labs, Ikotas Labs, and Nguyen Thanh Dat of Viettel Cyber Security all reached the Samsung Galaxy S26, while Vu Chi Thanh and Huynh Duc Tin of VinSOC topped the leaderboard with a seven-zero-day chain against a Philips Hue Bridge Pro and a five-zero-day chain against Oracle Autonomous AI Database, $40,000 each. Researchers also demoed LiteLLM zero-days, breached the Lexmark CX532adwe and Canon imageFORCE 1643F printers, took down the OpenAI Codex cloud agent with a single argument-injection bug, and recompromised a Sonos Era 300 with four vulnerabilities. The event runs through October 9 under an 80-entry cap ZDI set when it announced the contest on July 21.

These are lab demonstrations, not intrusions: no CVEs are assigned yet, no exploitation telemetry exists, and ZDI withholds technical detail while vendors patch. Two things the raw numbers hide matter more than the headline. Some of the 32 bugs were already known to vendors, so the day-one total overstates novelty, and the CVE ledger that follows, not the payout figure, is the real measure of new exposure. And the roughly $12,000 average per demonstrated bug is a public price floor for working exploits against phones, smart home hubs, printers, and now cloud AI services, which makes the target list a shopping list for anyone buying rather than researching. The Oracle Autonomous AI Database chain is the sharpest result: a managed cloud database fell to five bugs, showing the AI-era enterprise stack failing the way on-premises infrastructure always has.

No patches or workarounds are published yet, so defender work today is inventory and tracking, not remediation. Asset-list the exact demonstrated classes: LiteLLM gateways, which many organizations run as their LLM proxy; Oracle Autonomous AI Database instances; Philips Hue Bridge Pro and Sonos Era 300 devices on office or home networks; and Lexmark CX532adwe, Canon imageFORCE 1643F, and Brother MFC-L8970CDW printers. Segment those printers and smart home hubs off flat networks so a compromise cannot pivot. Follow ZDI's advisory feed for CVE assignments from this event and patch the named models within days of vendor release, because consumer devices like the Hue Bridge Pro sit unpatched in homes and offices for months after coordinated disclosure. Once ZDI publishes details, hunt through LiteLLM gateway and printer web interface logs for request patterns that do not match legitimate clients.

Two developments would change this assessment. Day two and day three results from the remaining entry pool will show whether AI infrastructure and the wellness category produce chains as expensive as VinSOC's, which would confirm ZDI's category bet as a map of where defenses are thinnest. And the CVE assignments for the Oracle and OpenAI Codex bugs, where cloud-side fixes land fastest, will reveal how many of the 32 were vendor-known. If a Galaxy S26 or Hue Bridge Pro bug surfaces in commercial exploit auctions or scanning traffic after details publish, this contest stops being a benchmark and becomes a threat.
