Pwn2Own Ireland 2026 is running in Cork from October 6 to 9 with seven target categories, including a new wellness category for healthcare devices, more than 60 total entries, and stricter entry requirements than prior editions. Day one closed with 32 unique zero-days exploited and $388,500 awarded across 21 attempts.

The Samsung Galaxy S26 was the headline target and fell multiple times, to Nguyen Thanh Dat of Viettel Cyber Security and to researchers from Interrupt Labs and Ikotas Labs. VinSOC's Vũ Chí Thành and Huỳnh Đức Tin topped the leaderboard with a $40,000 seven-zero-day chain against a Philips Hue Bridge Pro and a second $40,000 five-zero-day chain against the Oracle Autonomous AI Database. Researchers also demonstrated LiteLLM zero-days, hacked Lexmark CX532adwe and Canon imageFORCE 1643F printers, took down the OpenAI Codex cloud coding agent with a single argument-injection bug, and used four vulnerabilities to compromise a Sonos Era 300 again. Google Pixel 10 and Brother printer attempts failed. Organizers noted some exploited bugs were already known to vendors.

Day two carries 24 attempts with roughly $780,000 at stake, including three more Galaxy S26 attempts and five researchers queued against Home Assistant Green. The wellness and smart-home categories deserve the most attention from defenders: consumer-exposed healthcare and home automation devices historically have the weakest disclosure and patch timelines, and Pwn2Own bugs routinely reach CISA's KEV catalog within months.
