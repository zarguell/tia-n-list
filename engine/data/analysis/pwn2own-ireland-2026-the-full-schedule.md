Day One of Pwn2Own Ireland 2026 in Cork produced 32 zero-day exploits and $388,500 in awards across seven categories: mobile phones (iPhone 17, Galaxy S26, Pixel 10), printers, smart home, messaging apps, AI infrastructure, AI coding apps, and a new wellness healthcare device category. The Samsung Galaxy S26 flagship was hacked twice, by Interrupt Labs, Ikotas Labs, and Nguyen Thanh Dat of Viettel Cyber Security. Some bugs used were already known to the vendor, so not all 32 are new disclosures.

Top of the day's leaderboard: Vu Chi Thanh and Huynh Duc Tin of VinSOC earned $40,000 for a seven-zero-day chain against a Philips Hue Bridge Pro and another $40,000 for a five-zero-day chain against the Oracle Autonomous AI Database. Researchers also demoed LiteLLM zero-days, breached the Lexmark CX532adwe and Canon imageFORCE 1643F multifunction printers, took down the OpenAI Codex cloud coding agent with a single argument-injection bug, and recompromised a Sonos Era 300 with four vulnerabilities. Failures on the day: White Noise Club could not complete their Pixel 10 exploit in time, and Ikotas Labs missed the window on the Brother MFC-L8970CDW.

Two more contest days remain, with over 60 total entries registered. Watch the Master of Pwn standings, CVE assignments, and vendor patch releases that typically follow ZDI-coordinated disclosure. These are competition demos under lab conditions, not in-the-wild exploitation, though hardware like the Hue Bridge Pro and printers ends up in homes and offices unpatched for months.
