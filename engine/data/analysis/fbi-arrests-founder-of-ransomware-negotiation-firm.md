The FBI arrested Edward Dubrovsky, 54, a Canadian citizen and co-founder and former COO of Cypfer, a firm that specializes in negotiating ransomware payments for breach victims, with an affiliated role at Toronto firm CyberSteward. He was taken into custody Thursday in Pennsylvania, where he was attending the Cyber Risk Summit at the Loews Philadelphia Hotel (October 5-7), appeared in federal court in Philadelphia, and was ordered transferred to the Eastern District of Texas, where prosecutors are seeking detention. The complaint is under seal, but the docket lists conspiracy to threaten to impair the confidentiality of information with intent to extort money (18 U.S.C. 1030(a)(7)(B)) and Hobbs Act extortion conspiracy (18 U.S.C. 1951).

The arrest ties into the ShinyHunters investigation: FBI Director Kash Patel announced the bureau had caught "another suspected co-conspirator" of the group, and the New York Times described the suspect as a primary co-conspirator in the recent ShinyHunters hack of FBI Jobs systems, which exposed sensitive data on thousands of current and former agents. KrebsOnSecurity first identified Dubrovsky through sources, and Politico and CyberScoop confirmed the charges through court records. He had recently published a book on handling cyber extortion.

Why it matters: the arrest targets the extortion side of the ecosystem rather than the intrusion crew, and the suspect's day job sat unusually close to the victims' side of ransomware negotiations. Watch for the complaint being unsealed, which should clarify what conduct is alleged, for a detention hearing outcome in Texas, and for whether more arrests follow in the FBI Jobs case, since Patel's wording implies an ongoing crackdown and investigators have centralized the case at an FBI field office in Texas.
