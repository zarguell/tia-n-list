RIKEN's Center for Computational Science says an unauthorized party used the public dashboard that displays Fugaku's operational status to reach multiple internal servers between 10 and 13 November 2026, then planted malware aimed at user data. BleepingComputer reports that personal information and research data belonging to 4,597 users may have been exposed. RIKEN has not determined the exact intrusion method or attributed the attack, and says it found no evidence of further unauthorized access or theft of research results, though some stolen access keys were used in attempts to connect to other systems.

The operator shut Fugaku down on 14 November, disabled user login keys and told users to revoke and replace credentials they had stored on the machine. Fugaku entered shared use in March 2021 and runs at 537 petaflops, supporting drug discovery, severe-weather forecasting and simulations of airborne virus transmission. RIKEN also operates the 64 petaflops Rikyu system. The combination of research data access and credential reuse attempts makes the key rotation instruction the most actionable detail for researchers with accounts on either system.

Watch for RIKEN's final intrusion-path finding and any confirmation of what the malware actually read. The current account stops short of saying research results were stolen, so the 4,597 figure describes people whose data may have been accessible rather than a confirmed theft count.
