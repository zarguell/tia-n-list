RIKEN said an intruder gained unauthorized access to the operating system of Japan's Fugaku supercomputer between 10 and 13 November 2026. The reported entry point was the public dashboard displaying Fugaku's operational status, which the attacker used to reach multiple internal servers. Investigators say malware aimed at user data was planted on some of those servers. Account records and files from users' home directories were taken or exposed, putting personal information and research data for up to 4,597 users at risk.

RIKEN shut Fugaku down on 14 November, disabled user login keys and asked users to revoke and replace credentials stored on the machine. Some stolen access keys were used in attempts to connect to other systems, but the investigation did not find further unauthorized access or confirmed theft of research results. The intrusion method is still not pinned down and no actor has been named. The same center operates the 64 petaflops Rikyu system, while Fugaku is a 537 petaflops machine that entered shared use in March 2021 and supports drug discovery, severe-weather forecasting and airborne virus transmission simulations.

The exposure is significant because HPC accounts often carry keys into adjacent research networks, and the compromised material includes home-directory files rather than just service metadata. Watch for RIKEN's final intrusion-path disclosure, any attribution, confirmation of what was exfiltrated from home directories, and whether the credential replacement campaign uncovered follow-on access attempts.
