RIKEN's Fugaku supercomputer was breached between November 10 and 13 when an unauthorized party used the public dashboard showing Fugaku's operational status to reach multiple internal servers. RIKEN had not determined the exact access method. Its November 16 notice put personal information and research data of up to 4,597 users at risk, including account records and files from users' home directories. Investigators found malware planted on some servers that targeted user data, and said some stolen access keys were used in attempts to connect to other systems. No theft of research results was confirmed, and no actor has been named.

RIKEN took Fugaku offline on November 14, disabled user login keys and told users to revoke and replace credentials stored on the machine. Fugaku entered shared use in March 2021 and is rated at 537 petaflops, with workloads including drug discovery, severe-weather forecasting and simulations of airborne virus transmission. The same center operates the 64-petaflops Rikyu system used for AI scientific research.

What to watch: whether RIKEN identifies the intrusion path through the status dashboard, expands the scope beyond home directories, or links the key reuse attempts to further systems. The incident is a reminder that ancillary public-facing status infrastructure can become the entry point to a high-value research environment. Other reporting in the same window described the breach as coming through a basic status page and put the affected population near 4,500 researchers.
