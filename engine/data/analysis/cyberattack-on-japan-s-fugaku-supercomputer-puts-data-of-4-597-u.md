RIKEN says an intruder reached internal servers linked to the Fugaku supercomputer between November 10 and 13, 2026, through the public dashboard that displays Fugaku's operational status. The exact access method is still unknown. Investigators found that the intruder planted malware aimed at user data, and a November 16 notice put the potential exposure at 4,597 users. Account records and files from users' home directories were among the material taken or potentially taken. RIKEN has not attributed the intrusion.

Fugaku was shut down on November 14 while the investigation proceeded. Login keys were disabled, and users were told to revoke and replace credentials they had stored on the system. RIKEN also reported that some of the accessed keys were used in attempts to connect to other systems, but said it found no evidence of further unauthorized access or theft of research results. The machine is a 537 petaflops system used for drug discovery, severe-weather forecasting and simulations of airborne virus transmission; RIKEN's 64 petaflops Rikyu supercomputer is operated at the same center.

The operational detail to watch is how the public status dashboard became a path to internal servers, since that would explain why credential rotation alone may not close the entry point. The stolen key material creates a longer tail for downstream access attempts, especially for users who reused keys outside Fugaku. Further RIKEN findings on the intrusion method, the malware implanted on the servers, and whether research data left the network would determine whether the current 4,597-user scope holds.
