Japan's Digital Agency disclosed a breach exposing personal data of roughly 246,000 government employees and associated individuals. The attacker gained initial access by exploiting a medium-severity vulnerability in a VPN device used by the Government Solution Service (GSS). The agency detected suspicious large-scale file access on June 25 and traced the intrusion to a maintenance staff account on July 9, at which point the compromised account was suspended and the device was isolated from the network.

The exposed data includes approximately 236,000 names, 231,000 email addresses, 94,000 phone numbers, and 1,000 physical addresses. Affected individuals are government employees, public officials, and people associated with GSS-connected agencies. The agency confirmed the general public's data was not affected. The specific VPN product and CVE have not been disclosed, but the agency rated the vulnerability as medium severity and confirmed it was not a zero-day.

This breach underscores the persistent risk of VPN appliances as initial access vectors for government networks. The gap between initial detection on June 25 and containment on July 9 suggests the attacker had roughly two weeks of access before being cut off. The investigation is ongoing, and the agency has not yet clarified whether data was exfiltrated or merely accessed.
