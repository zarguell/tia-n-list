Japan's Digital Agency disclosed a breach exposing approximately 246,000 records of personal information belonging to government employees, public officials, and associated business contacts. The attacker gained initial access by exploiting a medium-severity vulnerability in a VPN device used by the Government Solution Service (GSS), a shared infrastructure platform. The agency detected the intrusion on June 25 after observing large-scale file access from a maintenance staff member's account, and confirmed the VPN exploit on July 9.

The exposed data includes 236,000 names, 231,000 email addresses, 94,000 telephone numbers, and roughly 1,000 physical addresses. Notably absent are My Number identification numbers, bank account details, and pension numbers. The agency says it has found no evidence of misuse but warns of elevated phishing and impersonation risks. The VPN product and specific CVE remain undisclosed, though the agency characterized the flaw as not a zero-day.

The incident highlights the ongoing risk to government shared-services infrastructure. GSS handles digital transformation for multiple Japanese government entities, meaning a single VPN compromise can cascade across agencies. The two-month gap between detection and public disclosure, attributed to investigation complexity, will likely draw scrutiny from Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission, which was notified on July 15. Organizations running shared government VPN appliances should treat this as a prompt to audit access logs and VPN firmware versions.
