Japan's Digital Agency confirmed a breach exposing approximately 246,000 sets of personal information belonging to government employees. The attacker gained initial access through a vulnerability in a VPN device used by the Government Solution Service (GSS). The investigation began June 25 after detecting large-scale file access from a maintenance staff account. On July 9, the agency identified the VPN intrusion and severed the compromised device from the network.

Exposed data includes 236,000 names, 231,000 email addresses, 94,000 telephone numbers, and roughly 1,000 physical addresses. The breach affected government employees, public officials, and associated businesses using the GSS system. General public data was not impacted. The agency rated the VPN vulnerability as medium severity and confirmed it was not a zero-day.

The incident highlights ongoing risks in government managed-service infrastructure. VPN appliances remain a primary initial access vector, particularly when maintenance accounts have broader permissions than necessary. Japan's Digital Agency, established in 2021 to digitize government services, has faced scrutiny over its security posture. The delay between detection (June 25) and public disclosure (September) is notable, though within typical incident response timelines for government breaches.
