U.S. federal investigators pursuing the ShinyHunters extortion group have identified executives at two ransomware negotiation firms who allegedly maintained unusually close ties to the criminals, with one U.S.-based executive suspected of helping ShinyHunters operate a site that pressured victims into paying. The reporting, based on sources close to the investigation, says the executives often knew about new ShinyHunters victims before the victims themselves did. ShinyHunters has claimed dozens of major breaches since 2019, extorted more than $70 million this year, and in late September hacked the FBI recruitment site fbijobs.gov, stealing medical and psychological records, Social Security numbers, addresses, and unit information.

The case centers on a Canadian cybersecurity executive, Edward Dubrovsky, arrested by the FBI on October 9, and a Dutch hacker referred to as Van der Stap, whose Signal messages reportedly show frequent contact with Dubrovsky and at least one executive at a large U.S. consulting firm. The original French members of ShinyHunters were arrested or jailed over time, and control of the brand later passed to "Rey," identified as a teenage hacker from Amman, Jordan.

What to watch: whether prosecutors file charges against the negotiation-firm executives, how the investigation affects trust in ransomware negotiation services, and any further victim disclosures tied to the fbijobs.gov breach. Organizations should treat negotiator relationships as a supply-chain risk and require transparency about law-enforcement contact.
