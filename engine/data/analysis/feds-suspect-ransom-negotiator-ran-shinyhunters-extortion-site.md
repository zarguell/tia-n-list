U.S. federal investigators pursuing ShinyHunters have identified executives at two ransomware negotiation firms with unusually close ties to the group, and one U.S. executive allegedly helped operate an extortion site used to pressure victims. The group, whose brand passed from original French operators to a Jordan-based actor known as "Rey," has claimed dozens of major breaches since 2019 and the FBI states it has extorted more than $70 million from victims so far this year. In late September the group compromised the FBI recruitment site fbijobs.gov and threatened to publish stolen files that included medical and psychological records, Social Security numbers, home addresses, and employee unit assignments.

The suspected collaboration between a professional ransom negotiator and the extortion operation undermines the assumption that negotiation firms are neutral intermediaries. If confirmed, victims who engaged these firms may have been directed toward a group they believed they were negotiating against, which changes how defenders should evaluate negotiation providers. This is not abstract: over $70 million in confirmed extortion this year, plus the FBI breach exposing sensitive government employee data, shows the group remains active and well-resourced.

Defenders should treat any engagement with the named negotiation firms as a potential conflict-of-interest risk until the investigation produces indictments or public identification of the executives involved. Monitor for updates linking the U.S. executive to specific extortion site infrastructure and for FBI releases about the fbijobs.gov breach scope. Network hunts should focus on the same victim-facing infrastructure ShinyHunters has used for extortion messaging, though no new IoCs were published in the events themselves.

Watch for formal indictments against the negotiation firm executives and any disclosure by the FBI about whether stolen recruitment records have been published or traded. A confirmation of the negotiator's direct operational role would likely trigger regulatory scrutiny of the ransomware negotiation industry.
