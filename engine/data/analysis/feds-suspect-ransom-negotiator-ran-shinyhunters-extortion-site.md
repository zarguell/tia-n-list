U.S. federal investigators pursuing the ShinyHunters group, which recently stole sensitive data from the FBI, have identified executives at two ransomware negotiation firms who maintained unusually close ties to the extortion group. Sources say one U.S.-based executive helped ShinyHunters operate a website that pressured victims into paying ransoms. ShinyHunters, originally run by French cybercriminals, passed to "Rey," a teenage hacker from Amman, Jordan, and has extorted more than $70 million this year, including a hack of the FBI recruitment site fbijobs.gov.

The allegations raise serious conflict-of-interest and legal questions about the ransomware negotiation industry, where intermediaries may be incentivized to prolong or amplify extortion rather than resolve it. If substantiated, the case could reshape how organizations select and oversee incident-response negotiators.

Organizations should vet ransomware negotiators for independence, document decision chains during incidents, and cooperate with law enforcement when extortion involves known threat groups.
