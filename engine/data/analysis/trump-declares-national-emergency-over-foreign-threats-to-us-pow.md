President Trump issued Executive Order 14420 on August 27, 2026, declaring a national emergency to address vulnerabilities in the US bulk power system from foreign-supplied electrical equipment. The order targets transmission lines rated 69 kilovolts and above, covering transformers, inverters, energy storage systems, industrial control systems (RTUs, PLCs), and associated firmware and software. Any acquisition, import, or installation of foreign-produced bulk-power equipment involving designated entities after August 26, 2026 is prohibited.

The order cites rapid growth in data centers, AI infrastructure, advanced manufacturing, and defense production as amplifying dependence on grid reliability. Officials note that foreign supply chain disruptions or embedded backdoors for remote access pose direct sabotage risks. While the order does not name specific countries, its structure mirrors a 2020 Trump-era bulk-power order that led to a DOE prohibition order targeting Chinese entities, later revoked under Biden.

For equipment already installed, the Energy Department can mandate security controls including identification, isolation, monitoring, disconnection, or replacement of components. The order explicitly excludes local distribution facilities, focusing only on bulk transmission infrastructure. This shifts the policy focus from active intrusion detection (such as Volt Typhoon campaigns) toward upstream supply chain risk mitigation and embedded hardware backdoor prevention.
