Anthropic's Frontier Red Team published an assessment of GLM-5.3, the new open-weight model from China's Zhipu AI (Z.ai), and its core finding is that a frontier-class autonomous exploit capability is now freely downloadable. On ExploitBench the model built working end-to-end exploits for Chrome V8 bugs in 50 of 410 attempts, close to Anthropic's own safeguarded Mythos Preview at 56 of 410, while earlier open models like GLM-5.2 scored near zero. In researcher-driven sessions, GLM-5.3 found several previously unknown vulnerabilities in a browser JavaScript engine and chained them into a working drive-by page that stole an SSH private key, and GLM-5.3-Flash turned the known Chrome flaw CVE-2026-11645 plus a second flaw into a reliable ARM64 exploit chain that bypassed pointer authentication, with 20 minutes of human attention and 8 hours of model time at about 20 dollars of API cost.

The safeguard picture is the sharper point. GLM-5.3 refuses overtly harmful requests out of the box, but Anthropic measured engagement rates of 64 percent with a simple false red-team cover story, 92 percent with prefilled thinking tokens, and 100 percent on an abliterated copy that stripped refusals almost entirely (over 90 percent refusal down to 3 to 12 percent across three benchmarks) with no meaningful capability loss. Abliteration took their inexperienced team roughly 2,200 GPU hours and 4,400 dollars, and third parties published abliterated weights within days of release. None of these techniques moved safeguarded Claude models in the same tests.

NIST's CAISI had already called GLM-5.3 the most cyber-capable open-weight model released to date in its September 17 assessment, placing it about four months behind the US frontier. The combination of near-frontier exploit capability, weights anyone can modify, and trivially bypassed guardrails is a step change in what low-skill attackers can buy off the shelf, and it puts open-weight release policy back at the center of cyber policy debates.

Watch for successors from Zhipu and rival Chinese labs, for the first incident reports attributing real-world exploitation to GLM-5.3-generated tooling, and for how defenders respond to Anthropic's argument that vetted access to frontier offensive capability needs to widen so defenders are not outgunned.
