APT29, also tracked as Cozy Bear, ran a mobile watering-hole campaign that compromised legitimate websites and the Ukrainian analytics tracker Plerdy to serve n-day exploit chains to iOS and Android visitors. On iPhones running iOS 16.7, the actor chained CVE-2025-43529 and CVE-2024-27834 to steal cookies; iOS 18.5 devices received a DarkSword-based chain using CVE-2025-43529, CVE-2026-20700, CVE-2025-14174, CVE-2025-43510 and CVE-2025-43520 to collect SMS, iMessage, WhatsApp, Telegram and Keychain data. Android phones running Chrome 139 through 145, except 143, were hit through CVE-2026-3910 in Chrome and CVE-2025-27038 in the Adreno graphics driver. These are n-day flaws that vendors had already patched, so the campaign succeeds only where devices remain unpatched.

A successful compromise gives the actor full device data, including message databases, the Keychain, cookies and active sessions, which means persistent access to the victim's personal and corporate accounts even after the browser is closed. The exposed population is every iOS 16.7 and iOS 18.5 user and every Android Chrome 139-145 user who visits a hijacked legitimate site, which the reporting places in the thousands. This story outranks a routine vulnerability disclosure because delivery is indiscriminate: victims are infected through ordinary browsing of trusted sites and a trusted analytics script, not through a phishing link they could be trained to avoid, and CISA had already added CVE-2024-27834 to its KEV catalog on 2026-11-04, so patching lag translates directly into compromise.

Defenders should apply the Apple updates that remediate CVE-2024-27834 (CISA KEV, added 2026-11-04), CVE-2025-43529, CVE-2026-20700, CVE-2025-14174, CVE-2025-43510 and CVE-2025-43520 on iOS and iPadOS, move Chrome past version 145 to close CVE-2026-3910, and apply the Adreno driver fix for CVE-2025-27038. Where patching is not immediate, restrict mobile devices from resolving or connecting to the compromised analytics tracker Plerdy and to the delivery domains documented by Sekoia, including theowo[.]club, static.flux-telemetry[.]com, america250[.]holiday and adsprism-stat[.]com. Hunt proxy and DNS logs for connections to those domains and for WebKit or Chrome renderer exploitation on unpatched iOS and Android devices, and alert on any KEV-listed CVE being triggered in the fleet.

Watch for confirmation that APT29 is exploiting any of these chains on fully patched iOS and Android builds, which would move the campaign from n-day to zero-day and sharply raise severity. Also watch for Apple and Google fixed-version advisories and further CISA KEV additions for CVE-2025-43529 and the other chained flaws, which would firm up the remediation baseline and the compliance deadline.
