ClickFix social engineering campaigns are increasingly abusing legitimate services to establish persistent access on victim machines. DarkReporting documented two related developments: a multi-hop Google redirect phishing technique that chains through legitimate Google infrastructure to evade detection, and ClickFix campaigns that leverage trusted platforms for command-and-control and persistence.

The core ClickFix technique trick users into copying and running malicious PowerShell or shell commands, often disguised as CAPTCHA verification or software installation steps. In the newer variants, attackers route their phishing through legitimate Google redirect services, making the initial URL appear trustworthy to both users and security filters. Once executed, the malicious commands establish footholds using legitimate cloud services rather than traditional C2 infrastructure.

What makes these campaigns notable is the abuse of trust relationships. By routing through Google redirects and persisting via legitimate services, the attacks blend with normal enterprise traffic. Security teams relying on domain reputation or URL blocklists will miss these entirely. The pattern signals a broader shift toward living-off-the-land techniques in the initial access phase, where the infrastructure itself is not malicious but is weaponized through abuse.
