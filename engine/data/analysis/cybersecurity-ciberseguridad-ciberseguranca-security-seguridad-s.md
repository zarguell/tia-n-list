Dark Reading documented two social engineering techniques that abuse legitimate online services to establish persistent access to target environments. The first is a multi-hop Google redirect phishing campaign that chains multiple legitimate Google services to obscure the final phishing destination, making detection by URL-based security tools more difficult. The second involves ClickFix campaigns using legitimate cloud services for command-and-control, blending malicious traffic with normal business usage.

Both techniques share a common theme: they weaponize trust in established platforms. The Google redirect approach exploits how security tools often whitelist Google domains, while ClickFix leverages legitimate service infrastructure for persistence. These methods are particularly effective against organizations that rely on domain-based allowlists or have limited visibility into how legitimate services can be abused.

For defenders, the takeaway is that blocking known-bad domains is insufficient when attackers operate entirely within trusted infrastructure. Behavioral detection, monitoring for unusual patterns in legitimate service usage, and user awareness of social engineering lures that arrive through trusted platforms are the more relevant controls.
