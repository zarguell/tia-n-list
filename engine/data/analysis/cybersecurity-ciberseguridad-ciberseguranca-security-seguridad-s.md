ClickFix is delivery by self-inflicted execution: the victim copies a command from a web page and runs it personally in Windows Run, PowerShell, or macOS Terminal to fix a fake error, pass a fake CAPTCHA, or install software that does not exist. The clearest instance in this story is PasteSwitch, where Hudson Rock and ADAMnetworks found the verified u/hbomax Reddit account hijacked to push 108 malicious advertisements in about 48 hours across five lure groups, funneling users to hbomaxx[.]us and a paste-and-run prompt: curl piped to zsh on macOS delivering MacSync, AMOS Helper, and fake wallet apps; MSHTA and PowerShell on Windows delivering Amatera Stealer, which validates stolen credentials manually and disguises its C2 as Facebook connections. CTM360's report, now echoed across multiple outlets, cataloged roughly 17,000 ClickFix URLs living on otherwise trusted websites and describes the technique as a leading initial access vector, and the feed shows the current frontline is compromised legitimate infrastructure: hacked Ukrainian business sites serving fake Cloudflare verification pages, more than 5,400 hacked small-business sites storing payloads in BNB Smart Chain contracts, and a Brevo CDN compromise.

The numbers argue this is not a niche trick. Hudson Rock's original ErrTraffic disclosure recorded a 58.8 percent conversion rate on the lure dashboard, twenty downloads from thirty-four views, and Sekoia's research found the framework operating as subscription malware-as-a-service at $380 a month with C2 resolved through EtherHiding smart contracts on the Polygon blockchain. Malvertising is normally measured in fractions of a percent, so a lure that converts at tens of percent while executing inside system utilities the browser never observes changes the economics of stolen credentials. The event list presents the HBO Max ads, the Brevo CDN incident, and the Ukrainian sites as separate stories, but they are one economy: infostealers harvest CMS credentials, those credentials inject new ClickFix lures into trusted sites, and the new lures harvest again.

Hunt at the paste boundary. Enable PowerShell ScriptBlock logging and alert on the ErrTraffic command shape Sekoia published: ScriptBlockText beginning with "<" and containing "#>", "-bxor[int][char]$", and "[convert]::ToInt32". Correlate browser-launched spawns of mshta.exe and powershell.exe, hunt curl piped into zsh on macOS, and flag egress to blockchain RPC endpoints followed by a PowerShell launch. On WordPress and Ghost estates, inspect for mu-plugin and injected JavaScript artifacts, patch Ghost against CVE-2026-26980, and rotate administrator credentials. Turn on the friction that now exists: the Terminal paste warning in macOS Tahoe 26.4, Opera's Paste Protect, and the ACSC's advisory on ClickFix-delivered Vidar as help-desk awareness material.

Two developments would change the assessment. Microsoft's TerminalFix warning showed ClickFix payloads deploying reverse tunnels after the paste, which converts a one-shot stealer into durable interactive access; if that variant spreads, this stops being a malvertising problem and becomes an intrusion problem. And if blockchain-resolved C2 keeps spreading past ErrTraffic into supply-chain positions like the Brevo CDN incident, domain takedowns and blocklists lose their grip, because the pointer lives on-chain.
