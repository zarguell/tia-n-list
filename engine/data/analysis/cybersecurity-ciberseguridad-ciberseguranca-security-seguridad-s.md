ClickFix social engineering campaigns are escalating through abuse of legitimate infrastructure. Dark Reading documented two converging techniques: multi-hop Google redirect phishing chains that route through Google's own redirect services to appear trustworthy, and ClickFix campaigns that persist via legitimate cloud platforms rather than traditional command-and-control servers.

The core ClickFix mechanic is unchanged: users are tricked into copying and executing malicious PowerShell or shell commands, typically disguised as CAPTCHA verification or software setup steps. The newer variants chain through Google redirect URLs, making the initial link pass both user scrutiny and automated domain-reputation checks. Once executed, the payload establishes persistence through legitimate cloud services, blending with normal enterprise traffic.

Ars Technica separately reported that ClickFix attacks targeting both Windows and macOS are going viral, driven by the technique's simplicity and the friction users face when trying to complete legitimate tasks. The pattern across these reports signals a broader shift toward living-off-the-land initial access, where the infrastructure itself is clean but weaponized through abuse. Security teams relying on URL blocklists or domain reputation will miss these entirely.
