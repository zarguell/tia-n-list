The ransomware group N0n claimed responsibility for the Hunter Neurology breach on October 13, 2026, with the claim published October 15 at 00:05:42Z. The target is hunterneurology.com.au, an Australian neurology practice. The attacker statement lists 15,252 patient images and scans, 11,819 letters and reports, 439 laboratory results, 839 patient records system files, and four complete weekly backups of the practice medical records system. Protected health information includes patient names, dates of birth, and clinical data, and the claim is marked NOT PAID - EXPOSED.

A successful release would expose tens of thousands of Australian neurology patients; 15,252 scans plus 11,819 reports represent over 27,000 discrete clinical artifacts, making this exposure larger than a typical small-practice leak. The inclusion of four complete weekly backups indicates the attacker had sustained access to the practice's record environment, not just a single file share, which raises the likelihood that the dataset is complete and that restoration from backup alone will not erase the exfiltration. The third-party host statement from Hunter Neurology suggests the intrusion may have originated through a technology provider rather than direct practice infrastructure, which widens the exposure footprint to other clients of that same vendor.

Defenders should treat the IoCs and telemetry related to N0n as active: hunt for backup-access events on practice and vendor systems, verify backup immutability, and restrict network paths to medical-record hosts. Monitor dark-web and leak-site channels for publication confirmation, since the Mastodon follow-up notes the hackers have not yet published but are expected to. If publication occurs, the dataset will likely include the four weekly backups mentioned in the claim, so assume full clinical history exposure rather than partial file loss.

Watch for vendor confirmation of the third-party intrusion path and any N0n publication countdown updates; either development would shift remediation from containment to breach-notification obligations.
