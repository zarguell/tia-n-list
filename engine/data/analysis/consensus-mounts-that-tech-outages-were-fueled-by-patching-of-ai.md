On 17 November CNN reported that NSANet went down while the NSA called it temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance; Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told Fox Business the outage came from applying a patch for major PHP flaws found the previous month, and CNN tied those to CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, the php2shell remote code execution bugs that AI tools helped researchers find. CISA added both CVEs to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 26 October after AWS reported exploitation before disclosure. The 18 November reports from the Washington Sun and implicator.ai elevate that single attribution into a claim that Wall Street executives and national security officials told Congress this week's outages were fueled by AI-discovered vulnerability patching, yet none carries a CVE, patch identifier, change record or affected-version list.

That framing is dangerous because the three named incidents do not share a mechanism. The FAA's own account describes legacy TDM instability when the FTI network saw about 1,000 connection attempts every 4 to 5 seconds, with 17 of 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers affected, a new L3Harris router at El Dorado, Arkansas, and a ground stop from about 3:30 p.m. to 8:15 p.m. ET on 13 November; CNBC counted more than 10,000 disrupted flights and about 1.5 million passengers. Microsoft's preliminary report blames a gateway overload, roughly 14 percent lost capacity and a latent bug from July, and Microsoft said the incident was not related to cyber or AI agents. Palo Alto counted more than 22.6 million potentially vulnerable PHP instances, so the patch pressure is real, but only the NSA failure has a direct patch attribution on the record. Calling this a consensus turns one agency's patch failure into an explanation for aviation and cloud outages that their own operators attributed to routers and gateway capacity, sending defenders toward a php2shell rollback in systems where no PHP patch was deployed.

Patch PHP to 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 or 8.2.35 as CISA directed, take affected web services offline where immediate patching is impossible, and prioritize mod_php, PHP-FPM, php-cgi, FrankenPHP and LiteSpeed deployments. Hunt with CISA's SIGMA rule across relevant PHP log sources for activity from 20 October onward, since AWS saw exploit attempts before disclosure and Cloudflare counted millions by the evening of 21 October, and remove internet-facing PAN-OS management interfaces where chaining with a Linux privilege-escalation flaw has been reported. For the outage side, test the record against the stated causes: L3Harris router work at El Dorado and the FTI traffic flood for the FAA, and the three-factor Microsoft sequence, rather than treating CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 as a universal rollback target.

The assessment changes if the hearing record or a follow-up agency statement names the exact patch, version and deployer for each disruption, or if Microsoft's final post-incident report revises the July gateway bug account to include a PHP change. Watch for evidence that the 13 November FAA ground stop involved php2shell rather than the L3Harris router and legacy TDM flood; absent that, the consensus stays a press narrative rather than a defender's causal map.
