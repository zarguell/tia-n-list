Officials and reporters are converging on rushed patching of AI-discovered bugs as a factor in a week of service failures. The Washington Sun reported that Wall Street executives and the NSA told members of Congress the outages were caused by AI-driven patches, and separate testimony coverage from implicator.ai names the NSA network failure, the FAA ground stop and Microsoft among the incidents discussed. Fortune described the PHP2Shell pair, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, as found with help from GPT-5.6 Sol and other AI tools, with several research groups independently finding them in August before coordinated fixes. Cloudflare saw tens of thousands of exploit attempts within 45 minutes of the October 20 reports, hundreds of thousands within two hours and millions by the evening of October 21; AWS said attempts appeared before disclosure.

The underlying outages do not all have the same explanation. PBS reported governments patching overnight after an FBI warning, with limited disruption or precautionary downtime in Britain, Finland and Japan and advice to take unpatchable sites offline. TechCrunch reported that Microsoft's roughly 18 hour outage began in Outlook and Teams and spread to Copilot, Defender and Intune; Microsoft said there was no evidence of malicious activity and no link to cyber or AI agents. Its preliminary report named a Teams gateway failure, about 14 percent of that gateway's capacity already out of service, and a latent bug from July, with no rollback possible. CNBC reported the November 13 FAA stop lasted more than four hours after telecom routers were overloaded, disrupting more than 10,000 flights and around 1.5 million passengers, with DHS seeing no indication of a cyberattack.

The new Italian post notes that officials said there was no proof of an attack before the patching explanation circulated. The story is therefore about an attribution fight: congressional and press accounts emphasize patch pressure from AI-found flaws, while Microsoft and DHS point to gateway and router failures. Watch the promised Microsoft final report, further congressional testimony and whether any incident is tied to a specific patch rather than to the general patching surge.
