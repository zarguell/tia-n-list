Wall Street executives and National Security Agency officials told members of Congress that recent widespread outages were tied to a surge in patching of AI-discovered vulnerabilities, according to Reuters and the Washington Sun. The same reporting does not erase other root causes. Microsoft said its Outlook and Teams outage was not related to cyber activity or AI agents, and its preliminary report pointed to a Teams gateway failure, about 14 percent of that gateway already out of service and a latent bug from July; Kentik measured roughly 18 hours of downtime. DHS officials said the FAA ground stop appeared mechanically driven after routers were overloaded, with more than 10,000 flights disrupted.

The AI-discovery concern is grounded in the PHP2Shell disclosures. Fortune reported that GPT-5.6 Sol and other tools helped find CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, with several research groups independently finding them in August before a coordinated fix. Cloudflare saw tens of thousands of exploit attempts within 45 minutes of the October 20 reports, hundreds of thousands within two hours and millions by the evening of October 21. AWS bulletin 2026-143 warned that customers running affected PHP versions could face remote code execution in the worker context, and PBS reported officials in Britain, Finland and Japan managing patch windows and precautionary downtime.

The outage list spans Microsoft 365, the FAA, banks, United Airlines, Apple and an NSA classified network. Bank regulators are described as alert to patch-driven risk. The useful distinction is between discovery speed and deployment quality: AI tools shortened the time to find native-code bugs, while rushed or uneven patching created operational exposure that looked like an attack even where officials saw no malicious activity.

Watch for final post-incident reports that separate patch deployment failures from independent service faults, for regulator guidance on emergency patch windows, and for more cases where AI-assisted discovery is credited. The Microsoft and FAA statements mean the congressional account should be read as a claim about a trend, not a confirmed single cause for every outage.
