Russian cloud provider Yandex confirmed that drone strikes destroyed two data centers and degraded a third, with Sasovo (October 8) and Vladimir (October 11) fully offline and Kaluga (October 9) partially disabled. This is not a software vulnerability; there are no CVE identifiers. The mechanism is physical destruction of infrastructure attributed to Ukrainian forces, which has cut off customer access to cloud-stored files and degraded ride-hailing, delivery, streaming, and business cloud services that the company describes on its status page.

Yandex is referred to as "Russia's Google" and serves both government and private-sector organizations, meaning a sustained outage disrupts national-level operational capacity rather than a single vendor's uptime. The event carries weight because EU MEP Tomáš Zdechovský drew a direct line between these outages and a sudden drop in communication from far-right accounts in Germany, suggesting the strikes may have collateral intelligence effects beyond the targeted infrastructure. With no injuries reported but three separate facilities hit across four days, the exposure footprint is broader than a localized data-center failure and touches much of Russia's internet backbone.

Defenders relying on Yandex cloud or connected services should treat this as a continuity event rather than a patchable bug: verify backup locations outside Yandex-controlled regions, expect degraded performance from the Kaluga site, and monitor the vendor status page for repair timelines. Hunting should focus on service-level telemetry rather than endpoint IoCs, since the threat is kinetic rather than malware. Watch for further strikes on remaining Russian data centers and any confirmation from Kyiv regarding the scope of the drone campaign.
