Revolut confirmed it disclosed sensitive customer data to fraudsters who impersonated government officials using a legitimate government email domain. The breach exposed passports, selfies, and Bitcoin transaction histories. Attackers sent data requests from a real government domain, exploiting Revolut's identity verification processes to obtain customer records without triggering standard fraud alerts.

The incident, first reported by TechCrunch, affects an unknown number of customers. Revolut's response focused on the robustness of its identity verification procedures rather than the scope of data exposed. Meterpreter.org provided additional technical detail confirming the use of a legitimate government email infrastructure for the social engineering attack.

This breach highlights a systemic weakness in how financial institutions handle government data requests. When fraudsters can obtain records by spoofing official channels, the problem extends beyond Revolut to any institution that processes such requests. Organizations should implement multi-factor verification for data disclosure requests, including callback verification to known government contacts, and audit logs of all customer data access against external requests.
