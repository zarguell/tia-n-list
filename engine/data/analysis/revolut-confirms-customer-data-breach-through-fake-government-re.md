Revolut confirmed a customer data breach in which attackers impersonated law enforcement to obtain user data through fake government requests. The fintech company said it notified affected customers and alerted the relevant authorities. The attackers used social engineering against Revolut's processes for responding to legitimate legal data requests, tricking the company into handing over customer information.

This is a growing pattern across the financial sector. Attackers are targeting the gap between legal compliance obligations and verification procedures. Fake government data requests bypass many of the technical controls that companies deploy because they exploit human trust in legal authority rather than technical vulnerabilities. Revolut did not disclose the number of affected customers or the specific data types exposed, but the breach was confirmed through TechCrunch reporting.

The incident underscores that social engineering of compliance teams is now a standard attack vector. Financial institutions need to strengthen verification procedures for data requests, including callback procedures to known law enforcement contacts and multi-party approval for sensitive disclosures. Customers of affected fintech platforms should monitor for identity theft and consider credit freezes.
