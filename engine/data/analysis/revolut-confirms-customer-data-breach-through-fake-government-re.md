Revolut confirmed a data breach on September 12, 2026, after attackers used fraudulent requests sent from a legitimate government agency email domain to obtain customer data. The exposed information included names, birth dates, postal and email addresses, phone numbers, copies of passports and driver's licenses, and potentially verification selfies, account statements, and transaction histories. Revolut has over 80 million customers globally and operates as a bank in more than 30 countries.

The attack exploited social engineering through domain spoofing rather than a technical vulnerability. The unauthorized party submitted requests to Revolut's data disclosure processes using what appeared to be a legitimate government email address. Revolut blocked the email address after discovery and notified the affected government agency, law enforcement, and financial regulators. The company stated that Revolut systems and customer funds remain unaffected.

Crypto security researcher ZachXBT published details about Revolut's notification email, suggesting the incident targeted high net worth users. The breach comes as Revolut weighs a potential IPO that could value the company at $200 billion, up from its $75 billion private valuation in November 2025. Organizations handling sensitive customer data should review their government data request verification procedures and implement additional identity confirmation steps before disclosing information.
