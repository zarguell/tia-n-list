Revolut confirmed it disclosed sensitive customer data to an unauthorized third party after attackers sent fraudulent requests from a legitimate government agency email domain. The exposed information includes birth dates, postal and email addresses, phone numbers, passport and driver's license copies, and potentially verification selfies, account statements, and transaction histories. Revolut characterized the impacted group as "limited" but declined to specify the number of affected customers or which government agency's domain was spoofed.

The attack vector is notable: social engineering via impersonation of official channels rather than a technical exploit. Crypto security researcher ZachXBT, who surfaced the customer notification, indicated the campaign targeted high net worth users specifically. Revolut said it blocked the unauthorized email address, notified the relevant government agency, law enforcement, and financial regulators, and that its systems and customer funds were not compromised.

The breach arrives at a sensitive moment for Revolut. The company recently received conditional approval from the U.S. Office of the Comptroller of the Currency to establish a national bank, with a planned launch in the first half of 2027. It is also reportedly weighing a public listing that could value the firm at up to $200 billion, up from its $75 billion private valuation in November. With 80 million-plus customers and banking operations in over 30 countries, the incident underscores the risk that even well-resourced financial institutions face from targeted social engineering against compliance and legal request workflows.
