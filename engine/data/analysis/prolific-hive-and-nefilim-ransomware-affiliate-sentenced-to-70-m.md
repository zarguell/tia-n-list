Ukrainian national Artem Aleksandrovych Stryzhak, 36, was sentenced to 70 months in US prison for conspiracy to commit computer fraud tied to ransomware operations. According to the event content (rss:c05f23439f0909e5 and updates), Stryzhak joined the Nefilim ransomware group in June 2021 as an affiliate, operating through October 2021. He agreed to pay Nefilim administrators 20% of ransom proceeds and used the malware against organizations in the US and abroad, including an unnamed oil and gas transportation company. He was also an early affiliate of Hive ransomware, which hit over 1,500 victims worldwide between June 2021 and January 2023; the documents show he used Hive against seven victim organizations (a dental office, a healthcare provider, and law firms). Some victims paid ransoms and received decryption keys. Stryzhak was arrested in Spain in June 2024, extradited to the US in April 2025, and pleaded guilty in December 2025. The co-conspirator Volodymyr Tymoshchuk remains at large with an $11 million reward.

This is significant not for a new vulnerability but for law-enforcement attribution and the documented scale of the Nefilim/Hive affiliate model. The event notes that Nefilim attacks caused millions in losses from extortion and network damage, and that authorities identified approximately 74 cryptocurrency wallets tied to about 800 victims in a related Madagascar-based NFT-scam scheme (though that scheme is separate, it shows the broader criminal ecosystem). The sentencing reinforces that international cooperation (Spain to US extradition) remains active against ransomware affiliates.

What to watch: whether the $11 million reward for Tymoshchuk produces leads, and whether the sentencing triggers further cooperation from other affiliates. The Hive/Nefilim operation is largely dismantled, but the affiliate model persists across other ransomware groups. There is no new CVE or exploitation claim here; the value is in tracking the legal aftermath of a high-impact ransomware campaign.
