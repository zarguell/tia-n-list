Artem Aleksandrovych Stryzhak, a 35-to-36-year-old Ukrainian national, was sentenced to 70 months in prison in the United States for conspiracy to commit computer fraud after pleading guilty in December 2025. He was arrested in Spain in June 2024 and extradited to the U.S. on April 30, 2025. Stryzhak joined the Nefilim ransomware operation as an affiliate in June 2021, paying administrators 20% of ransom proceeds, and used the malware against organizations in the U.S., Canada, and Australia with annual revenues exceeding $100 million, later encouraged to target firms above $200 million. He also used Hive ransomware against seven victims, including a dental office, a healthcare provider, and law firms. Nefilim and Hive together attacked roughly 1,500 victims worldwide, with attacks causing more than $100 million in losses and disrupting patient care at hospitals.

Stryzhak's alleged co-conspirator, Volodymyr Tymoshchuk, remains at large, with an $11 million reward offered for information leading to his arrest or conviction. The FBI's Cyber Branch Operations Director noted the sentence reflects the seriousness of the crimes and the agency's global reach. The case underscores the affiliate model behind both ransomware families and the continued focus on arresting and extraditing individual operators rather than only disrupting infrastructure.
