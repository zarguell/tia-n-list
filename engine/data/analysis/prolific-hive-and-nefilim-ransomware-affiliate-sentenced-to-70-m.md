Artem Aleksandrovych Stryzhak, a 36-year-old Ukrainian national, was sentenced to 70 months in prison for operating as an affiliate for both Hive and Nefilim ransomware from 2018 through late 2021. Court records show he paid 20% of collected ransom payments to access custom Nefilim malware and decryption keys beginning in June 2021, and he specifically targeted corporations in the United States, Canada, and Australia with annual revenues exceeding $100 million. The operations disrupted hospital patient care, hit government entities, and caused over $100 million in total losses across roughly 1,500 victims worldwide; authorities also note an $11 million reward remains active for co-conspirator Volodymyr Tymoshchuk.

This is not a symbolic sentencing; it confirms that multi-year ransomware affiliate networks generate real, quantifiable harm that outlasts any single takedown. The 70-month term follows extradition from Spain in April 2025 and a December 2025 guilty plea, meaning the full legal cycle from arrest in June 2024 to final judgment spans more than two years. For defenders, the key exposure is historical: Stryzhak used custom-tailored malware and double-extortion via dark web "Corporate Leaks" sites, so any organization that faced Hive or Nefilim between 2018 and 2021 should treat those incidents as part of this same affiliate chain rather than isolated events.

Defenders should hunt for historical Hive and Nefilim indicators of compromise tied to large-corporate targeting with revenues above $100 million, review backup and recovery plans against double-extortion tactics, and monitor for references to "Corporate Leaks" extortion portals. Network segmentation and patch management remain the primary controls, but given the 2018-2021 campaign window, incident-response reviews of past breaches in healthcare, oil and gas transportation, and government entities are now warranted. Watch for any update on Tymoshchuk's status or a DOJ announcement linking additional affiliates to the same custom Nefilim infrastructure; either development would expand the assessed victim footprint beyond the 1,500 already documented.
