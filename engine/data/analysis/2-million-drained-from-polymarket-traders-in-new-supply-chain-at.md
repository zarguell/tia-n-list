Polymarket halted trading on its international platform on October 29 after users reported unauthorized withdrawals, later disclosing that one of its third-party providers had been compromised and referring the matter to law enforcement. Trading resumed roughly an hour later in cancel-only mode first, then normal operation, with Polymarket pledging to reimburse all affected traders. The company has not named the vendor or disclosed total losses, though the headline figure cited is over $2 million drained from traders. The US platform was reportedly unaffected.

Researchers linked the theft to malicious JavaScript served from DebugBear, a UK-based website performance monitoring firm that Polymarket used. DebugBear confirmed it was impacted by an ongoing security incident and urged users of its real user monitoring product to remove its snippet from their sites, saying it had brought in specialist incident responders. Hudson Rock obtained a copy of the malicious JavaScript from a DebugBear address used by Polymarket. The payload hunted for a logged-in user's trading tools and portfolio, then either sold positions and moved the proceeds or sent position tokens directly to an attacker-controlled address. It also read credential-related material. The case is a classic third-party script supply-chain compromise: the prediction market's own code was not breached, but a trusted vendor's snippet delivered attacker code to authenticated users.

Defenders should treat any site embedding third-party monitoring or analytics snippets as a supply-chain surface, inventory those scripts, and be ready to pull them quickly. Affected Polymarket users should assume session and credential exposure even where funds are reimbursed.
