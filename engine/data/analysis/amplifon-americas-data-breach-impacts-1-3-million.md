Amplifon Americas, the Minneapolis-based arm of Italian hearing healthcare giant Amplifon, disclosed a data breach affecting more than 1.3 million individuals in the United States. Attackers broke into internal systems in January 2026 and copied files from certain systems between January 18 and February 8. The company says it contained the incident immediately and launched an investigation, but needed eight months to review the stolen data. The compromised information includes names, contact details, driver's license numbers, other government ID numbers, dates of birth, hearing and medical information, dates of service, other health information, and health insurance information. For some individuals, Social Security numbers and financial information were also stolen. A filing with the Texas Attorney General's Office reportedly lists 1,332,826 people affected nationwide. Amplifon says it is not aware of any fraudulent use of personal information as a result of the incident.

The breach matters because it combines healthcare and biometric-adjacent hearing data with government identifiers and, for some victims, Social Security numbers and financial details, creating medical identity theft and fraud exposure. The eight-month gap between containment and scope determination also raises notification-timing concerns for a provider network that claims to serve over 80 million members. What to watch next is whether Amplifon confirms the full national scope, whether additional state regulators open inquiries, and whether the company offers monitoring or other remediation to the affected individuals.
