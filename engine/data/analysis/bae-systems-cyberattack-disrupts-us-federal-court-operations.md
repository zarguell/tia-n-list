A cybersecurity incident at defense contractor BAE Systems has disabled the Bankruptcy Noticing Center (BNC), the system that produces and transmits bankruptcy notices for every federal bankruptcy court in the United States. According to the events, BAE first told the Administrative Office about the disruption late on October 9, 2026, and by October 13 individual courts in the Eastern District of Texas and the Northern District of Georgia had posted service-disruption notices listing the affected systems: bnc.uscourts.gov, bankruptcynotices.uscourts.gov, notices.uscourts.gov, and the National Creditor Registration Service. The Administrative Office of the US Courts did not disclose the incident publicly until October 15, 2026, meaning the contractor-level outage had persisted for roughly a week before the judiciary confirmed it was a cybersecurity incident rather than a routine interruption.

This is not a commercial outage; it is a government service disruption with procedural consequences. Because the BNC handles notices for the entire federal bankruptcy system, creditors and debtors nationwide cannot generate the creditor mailing lists they use to serve documents through CM/ECF, and paper and electronic notice production is halted. The events note that several bankruptcy courts posted notices on October 13 that called the problem a service disruption without mentioning a cybersecurity incident, which suggests the contractor initially framed the event as operational rather than malicious. That delay in labeling matters: defenders monitoring judicial infrastructure for security events would not have identified the root cause from those early court posts alone.

Defenders should treat this as a contractor supply-chain exposure in government IT services rather than an isolated vendor breach. There is no CVE assigned, no confirmed attribution, and no ransomware claim in the records, so the hunt should focus on BAE-operated endpoints and the four named BNC websites. Monitor restoration timelines from the Administrative Office and any follow-up disclosure from BAE regarding data-access claims. Watch specifically for whether the National Creditor Registration Service remains unavailable, since its outage blocks CM/ECF access to address labels and therefore stops the notice pipeline even if the other BNC systems are partially restored.
