The FBI seized seven domains on October 8, 2026, linked to two Chinese APT tools operated by Integrity Technology Group: MicroScan, a vulnerability scanner that mapped victim networks using old flaws like CVE-2014-6278 (Shellshock) and CVE-2019-11510 (Pulse Secure VPN), and FishHub, which delivered spear-phishing payloads and pulled additional malware onto targets. The joint CISA advisory AA26-281A, released the same day, names eight commonly targeted CVEs and confirms no zero-day is involved; the newest flaw is CVE-2023-22894 (Strapi). The court documents referenced in coverage list targets including a South Carolina power company, airports in Japan and Poland, Taiwanese energy firms, and about 20 Taiwanese universities, with files from more than 20 entities found on a FishHub server. Persistence ran through SoftEther VPN software left on victims, tied to the domain 98aiblog[.]com.

This is not a patch-and-forget story; the seizure removes command-and-control, not the underlying exposure. The advisory lists government, critical manufacturing, healthcare, IT, and education as targeted sectors, and because the actor uses open-source EBurst for Exchange password spraying and legacy CVE exploitation rather than novel exploits, the same inventory of exposed services that enabled these breaches still exists in most environments. A defender should treat this as a direct exposure audit prompt: any unpatched ProFTPD, BIND, Pulse Secure, or GitLab instance visible externally matches the exact profile Integrity Technology Group clients scanned for. The attack chain is low-complexity, high-scale, and relies on forgotten internet-facing services rather than user interaction.

Patch against the full advisory CVE list (CVE-2014-6278, CVE-2015-3306, CVE-2015-5477, CVE-2016-3081, CVE-2019-11510, CVE-2021-22205, CVE-2021-3199, CVE-2023-22894), disable unused ports and services, enforce MFA on all Exchange and VPN access, and sanitize web inputs to block cross-site scripting payloads delivered by FishHub. Hunt for SoftEther VPN installations and outbound connections to the five malware-delivery lookalikes (outlook3650[.]com, linkedinns[.]net, youtubecard[.]com) plus the persistence domain 98aiblog[.]com. Review Exchange sign-in logs for EBurst-style spraying patterns and retain logs back to March, as the advisory implies sustained access rather than one-time intrusion.

Watch for replacement domains operating MicroScan or FishHub infrastructure; a second Justice Department disruption of Integrity Tech occurred in 2024, and domain seizures historically trigger rapid infrastructure rotation within weeks. Also watch whether CISA extends the KEV due date past October 11, 2026, or adds additional CVEs to the catalog based on new victim reporting from Southeast Asia and Africa.
