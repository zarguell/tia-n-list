OFAC sanctioned eight members of Tren de Aragua for their role in ATM jackpotting attacks that stole millions of dollars from US banks and credit unions. The designations include Anibal Alexander Canelon Aguirre, known as Prometheus, who allegedly developed the Ploutus jackpotting malware and has been on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted fugitive list since March, plus six associates: Eric Gabriel Cardenas Arzola, Jose Dario Galeano Bazurto, Anthony Wuiliam Hernandez Guerrero, Carlos Javier Martinez Armenta, Oscar Leonardo Martinez Pirona, and Alejandro Mejia Castillo.

Jackpotting works by deploying malware such as Ploutus, Alice, RIPPER, Skimer, SUCEFUL, ATMii, ATMitch, or GreenDispenser onto ATMs, then forcing the machines to dispense cash through an attached USB keyboard or the built-in PIN pad while evidence is deleted. Treasury says the Prometheus network is based in Mexico and Venezuela but targets US ATMs, with stolen funds laundered and transferred to TdA members across multiple countries.

The action is part of what Treasury calls a sustained whole-of-government campaign: over 30 actions against more than 300 individuals and entities tied to the gang. Worth watching is whether follow-on designations name the money laundering channels and whether jackpotting crews adapt as banks harden ATM fleets against malware-based cash-out.
