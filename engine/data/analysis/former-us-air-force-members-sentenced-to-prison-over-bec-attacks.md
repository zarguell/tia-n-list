Two former US Air Force members, Chijioke Timothy Odimegwu, 25, and Harafat Mogaji, 26, were sentenced on September 25, 2026 to a combined 189 months in federal prison for a business email compromise and phishing operation run while both were stationed at Dover Air Force Base in Delaware. Odimegwu drew 111 months with $366,617.59 in restitution and Mogaji 78 months with $995,680.45, each followed by three years of supervised release, and both were taken into custody at sentencing. Per the Justice Department, they phished employee email credentials, then used spoofed addresses mimicking business partners to push "updated" wiring instructions into live payment threads, diverting a more than $1.68 million wire from an Iowa City victim to a Chicago account and a more than $720,000 wire from an Ohio victim, against at least 15 victim organizations. Both pleaded guilty in June to wire fraud, identity theft, and access device fraud; the charging document, case 1:25-mj-00633-UNA in the District of Delaware, was filed November 18, 2025 and only surfaced publicly on DocumentCloud about an hour before the sentencing coverage broke, so the case ran from sealed complaint to guilty pleas with no public charging coverage at all.

The insider angle is real but not the one defenders usually fear: no military system, clearance, or network was in the kill chain, and what Air Force membership supplied was cover and a trusted identity while two twenty-somethings ran a commodity BEC playbook against private businesses for close to two years. Along the way they harvested card data, including a Pella, Iowa nonprofit's credit card, bought more from co-conspirators, traded it with each other, and resold mailbox access to other hackers, which makes this a resale business, not a single crew's spending money. The loss arithmetic is the argument: the two documented wires alone exceed $2.4 million while combined court-ordered restitution comes to about $1.36 million, so even a clean prosecution recovered roughly half of the named losses, and the FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report logged 24,768 BEC complaints and over $3 billion in losses, meaning most victims never see a case like this one.

The defense actions follow directly from the mechanism. Treat any change of banking details inside an ongoing email thread as unverified until confirmed by phone on a number held before the thread started, because thread insertion works identically against a spoofed lookalike domain and a phished live mailbox. After any credential-phishing wave, audit affected mailboxes for forwarding and deletion rules and review sign-in history, since the operators watched payment discussions from inside compromised accounts before injecting their wiring changes. And because prosecutors count at least 15 victim organizations while the crew resold both card data and mailbox access, any organization that transacted with these accounts should treat exposed credentials and card numbers as still circulating in resale channels and reissue rather than monitor.

Two developments would change this assessment. First, follow-on indictments of the co-conspirator and cash-out network in the United States and abroad: the FBI Omaha and Air Force Office of Special Investigations team that closed this case has named partners still at large, and the Chicago account that received the Iowa wire plus the mules behind it typically outlive the principals, so new complaints touching the same victims would confirm the infrastructure kept running. Second, any accounting from the Air Force or AFOSI of how a roughly two-year fraud operation ran from Dover Air Force Base without internal detection, which would show whether base networks and equipment were incidental or materially used, and whether other service members' side operations deserve the same audit.
