Two former US Air Force members stationed at Dover Air Force Base were sentenced to a combined 189 months in federal prison for business email compromise and phishing campaigns run during their service. Chijioke Timothy Odimegwu, 25, and Harafat Mogaji, 26, stole employee email credentials through spamming and phishing, then used spoofed addresses mimicking business partners, plus the stolen credentials, to redirect payments to accounts controlled by accomplices in the United States and abroad.

The documented losses include a wire of more than 1.68 million dollars from an Iowa City, Iowa victim diverted to a Chicago account controlled by the conspiracy, and a wire of more than 720,000 dollars from an Ohio victim, with additional attempted diversions cited by the Department of Justice. The pair also made unauthorized financial transactions using stolen account information, PINs, and card numbers, supplemented by data purchased from criminal partners.

The case is a useful data point on insider-adjacent fraud: the insider angle here was not access to classified systems but the credibility, employment, and identity of service members, abused to run ordinary BEC. Sentencing terms of this length for BEC roles show prosecutors treating payment-diversion fraud as serious felony work rather than a footnote.

Watch for co-conspirator prosecutions in the US and abroad, since these schemes typically involve cash-out networks that continue operating after the principals are sentenced, and for whether the Air Force discloses any internal review of how the activity went undetected while both men were stationed at Dover.
