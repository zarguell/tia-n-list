Gamers Nexus, working with Level1Techs and independent security researchers, used Wireshark packet captures to demonstrate that LG TVs, including the G5 OLED, actively scan the local network for connected devices. The TV identified smartwatches, phones, and dozens of unrelated devices on the LAN, collecting IP addresses, geolocation data, nearby Wi-Fi network names, signal strengths, and channel numbers. LG Ad Solutions has marketed the ability to reach 363 million "addressable secondary devices" in the U.S.

More concerning, the researchers captured microphone audio being recorded and stored in plaintext on the TV while it was unplugged from the network. LG stated that audio processing occurs only when the user has enabled the Far-Field feature and a wake word like "Hi LG" is detected, and that local audio is "promptly deleted." The researchers argue that the data persists locally and could be exfiltrated if the TV reconnects.

This is native functionality, not a vulnerability requiring exploitation. LG characterized it as standard smart TV behavior for device connectivity and content sharing. The findings undermine LG's reputation for privacy compared to budget TV brands, and the researchers noted that even using an LG TV as a dumb display does not necessarily disable the scanning. Separate remote code execution vulnerabilities are reportedly in the disclosure process.
