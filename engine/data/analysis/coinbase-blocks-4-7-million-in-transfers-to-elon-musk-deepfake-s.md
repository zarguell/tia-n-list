Coinbase says it blocked about $4.7 million in attempted cryptocurrency transfers to a giveaway scam built around an AI-generated Elon Musk deepfake. The fake YouTube livestream impersonated Tesla's "flying car" demonstration and drew more than 500,000 viewers. It showed a synthetic Musk alongside wallet addresses and a QR code, promising that any crypto sent would be doubled. Hundreds of Coinbase customers attempted transfers over roughly two hours, including long-time users of the exchange.

The operational detail is that these were legitimate, customer-approved transactions. Coinbase says its fraud systems stopped about 91 percent of the attempted transfers because the accounts and approvals were genuine, unlike credential theft or account takeover. The company notes that most fraud defenses are built to spot activity the customer did not authorize, which does not fire when a logged-in customer presses confirm after believing the giveaway is real.

What to watch is whether other exchanges report the same campaign and whether the remaining share of attempted transfers reached the scammers' wallets. The $4.7 million figure is blocked attempt volume, not a confirmed loss total, and the account of the incident comes from Coinbase and Malwarebytes.
