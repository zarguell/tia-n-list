A joint FBI, NSA, and US Cyber Command advisory released on 26 August 2026 attributed Chinese hacking group QTFY to Nanjing Xinjiuwei Network Technology Co. (XJW), linking the company to China's Ministry of State Security (MSS) and People's Liberation Army (PLA). QTFY has operated since at least 2018, developing malicious tooling, trading exploits within freelance hacking networks, and maintaining an obfuscation botnet. Targets have included NASA, the Federal Reserve, the Department of Energy, the Department of Justice, HHS, NIH, and the US Senate.

The Natto Thoughts analysis goes deeper than the advisory itself, examining two of the five companies named as XJW business partners: ELEX (Bozhi Security Technology) and Nanjing Lexbell Information Technology. ELEX's own website, before removal, publicly listed approximately 300 clients including MSS and Ministry of Public Security provincial bureaus alongside PLA-affiliated entities, an unusual level of disclosure for a firm engaged in this type of work. Lexbell's products have explicit military applications, its leadership has PLA-linked backgrounds, and the company has won contracts with the PLA-affiliated National University of Defense Technology (NUDT).

This matters because it maps the ecosystem of Chinese offensive cyber operations beyond state-sponsored units to the commercial firms that develop tools and share infrastructure with military and intelligence clients. The advisory names two MSS units (0718 and 9086) and two state entities as XJW's contacts, but the Natto analysis reveals the network extends to well-known cybersecurity firms operating in plain sight. Organizations should treat the expanded actor profiles as indicators for threat hunting, particularly against critical infrastructure networks that align with QTFY's known targeting priorities.
