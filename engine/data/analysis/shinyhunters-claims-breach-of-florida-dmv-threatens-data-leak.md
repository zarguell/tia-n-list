The ShinyHunters extortion group claims to have breached the Florida Driver and Vehicle Information Database (DAVID), operated by the Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles agency, and exfiltrated over 200,000 driver records. DAVID is a restricted law enforcement database containing driver's license applications, photographs, signatures, addresses, vehicle histories, and insurance information. As proof, ShinyHunters released a screenshot of Jeffrey Epstein's DAVID record showing his address, Social Security number, birth date, license ID, and registered vehicles.

ShinyHunters told CyberInsider they gained access through a password-reset exploit and then compromised accounts linked to FBI personnel who had access to the state motor-vehicle portal. The group listed the "State of Florida DMV" on its leak site September 7 with a September 11 deadline, warning it would publish the full dataset if FLHSMV did not negotiate. FLHSMV has not publicly confirmed a breach and has not responded to inquiries.

The DAVID database is a high-value target because it aggregates sensitive PII used across law enforcement and criminal justice. If the claims are legitimate, the exposure of 200,000 records including Social Security numbers creates significant identity theft risk. The claimed use of FBI-linked accounts to pivot into a state system also raises questions about federal-state access controls and credential hygiene across interconnected justice systems.
