Apollo Global Management disclosed that attackers gained unauthorized access to its cloud platforms between July 6 and July 10, 2026, after a social-engineering attack compromised its cloud environment. The intrusion exposed names, birth dates, mailing addresses, and Social Security numbers. Apollo has not attributed the activity to a named threat group or detailed the specific social-engineering vector.

The data categories are a direct identity-theft cocktail. SSNs combined with full names and addresses enable fraudulent account applications, tax fraud, and synthetic identity creation. Apollo manages over $700 billion in assets and serves institutional investors, pension funds, and retirement plans, meaning the breach touches a broad population of high-net-worth individuals and their employees.

No CVEs are involved, but the incident underscores a recurring pattern: social engineering remains the dominant initial-access vector for high-value financial targets. Apollo's disclosure timeline (breach July 6-10, notification August 22) is roughly six weeks, which is within the norm but leaves a wide window for credential misuse. Watch for follow-on fraud campaigns using the exposed PII.
