SOCRadar's AI Identity Exposure Report, covered by BleepingComputer on September 28, puts numbers on the stolen-AI-login problem. Starting from more than one million infostealer records tied to AI services across 80,000-plus corporate domains, the research narrowed to 482 major enterprises: 68% are billion-dollar organizations across 36 countries and eight sectors, with 5,434 stealer-log records tied to 1,500 distinct corporate email addresses. 295 of the 482 companies surfaced in stealer logs within the last 90 days.

ChatGPT dominates the dataset. Captured ChatGPT or OpenAI sessions appear for 358 of the 482 companies, carrying roughly 90% of all records, with Zapier, Notion, Hugging Face, Replit, Lovable and ElevenLabs trailing. The report reads that skew as a shadow-AI signal rather than a vendor security verdict: employees sign up with work email on personal devices, and infostealers scrape exactly that population. The backdrop is Anthropic's late-August response to infostealer-driven Claude session hijacking, where it signed users out, wiped saved payment methods and refunded unauthorized charges.

The reason this matters beyond ordinary credential theft is what an AI account is: a searchable archive of what employees pasted, a live session cookie that walks past MFA, an execution surface through automation platforms holding standing OAuth grants into CRM and email, and a billable resource. Stolen API keys feed LLMjacking, with underground vendors selling discounted access to Claude, Gemini and Cursor accounts. Technology firms lead exposure at 144 companies and 40% of records.

Worth watching: whether SSO coverage for AI platforms with short-lived sessions becomes standard, and whether stealer-log monitoring for corporate domains against AI platforms becomes a routine control. The report's practical point stands: an employee appearing in a stealer log is an endpoint incident, not a password reset.
