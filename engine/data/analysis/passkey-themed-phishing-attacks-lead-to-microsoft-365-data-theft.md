Microsoft is warning of phishing campaigns where threat actors linked to ShinyHunters, Helix, and other extortion groups use passkey and single sign-on lures to compromise corporate Microsoft 365 accounts. Active since May 2026, the attacks begin with extensive pre-attack reconnaissance on target organizations and employees.

Attackers impersonate corporate IT help desks via phone calls or messages, telling victims they must urgently update passkey, MFA, or SSO settings. Targets are directed to adversary-in-the-middle phishing sites mimicking legitimate Microsoft login pages, with links sometimes sent via SMS to personal phones. Despite the passkey framing, the attackers are not enrolling passkeys. Instead, they use the urgency to trick victims into signing into AiTM proxy pages or completing device-code authentication flows, which capture credentials and session tokens.

The campaigns combine social engineering with technical phishing infrastructure. AiTM proxy attacks defeat standard MFA by proxying the authentication handshake in real time. Device-code phishing exploits Microsoft's legitimate OAuth flow to grant attacker-controlled clients access to victim accounts. Organizations should warn employees about IT help desk impersonation and consider restricting device-code authentication for unmanaged devices.
