Microsoft has identified a campaign of passkey-themed and single sign-on-themed phishing attacks targeting corporate Microsoft 365 accounts, attributed to threat actors linked to ShinyHunters, Helix, and other extortion gangs. The activity has been observed since May 2026. Attackers research targeted organizations and employees, then call or message victims while impersonating corporate IT help desks. Victims are told they must urgently update a passkey, multi-factor authentication (MFA), or single sign-on (SSO) configuration to avoid losing access. They are then sent to phishing sites designed to look like legitimate Microsoft login pages, with links sometimes sent via SMS to personal phones.

The specific technique involves adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) phishing or device-code authentication flows. AiTM attacks capture credentials and session tokens; device-code phishing tricks victims into authorizing access to their account via an attacker-controlled client. For analysis, the passkey-themed phishing campaign is a notable evolution in social engineering against M365 environments, leveraging the growing adoption of passkeys and SSO to deliver AiTM attacks. The involvement of ShinyHunters and Helix gangs suggests the campaign is coordinated and part of a larger extortion operation. The use of device-code authentication flows is a significant elevation in sophistication compared to standard phishing, as it enables the attacker to obtain a persistent session token without ever needing to have the victim's actual credentials. Organizations should assess their passkey deployment, ensure help desk impersonation protections are in place, and monitor for unexpected device-code authorization events. The campaign is ongoing and has not yet been linked to a specific CVE, which suggests it is behavior-based rather than vulnerability-based.
