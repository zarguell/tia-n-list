The Revolut data breach has escalated with a $3M ransom demand from a group calling itself "iamnotavillain." The attackers gave Revolut 24 hours to pay 6,000 Monero, threatening to sell customer data to other criminal groups. Revolut says it has received no direct contact or ransom demand and has not negotiated with the attackers.

The breach affected approximately 680 high-profile customers. Attackers used a compromised Italian government email system to pose as law enforcement officials and request customer information over several months. They used blockchain analysis to identify customers with significant cryptocurrency holdings. Exposed data includes names, dates of birth, addresses, phone numbers, passports, verification selfies, bank statements, and transaction histories including Bitcoin transactions.

Italian authorities have opened an investigation into unauthorized access to a government computer system. The National Anti-Mafia and Anti-Terrorism Directorate is involved because the intrusion involved a government entity. The attackers have begun publishing customer data and accused Revolut of improperly disclosing information across jurisdictions. This is the fourth ScreenConnect security issue CISA has flagged as actively exploited since 2024.
