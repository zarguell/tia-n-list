Revolut's data breach, first disclosed September 17, 2026, involved a threat actor impersonating a government agency to obtain customer data through fake legal requests over a five-month period. The attacker, using the handle "IAmNotAVillain," demanded $3 million in ransom and published samples of 680 high-profile customer records including personal information, passport details, email addresses, phone numbers, and financial data. Revolut confirmed the breach but said it received no direct contact from the actor. The attack exploited Revolut's legal compliance obligations: because the company is required to respond to law enforcement requests, it handed over data when presented with what appeared to be legitimate government demands. SecurityWeek reporting indicates the breach affected an unspecified number of customers, with 147GB of police data also allegedly in the attacker's possession. The case highlights a growing attack vector targeting fintech and financial services companies through their compliance and legal request fulfillment processes. Organizations that automatically respond to legal data requests without independent verification of the requesting authority are exposed to this type of social engineering.
