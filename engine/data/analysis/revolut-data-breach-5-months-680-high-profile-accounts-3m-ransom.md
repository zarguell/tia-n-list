Revolut has disclosed a data breach in which attackers siphoned customer data over five months by impersonating government agencies and submitting fake legal requests for user information. Because Revolut is legally required to comply with law enforcement data requests, the company fulfilled the spoofed orders, giving attackers access to personal information, passport details, email addresses, phone numbers, and financial data. The threat actor, operating under the moniker "IAmNotAVillain," has publicly demanded a $3 million ransom and threatened to sell the data, though Revolut says it has received no direct contact from the attacker.

The Duel investigations team established contact with the threat actor and learned that 680 Revolut customers were specifically targeted, with the attacker also claiming access to 147GB of police data from the impersonated government agency. A sample of the exfiltrated information was reportedly shared with a former associate who also claimed responsibility. Revolut notified potentially affected users last week but declined to name the impersonated government agency or specify how many individuals were impacted.

The attack vector, social engineering of compliance teams via spoofed legal requests, is a well-known but underappreciated threat against financial institutions. The five-month duration suggests the attackers operated with impunity for an extended period before detection. Organizations that process law enforcement data requests should consider enhanced verification procedures for such requests, including callback verification to known government contacts and rate-limiting on data disclosures.
