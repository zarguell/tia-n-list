Kinryū Labs discovered an Elasticsearch cluster named "pax-info" on June 3, 2026 while surveying exposed databases during ransomware research. The cluster, hosted in Viettel-assigned IP space in Hanoi, contained 210,318,069 passenger records and 10,465,631 crew records across 29 indices totaling roughly 107 GB. The system appears to be an Advance Passenger Information System (APIS) linked to a Vietnamese organization, though the operator has not been publicly identified.

The exposed data spans January 2017 through April 2026 and includes names, dates of birth, sex, nationalities, passport and travel-document numbers, document expiration dates, issuing countries, flight numbers, dates, and airlines. This covers travelers of many nationalities who flew to, from, or through Vietnam over a nine-year period.

APIS databases are government-mandated systems used worldwide to collect identity and flight information before passengers arrive at or depart from a country. The breadth of this exposure, 220 million records including passport numbers, makes it one of the larger travel-data leaks in recent years. BleepingComputer could not confirm which Vietnamese organization operated the system. The Elasticsearch cluster was accessible online due to a chain of security misconfigurations, no authentication or access controls were in place.
