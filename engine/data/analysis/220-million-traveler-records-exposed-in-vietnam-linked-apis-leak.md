Kinryu Labs discovered an Elasticsearch cluster containing 220,783,700 passenger and crew records, accessible online due to security misconfigurations. The cluster, named 'pax-info', held 29 indices across roughly 107 GB of data, with records spanning January 2017 to April 2026. It was hosted in Viettel-assigned IP space in Hanoi and is linked to a Vietnamese Advance Passenger Information System (APIS).

The exposed data included names, dates of birth, sex, nationalities, passport and travel-document numbers, document expiration dates, issuing countries, flight numbers, dates, and airlines. The records potentially involve travelers of many nationalities who flew to, from, or through Vietnam during the nine-year period. APIS databases collect identity and flight information from airlines before passengers arrive at or depart from a country.

This is a massive identity-theft and surveillance resource. Passport numbers and travel histories for 220 million people, spanning nearly a decade, represent exactly the kind of dataset that nation-state actors and organized crime groups would exploit. The exposure appears to stem from misconfiguration rather than a breach, but the impact is identical.
