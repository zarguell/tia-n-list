Microsoft Defender Research and the Microsoft AI Red Team published two studies on post-training supply chain attacks against AI coding agents. Dataset poisoning hides crafted examples in a fine-tuning dataset, teaching a model to find and misuse any available command-execution tool when a routine, even paraphrased, user request appears; the approach reached roughly 78% attack success on in-distribution harnesses and transferred to an untested harness. Model poisoning builds backdoors that persist and transfer across deployments.

The risk is that a backdoored agent can invoke tools, execute commands, modify files, or access data when triggered, rather than merely changing a text response. Microsoft's guidance emphasizes independent observability and controls outside the model, since trust cannot rest on evaluation-time behavior alone. Organizations deploying specialized agents should treat fine-tuning data and model provenance as supply chain inputs.
