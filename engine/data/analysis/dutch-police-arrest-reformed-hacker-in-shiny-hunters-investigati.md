Dutch police arrested a 24-year-old Amsterdam man on September 15 or 16 as an alleged participant in the ShinyHunters extortion group; a tactical unit searched the home he shared with his mother and seized electronic devices. Police confirmed the arrest Monday and named the investigation into ShinyHunters, with the suspect appearing before the Rotterdam District Court on September 29, which ordered him to remain in detention. Officials have not named him, but KrebsOnSecurity and DataBreaches identify him as Pepijn van der Stap, previously convicted under the handle Umbreon, who at arrest was on supervised release after serving three years of a four-year sentence and working as offensive security lead at Neo Security.

The record matters here. Van der Stap was arrested in January 2023 for hacking and blackmailing more than a dozen companies, pleaded guilty, and was sentenced in late 2023 over thefts and extortions prosecutors valued at 1.5 to 2.7 million euros. He admitted living a double life: software engineer at Amsterdam security startup Hadrian and DIVD volunteer by day, extortionist posting on RaidForums and Breached by night. In 2021 the Umbreon identity sold a database covering 2.3 million Dutch residents.

FBI Cyber Division assistant director Brett Leatherman said the group and its co-conspirators have breached more than 140 organizations and collected at least 70 million dollars in extortion payments since last year, often through third-party vendors on cloud platforms. Dutch police say they recovered a large volume of evidence from the suspect's laptop, reportedly including details about two murders he allegedly ordered abroad. The arrest preceded the group's claimed FBI breach, in which ShinyHunters defaced an FBI jobs site with an oversized Umbreon Pokemon image, likely using a modified CVE-2026-35273 PeopleSoft exploit, and the group's current leader, the teenage Ray of Scattered LAPSUS$ Hunters, appears to have pinned that operation on van der Stap.
