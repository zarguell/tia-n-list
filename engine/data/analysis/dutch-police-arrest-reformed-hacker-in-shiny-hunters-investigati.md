Dutch police this month arrested Pepijn van der Stap, a 23-year-old from Almere and Lelystad, on suspicion of aiding data thefts and extortions by ShinyHunters. KrebsOnSecurity named him via three sources familiar with the matter. He was already convicted in 2023 for data thefts and extortions Dutch prosecutors valued between 1.5 and 2.7 million euros. At that trial he admitted running the handle Umbreon, selling a database on RaidForums in September 2021 with information on 2.3 million Dutch residents and posting stolen data on RaidForums and Breached. While doing this he worked as a software engineer at the Amsterdam security startup Hadrian and volunteered at the Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure.

The arrest matters because of what happened next. In the days after it, the remaining ShinyHunters members escalated: they stole highly sensitive data from the FBI and moved to extort the Russian ransomware group Cl0p. A group that keeps operating and even attacks peer criminals after losing a member is not dependent on any single operator, and the FBI data theft shows its targets now include government personnel records.

Watch whether Dutch prosecutors file new charges linking van der Stap to specific ShinyHunters operations, and whether the post-arrest escalations get attributed to named members. His case also raises an insider-risk question for the security industry itself: a convicted extortionist spent years employed at a security startup and a responsible-disclosure nonprofit while apparently still active under an old handle.
