Dutch police have arrested 24-year-old Pepijn van der Stap of Amsterdam, identified by KrebsOnSecurity and DataBreaches as the hacker formerly known as Umbreon, in an investigation tied to the ShinyHunters extortion group. Van der Stap was convicted in 2023 for hacking and blackmailing more than a dozen companies and sentenced to four years with one suspended; he was on supervised release and working as offensive security lead at Neo Security when a tactical unit searched his home on September 15 and seized his devices. The FBI and Dutch National Police have publicly framed the arrest as taking down one of ShinyHunters' alleged leaders, citing more than 140 victim organizations and at least 70 million dollars in extortion payments.

The reporting links van der Stap's Umbreon handle to ShinyHunters branding, including the oversized Umbreon Pokemon image left in the defacement of the FBI jobs site, an intrusion that reportedly used a modified exploit for CVE-2026-35273, the Oracle PeopleSoft flaw ShinyHunters exploited as a zero-day in June. Following the arrest, the group's brand passed to a teenage operator from Amman, Jordan known as Rey, identified by Krebs as Saif Al-din Khader.

The October follow-up shows the takedown is still expanding. KrebsOnSecurity reports Rey was detained by Jordanian authorities and is reportedly cooperating with the FBI, and that he was detained mid-operation while extorting Jeppesen ForeFlight, the aviation software unit Boeing divested last year in a 10.55 billion dollar transaction. Rey had earlier boasted about stealing sensitive FBI data and extorting Cl0p.

Watch for further arrests as Rey's cooperation matures, indictments clarifying how much of the group's recent activity, from the FBI breach to the ScatteredLapsus$Hunters campaign wave, gets formally attributed, and whether the brand reconstitutes under new leadership as ShinyHunters predecessors have done.
