Dutch police this month arrested Pepijn van der Stap, a 23-year-old from Almere and Lelystad, on suspicion of aiding data thefts and extortions by ShinyHunters; KrebsOnSecurity named him through three sources familiar with the matter. He was convicted in 2023 for a string of data thefts and extortions Dutch prosecutors valued between 1.5 and 2.7 million euros. At trial he admitted to the double life: a software engineer at the Amsterdam security startup Hadrian and a volunteer at the Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure by day, the handle Umbreon by night, selling a database with information on 2.3 million Dutch residents on RaidForums in September 2021 and posting stolen data on RaidForums and Breached. Days after his arrest, remaining ShinyHunters members stole highly sensitive data from the FBI and moved to extort the Russian ransomware group Cl0p.

The correct reading is not that a leader's removal wounds this group. French police arrested four suspected BreachForums administrators and ShinyHunters members in June 2025, and the FBI seized BreachForums domains again that October, yet the months between brought the collective's richest run: an extortion blog listing more than three dozen Salesforce victims including Toyota, FedEx, and Disney/Hulu, about 1.5 billion records taken from 760 companies through stolen Salesloft Drift tokens, and a PeopleSoft campaign Google tied to CVE-2026-35273 across more than 100 organizations. Arrests have become a cost of doing business rather than a disruption event, so the value of the Dutch case lies in the intelligence it extracts and the precedent it sets, not in the operator it removes. It also puts a face on an insider problem the security industry keeps underweighting: the collective recruits employees on Telegram for a cut of ransoms, CrowdStrike fired a staff member in November 2025 for allegedly passing internal screenshots to hackers, and this suspect held trusted roles at a security startup and a disclosure nonprofit while extorting people under an old handle.

Hunt the group's entry patterns rather than the person. Review OAuth grants and refresh tokens on SaaS platforms first, since the August 2025 Salesloft Drift and November 2025 Gainsight campaigns began with stolen integration tokens, and Salesforce Setup Audit Trail or Event Monitoring exports will show mass data pulls. Verify PeopleSoft estates carry the fix for CVE-2026-35273, which Google observed being exploited between May 27 and June 9, 2026 before notifying more than 100 organizations. The January 2026 Okta and SSO wave relied on voice phishing for credentials and MFA codes rather than a product bug, so phishing-resistant MFA and hardened help desk verification do more work here than any signature. Concrete markers worth watching for: the RapeFlake and RapeForce user agents used in Snowflake and Salesforce Experience Cloud attacks, the Google cluster labels UNC6040, UNC6240, and UNC6395, and victim listings on the Scattered LAPSUS$ Hunters extortion blog.

Two developments would change this assessment. Charges from Dutch prosecutors tying van der Stap to specific ShinyHunters intrusions would confirm how deep the reach into security firms ran and whether the 2023 conviction ever actually interrupted his activity. A visible operational pause after the arrest would suggest law enforcement finally hit more than a replaceable operator; the FBI and Cl0p operations in the days that followed point the other way. The suspect appears before the Rotterdam District Court on September 29, when police say further information will be released, and the arrest has now syndicated widely under the "Umbreon" handle framing.
