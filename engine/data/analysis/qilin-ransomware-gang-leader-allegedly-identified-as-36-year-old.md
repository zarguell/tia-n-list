A KrebsOnSecurity investigation published November 11, 2026 identifies the administrator of the Qilin ransomware operation as Aleksey Mikhailovich Yakovtsev, a 36-year-old resident of St. Petersburg. The linkage runs from the underground handle "Haise" to Yakovtsev through historical forum accounts, Telegram identifiers and records from earlier breaches. The identification is an allegation built on open-source correlation, not a charge, and no indictment or sanction is reported alongside it.

Qilin runs a conventional double-extortion operation: steal data, encrypt what is worth encrypting, then auction or leak the files when payment does not arrive. Naming the person behind the brand matters less for the group's capability than for the pressure it creates, because ransomware crews rebrand and re-register infrastructure quickly but the operators behind them are harder to replace. It also creates a concrete misattribution risk, since an individual named from forum and Telegram artifacts can be wrong.

Watch for whether any government repeats the claim, which would move it from reporting to formal attribution, and for changes in Qilin's leak site, negotiation portals and affiliate recruitment after publication. Italian threat-intelligence write-ups of the same multi-firm OSINT effort are circulating, so expect more outlets to attach names and handles to the same evidence set.
