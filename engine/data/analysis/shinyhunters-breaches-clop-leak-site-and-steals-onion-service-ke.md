The ShinyHunters extortion gang breached the Clop (aka Cl0p) ransomware operation's data leak site, defacing the Tor site and allegedly stealing server data and the private keys for its onion service. The attack began Friday night when ShinyHunters exploited what they claim was an unauthenticated file upload vulnerability in Grav CMS, which Clop's leak site runs on. They uploaded a text file to the site containing a message: "THIS SITE HAS BEEN PWN3D BY SHINYHUNTERES #Skids10p - Maybe don't try to threaten us next time."

BleepingComputer confirmed the file was uploaded directly to Clop's server and could be downloaded from the ransomware gang's Tor site. Several hours later, ShinyHunters reported they had "completely defaced" the Clop site, replacing it with ASCII art of Umbreon (their Pokémon logo) and a link to their own Tor site with the message "rooting your systems since '19 ;)". The attackers claim to have stolen server data and the private keys for Clop's onion service.

This represents a rare inter-gang confrontation where one threat actor targets another's infrastructure. The theft of onion service private keys could allow ShinyHunters to impersonate Clop or redirect their traffic. The unauthenticated file upload in Grav CMS is the specific vulnerability exploited. Clop has been one of the most active ransomware groups in 2025-2026, responsible for mass exploitation of MOVEit and other file-transfer vulnerabilities. This incident could disrupt their operations or expose their victim data.
