ShinyHunters breached the Clop ransomware gang's data leak site on September 19, exploiting an unauthenticated file upload vulnerability in Grav CMS. The attackers uploaded a text file containing a taunting message and a link to their own leak site, then defaced the Tor site with ASCII art of their Umbreon mascot. BleepingComputer confirmed the file was accessible directly from Clop's Tor site. ShinyHunters claims to have stolen server data and the private keys for Clop's onion service, which would allow them to impersonate or shut down the leak site.

The attack escalated over the following days. By September 21, ShinyHunters had seized the Clop domain entirely, replacing its content with their own leak site interface and a "Note to Cl0p" with escalating demands. The note states that every 24 hours Clop fails to engage, demands increase, now including a mandatory payment. This represents a direct confrontation between two major extortion operations, with ShinyHunters leveraging their access to pressure a competitor.

Clop is associated with large-scale exploitation of enterprise software vulnerabilities, including its recent PTC Windchill campaign targeting over 40 organizations including Shell, Philips, Fiserv, and Zebra Technologies. ShinyHunters is known for high-volume data theft, including the 3.65 TB breach of Instructure (Canvas LMS) affecting roughly 9,000 academic institutions. The incident exposes operational security failures in ransomware infrastructure and demonstrates how inter-gang rivalries can produce intelligence opportunities for defenders.
