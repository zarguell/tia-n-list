Group-IB researchers documented Balonx Sistema, a phishing-as-a-service platform targeting more than 20 Mexican banks. The operation combines real-time credential phishing, MFA interception, an Android remote access trojan, and AI-generated voice vishing calls. It has harvested credentials and financial data from over 1,100 victims since at least October 2025. The platform operates on a subscription model, lowering the technical barrier for affiliates to run banking fraud campaigns.

The attack chain is notably sophisticated for a PhaaS operation. Balonx intercepts one-time passwords and session tokens in real time, meaning victims who enter credentials on the phishing page are immediately compromised for live banking sessions. The Android RAT intercepts SMS-based 2FA codes, and the vishing component uses AI-generated voice calls to convince victims to authorize transactions or install the malware. The platform is operated from Mexico and promoted through Facebook groups involved in data trading and telemarketing fraud.

This represents an escalation in PhaaS capability. The combination of real-time session hijacking, Android malware for 2FA bypass, and social engineering via AI voice calls makes this significantly harder to detect than static credential harvesting. Mexico's heavy reliance on SMS-based 2FA across its banking sector makes it particularly vulnerable. Financial institutions in the region should be monitoring for Balonx indicators and reconsidering SMS as a second factor.
