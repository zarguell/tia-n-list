Switzerland's National Cyber Security Centre said on 24 November that attackers used agentic frameworks to find vulnerabilities, choose tools, judge results and plan subsequent steps. The later event tally is five confirmed attacks, four successful compromises, with three targeting critical-infrastructure operators and two of those succeeding; no major disruption was reported, victims were unnamed and no actor was attributed. The NCSC named ARTEX and CyberStrikeAI as examples of public tooling, not as confirmed tools in these incidents, and disclosed no CVE, affected product or vulnerable version.

The mechanism matters more than the AI label. The NCSC says these agents can exploit standard or easily guessed passwords and known flaws, then change methods when blocked; Google and Mandiant observed ARTEX execution bursts of two to 25 seconds, including 27 events in 23 seconds. The four-in-five result is not an exposure estimate because five confirmed attacks are a selected denominator, and the official bulletin only places victims across at least two sectors. Still, two successful critical-infrastructure intrusions show that routine exposure and credential weaknesses can now be chained fast enough to compress defender response time.

Apply the NCSC's actual controls: minimize internet exposure, use geo-blocking or IP and network allow lists where feasible, patch critical issues on exposed systems within 24 hours of release, require MFA or passkeys for internet-facing internal resources, and enforce least privilege. No product hotfix can be named from this disclosure. Hunt behavior in web, proxy, identity and cloud audit logs by correlating rapid discovery, credential access, API probes and execution under one identity or source; Google's `artex-probe-*` filenames and Elastic's dense tool-family bursts are research leads, not Swiss attribution indicators.

Watch for technical indicators the NCSC is sharing with critical-infrastructure operators, victim or sector disclosures, and any safety or availability impact from the two successful intrusions. Tool identification would materially change the hunt: Unit 42 counted 1,323 internet-facing CyberStrikeAI hosts and 609 ARTEX hosts in October 2026, but those totals measure exposed tooling, not Swiss targets or victims.
