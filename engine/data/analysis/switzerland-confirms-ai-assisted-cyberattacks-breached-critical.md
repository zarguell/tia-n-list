Switzerland's National Cyber Security Centre confirmed its first AI-agent-assisted cyberattacks against Swiss organizations. It reported five attacks, four of which succeeded. Three targeted critical-infrastructure operators and two of those compromises succeeded, with no major disruption reported. Victims and the actors behind the attacks were not identified.

The NCSC said the agents could search for vulnerabilities, select tools, evaluate results and plan subsequent steps. The intrusions exploited standard or easily guessed passwords and known software flaws. The bulletin cited publicly accessible systems including ARTEX and CyberStrikeAI as examples, rather than attributing these incidents to one named framework.

The cases show agentic tooling being used in actual intrusions, but the public detail does not establish autonomous targeting at machine speed or a new class of vulnerability. The agency's guidance is concrete: reduce internet exposure, patch critical flaws within 24 hours, require MFA or passkeys, apply least privilege and monitor behavior. Watch for victim disclosure, initial-access evidence and whether follow-on activity relied more on credential abuse or unpatched systems.
