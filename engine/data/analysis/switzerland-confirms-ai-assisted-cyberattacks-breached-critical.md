Switzerland's National Cyber Security Centre confirmed five recent AI-agent-assisted cyberattacks against Swiss organizations, four of which succeeded. Three attacks targeted critical-infrastructure operators and two of those succeeded. The victims were not named, no major disruption was reported, and the activity spanned at least two sectors. NCSC described these as the country's first confirmed cases of attackers using AI agents in this way.

The agents could search for vulnerabilities, choose tools, assess results and plan subsequent attack steps. NCSC said they abused standard or easily guessed passwords and known software flaws, and named ARTEX and CyberStrikeAI as examples of publicly accessible agents. Elastic separately compared ARTEX, CyberStrikeAI, Cairn, PentAGI and HexStrike, reporting that all five had appeared in intrusions and that none enforced authorization in code by default. Elastic observed ARTEX activity in bursts of two to 25 seconds.

The important change is operational tempo and tool selection, not a new exploit class. Elastic's proposed hunts focus on request tempo, tool-family diversity and sequences across web, proxy and cloud audit logs; source addresses and framework fingerprints age quickly as agents rotate infrastructure and tools. NCSC urged reduced internet exposure, patching critical flaws within 24 hours, MFA or passkeys, least privilege and behavioral monitoring. Watch for victim attribution and evidence of agent-driven authentication or cloud activity, rather than relying on a single framework signature.
