Switzerland's national cybersecurity agency says attackers using AI agents broke into systems at Swiss organizations in four of five confirmed attacks, the first cases of their kind recorded in the country. Three of the five targeted critical infrastructure operators and two of those intrusions succeeded, with no major service disruptions reported. The victims were not named and the agency did not attribute the activity.

The notable part is not the intrusion count but the ratio. A four-in-five success rate against confirmed targets, with AI agents doing the work, pushes AI-assisted attack tooling from experiment into operating capability in a country that has published little on the topic. The agency's statement also confirms the targeting of critical infrastructure, which is where the operational consequences would be largest regardless of whether this specific round succeeded quietly.

This sits alongside the European Commission's confirmation this week that an AI-assisted cyberattack disrupted its services, so two European public bodies have now described AI-driven intrusions within days of each other. Neither has released technical detail. Watch for a fuller Swiss report naming the tooling, the access paths and whether the defeated attack was stopped by detection or by chance, plus any indicators other EU agencies publish as they compare notes.
