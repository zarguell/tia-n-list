A KnowBe4 Threat Lab analysis exposes a large-scale phishing campaign that weaponizes Google's own infrastructure as a trust proxy. Attackers route victims through Google Meet links, Google Search redirects, DoubleClick click-tracking endpoints, Custom Search parameters, Image Search domains, Tag Manager, and Analytics services before delivering credential-harvesting pages or installing ScreenConnect remote-access software. Security gateways and URL scanners trust these Google-owned domains, allowing the phishing chain to pass initial inspection.

The campaign targets manufacturing, government, finance, and non-profit sectors with lures themed around document reviews, Microsoft 365 expiry notices, FedEx deliveries, OneDrive payments, government benefits, and voicemails. Some emails impersonate DocuSign, QuickBooks, Microsoft, FedEx, and Social Security services. The multi-redirect chain obscures the final malicious destination, and some variants hide target email addresses in the URL parameters to track which recipients engaged.

The abuse of legitimate Google services as redirect infrastructure represents a structural problem for URL-based security controls. Organizations should implement DMARC enforcement, deploy link-expansion analysis in email gateways that resolves through all redirects before verdict, and train users to verify unexpected document-sharing notifications through independent channels. ScreenConnect installations should be monitored as a potential indicator of compromise.
