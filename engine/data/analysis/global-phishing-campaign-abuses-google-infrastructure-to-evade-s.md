A KnowBe4 Threat Lab analysis has uncovered a large-scale phishing campaign that abuses Google services as a trust proxy to bypass email security controls. The campaign routes victims through Google Meet redirects, Google Search, DoubleClick click-tracking endpoints, Google Custom Search, Image Search, Tag Manager, and Analytics before reaching attacker-controlled credential-harvesting pages or triggering ScreenConnect remote-access software installation. Because security gateways and URL scanners trust Google-owned domains by default, the phishing chain passes initial inspection at nearly every stage.

The campaign targets organizations in manufacturing, government, finance, and non-profit sectors. Lure themes include document review notices, Microsoft 365 expiry alerts, FedEx delivery notifications, OneDrive payment requests, government benefit messages, and voicemail notifications. Some emails impersonate DocuSign, QuickBooks, Microsoft, FedEx, and Social Security services. The attackers hide target email addresses in redirect parameters, making each phishing URL unique and harder to flag at scale.

What makes this campaign operationally significant is the multi-stage redirect architecture. One observed chain begins with a Google Meet link redirect, moves through Google Search, passes through DoubleClick, and only then reaches the final payload server. Other variants chain through Google Custom Search, regional Image Search domains, Tag Manager, or Analytics. The end goal is either credential theft or ScreenConnect deployment for persistent remote access. Organizations should treat Google-domain URLs with the same scrutiny as any other external link and monitor for anomalous ScreenConnect installations following email delivery.
