mSeven Software, developer of the mSecure password manager, warned current and former customers on November 3, 2026 to change all stored passwords, reset or re-enroll two-factor authentication, and remove existing passkeys after detecting suspicious activity tied to the application. The vendor notice, published by The Hacker News and reviewed by The Register, gives no technical details: it does not identify the activity, when it occurred, which systems were involved, or whether customer data was accessed. One report states the incident reportedly exposed an encrypted customer database, but that is not confirmed in the vendor's own notice.

The gap between the sweeping remediation advice and the lack of scope or impact detail has left customers unable to judge their exposure, with forum replies describing the email as vague. mSeven says it launched an investigation, took mitigation steps, and engaged external cybersecurity experts, but has not published indicators, a timeline, or a count of affected users.

Customers should treat the advice as precautionary and rotate credentials stored in mSecure, re-enroll 2FA and passkeys on a separate device where possible, and monitor mSeven's support forum for the follow-up notice. Watch for whether the company clarifies whether the encrypted database was actually accessed and whether master passwords or vault contents were at risk.
