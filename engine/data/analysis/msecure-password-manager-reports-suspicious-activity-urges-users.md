mSeven Software, developer of the mSecure password manager, emailed current and former customers on November 3, 2026 warning of suspicious activity associated with the mSecure application and urging users to change stored passwords, reset or re-enroll two-factor authentication, and remove existing passkeys. One report describes the trigger as a cyberattack that reportedly exposed an encrypted customer database. mSeven said it launched an investigation, took mitigation steps, and engaged external cybersecurity experts, but the notice did not identify what the suspicious activity was, when it occurred, which systems were involved, or whether any customer-stored information was accessed.

The company's vague notice left customers without enough detail to judge their own risk: The Register reviewed the same email and noted mSecure had not explained whether customers' secrets were exposed. An independent analysis of the shipped mSecure macOS client, published at rentry.co/msecure-notice-explained, was written to help users work out what the November 3 notice means, but it does not close the gap on what was taken.

Because the scope is undetermined, mSecure users should treat stored credentials as potentially exposed: change passwords for critical accounts, reset 2FA, and remove passkeys until mSeven clarifies the incident. Watch for mSeven's follow-up on which systems were involved and whether the encrypted database was actually accessed; an encrypted database is only as safe as the encryption and the keys protecting it.
