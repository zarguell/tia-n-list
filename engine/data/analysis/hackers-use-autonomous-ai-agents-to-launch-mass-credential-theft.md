Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant documented a financially motivated threat actor that used autonomous AI agents to plan, build, and execute a credential-harvesting campaign in under six hours. The attackers compromised cloud infrastructure and deployed a multi-agent framework that managed scanning, vulnerability exploitation, and credential extraction with minimal human oversight. Markdown-based instruction files served as operational playbooks guiding the agents through each stage.

The framework demonstrated real-time problem solving: agents rotated IP addresses, troubleshot technical failures, and adapted scanning pipelines without operator intervention. Separately, BleepingComputer reported that cybercriminals used Anthropic's Claude to analyze approximately 1.8 million Android applications at scale, extracting secrets and identifying vulnerable targets. This represents a shift from using AI as a coding assistant to deploying it as an autonomous attack coordinator.

The operational tempo is the key takeaway. What previously required a team working days now happens in hours with a single operator orchestrating AI agents. Defensive teams should expect faster attack cycles and adjust detection timelines accordingly. Monitor for rapid credential harvesting patterns and automated scanning behaviors that compress typical kill chain stages.
