Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant observed a financially motivated threat actor compromise an organization's cloud infrastructure and deploy a multi-agent AI attack framework that executed a large credential-harvesting campaign in under six hours. The attackers used an AI coding chatbot with Markdown-based operational playbooks that instructed autonomous agents to scan internet-facing systems, attempt credential harvesting, manage vulnerability-scanning pipelines, troubleshoot problems in real time, and rotate IP addresses without human intervention.

Separately, a researcher acquired 6TB of Anthropic Claude data dumps from a China-based LLM router, finding evidence of data leakage. GBHackers reported on Claude agents being used to automate cyberattacks and exfiltrate sensitive data, representing a shift from AI-assisted attacks to fully autonomous AI-driven operations. The pattern shows threat actors moving beyond basic AI prompts to agentic systems that independently perform multiple attack stages.

Defenders should monitor for indicators of autonomous attack tooling: rapid IP rotation, automated credential stuffing at scale, and Markdown-based instruction files in cloud environments. The speed of these campaigns (under six hours from compromise to full credential harvesting) compresses incident response timelines dramatically. Organizations should assume credential compromise and rotate credentials proactively when indicators are detected.
