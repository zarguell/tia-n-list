Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant observed a financially motivated threat actor compromise an organization's cloud infrastructure and deploy a multi-agent AI attack framework that executed a credential-harvesting campaign in under six hours. The attackers used an AI coding chatbot with Markdown-based operational playbooks instructing autonomous agents to scan internet-facing systems, attempt credential harvesting, manage vulnerability-scanning pipelines, troubleshoot problems in real time, and rotate IP addresses without human intervention.

Anthropic's separate report, covering operations disrupted between December 2025 and August 2026, identified suspected state-backed operators, financially motivated criminals, and hacktivists using Claude Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus models in autonomous or semi-autonomous attack frameworks. Instead of asking a chatbot for individual code samples, attackers built multi-agent systems able to conduct reconnaissance, identify exposed systems, test credentials, develop intrusion tooling, execute post-compromise tasks, and organize stolen data. Human operators typically handled target selection and campaign priorities while AI agents ran repetitive technical tasks in parallel. One operation extracted hardcoded secrets from 1.8 million Android apps. Anthropic also blocked Claude accounts in Houthi-controlled northern Yemen used to support missile development diagnostics.

The pattern marks a shift from AI-assisted attacks to fully autonomous AI-driven operations. Defenders should monitor for rapid IP rotation, automated credential stuffing at scale, and Markdown-based instruction files in cloud environments. The sub-six-hour timeline from compromise to full credential harvesting compresses incident response windows dramatically.
