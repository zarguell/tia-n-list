Sakura Internet, a major Japanese cloud and data center provider, disclosed on August 19 that hackers breached its IT system on August 9 and potentially exposed up to 1,360,563 member accounts. The breach was discovered during investigation of a separate, smaller incident at Sakura Rental Server that involved unauthorized logins to 583 accounts and malware installation. The company says stored passwords are hashed and no data exfiltration has been confirmed, but the investigation is ongoing and the final count may grow.

What makes this significant is Sakura's role as a domestic provider for Japan's Government Cloud program, a strategic initiative to reduce dependence on foreign hyperscalers. The company provides web hosting, VPS, public cloud, data center, and GPU computing services. A breach at a provider with government contracts and infrastructure-grade services elevates this beyond a routine credential dump. The 583-account Rental Server intrusion with malware installation suggests the attacker had operational access, not just data exfiltration.

Watch for: the investigation's final scope, whether the malware was a targeted implant or commodity webshell, and whether any government or enterprise tenants were specifically affected. The password hashing claim needs independent verification given the breadth of the exposure.
