HUMAN's Satori team disclosed and disrupted BADBOX 3.0 on November 18. At peak, the botnet generated about 40 billion fraudulent advertising bid requests a day and accounted for more than 4 percent of CTV traffic seen by the Human Defense Platform. HUMAN says that is more than three times the previous record holder, VASTFLUX, and a larger peak than the last five years of published Satori investigations combined. BleepingComputer and SecurityWeek described it as the largest ad fraud botnet measured by bid volume.

The infected devices are low-cost, uncertified Android Open Source Project hardware. Satori found a maintained module catalog in public code repositories with primary and backup delivery paths, including a public CDN mirror and a self-hosted GitHub proxy at gh.drgdj[.]top. Modules render hidden video ads under the identities of legitimate CTV apps and enroll devices in eleven residential proxy networks, among them PXYEDGE, which Satori assesses as the successor to IpMoYu. HUMAN links the catalog to manufacturer TXCZ through C2 infrastructure, build-property checks, a preinstalled launcher and repository history, and published indicators covering drgdj[.]top, shzhtxcz[.]com and PXYEDGE servers. Earlier reporting gives additional context: the March 2025 disruption cut off more than 500,000 BADBOX 2.0 devices, Google later said the earlier botnet had infected over 10 million devices by April 2025, and its XCPE takedown covered over 1.3 million Android set-top boxes.

Nokia Deepfield's July 2026 hunt identified a client-pull host as a discriminating DNS indicator, while Kaspersky tied a car head unit downloader chain to the MoYu Group through the zhima proxy module and C2 address 128.14.210[.]58. The useful watch items are the module catalog mirrors, the residential proxy enrollments and whether the disruption holds. Device owners are advised to disconnect affected hardware, since the backdoor sits in firmware or preinstalled software rather than in a user-installed app.
