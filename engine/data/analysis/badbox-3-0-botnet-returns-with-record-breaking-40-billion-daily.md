BADBOX 3.0 is the latest iteration of a botnet on low-cost, uncertified Android Open Source Project devices. HUMAN's Satori team disclosed and disrupted it on November 18, reporting a peak of roughly 40 billion fraudulent bid requests per day, more than 4% of all CTV traffic seen by the Human Defense Platform and over three times the volume attributed to the earlier VASTFLUX operation. The backdoor pulls a maintained module catalog from public code repositories with primary and backup delivery locations, including a mirror on a public CDN and a self-hosted GitHub proxy at gh.drgdj[.]top. Modules render hidden video ads under the identities of legitimate CTV apps and enroll devices in eleven residential proxy networks, among them PXYEDGE, which Satori assesses as the successor to IpMoYu.

The operation follows earlier disruptions that did not hold. BADBOX was found in 2023 on a T95 Android TV box; Germany's BSI sinkholed control domains for at least 30,000 devices in December 2024; HUMAN and Shadowserver cut off more than 500,000 BADBOX 2.0 devices in March 2025 and Google removed 24 Play apps. Google later described more than 10 million infected devices by April 2025 and took down the XCPE botnet of over 1.3 million set-top boxes, part of whose infrastructure overlapped BADBOX 3.0. Kaspersky tied a car head unit update chain to the same MoYu group, with eight zhima proxy variants and command server 128.14.210[.]58.

What to watch: DNS for client-pull subdomains and the published indicators drgdj[.]top, shzhtxcz[.]com and PXYEDGE servers, plus device inventories for cheap TV boxes and photo frames with unexpected proxy traffic. HUMAN links the module catalog to Chinese electronics maker TXCZ through infrastructure, build properties and a preinstalled launcher, so supply-chain checks on device firmware remain part of the response.
