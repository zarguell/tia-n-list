HUMAN's Satori researchers report that the BADBOX botnet has rebuilt after earlier disruptions as BADBOX 3.0, generating roughly 40 billion fraudulent advertising bid requests per day from infected Android-based devices. HUMAN says that peak was more than 4% of all CTV traffic seen by its Defense Platform, more than three times the previous record holder VASTFLUX, and larger than the combined peak of the last five years of published Satori investigations. The operation was first found in early 2023 on a T95 Android TV box sold on Amazon.

The scale shows that the 2025 takedown did not end the activity. In March 2025, HUMAN and Shadowserver cut more than 500,000 BADBOX 2.0 devices off from control servers and Google removed 24 apps from Google Play that spread the malware. Google later sued the operators, saying BADBOX 2.0 had infected over 10 million Android devices by April 2025; German agencies had warned in December 2024 that tens of thousands of devices were affected. The identical device ecosystem and the new request volume indicate the operators recovered their ad-fraud capacity rather than shifting to a different business.

Watch for fresh infrastructure details from HUMAN and for evidence that the rebuilt botnet uses new distribution beyond low-cost Android TV boxes. Advertisers and CTV operators should treat anomalous bid-request volume as the most direct signal, since the reported impact is measured in requests rather than in confirmed victim accounts.
