HUMAN's Satori team says BADBOX 3.0 came back after earlier disruptions and reached roughly 40 billion fraudulent ad bid requests per day, more than 4% of all CTV traffic seen by the Human Defense Platform. That is over three times the volume of the previous record holder, VASTFLUX. The botnet runs on low-cost, uncertified Android Open Source Project devices with backdoors that deliver modules for hidden video ads and for enrolling devices into residential proxy networks. HUMAN says it disrupted command-and-control domains used by the operation.

The earlier waves give context. BADBOX was found in early 2023 on a T95 Android TV box, and the first botnet took more than 190,000 devices. German agencies warned about tens of thousands of infected devices in December 2024. In March 2025, HUMAN and Shadowserver cut more than 500,000 BADBOX 2.0 devices off from control servers and Google removed 24 malicious Play apps. Google later said BADBOX 2.0 had infected over 10 million devices by April 2025. Part of the 3.0 infrastructure ran through C&C domains taken down with Google's XCPE operation, which involved more than 1.3 million Android set-top boxes. HUMAN also found a maintained catalog of fraud modules on public repositories and links to Chinese electronics manufacturer TXCZ.

Watch for uncertified Android TV boxes and other low-cost AOSP hardware showing hidden ad traffic or proxy behavior. Some modules in the 3.0 catalog appeared in BADBOX and BADBOX 2.0, so device replacement and network monitoring matter more than a single takedown.
