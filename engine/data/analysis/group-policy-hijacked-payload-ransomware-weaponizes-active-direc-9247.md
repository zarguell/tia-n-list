In April 2026, Kaspersky's GERT responded to an incident at a Middle Eastern manufacturing firm where attackers gained domain-admin-equivalent control through compromised FortiGate SSL VPN credentials. Rather than deploying a traditional encryptor, the threat actor created a malicious Group Policy Object named "PAYLOAD" and linked it at the Active Directory domain root. A second GPO named "win Firewall Off" disabled Windows Firewall across all profiles. Through these two objects alone, the attackers delivered ransom notes to every domain-joined workstation, replaced desktop wallpaper and lock screens with ransom messages, configured a logon banner, and disabled the built-in local Administrator account. No ransomware binary was dropped on endpoints.

Data exfiltration was observed from file servers and additional systems, with stolen material later published on the dark web. The only ransomware payload found was a PAYLOAD sample targeting ESXi on Linux servers. This case illustrates the growing 2026 trend of encryptionless extortion, where attackers combine data theft, operational disruption, and threats of public exposure without relying on cryptographic file locking. Group Policy is a signed, allowlisted, SYSTEM-privileged distribution channel that most EDR tools are designed to trust, making GPO abuse particularly difficult to detect and block. The technique is not new but its weaponization for pure disruption without encryption is gaining traction across ransomware operations.
