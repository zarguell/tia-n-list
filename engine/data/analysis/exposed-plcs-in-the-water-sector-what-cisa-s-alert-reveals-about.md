CISA's July 30, 2026 alert warned of active targeting of programmable logic controllers (PLCs) in the U.S. water and wastewater sector. Threat actors are reaching internet-exposed PLCs, changing passwords to lock out operators, and altering IP addresses to disconnect devices from the network. The agency reported this activity has already caused boil water notices and forced sustained manual operations at affected utilities.

The core issue is not a specific software vulnerability but direct internet exposure of industrial control devices. CISA's advisory emphasizes that cellular modems installed by vendors or integrators may not appear in organizational asset inventories, meaning the actual attack surface is often larger than documented. ZoomEye data from September 15 shows 41,601 EtherNet/IP endpoints and 37,792 Modbus endpoints reachable from the public internet globally. CISA recommends disconnecting all PLCs from direct internet access, routing remote connectivity through VPNs or gateways, enabling password protection, and maintaining clean PLC image backups. The advisory also references Rockwell Automation MicroLogix 1400 guidance for password recovery on locked-out controllers.
