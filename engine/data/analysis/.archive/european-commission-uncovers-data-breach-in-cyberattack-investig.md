The intrusion the European Commission detected on 22 October was first described as an AI-assisted campaign using the open-source ARTEX penetration-testing framework that took internal systems offline, including the Sysper HR platform, MyPMO medical reimbursements, JSIS and translation services, with most applications later restored. On 23 November spokesperson Thomas Regnier confirmed that the same campaign also breached EU Academy, a learning platform for people outside the Commission, and leaked user names and email addresses. The scale is still under investigation, EU Academy access is suspended, the EDPS has been notified, and users received a website notice instead of direct contact. No ransom demand has been received and no actor has been named.

This is a scope inversion, not an uptime update. The first statement said there was no evidence of a breach affecting Commission staff as a whole, but the confirmed loss came from an external-facing learning portal, precisely the peripheral application class the FBI says ARTEX operators favor; its 13 November FLASH-20261113-001 counted more than 1,600 exposed consoles in early November and cited a South Korean campaign that stole data from about 67,000 people plus attempted sign-ins to more than 2,250 downstream ConnectWise tenants. An undisclosed number of EU Academy users now have names and email addresses in attacker hands, and a site notice is the only warning, which makes EU-branded course mail a strong phishing pretext. With no ransom and no attribution, this cannot be filed as ordinary crime or as espionage: CERT-EU noted on 5 November that ARTEX is public and used by multiple actors, and the FBI stresses that the same tooling serves different motives.

No CVE or vendor hotfix applies, so the response is exposure reduction and targeted hunting. Inventory internet-facing portals, legacy services and third-party integrations; restrict cloud administration, Kudu/SCM endpoints, hypervisors and backup platforms to trusted networks; disable basic publishing authentication where unnecessary; and move public learning and partner portals behind Zero Trust access. Per FLASH-20261113-001, preserve Microsoft Entra sign-in and audit records, Microsoft Purview Audit, Microsoft Graph telemetry and SIEM data, then review service-principal sign-ins by source network and look for sequential record lookups, unusual records-per-session volume, web shells in unexpected .aspx or .jsp paths, and egress to api.deepseek.com, open.bigmodel.cn, api.x.ai or the reseller xcai[.]pro. Treat the FBI's historical indicators, including 38.244.50[.]120 and the Storm-3358 WISP nodes 199.19.95[.]66 through 199.19.95[.]68, as leads to corroborate with current telemetry before blocking.

The assessment changes if the Commission publishes an account count, admits data beyond names and email addresses, or faces EDPS action over the 72-hour notification rule in Regulation 2018/1725. It also changes if investigators name an actor, confirm ARTEX was the entry path rather than a tool merely seen in the environment, or find that Sysper or JSIS data was accessed before the stated 14 January end of the EU Academy outage.
