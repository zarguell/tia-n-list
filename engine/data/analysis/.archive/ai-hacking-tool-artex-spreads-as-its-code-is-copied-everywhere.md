ARTEX is not a software vulnerability or a stand-alone AI model. It is an open-source, LLM-driven penetration-testing framework released by Autumn-27 on July 26, 2026, under AGPL-3.0, with a Go backend, Next.js console and PostgreSQL store. Planner and worker agents invoke tools such as Nmap and curl and iterate from reconnaissance through persistence and exfiltration. Its malicious use is observed, not hypothetical: CrowdStrike found it in a late-September to early-October campaign against South Korean financial organizations, using DeepSeek v4.1-flash plus GLM-5.3 and Grok 4.6. Microsoft attributed the ConnectWise Cloud Backup supply-chain compromise to Storm-0940 and later documented Storm-3358 using ARTEX with WISP, vshell and AdaptixC2. No ARTEX CVE or patched release is established; the original repository was removed on October 8 after the author announced that the project would close.

The decisive risk is trusted identity, not novelty in the AI model: a successful chain can turn one supplier's stolen app secret into persistent app-only access, mailbox and file theft, and bulk cloud-secret reads across customers. In Microsoft's October 10-14 password-spray subset, ARTEX-driven Storm-0940 traffic hit 327 accounts across eight organizations, generated more than 151,000 failures and peaked at 2,900 per minute on October 11; that burst produced no successful sign-in. The separate ConnectWise operation attempted sign-ins to more than 2,250 tenants, and Microsoft observed privileged cloud access in multiple environments in under three hours. That tenant figure measures attempted reach, not victims, and exposed consoles include authorized testers. Even so, Censys counted 1,465 public consoles on November 4 but estimated only about 815 distinct deployments after accounting for two templated hosting blocks; F5 subsequently found over 1,000 repositories and over 1,600 public consoles. The repository takedown therefore interrupts distribution, not capability, while overprivileged OAuth apps still convert one supplier compromise into cross-tenant access.

No ARTEX patch or hotfix version is established; this is not a CVE remediation. Keep affected ConnectWise application connections disabled until the vendor issues formal restoration instructions, preserve evidence, inventory Microsoft's listed app IDs including Cloud Backup `506e08f9-6e9a-4da4-942b-a099a03644ae`, and review every tenant where they were authorized. Revoke exposed credentials and sessions, remove unauthorized app credentials and grants, and investigate the published `144.172.64.60`, `144.172.64.99` and `144.172.64.128` indicators for workload identities. Hunt `EntraIdSpnSignInEvents` against `GraphAPIAuditEvents`, Entra audit and Microsoft 365 audit records from October 1, correlating `ApplicationId`, `ServicePrincipalId`, `IPAddress`, `UserAgent`, `ErrorCode` and `CorrelationId`; investigate credential additions, app-role self-assignment and unusual Graph mailbox or file access. In `CloudKeyVaultEvents`, flag successful `SecretGet` activity at Microsoft's threshold of at least 20 reads across at least three vaults per identity in 15 minutes. For any authorized ARTEX deployment, remove public console ingress on default ports 8787 and 8790, restrict administrative access and LLM-endpoint egress, and run only a reviewed, pinned build in an isolated environment.

Watch for ConnectWise's formal restoration criteria and evidence that vendor credential rotation plus tenant-level cleanup has ended unauthorized app-only access. Also watch whether recovered ARTEX sessions show agents selecting unreleased vulnerabilities on their own; the documented campaigns chiefly accelerate known exploitation, credential attacks and trust abuse. That distinction would determine whether this remains an identity and exposure-management problem or becomes a broader patch-emergency problem.
