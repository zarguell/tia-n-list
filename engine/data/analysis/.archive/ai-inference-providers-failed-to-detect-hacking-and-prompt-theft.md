Dragos reports that attackers breached at least three AI inference providers and stole customer prompts, and that none of the providers detected the intrusions before Dragos notified them. Chief research officer HD Moore's team sent roughly two million prompts to widely used AI services between July and November, embedding trackable bait such as URLs, hostnames, and credentials including AWS keys, then monitoring for reuse. The largest case involved about 200 AWS test keys sent to one provider and stolen in late October.

Some of those keys came from requests the provider rejected before a model ran, which Moore reads as evidence that the attacker copied requests at the provider's front door rather than deeper in the inference stack. Dragos saw the attackers validating stolen keys and trying to use them to obtain data from the associated accounts. Moore says the bait prompts were processed alongside real customer requests that were almost certainly copied too. Watch for provider disclosures and for signs that captured prompts are being used in follow-on intrusion or extortion.
