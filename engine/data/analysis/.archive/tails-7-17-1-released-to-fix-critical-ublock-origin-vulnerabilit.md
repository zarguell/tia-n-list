The Tails project shipped 7.17.1 on November 17 as an emergency release that removes uBlock Origin from its bundled Tor Browser. The upstream defect is GHSA-cmqw-9r2w-6wv2, a High-severity one-click universal XSS in the Firefox MV2 extension affecting uBlock Origin 1.75.2 and lower. Raymond Hill published the fix in 1.75.4 on November 3, launching the element picker only with a verified secret token, and added filter validation and war:-filter blocking in 1.76.0 on November 8. A malicious page can recover the per-install extension UUID through a scriptlet, embed the picker at opacity zero, and put a newline in the `opt` field so a second trusted scriptlet filter is created for an arbitrary domain. Tails rates Standard and Safer as affected, Safest as unaffected, and says it knows of no use in practice.

The risk is not an ad blocker glitch but a cross-origin write into the browser Tails users rely on to keep sites separate: one click on an attacker page can leave a trusted scriptlet that runs arbitrary JavaScript in webmail or banking tabs opened later, and the planted rule survives until a filter recompile even though a Tails restart clears it. That volatility cuts both ways for responders. Because the story carries no CVE, CVE-driven patch dashboards will not surface it; GHSA-cmqw-9r2w-6wv2 is the only identifier that will match, and Firefox installations still on uBlock Origin 1.75.2 or lower outside Tails remain exposed even after Tails removes its copy. Removal also strips ad and tracker blocking from the privacy distro, trading one layer of protection for certainty about this specific path.

Upgrade Tails USB sticks to 7.17.1, using the automatic path from 7.0 or later and a manual upgrade if that fails, then confirm uBlock Origin is absent from Tor Browser. Elsewhere, move uBlock Origin to at least 1.75.4 and preferably 1.76.0; Tor Browser's Safest level is an interim containment because the release note says it is unaffected, not a replacement for the upgrade. The hunt is configuration-level rather than packet-level: inspect My Filters for an unexpected trusted scriptlet rule such as `google.com##+js(trusted-rpnt, script, ...)` planted next to a resource type like `image`, remove it and force a recompile, and treat its presence as an incident signal given the stated lack of known exploitation. The 7.17.1 image also carries Linux kernel 6.12.117, which is a quick upgrade verifier.

Two developments would change this assessment: credible exploitation telemetry that contradicts the no-use-in-practice statement, which would turn a contained design flaw into active targeting of Tor users, or the return of uBlock Origin to Tails, which should be checked against 1.75.4 and the 1.76.0 hardening before it is treated as safe. A CVE assignment would mostly change detection coverage rather than the underlying risk, since the advisory and version bounds already define the exposed set.
