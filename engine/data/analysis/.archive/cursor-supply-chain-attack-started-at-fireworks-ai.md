Cursor says its October 29 supply chain incident began with the compromise of AI inference platform Fireworks AI, which was hacked twice in October. In the first intrusion, internal Fireworks credentials exposed stored secrets and account and billing data. In the second, a third-party cloud provider was breached, Fireworks development machines were reached and customer data was stolen. A Cursor access key issued to Fireworks was among the stolen credentials and was used to copy data from a read-only storage bucket, including unredacted credentials for SaaS tools used by Cursor.

From those tools the attacker reached selected archived non-production Cursor source repositories, limited customer support data and email conversations, usage metrics and contact details, summaries of some coding agent conversations, and credentials users had sent to support. Two modified copies of the anthropic.claude-code extension, versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313, were offered on the Cursor VS Code Extension Marketplace for roughly 11 hours. Cursor stresses that Anthropic itself was not compromised.

The incident shows how a vendor access key and unredacted bucket contents can turn one provider breach into a marketplace distribution path. Watch for the final scope statement on affected customers, extension version pinning in developer environments, and how Cursor and Fireworks handle secrets in support and storage systems.
