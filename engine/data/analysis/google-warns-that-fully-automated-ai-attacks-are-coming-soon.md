Google Threat Intelligence Group's fourth-quarter 2026 AI threat tracker, published 16 to 17 November 2026, documents what it calls the first observed case of a multi-agent system running vulnerability discovery and exploitation end to end without human input. The system, named exploit_agent in its configuration, was found on an exposed staging server. It continuously compares web application code before and after vendor patches, infers the likely flaw from the diff, drafts an exploit and executes it. Russian-language comments in the code, including a configuration note telling the user to enter API keys for "auto-exploitation", underpin the attribution; Google did not name a group or state. The same server held victim data from French media, entertainment and education sites, plus scripts for reconnaissance, password guessing, webshell installation and removal of rival webshells.

The wider report shows agentic frameworks being used at scale. GTIG names ARTEX, CAIRN, CyberStrikeAI and PentAGI, with CAIRN seen in an attack on the US aerospace sector and in activity against advanced manufacturing, defense, and civil and human rights organizations. Suspected North Korean operators used Claude Code to deliver malware. The FBI's 13 November FLASH says ARTEX has been weaponized since at least August 2026 across financial services, government, technology, defense and healthcare, with intrusions moving from targeting to privileged access in under three hours, personal data theft affecting about 67,000 people in South Korea, and access to more than 2,250 ConnectWise Cloud Backup tenants. Reported indicators include the ARTEX host 38.244.50[.]120, WISP nodes 199.19.95[.]66 through 199.19.95[.]68, and the reseller domain xcai[.]pro; more than 1,600 ARTEX consoles were publicly exposed by early November.

GTIG estimates that actors will be able to run low-complexity attacks with little human interaction within three months and that sophisticated, fully autonomous attacks are likely within six months. Those are projections, not current capability across the threat landscape, but the exploit_agent case and the ARTEX intrusions are observed activity. Watch for repetitions of the patch-diff pipeline against other software ecosystems, further FBI or vendor FLASH updates, and whether defenders see the compressed, iterative activity patterns called out in the FBI guidance. Restricting Kudu and SCM endpoints, reviewing cloud administration and Entra credential additions, and hunting short bursts of automated requests are the concrete defensive actions named in the reporting.
