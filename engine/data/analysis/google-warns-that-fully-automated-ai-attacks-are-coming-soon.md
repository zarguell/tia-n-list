Google Threat Intelligence Group reports a threshold change in offensive AI use: attackers are running multi-agent frameworks that find and exploit flaws with little or no human input. The clearest case is a system named exploit_agent, found on an exposed staging server. It compared software before and after patch releases, inferred the likely vulnerability from the diff, drafted an exploit and executed it automatically. The same server held victim data and scripts for reconnaissance, password guessing, webshell installation and removal of rival webshells. Russian-language comments in the configuration pointed to auto-exploitation API key entry, and victim data indicated French media, entertainment and education sites.

GTIG also observed CAIRN used against the US aerospace sector and in attacks on advanced manufacturing, defense and civil society groups, with suspected North Korean operators using Claude Code to deliver malware. The FBI's November 13 FLASH describes ARTEX being abused since at least August 2026, intrusions reaching privileged access in under three hours, personal data theft affecting about 67,000 people in South Korea and access to more than 2,250 ConnectWise Cloud Backup tenants. Reporting counts more than 1,600 publicly exposed ARTEX consoles by early November, even after the original GitHub repository was removed on October 8. Google names ARTEX, CAIRN, CyberStrikeAI and HexStrike AI as frameworks in use.

Google expects low-intervention attacks to spread within three months and sophisticated fully autonomous operations within six. The near-term defensive problem is speed: automated reconnaissance and exploitation compress the window between patch release and attack. Watch for new patch-to-exploit pipelines, more FBI or vendor advisories on these frameworks, and whether the projected timelines hold in incident data.
