Google Threat Intelligence Group reports that threat actors have moved from experimenting with AI in isolated attack steps to running agentic workflows at scale. The report names financially motivated and state-sponsored groups using AI-based offensive frameworks for vulnerability discovery and exploitation. Examples include CAIRN in an attack against the U.S. aerospace sector and suspected North Korean hackers using Claude Code to deliver malware. GTIG names ARTEX, CAIRN, CyberStrikeAI and HexStrike AI as open-source pentesting suites now being abused for automated attacks.

The FBI warned in an advisory last week that multiple actors had weaponized ARTEX since August, hitting organizations in financial services, government, technology, defense and healthcare. Google describes adaptive attacks against internet-exposed endpoints with low-complexity flaws, where agents can chain discovery and exploitation at machine speed rather than requiring a human operator for each step.

GTIG's forecast is concrete: within three months it expects unsophisticated attacks needing little or no human interaction, and within six months it expects sophisticated, fully autonomous agentic attacks to emerge. Those are projections, not observed capability. What to watch is whether those timelines hold and whether the same frameworks keep showing up in intrusions, especially against public-facing systems where a known low-complexity flaw is enough for an agent to gain a foothold.
