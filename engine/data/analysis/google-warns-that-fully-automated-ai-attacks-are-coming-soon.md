Google Threat Intelligence Group says it has seen the first case of an AI framework carrying out vulnerability discovery and exploitation without a human in the loop. The system, named exploit_agent in its configuration, was found on an exposed staging server and continuously compared web application code before and after patches, inferred the flaw, drafted an exploit and ran it. The same server held stolen victim data and scripts for internal reconnaissance, password guessing, webshell installation and removal of rival attackers' webshells. Russian-language comments in the code and an API-key configuration file are the basis for that attribution; data on the server pointed to French media, entertainment and education sites. GTIG did not name a specific group or state.

The wider report documents adoption of multi-agent offensive frameworks including ARTEX, CAIRN, CyberStrikeAI, PentAGI and HexStrike AI. CAIRN was used in an intrusion against the US aerospace sector and in activity against advanced manufacturing, defense and civil rights organizations. Google expects low-complexity attacks needing little human interaction within three months and sophisticated fully autonomous attacks within six months. The FBI's 13 November FLASH adds corroboration: ARTEX has been weaponized since at least August 2026, some intrusions went from targeting to privileged access in under three hours, personal data of about 67,000 people in South Korea was stolen, and attackers reached more than 2,250 downstream ConnectWise Cloud Backup tenants.

The FLASH gives defenders concrete indicators: ARTEX host 38.244.50[.]120, WISP nodes 199.19.95[.]66 through 199.19.95[.]68 and reseller domain xcai[.]pro. CrowdStrike's account of the South Korean financial sector campaign matches that host and model setup, including Claude Code session histories and DeepSeek v4.1-flash as the ARTEX backend. Recommended actions include restricting Kudu/SCM and cloud administration endpoints, reviewing new Entra credentials and hunting bursts of iterative activity. A 17 November Hill piece repeats Google's warning about autonomous attacks against banks, airlines and utilities.

The useful distinction is between systems that assist an operator and the patch-to-exploit pipeline that GTIG says ran end to end. Watch for further victim disclosures tied to exploit_agent, for whether the three-month and six-month projections hold, and for additional FLASH indicators as ARTEX forks and exposed consoles proliferate.
