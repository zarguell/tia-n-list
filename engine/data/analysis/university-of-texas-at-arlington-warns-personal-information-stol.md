The University of Texas at Arlington has confirmed a data breach in which personal information was stolen during a cyberattack. In a statement to Recorded Future News, a university spokesperson said an investigation had confirmed that data was taken but declined to specify what categories of information the threat actors obtained. Follow-up reporting ties the intrusion to a Qilin ransomware attack against the university.

The incident matters as another instance of the ransomware double-extortion pattern hitting a public research university, where the stolen-data threat rather than encryption is the leverage point. Qilin has been among the more active ransomware operations targeting education and municipal victims, and a confirmed exfiltration at a large Texas university system raises the stakes for students, staff, and research affiliates whose records may be in scope. The university has not published the number of affected individuals or the data types involved.

What to watch is whether UTA issues formal notification letters that quantify the affected population and name the exposed data elements, and whether Qilin lists the university on its leak site with a countdown or sample files. Any state attorney general disclosure or credit-monitoring offer would confirm the scale. Attribution to Qilin currently comes from a single event-details aggregator, so treat the group naming as reported pending confirmation from the university or a primary ransomware leak posting.
