Capcom confirmed a breach of its CAPCOM ID membership service, disclosing on October 22, 2026 that an unauthorized third party accessed 11,774,247 records worldwide. The company detected suspicious activity on October 19 and started blocking access while investigating. The exposed dataset includes names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, dates of birth, and gameplay data, with 196 accounts seeing unauthorized password changes and 43 records also exposing birth dates, gender, nicknames, and countries. Capcom states no credit card information was compromised and urges users to watch for phishing.

The incident affects the shared CAPCOM ID service used for web services and some online games. Capcom reset the 196 changed passwords, notified affected customers sequentially, reported to authorities, and kept services running. The breach timeline runs from detection on October 19 through confirmation and disclosure within days, showing a rapid incident response, though the initial access vector remains undisclosed.

For security teams, the scale (11.7 million emails and account metadata with last-login IPs) makes this a high-value target for follow-on phishing and credential-stuffing attempts. No CVEs or exploit code are tied to the intrusion; it appears to be direct unauthorized access rather than a vulnerability exploitation. Monitor for impersonation campaigns referencing Capcom and remind users that legitimate notices will not ask for passwords.
