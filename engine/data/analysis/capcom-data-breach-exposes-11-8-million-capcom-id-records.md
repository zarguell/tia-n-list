Capcom confirmed that a third party gained unauthorized access to its CAPCOM ID online membership service, affecting 11,774,247 account records worldwide. The company detected suspicious activity on October 19, 2026 and immediately began blocking access, then published its notice and apology on October 22, 2026. Exposed fields include names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, dates of birth, and gameplay data, with login passwords changed without owner consent on 196 accounts. Capcom states no credit card information was compromised.

The exposure is not a small leak: 11.7 million real-world identities with email addresses and dates of birth is a ready-made phishing and account-takeover kit, even without passwords, because CAPCOM ID ties into online titles such as Street Fighter 6. The 196 password changes are a small but telling signal that the attacker had write access to at least some accounts, not just read access to a database export. This story matters more than the daily breach noise because the affected population is gamers with weakly guarded accounts and the notification channel itself is fragile.

Defenders and users should treat any Capcom-branded message with suspicion until verified through official channels, since the breach notification emails failed DMARC checks and were routed through an external delivery service (cuenote.jp). Hunt for suspicious login events and password-reset emails dated around October 19, 2026, rotate CAPCOM ID passwords and enable multi-factor authentication where available, and monitor for phishing that leverages the exposed email and date-of-birth data. Organizations should also flag cuenote.jp-sourced mail claiming to be Capcom as high-risk during this window.

Watch whether Capcom discloses the intrusion vector and whether the weak email authentication on its notifications leads to active phishing campaigns against the 11.7 million exposed addresses. A follow-up confirming credential stuffing against CAPCOM ID or linked game accounts would raise the severity well beyond data exposure.
