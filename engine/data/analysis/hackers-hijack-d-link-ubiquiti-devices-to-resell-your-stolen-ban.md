A threat actor tracked as skynox7 is proxyjacking edge devices by exploiting known flaws in D-Link ShareCenter NAS units (CVE-2024-3272 and CVE-2024-3273), Ubiquiti UniFi Video NVRs via CVE-2021-44228 (Log4j), and SAP NetWeaver Visual Composer via CVE-2025-31324, then installing the legitimate Pawns.app bandwidth-sharing client without consent. GreyNoise identified at least 143 compromised devices across 34 countries, with Hikvision and Dahua DVR/NVR units also targeted through unspecified vulnerabilities. In some cases the attacker added an SSH key as backup access, and GreyNoise attributed the payout account skynox7@proton.me with exploitation source 209.99.190.80.

The exposure is not just bandwidth theft: residential proxy nodes sit inside trusted IP space, so traffic from unrelated intrusions rides on victims' addresses, and enterprise users have already been observed authenticating from the same residential proxy IP. GreyNoise found the campaign only because a live product demo surfaced malicious IPs in an unnamed organization's Entra ID sign-in logs, which means the 143 confirmed devices are a floor, not a census, and defenders relying on geolocation-based impossible-travel rules will not catch it. That is why this story matters more than a routine proxyware advisory: the same mechanism turns a consumer NAS into a stealthy hop for someone else's intrusion.

Defenders should patch or retire the named products, hunt Entra ID and other authentication logs for multiple users sharing a single residential proxy IP, and scrutinize device identifiers, session identifiers, and User-Agent strings rather than geolocation alone. On edge devices, look for unauthorized Pawns.app binaries and unexpected SSH authorized_keys entries; GreyNoise offers a free check-my-IP service to see whether an IP has touched their Global Observation Grid. Network egress restrictions on legacy NAS and NVR units, plus firmware updates on D-Link ShareCenter and Ubiquiti UniFi Video, close the primary entry paths.

The assessment changes if GreyNoise publishes the full IoC set (binary hashes, additional exploitation IPs) or if the actor expands beyond the three named CVEs into newer edge firmware; conversely, a vendor advisory confirming patched firmware for the D-Link and Ubiquiti models would materially shrink the exposure window.
