A G7 cybersecurity working group is pressing governments and industry to accelerate migration to post-quantum cryptography. The report, prepared at the June 2026 G7 Summit in France, frames quantum computing as a near-term business risk rather than a distant theoretical threat, calling on leaders to stop treating it as solely a cryptographic concern.

The working group flagged "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks as an active today: adversaries can collect encrypted data now and decrypt it once quantum-capable machines exist. The report also warned that quantum computers could compromise authentication and digital signatures, undermining secure communications and legal contracts. Organizations were urged to begin inventorying cryptographic dependencies and prioritizing migration of critical systems and data.

This matters because it elevates PQC from a standards-body discussion to a heads-of-state mandate. Enterprises that have been deferring crypto-agility work are now facing explicit government pressure, and the timeline framing (not just critical infrastructure but all sectors) signals that sector-specific regulation may follow. Watch for national PQC migration mandates and procurement requirements in the next 12-18 months.
