Akira claimed three ransomware attacks between August 26 and September 4, 2026, targeting small to mid-sized firms in healthcare, manufacturing, and energy across three countries. Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery (Brockton, MA) was hit with a claimed 14GB data exfil including patient records and employee PII. PA-ID GmbH (Germany), a mechanical construction and industrial software firm, was threatened with 119GB of corporate data release. Stransky Heiz-Mess-Regeltechnik GmbH (Austria), a heating technology company, was hit with a 50GB exfil claim covering employee data, contracts, financials, and NDAs.

The healthcare target is the most sensitive: patient data including medical records creates HIPAA exposure and direct harm risk for the Brockton practice. The two European manufacturing targets suggest Akira is maintaining its pattern of hitting industrial firms with large data volumes. All three claims follow Akira's standard playbook of publishing target descriptions and specific data categories (passports, NDAs, financials) to pressure negotiation.

What to watch: whether the healthcare breach triggers a formal breach notification under Massachusetts or federal law. Akira's volume of claimed exfil (14GB to 119GB across three targets in nine days) suggests active exploitation of common entry vectors, likely VPN or RDP. The European targets may trigger GDPR notifications if confirmed. Monitor for whether any of these victims engage with Akira or go silent, which often indicates negotiation.
