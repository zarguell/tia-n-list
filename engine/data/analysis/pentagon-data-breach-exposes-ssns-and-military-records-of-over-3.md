The Pentagon confirmed a breach of a Defense Manpower Data Center file-sharing system that exposed unencrypted personal records. A Department of War official put the affected population at 2.76 million living people and 294,000 deceased people. The exposed fields varied by individual and included Social Security numbers, names, dates of birth, contact details, demographic data and, in some cases, military occupational specialty.

Unauthorized users accessed files between October 2025 and July 16, 2026. DMDC discovered the vulnerability on July 16, patched the file-sharing system and restored it, then began incident-response and privacy procedures. The notification letter said a small number of unauthorized users were involved and that there was no indication of misuse at the time. It did not identify the file-sharing product or describe the vulnerability. The Pentagon offered 12 months of credit monitoring through IDX.

The combination of Social Security numbers, biographical data and military job details creates risks beyond credit-card fraud, including targeted phishing and long-term identity misuse. The November 27 Military.com follow-up emphasized risks beyond credit but added no new breach mechanics in the supplied text. Watch for evidence of misuse, updates to the affected count, identification of the vulnerable product and details on how the exposure persisted for roughly nine months.
