The Pentagon has confirmed a breach of a Defense Manpower Data Center file-sharing system that exposed unencrypted personal data on more than three million people: 2.76 million living individuals and roughly 294,000 deceased. Records included Social Security numbers, names, dates of birth, contact details, demographic data, and in some cases military occupational specialty information. Unauthorized users accessed the server between October 2025 and July 2026, about nine months, through a vulnerability in the file-sharing system that let external users reach stored files. DMDC discovered it on July 16, 2026, patched the flaw, restored the system, and started notifications with a letter dated September 18; the department reports no indications of misuse so far.

The exposure is severe because SSNs were stored unencrypted alongside enough biographical data for durable identity theft, and the affected population includes people with security clearances whose personnel details are directly useful for targeting and impersonation. The notification letter does not name the file-sharing product or describe the vulnerability, so the broader exposure question, meaning how many other DMDC or DoD systems run the same software, is open.

Pairing this with the separate FBI job portal theft makes September a bad month for US government personnel data holdings. Watch for the count of notified individuals against the 3.05 million total, litigation and congressional interest in why PII sat unencrypted on an internet-reachable file share, and any attribution if threat actors begin using the MOS data for spear phishing of defense personnel.
