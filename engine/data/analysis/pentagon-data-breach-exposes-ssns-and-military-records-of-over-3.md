The Pentagon has confirmed a breach of a Defense Manpower Data Center (DMDC) system exposing personal data of more than three million people tied to the Department of Defense: 2.76 million living individuals and about 294,000 deceased. Unauthorized users had access to a vulnerable file-sharing server from October 2025 until discovery on July 16, 2026, roughly nine months. Records included Social Security numbers, names, dates of birth, contact details, demographic data, and in some cases military occupational specialty information, stored unencrypted.

The entry point was a vulnerability in a file-sharing system that let external users reach files on the server; DMDC patched the flaw and restored the system on discovery, and notification letters dated September 18 started going out in late September. The department says it has no indications of misuse so far and has not named the affected product or the actors. No ransomware group has claimed the data.

For military personnel, an SSN paired with biographical and occupational data is a permanent identity-theft kit and a targeting packet: national security experts cited in coverage raised counterintelligence concerns, since such data supports phishing, coercion, and identification of service members in sensitive roles. Watch for the data surfacing on leak markets, follow-on fraud against notified personnel, and whether Congress presses DoD on why a personnel file server holding unencrypted SSNs stayed vulnerable for nine months.
