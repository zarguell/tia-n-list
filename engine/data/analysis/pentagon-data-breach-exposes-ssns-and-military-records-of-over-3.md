The Pentagon confirmed a breach of a Defense Manpower Data Center information system that exposed unencrypted records of 2.76 million living people and roughly 294,000 deceased individuals, more than three million in total. Unauthorized users accessed a file-sharing server between October 2025 and July 2026 through a vulnerability in the file-sharing system. DMDC discovered the issue on July 16, patched it, and restored the system.

Exposed records varied by individual and included Social Security numbers alongside names, dates of birth, contact details, demographic data such as race and sex, and military occupational specialties. The notification letter, dated September 18, does not name the affected file-sharing product or describe the vulnerability. The Department of War says it has no indications of misuse and is offering 12 months of credit monitoring through IDX.

Time reports the accessed data covered both living and deceased service members, and the combination of SSNs with biographical and military data creates long-term identity-theft and targeting risk for personnel. Watch for identification of the file-sharing product and vulnerability, whether the incident draws the same remediation mandates as other federal breaches, and any evidence of misuse surfacing after the nine-month access window.
