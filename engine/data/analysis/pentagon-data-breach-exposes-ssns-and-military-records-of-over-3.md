The Pentagon has confirmed a breach of a Defense Manpower Data Center (DMDC) file-sharing system that exposed unencrypted personal data for more than three million people: 2.76 million living individuals and about 294,000 deceased. Unauthorized users accessed files between October 2025 and July 2026 through a vulnerability that let external users reach files on a vulnerable server. DMDC discovered it July 16, 2026, patched the flaw, and restored the system. Notification letters dated September 18 went out to affected people.

Exposed data varies by individual and includes Social Security numbers paired with at least one other identifier: names, dates of birth, contact details, demographic data such as race and sex, and in some cases military occupational specialty. The combination of SSN plus biographical data creates long-term identity-theft risk, and records were reportedly unencrypted. Officials say there is no indication of misuse so far, and the Pentagon is offering 12 months of credit monitoring through IDX. The notification letter does not name the file-sharing product or describe the vulnerability, and a Department of War official gave the affected counts to CNN; Military Times first reported the incident and noted around four million DoD personnel may be affected per two people familiar.

Two things stand out. The access window ran roughly nine months before discovery, on one of the department's central personnel-data systems, which is a poor result for detection capability on high-value infrastructure. And the root cause was a file-sharing system vulnerability rather than a sophisticated intrusion, meaning basic patching and external exposure review are again the difference.

Watch for the affected product and vulnerability details, litigation and congressional interest given the scale, whether any of the accessed data surfaces in criminal markets, and how the death records exposure is handled, since SSNs of deceased people feed long-running fraud schemes.
