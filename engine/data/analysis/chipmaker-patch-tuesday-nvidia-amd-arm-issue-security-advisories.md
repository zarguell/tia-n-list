The September 9 roundup packages work that was mostly finished before it existed. AMD-SB-6034, dated September 8, covers CVE-2026-43603, a NULL pointer dereference (CVSS 6.9, CWE-476) in the AMD Linux GPU kernel driver that lets a local user crash the kernel through a graphics memory clear operation, but the fixes shipped in July: Radeon Software for Linux 26.13 on July 20 and Linux GPU Driver 31.40 on July 15 for Instinct MI210 through MI355X, with EPYC Embedded and Ryzen Embedded patches not due until October 2026. Arm's Mali GPU Driver Security Bulletin (article 111552) is dated September 8 and lists ten CVE ids across the Bifrost, Valhall, and 5th Gen GPU Architecture kernel and userspace drivers, one more than the roundup's nine. NVD records also dated September 8 name the two Triton Inference Server flaws the article left unidentified: CVE-2026-16497, excessive iteration to denial of service, and CVE-2026-47625, missing authorization at CVSS 7.5 leading to information disclosure, data tampering, and denial of service, affecting Triton for Linux through the 26.03 and 26.06 trains per Canada's AV26-900. None of the three vendors reports exploitation.

The reason this batch matters more than its DoS-heavy scoring suggests is that the three vendors bracket the AI compute stack: Triton serves inference, Instinct and Radeon accelerate it, Mali renders on the device edge. CVE-2026-47625 is the one to weight, because missing authorization on an inference server with data tampering in its impact list means an attacker who can reach the service can alter what the model reports, and downstream consumers treat those outputs as ground truth. Arm's CVE-2026-9034 and CVE-2026-11891 remove the local-user precondition entirely: they are reachable from a non-privileged process including via WebGL or WebGPU, so any web page is an attack vector against unpatched Mali devices. The affected tables make the breadth concrete: essentially every Ryzen, EPYC, Athlon, and Radeon line on Linux for AMD, and for Arm a Bifrost kernel driver affected from r12p0 onward with no vendor-neutral fixed version, which leaves that remediation entirely to device vendors.

Actions, in order of proof. On AMD Linux GPU hosts, verify the driver level: 26.13 or later Radeon Software, 31.40 or later on Instinct, and until the October embedded drop, restrict local users on EPYC Embedded and Ryzen Embedded systems, reviewing kernel crash traces on GPU compute nodes for crashes triggered by graphics memory clear operations. For Triton, inventory deployments including containers, move off the 26.03 and 26.06 trains to the September 2026 bulletin release, and restrict network reach to the inference service, since CVE-2026-47625 requires reaching it; treat unexplained writes or altered responses from inference endpoints as evidence of tampering, not just errors. For Mali, the fix is r56p0 or later kernel and userspace drivers, delivered through OS and device vendor updates, so the defender task is an inventory of Mali-based devices by GPU generation and driver level, with WebGL/WebGPU-capable browsers on unpatched units treated as exposed to CVE-2026-9034 and CVE-2026-11891.

Two developments would change this assessment. Scanning or exploitation telemetry against Triton endpoints would turn CVE-2026-47625 from patch hygiene into incident response, given how many inference servers sit flat on internal networks with no authentication tier in front of them. And October brings the test of whether the lagging tails close: AMD's EPYC Embedded and Ryzen Embedded fixes, and whether Mali device vendors actually ship r56p0, since a Bifrost fix that exists only at Arm and never reaches handsets leaves the oldest exposure standing.
