A threat actor using the ShinyHunters handle posted a listing on an underground forum claiming to have stolen 335 million verified Binance user records, described elsewhere as 335.9 million lines tied to users in roughly 170 countries. The alleged data included Know Your Customer documents, internal infrastructure information, account passwords, crypto wallet private keys, transaction history, emails, and phone numbers. The seller asked for $900,000 payable in Bitcoin or Monero and framed it as two options: pay or leak. ThreatMon, Brandefense, and Dataconomy reported on the listing, and Brandefense noted similar advertisements dating back to October.

Binance denied the breach. A spokesperson told BleepingComputer the company found no evidence that Binance systems, customer KYC data, credentials, wallet private keys, or internal infrastructure were compromised in connection with the listing, and called it likely a scam. Binance said it does not hold private keys for users' external wallets, does not store passwords in plaintext, and pointed to inconsistencies in the claim. Brandefense said on November 3 it had reviewed four versions of related Telegram advertisements without opening or testing any sample; Binance's customer-support account published its initial assessment on November 8. One update mentioned a separate October 29 wallet attack involving about $6.34 billion in digital assets as unrelated.

What to watch: whether any sample data surfaces behind the forum paywall, whether verified records appear in later leak sites, and follow-on phishing or social-engineering campaigns built around KYC-style lures.
