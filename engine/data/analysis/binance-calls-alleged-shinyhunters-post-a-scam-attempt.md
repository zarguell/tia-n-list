A threat actor using the "ShinyHunters" handle on a newly emerged underground forum listed alleged Binance data covering 335 million users, including KYC documents, account passwords, wallet private keys, transaction history, emails, and phone numbers, for $900,000. Binance responded that it found no evidence of a breach and considers the listing a scam, noting that the exchange does not store user passwords in plaintext, does not hold private keys for external wallets, and no longer operates in some of the referenced regions. Past similar listings have involved infostealer data harvested from users' own devices rather than a compromise of Binance systems.

The distinction matters for defenders and users: the claim mixes a vendor breach with endpoint credential theft, and the two require different responses. Even if Binance's systems were not compromised, the listing can still drive phishing, credential stuffing, and extortion attempts against customers whose data was collected by infostealers.

Watch items include whether any data samples validate the leak, independent reporting on the forum and seller, Binance's follow-up on customer notifications, and any password-reset or MFA-hardening guidance issued to users.
