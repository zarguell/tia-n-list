The French Ministry of the Economy and Finance disclosed a data breach affecting 678,000 individuals after an attacker accessed the General Directorate of Public Finances (DGFiP) systems. The breach was discovered after a threat actor using the handle "ZeroBytes" claimed the attack and listed a stolen database for sale on the PwnForums hacking forum on August 12. The stolen data includes tax information such as reference tax income, family quotient, and withholding tax rates, plus cadastral data on addresses and property sizes. For businesses, exposed data includes company names and SIREN numbers.

The French Finance Ministry confirmed that in-depth investigations established the access points were used to consult and extract data concerning 678,000 individuals and professionals before being interrupted. User IDs and passwords were not compromised, and online accounts were not accessed. The DGFiP notified the French Data Protection Authority (CNIL) and is working with ANSSI (the National Cybersecurity Agency of France) to assess the full scope.

Targeting a national tax authority gives an attacker a high-value dataset for identity theft and social engineering. Tax income figures, family quotient data, and property records are precisely the kind of information used to make phishing campaigns convincing. The SIREN numbers for businesses enable corporate impersonation. Organizations should monitor for fraudulent tax communications impersonating the French government and advise affected individuals to watch for identity theft attempts using their financial data.
