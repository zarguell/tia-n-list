CloudSEK's TRIAD team gained administrative access to BigBear 2.0, a phishing-as-a-service platform built on a rebranded Evilginx2 framework, and found it had been used to bypass multi-factor authentication at 258 organizations and harvest more than 5,000 Microsoft 365 credentials. The service managed 42 VPS nodes, mostly on Vultr, and was first identified in June 2026.

The panel data showed 5,137 records from 3,331 unique IP addresses across 40-plus countries. India had the highest victim count, followed by France, Saudi Arabia, New Zealand, and Germany. The haul included 1,032 plaintext passwords, 4,148 session cookies, and 474 completed authentications where MFA was successfully bypassed. At least five affiliates received stolen credentials and session data through dedicated Telegram bots in real time, operated by someone using the alias "General Boss."

Unlike standard credential phishing, BigBear 2.0 uses an adversary-in-the-middle proxy that sits between the victim and Microsoft's legitimate login page, relaying traffic while capturing passwords and session tokens. The "offy" template proxies the Microsoft sign-in flow exactly, so the victim completes MFA normally while the attacker receives a usable session cookie. These cookies can be replayed to access Exchange Online, Teams, SharePoint, and OneDrive without triggering additional authentication challenges. The multi-tenant affiliate model with an 80/20 revenue split lowers the barrier for less technical operators to run effective MFA-bypass campaigns at scale.
