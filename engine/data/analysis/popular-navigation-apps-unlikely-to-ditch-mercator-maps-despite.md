Popular navigation applications are unlikely to adopt the UN-backed Equal Earth map projection despite a recent UN resolution endorsing it as an educational alternative to the Mercator projection. The Mercator projection, which distorts the size of landmasses away from the equator, remains the standard for web mapping due to its conformal properties that preserve angles and shapes at local scales.

The Equal Earth projection, developed in 2018, presents landmasses at their true relative sizes while maintaining reasonable shape accuracy. The UN resolution highlights the Mercator projection's historical role in exaggerating the size of northern hemisphere continents relative to those near the equator. However, navigation apps rely on the Mercator projection's mathematical properties for tile rendering and zoom-level calculations, making a switch technically complex.

Google Maps, Apple Maps, and Waze all use Mercator-derived projections for their tile systems. Switching would require rearchitecting tile servers and potentially breaking existing integrations. The resolution's educational value is acknowledged, but practical adoption in navigation software remains unlikely in the near term.
