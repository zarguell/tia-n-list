Google reports that Chrome's anti-abuse systems cut unwanted notifications on Android by more than 7 billion per day in the first quarter of 2026. Notification abuse has increasingly been used to distribute scams, malware, phishing, and fraudulent payment requests. Chrome uses a layered defense model: automatic revocation of permissions for unused sites, behavioral analysis across networks of related sites including coordinated service-worker activity, and rate limiting that caps disruptive sites at 1,000 messages per minute.

The numbers are vendor-reported and hard to independently verify, but the mechanism is the point. Notification spam is a real abuse channel that defenders can no longer treat as a minor annoyance, and browser-level revocation plus cross-site behavioral analysis is the emerging countermeasure. For users, the practical guidance is to treat notification permission as a privilege and revoke it for sites that are not actively used; permission grants remain a primary vector for this kind of abuse.
