UpGuard research found around 16,000 databases hosted on Supabase, the Postgres-based development platform, exposing some degree of sensitive data to the public web. TechCrunch's Zack Whittaker reported the findings, and the coverage frames the exposure wave as a side effect of AI-generated and vibe-coded applications shipping with insecure defaults.

This is a customer misconfiguration story rather than a Supabase platform vulnerability, which is what makes it broadly relevant. Development platforms that make databases one click away from the internet keep converting application velocity into accidental public data exposure, and AI-assisted coding is scaling that failure mode.

Watch for vendor guidance on locking down Supabase projects and for follow-up research quantifying how many of the exposed databases hold regulated personal data. The 16,000 figure is a floor, since it counts databases with some degree of exposure found during one scan.
