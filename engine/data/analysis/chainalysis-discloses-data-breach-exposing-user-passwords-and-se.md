Chainalysis, the blockchain analytics platform whose customers include law enforcement and national security teams, disclosed on November 6, 2026 that an external actor using offensive agentic techniques exposed names, usernames, password hashes and live API tokens for every user on its platform. The intrusion runs back to October: Chainalysis detected an actor accessing internal systems and internal tools, and on October 30 it disclosed that non-sensitive customer data (user IDs, full names, email addresses, organization role labels) was accessed, with no evidence of credential compromise. That assessment collapsed on November 2, when the company discovered the all-user credential exposure, and on November 4 it confirmed an on-path attack had captured a subset of customer passwords. Reactor, Data Solutions, KYT, address screening, and the login page were taken offline.

The decisive fact is the live tokens: unlike password hashes, a live authentication token lets an attacker impersonate any Chainalysis user immediately with no cracking required, and the exposed identities include investigators working criminal and national-security cases. The five-day gap between the October 30 all-clear on credentials and the November 2 discovery means customers who relied on the initial statement had no chance to rotate secrets while the actor likely already held them. That reversal from no-evidence to all-users-exposed is why this story matters more than the other routine disclosures: it is a trust inversion at a company whose product is attribution, and the blast radius is every provisioned user rather than a segmented subset.

Defenders should assume every Chainalysis user password and API token was compromised: force password resets, rotate all API keys, enable two-factor authentication, and submit trusted IP addresses for the allowlist Chainalysis is enforcing before restoring access. Hunt for unusual-activity alerts and unauthorized access in KYT and address screening, review the KYT accounts Chainalysis is directly notifying as accessed without authorization, and watch for password-reset phishing leveraging the exposed email and role data, since the on-path attack shows the actor can intercept authentication traffic. Treat any workflow built on exposed Chainalysis tokens as burned until credentials are reissued.

Watch whether Chainalysis revises its finding that there is no evidence investigations data, graphs, cases, or attribution data was accessed or manipulated, since live tokens weaken that assessment. Also watch for a follow-on phishing campaign against Chainalysis customer contacts using the stolen names, emails, and organization labels, which would confirm the actor is monetizing the exposed non-sensitive data.
