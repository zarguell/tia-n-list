Cloudflare's H1 2026 DDoS threat report documents a sharp escalation in large attacks: 935 network-layer DDoS attacks exceeding 1 Tbps were mitigated in the first half of the year, a more than fivefold quarter-over-quarter increase from 130 in Q1 to over 800 in Q2. The attack-vector mix shifted away from botnet floods toward reflection and amplification, with DNS-based attacks accounting for 34.3% of the traffic mix. Cloudflare also mitigated a record-breaking attack peaking at 31.4 Tbps and 200 million requests per second.

The takeaway for defenders is that multi-terabit attacks are no longer rare or reserved for the largest targets. Reflection and amplification vectors mean comparatively small botnets can generate enormous volumes, and DNS infrastructure is a growing choke point. Organizations should validate that their upstream capacity and DDoS mitigation provider can absorb sustained terabit-scale floods, and treat DNS infrastructure as a priority asset for hardening.
