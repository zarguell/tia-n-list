Used-car platform CARS24 has filed a police complaint alleging that approximately 3,100 customer records were extracted by insiders and sold to a competing business, Direct-Cars, and external dealers for roughly Rs 1,000 per lead. The data included names, phone numbers, vehicle details, appointment information, inspection reports, and pricing data. The company estimates commercial losses of Rs 5.70 crore (roughly $680,000).

The FIR names five individuals: Preeti, Pallavi, Kalpana, Sahil Rana, and Mohit. The alleged theft occurred between March and August 2026. CARS24 discovered the leak when contacts associated with a rival business began calling potential sellers who had been in touch with CARS24. The data is particularly valuable because it identifies people actively considering selling vehicles, allowing competitors to bypass standard lead-generation costs.

This case highlights insider threat risks in platforms handling sensitive consumer data. The commercial value of the stolen information, combined with the structured pricing model (Rs 1,000 per lead), suggests this was not a random breach but a deliberate, profit-driven operation. No independent verification of the loss estimate has been established by police or courts.
