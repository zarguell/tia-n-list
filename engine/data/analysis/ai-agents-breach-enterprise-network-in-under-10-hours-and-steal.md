Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 documented an incident where a threat actor used frontier AI models and agentic frameworks to breach an enterprise environment, harvest root credentials, and hijack cloud AI infrastructure in under 10 hours. The attack chain compressed operations that typically require multiple human operators and roughly two weeks into a single machine-speed campaign.

The attacker employed an automated loop where specialized AI agents monitored tool output, evaluated options, executed actions, and replanned next steps. Evidence included parallel LLM calls, structured Markdown files for inter-agent context sharing, and custom automation scripts believed to be AI-generated. The agents collectively executed more than 50 MITRE ATT&CK techniques across reconnaissance, credential collection, privilege escalation, and persistence.

Crucially, the actor did not use novel vulnerabilities or exceptionally creative tradecraft. The existing attack methods were applied at a higher operational tempo than human operators typically achieve. The human operator retained responsibility for setting objectives and consequential decisions, while individual agents performed tactical execution. The campaign began by exploiting a publicly accessible web service to establish an initial tunnel. This incident signals a shift where AI-assisted automation becomes the differentiator rather than zero-day exploitation or novel techniques.
