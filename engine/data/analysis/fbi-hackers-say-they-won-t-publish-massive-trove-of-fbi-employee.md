ShinyHunters, tracked by Mandiant as UNC6240, infiltrated the FBI job portal at fbijobs.gov and claims data on roughly 60,000 current and former employees and applicants, two to three terabytes in total. On Monday the group told 404 Media it never intended to publish, saying the operation existed only to force the FBI to retract or amend its May 15 IC3 warning (PSA I-051526-PSA) that placed sextortion in the group's portfolio; days earlier it had threatened release within five days. The haul is not a bluff. Reuters matched at least 10 of the 5,000 sample records against credit bureau and prior leak data, including Director Kash Patel, and the BBC reviewed health screening extracts carrying blood and urine results and specific allergy entries. ShinyHunters says it came in through CVE-2026-35273, the unauthenticated Java deserialization RCE in Oracle PeopleSoft PeopleTools 8.61 and 8.62 (CVSS 9.8) that Oracle patched out of band on June 10, 2026 after zero-day exploitation dating back to May 27.

Refusing to publish is not de-escalation, it is a change of monetization. Names, home addresses, Social Security numbers, duty assignments, spouse names, and medical records on the people who run US counterintelligence stay fully exposed whether or not they hit a leak site, and the data keeps its value for fraud, blackmail, forged law enforcement identities, and resale precisely because it stays out of public view, where a dump would trigger resets, takedowns, and credit freezes. The deeper argument sits in the vector: the FBI's own hiring front door ran the same PeopleSoft stack that Mandiant watched ShinyHunters mass-exploit in September across dozens of organizations, deliberately going after sites that answered the June disclosure with WAF rules instead of the patch. A breach of a US law enforcement agency by a three-month-old n-day in HR middleware is the clearest signal yet that unpatched PeopleSoft should be treated as already compromised, and that a group demanding the deletion of a government report as its ransom is experimenting with extortion by policy ultimatum.
