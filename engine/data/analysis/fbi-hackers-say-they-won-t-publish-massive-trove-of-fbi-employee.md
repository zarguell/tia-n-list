ShinyHunters claimed on September 22 that it breached the FBI jobs portal at fbijobs.gov, citing anger over the FBI's May 15 IC3 PSA that groups victims into sextortion categories, and demanding retraction or correction within a week. Reuters matched sample records against credit bureau and prior leak data with at least 10 matches, including Director Kash Patel, and the samples carried names, home addresses, Social Security numbers, and duty assignments. The BBC reviewed samples from FBI agent health screenings that included blood and urine test results and specific allergy information. The group claims the trove covers around 60,000 current and former FBI employees, two to three terabytes, and says the intrusion came through an Oracle PeopleSoft flaw it has attacked since mid-June.

On Monday the group told 404 Media it never intended to publish the data and that its sole objective was forcing deletion or correction of the FBI report. The leak site entry was removed, the portal was left defaced with ASCII art of the Pokemon Umbreon, and an FBI statement left open whether the attackers hit a third party or FBI systems. CNN reports the bureau is reviewing counterintelligence exposure and monitoring for misuse of stolen personal data across prior breaches.

The group's pivot carries mounting legal risk. Dutch police arrested 24-year-old Pepijn van der Stap, previously convicted in 2023 as Umbreon, on or around September 16, and he was due before the Rotterdam District Court on September 29. KrebsOnSecurity links the group's aggressive pivot to new leadership under a teenager known as Rey of ScatteredLapsussHunters, and cites a Mandiant estimate that ShinyHunters is on track for nearly 100 million dollars in 2026 extortion payments.

The claimed intrusion vector remains under active attack. Mandiant and GTIG report renewed mass exploitation of CVE-2026-35273 (CVSS 9.8, PeopleSoft PeopleTools 8.61 and 8.62) by UNC6240, bypassing literal-path WAF rules with requests to /%50SEMHUB/ instead of /PSEMHUB/, and deploying x.jsp and u.jsp web shells, the SIDEEYE backdoor via a trojanized EV-signed Ple64.exe installer, Neo-reGeorg tunnels, and MeshCentral agents with C2 at 162.219.30.165 on TCP 3333 and 3334. Watch for FBI confirmation of the entry point, further identifications of group members, and continued victim notifications across education, healthcare, and government.
