ShinyHunters' claim that it stole data on FBI employees checks out. The group infiltrated the FBI's job portal and claims data on almost all agents; a 5,000-entry sample was judged plausible by Reuters, and the BBC reviewed samples from agent health screenings including blood and urine test results and specific allergy information. The full trove reportedly covers around 60,000 current and former FBI employees across two to three terabytes, with full names and addresses that expose agents to fraud, blackmail, targeted attacks, and forged law-enforcement identities. On Monday the group told 404 Media it never intended to publish; its stated objective was forcing the FBI to delete or correct a report attributing sextortion to the group.

The claimed vector is a vulnerability in an Oracle cloud storage system, possibly PeopleSoft, a flaw the group says it has attacked since mid-June. Google's Mandiant renewed its warning that ShinyHunters is mass-exploiting the PeopleSoft vulnerability CVE-2026-35273, CVSS 9.8, bypassing WAF rules with URL encoding such as /%50SEMHUB/ in place of /PSEMHUB/. That connects this breach to a much larger victim set, with 100+ organizations previously reported targeted, primarily in higher education.

Enforcement pressure is building: Dutch police arrested a 24-year-old Amsterdam man mid-September, previously convicted in 2023 as "Umbreon" for data theft and extortion, and the FBI portal was left defaced with ASCII art of the Pokemon character. CNN reports operators are being urged to review counterintelligence exposure and monitor for misuse of the stolen personal data. Watch for the FBI's account of the intrusion path, whether the PeopleSoft link is confirmed, and any second-order targeting of agents whose screening data leaked.
