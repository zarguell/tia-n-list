Burger King Japan disclosed that attackers accessed a server managing official smartphone app member information between October 2 and October 7, 2026, with the investigation concluding on October 13. The breach exposed 11.18 million records containing names, dates of birth, gender, email addresses, phone numbers, and order history. The company stated the server did not store credit card numbers and that no user credentials were accessed, and it blocked the source of access upon discovery while reporting to Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission.

The scale of 11.18 million members in a single-market restaurant app makes this one of the larger consumer database exposures in Japan's retail sector this quarter, and the absence of financial data does not neutralize the risk: the dataset pairs birth dates and order history with direct contact details, which creates a high-value profile for targeted social engineering and identity fraud against a known, geolocated user base. The timeline also matters; with unauthorized access occurring over six days in early October and disclosure following a completed internal investigation on October 13, defenders should treat any similar restaurant-app telemetry patterns in the region as potentially pre-disclosure activity rather than unrelated noise.

Defenders should verify patch states for any member-management server infrastructure, restrict outbound access from app-backend hosts to only required endpoints, and hunt for access between October 2 and October 7 using logs tied to the management system. Monitor for references to the exposed data types in phishing campaigns targeting Japan and watch for claims of responsibility or data-market listings referencing Burger King Japan app records.

Watch for the Japan PPC's regulatory response timeline and any threat-actor publication of sample data that would confirm exploitation beyond simple access. A second development that would change the assessment is evidence that credentials or payment tokens were also present on the breached server despite the company's current denial.
