Burger King Japan disclosed a breach affecting 11.18 million records from its official smartphone app. Unauthorized access occurred between October 2 and October 7, 2026, and was confirmed on October 13. Exposed data includes names, dates of birth, gender, email addresses, phone numbers, and order history. The company states that credit card numbers were not stored on the compromised server and that it found no evidence user credentials were accessed. The breach has been reported to Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission.

The scale of the exposure, over eleven million customer records, makes this a significant consumer data incident, though the absence of payment card and credential data limits immediate financial fraud risk. The leaked information is still valuable for phishing, social engineering, and account takeover attempts using email and phone combinations. Customers should expect targeted scam messages referencing Burger King orders or membership details.

Affected users should be wary of unsolicited emails or calls claiming to be from Burger King Japan, and the company should monitor for misuse of the leaked contact data. Regulators will likely assess notification timeliness and data minimization practices, given the volume of personal information retained.
