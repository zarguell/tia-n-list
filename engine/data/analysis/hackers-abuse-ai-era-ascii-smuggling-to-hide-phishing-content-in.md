Microsoft documented a phishing campaign that repurposed ASCII smuggling, a technique originally identified in AI prompt-injection research, to evade email security filters at scale. The attackers inserted invisible Unicode tag characters (U+E0020 and others from the U+E0000-U+E007F range) into financial lure words like "funding," "loan," and "credit." To human readers the words appear normal, but at the processing level the invisible characters split the strings, causing keyword-based detection tools to fail to match the suspicious terms. Microsoft observed the campaign reaching more than 2.3 million messages per day.

The technique exploits the Unicode Tags block, which contains characters that are invisible in standard fonts and most email clients. While ASCII smuggling became well known through research on AI prompt injection, this campaign used it purely for traditional phishing evasion rather than instructing AI models. A single invisible character placed between characters in a financial word breaks exact-string matching in detection engines without affecting human readability.

Two outlets covered this story, giving it a score of 4.0. The volume (2.3M messages/day) and the cross-pollination from AI security research into commodity phishing operations make this notable. Watch for adoption by other phishing groups and for email security vendors updating their filters to account for Unicode tag characters in message content.
