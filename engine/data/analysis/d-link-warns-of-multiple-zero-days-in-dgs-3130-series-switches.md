D-Link disclosed multiple unpatched security flaws in its DGS-3130 series managed Gigabit stackable switches, used by small and medium businesses and in metro Ethernet deployments. The bugs were found by Spectrum Community Solutions network engineer Giresh Kumar Enugu and sit in the D-Link Discovery Protocol, covering authentication, access control, and information handling. D-Link said exploitation could let attackers obtain sensitive device data, make unauthorized credential or configuration changes, leak SNMP communities, or cause service disruptions and device resets. The flaws were tested on DGS-3130-30TS rev B1 switches, and D-Link is still investigating whether other D-Link switching platforms and firmware versions are affected, including SSH service security, operating-system credential safeguards, and firmware encryption or embedded cryptographic keys.

There is no patch yet and D-Link has not published CVE IDs or a fix timeline. The advisory, SAP10523, dates from November 6. Because the issues touch credential storage and firmware encryption, an exposed switch on a management network could give an attacker footholds beyond the device itself, including SNMP community leakage and configuration changes that persist after a reboot. The affected models are edge-facing network infrastructure, so unpatched flaws in discovery and access control are directly reachable from the local network segment.

Administrators running DGS-3130 switches should watch D-Link support announcements for CVE assignments and firmware patches rather than assuming coverage. Until fixes arrive, they should restrict access to switch management interfaces, review SNMP community strings, and monitor for unexpected configuration changes or device resets. D-Link's next advisory will determine whether the scope stays with DGS-3130-30TS or widens to other platforms.
