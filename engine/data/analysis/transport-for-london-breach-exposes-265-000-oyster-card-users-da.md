Transport for London says about 265,000 Oyster photocard customers may have had account details exposed. The incident was detected on 16 November after customers received unexpected emails asking them to create or validate accounts, sent from TfL's own mail systems as a result of the compromise. The photocard website was taken offline, the Information Commissioner's Office was notified, and breach notifications went out. Names, email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses, dates of birth and security questions with answers were affected. TfL says passwords, bank and payment card details, journey information, photos, and names and dates of birth of children with Zip Oyster cards were not compromised, and it has found no evidence of misuse so far.

The affected accounts span Zip Oyster cards for ages 5-10, 11-15 and 16+, 18+ Student, 18-25 Care Leaver, 60+ London, Apprentice and Veterans photocards. Cards remain valid for travel, while renewals and journey-history requests go through Customer Services on 0343 222 1234; Freedom Pass applications are handled by London Councils and are unaffected. TfL is asking all photocard customers to change passwords and security questions and answers, including on other services that use the same details. The Register notes TfL has not disclosed how the intruders entered its systems.

The security questions and home addresses are the most useful material for follow-on fraud, even without card data. The incident also echoes the September 2024 TfL attack, when more than 7 million customers were notified, about 5,000 were contacted over possible bank detail access, two teenagers were later charged and authorities linked the attack to Scattered Spider. No link between the two incidents had been reported at publication; a February 2025 ICO decision found formal regulatory action was not proportionate for the earlier breach.

Watch for TfL disclosing the entry path, confirmation of whether any of the exposed records were used, and an ICO assessment. The fact that the lure emails came from TfL's own mail systems is a concrete artifact for customers checking their inboxes.
