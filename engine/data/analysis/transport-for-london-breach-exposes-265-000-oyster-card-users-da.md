Transport for London detected suspicious activity on the Oyster photocard website on November 16 after customers received unexpected emails asking them to validate or create accounts, sent from TfL's own mail systems. The site was taken offline and TfL notified the Information Commissioner's Office. About 265,000 photocard customers may have had account details exposed.

The potentially exposed fields are names, email and home addresses, phone numbers, dates of birth, and security questions and answers. TfL said passwords, bank and payment card details, journey information, photos, and names and dates of birth of children with Zip Oyster cards were not compromised. All photocard customers were told to change passwords and security questions. Affected card types include Zip Oyster for ages 5 to 10, 11 to 15 and 16 plus, 18+ Student, 18-25 Care Leaver, 60+ London, Apprentice and Veterans photocards.

TfL has not disclosed how the attackers got in and said it had no evidence at publication that the data had been misused. Renewals and journey-history checks moved to Customer Services while the site stayed offline, and Freedom Pass applications were unaffected because London Councils handles them. The Register compared this with the September 2024 TfL attack, which led to more than 7 million customer notifications, about 5,000 heightened-risk contacts and later charges against two teenagers, with links to Scattered Spider. There is no evidence the two incidents are related. Watch for a disclosure of the entry path and for any misuse of the exposed security answers.
