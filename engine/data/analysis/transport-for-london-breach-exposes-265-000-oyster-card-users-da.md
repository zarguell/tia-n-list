Transport for London says suspicious activity on its Oyster photocard website on 16 November 2026 led to unauthorised access to web account data for about 265,000 customers. The visible trigger was emails from TfL's own mail systems telling existing photocard holders to create accounts; TfL took the site offline and says the incident is contained. Reported exposed fields are names, email and home addresses, phone numbers, dates of birth, and security questions with answers. TfL says passwords, bank and payment card details, journey information, photos, and names and dates of birth of children with Zip Oyster cards were not accessed. It has not disclosed the intrusion method and says no misuse has been seen so far.

This matters beyond the headline count because the exposed combination of home address, date of birth and security-question answers is the material used for identity verification and account recovery on unrelated services, so a password-free breach at TfL can still weaken accounts TfL never touches. The 265,000 estimate covers Zip Oyster cards for ages 5-10, 11-15 and 16+, 18+ Student, 18-25 Care Leaver, 60+ London, Apprentice and Veterans photocards, including children and people dependent on discounted travel. The exposure lands against 3.53 billion passenger journeys recorded in 2025/26 and a September 2024 TfL breach in which more than 7.1 million customers were notified and about 5,000 were told bank details might be involved; two teenagers were later charged and that attack was linked to Scattered Spider, though there is no evidence the two incidents are connected.

No patch or CVE applies. Rotate photocard passwords and security questions and answers, and change credentials or answers reused on other services; TfL says it will require photocard customers to make those changes. While photocard.tfl.gov.uk is offline, renewals and journey-history checks go through Customer Services on 0343 222 1234, and top-ups remain available at London Underground and National Rail ticket machines. Hunt in mail-system records for account-creation messages sent to existing photocard holders on 16 November 2026; TfL has published no log path, telemetry field or actor name, so an IoC-led hunt is not supportable yet.

Watch for TfL publishing the entry vector or revising the 265,000 figure and field list, and for the ICO moving from notification to a formal investigation; it declined enforcement action over the 2024 breach in February 2025. A second trigger would be phishing or account-recovery attempts that reuse the security-question answers or the account-creation email theme against photocard holders and other services.
