Transport for London says around 265,000 Oyster photocard customers may have had account details exposed in a breach. TfL discovered the incident on 16 November 2026 after customers reported emails asking them to create an account, sent from TfL's own mail systems as a result of the compromise. The Oyster photocard website has been taken offline and affected customers are receiving breach notifications. Existing photocards remain valid for travel, and renewals or journey-history checks are being handled through Customer Services.

TfL's current assessment is that passwords, bank and payment card details, journey information, and the names and dates of birth of children with Zip Oyster cards were not compromised. The authority also says it has found no evidence so far that the exposed data has been misused. Oyster photocards give eligible passengers free or discounted travel, and TfL recorded 3.53 billion passenger journeys in 2025/26, including 1.78 billion bus journeys and 1.20 billion Tube journeys, which gives the exposed customer set a large public-transport context.

An Infosecurity Magazine post picked up the same notification, noting that the attackers' visible move was to send account-creation emails from TfL systems. What to watch is whether TfL revises the scope of exposed fields or the 265,000 figure, and whether the data surfaces in targeted phishing against photocard holders. The account-creation email theme is a concrete lure that other organisations should expect to see reused.
