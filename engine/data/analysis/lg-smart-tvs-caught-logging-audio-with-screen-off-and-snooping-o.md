LG smart TVs were caught logging audio while the screen appeared to be off and scanning local network devices. During bench testing, researchers found the TV could capture audio in what should be a powered-down or standby state. The TVs also performed reconnaissance on other devices connected to the same local network, probing for information about what else is on the home network.

This is not the first time LG has faced privacy scrutiny over TV data collection. Earlier reports documented LG TVs tracking user activity even when offline, and separate findings showed the TVs could listen in during standby mode. The pattern suggests persistent, intentional data collection behavior rather than isolated bugs. LG has previously been accused of egregious invasion of privacy over its TV data collection practices.

For consumers, the practical implications are clear: a smart TV in the living room may be an always-on surveillance device. Network administrators and privacy-conscious users should consider blocking TV traffic at the firewall level, placing IoT devices on isolated VLANs, or simply not connecting smart TVs to the internet. The audio logging with screen off is particularly concerning because users have no visual indication that recording is occurring.
