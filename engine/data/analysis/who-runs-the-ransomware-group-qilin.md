KrebsOnSecurity attributes the Qilin ransomware-as-a-service operation to a 36-year-old man in St. Petersburg, Russia, working under the handle "Haise" after a decade of operational security mistakes that tie the persona to a real identity. Qilin has run since September 2022 and is now the most active ransomware affiliate program tracked, claiming at least 365 victim organizations in the past three months per NCC Group's Q3 report published 2026-10-20. The program runs a double-extortion model, hands affiliates at least 80 percent of any ransom, and unlike most Russia-based crews it recruits English-speaking affiliates while its leader has claimed on Telegram that it has worked alongside Scattered Spider and RansomHub.

The reason this unmasking matters is not the body count, which was already public, but that it converts an anonymous high-volume brand into an attributable target. Naming one operator behind 365 attacks in a quarter gives sanctions, seizure, and prosecution a single point of pressure that a RaaS label never did, and the operational history that produced the identification is the same material investigators use to justify takedowns. It also matters because Qilin leads The Gentlemen by only seven attacks, 365 to 359 in Q3, so the "most active" crown is fragile; a successful disruption against a named operator could hand the top spot to a rival rather than end the threat, and defenders should assume the affiliates and tooling persist even if the brand stumbles.

Concrete defense follows from the fact pattern. Because Qilin works through affiliates rather than a fixed intrusion set, prioritize the double-extortion choke points: egress monitoring for large outbound transfers, backups kept offline against encryption, and rapid response to the initial access brokers who feed these crews. Treat any confirmed Qilin or Scattered Spider or RansomHub overlap as a cue to hunt for hands-on-keyboard activity, not just commodity ransomware, since the affiliate pool carries intrusion tradecraft from multiple brands. The NCC Group monthly threat pulses are the running scoreboard for where Qilin is concentrating, with industrials the top-targeted sector at 838 attacks in Q3, so sector risk scoring should weight industrials accordingly.

Watch two developments. First, whether a named-operator attribution produces an indictment, sanctions designation, or infrastructure seizure, which would test whether the identification is actionable or merely descriptive. Second, whether Qilin's victim volume or branding shifts after exposure; a sudden rebrand or a drop below The Gentlemen would signal the group is absorbing pressure rather than continuing unchanged, and that shift is the real measure of whether the unmasking achieved anything.
