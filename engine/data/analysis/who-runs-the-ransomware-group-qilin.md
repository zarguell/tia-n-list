KrebsOnSecurity identifies the administrator of Qilin, the most active ransomware-as-a-service program, as a 36-year-old man in St. Petersburg, Russia, operating under the handle "Haise" since September 2022. The attribution rests on a decade of operational-security mistakes. Qilin pays affiliates at least 80 percent of extortion proceeds and runs double extortion, stealing data before encrypting systems. It recruits English-speaking affiliates and has collaborated with Scattered Spider and RansomHub.

NCC Group recorded Qilin as the most active threat group of Q3 2026 with 365 attacks, edging The Gentlemen at 359. It led February with 15 percent of recorded attacks, was the most prolific operator in April, and accounted for 14 percent of Q2 attacks (79 in June). Industrials was the top-targeted sector in each period: 31 percent in February, 28 percent in April, 30 percent in Q2, and over 838 in Q3. Global ransomware rose 3 percent quarter-on-quarter to 2,229 attacks in Q2 and 23 percent in Q3, though September fell 26 percent to 821.

The unmasking may not disrupt operations, since Qilin's affiliate model depends on recruited partners rather than the administrator alone. Defenders should watch for Qilin recruitment on RAMP and Telegram and prepare for double-extortion data theft. The identification could support law-enforcement pressure, but affiliates are likely to continue deploying the payload.
