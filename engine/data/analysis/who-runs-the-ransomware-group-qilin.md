KrebsOnSecurity traces the administrator of the Qilin ransomware-as-a-service program to a 36-year-old man in St. Petersburg, Russia, citing a decade of operational-security mistakes. Qilin, operating since September 2022, is described as the most active ransomware affiliate program, with at least 365 victim organizations claimed in the past three months. The group runs a double-extortion model, exfiltrating data before encryption and threatening publication, and offers affiliates at least 80 percent of ransom proceeds; it also collaborates with English-speaking affiliates and has worked with groups such as Scattered Spider and RansomHub.

NCC Group data cited across the coverage shows Qilin's dominance predating the unmasking: most active threat group in February 2026 (15 percent of attacks), Q2 (14 percent, 79 attacks in June), Q3 (365 attacks, edging The Gentlemen at 359), with ransomware up 23 percent in Q3 over Q2 and September falling 26 percent month on month to 821 attacks. Industrials was the most targeted sector throughout (31 percent in February, 28 percent in April, 30 percent for Q2, over 838 attacks in Q3), and North America was the most impacted region in April.

What to watch: confirmation of the individual's identity by law enforcement, and whether exposure of the administrator disrupts Qilin's affiliate recruitment and operations. The group's continued lead over The Gentlemen by a narrow margin (365 versus 359 attacks in Q3) suggests affiliates can shift quickly if Qilin's trust erodes.
