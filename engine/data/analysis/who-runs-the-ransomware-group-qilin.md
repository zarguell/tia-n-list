Qilin has emerged as the most active ransomware affiliate program in operation, claiming at least 365 victim organizations in the past three months. Operating since September 2022 from a Russia-based base, the group runs a ransomware-as-a-service model that pays affiliates at least 80 percent of any extortion payment and practices double extortion by stealing data before encrypting systems and threatening to publish it.

The operation's administrator, known as "Haise," is profiled as a 36-year-old man living in St. Petersburg, Russia, identified through operational security mistakes made over roughly a decade. Qilin's openness to partnering with affiliates outside the usual Russian ransomware circles, combined with its high payout share, helps explain the volume of attacks attributed to it.

Defenders should treat Qilin as a high-frequency, financially driven threat rather than a targeted espionage actor. Watch for new victim postings on its leak site, changes to affiliate recruitment, and any law-enforcement or sanctions action that could disrupt payment channels or hosting infrastructure.
