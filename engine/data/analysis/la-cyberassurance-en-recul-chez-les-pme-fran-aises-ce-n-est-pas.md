Cyber insurance adoption among French SMEs is declining sharply, according to a Hiscox report published September 15. The share of French small and medium enterprises with a dedicated cyber policy fell from 33.6% to 27.6% in one year, while 45% now have no cyber coverage at all, up from 39% in 2025. Among uninsured firms, 12% say they do not plan to purchase coverage, up from 9% a year ago.

The financial impact of attacks tells a different story. French SMEs that suffered cyberattacks reported average losses of $50,489 (roughly EUR 45,900) over the past 12 months. Payment fraud was the most common consequence at 40% of incidents, followed by human error (26%), primarily through phishing and social engineering. 66.8% of incidents caused less than one day of disruption, but the overall average outage reached 24.5 hours when severe incidents are included.

The broader effects are significant: 96% of attacked firms said the incident affected their strategy or finances, with growth delays (36%), valuation impact (33%), and postponed tech investments (31%) cited most frequently. On the human side, 37% of employees reported high stress, 27% reported degraded workplace atmosphere, and 25% of companies saw increased sick leave. Nearly half of French decision-makers (49%) view data poisoning as the top emerging cyber threat over the next five years.
