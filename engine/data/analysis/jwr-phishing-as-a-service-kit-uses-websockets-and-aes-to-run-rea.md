JWR is a previously undocumented phishing-as-a-service (PhaaS) framework that enables real-time banking and payment fraud through operator-led victim interaction. Unlike traditional credential harvesting, JWR streams victim keystrokes to attackers over AES-CTR-encrypted WebSocket channels, allowing operators to watch victims type card numbers, passwords, and one-time codes in real time. The framework was identified by Cisco Talos targeting banking and payment-service customers in Singapore, the UAE, and Southeast Asia.

The toolkit uses SMS lures themed around unpaid road-toll notices, postal charges, and parcel-delivery fees. Victims who click the malicious link land on cloned websites resembling transport authorities, couriers, banks, or payment platforms. JWR operators can then guide victims through fake login, payment, and verification pages designed to steal credentials, card data, identity information, and OTPs. The real-time WebSocket connection gives attackers immediate feedback on victim actions.

This represents an evolution in phishing infrastructure from passive credential collection to active, operator-driven fraud. The AES-encrypted WebSocket channel adds operational security for attackers while enabling the kind of real-time social engineering previously limited to vishing campaigns. Financial institutions in the targeted regions should monitor for JWR-related indicators and update fraud detection models to account for this real-time interaction pattern.
