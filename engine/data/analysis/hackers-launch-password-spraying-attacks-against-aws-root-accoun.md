Datadog Security Labs documented a password-spraying campaign targeting AWS root user accounts at more than 150 organizations between July 24 and August 23, 2026. The attackers generated repeated failed authentication attempts, with most affected organizations seeing a median of two attempts and some up to eight. No confirmed successful login has been identified, leaving the ultimate objective unknown. The affected organizations spanned multiple industries and geographies, suggesting opportunistic rather than targeted activity.

The campaign routed authentication requests through a proxy-backed infrastructure spanning multiple countries and autonomous systems. Datadog classified these sources as hosting providers, residential proxy networks, or anonymization services, making IP-based blocking unreliable. Two consistent browser user-agent strings were associated with the suspicious attempts. The lack of successful logins does not reduce the risk: failed attempts against the root account signal active reconnaissance, and organizations that share credentials across services may already be exposed. AWS root accounts carry unrestricted access to billing, IAM, and all service configurations, making them the highest-value identity in any AWS environment.
