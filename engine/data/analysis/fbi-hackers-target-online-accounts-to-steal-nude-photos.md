The story covers an FBI public service announcement warning that cybercriminals are targeting adults' and children's social media and other online accounts to steal sexually explicit images or videos. The FBI states that attackers use stolen content to blackmail victims, sell it on criminal marketplaces, and share victims' personal information (names, dates of birth, emails, phone numbers, and social media usernames) with other criminals who use it to pressure victims into providing more content. The same day, the FBI and the National Collegiate Athletic Association issued related warnings that student-athletes are also targeted by these sexual exploitation schemes.

The significance is the repeat-victimization pattern: stolen explicit content is posted or sold on criminal marketplaces, often with the victim's personal information and without their knowledge, leading to harassment, sextortion, stalking, and other targeted attacks. This is a standing threat class rather than a single campaign, so the practical value is the FBI's recommended defenses for individuals.

Watch for updates to the FBI's PSA with specific threat actor or campaign detail, and for institutional adoption of the guidance by schools and athletic programs. The event is a public service announcement with no specific campaign, actor, or breach named.
