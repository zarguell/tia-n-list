Trezor confirmed that a cyberattack on Brevo, a marketing technology company used for sending newsletters, exposed the personal information of approximately 347,000 Trezor customers. Hackers exploited a flaw in Brevo's system that allowed access to 138 Brevo accounts, enabling the sending of phishing emails that appeared to come from Trezor. The phishing emails contained malicious links designed to steal wallet backup passwords, which could lead to irreversible cryptocurrency theft. One email subject line referenced a "Critical Security Alert: STM32 Entropy Vulnerability" to appear legitimate.

This is Trezor's second data breach notification in two months. In August, a breach of shipping partner ShipMonk exposed personal data of 81,000 customers and led to physical "wrench" attacks where criminals sent fake Trezor communications via mail with QR codes to steal passwords. Trezor emphasized that its products, wallets, and account systems were not compromised in either incident, but warned that customer email addresses may be targeted in future phishing attempts.

The incident highlights the growing risk of third-party data vulnerabilities in the cryptocurrency industry, where customer data is managed by external service providers. Brevo acknowledged that the hackers' access was "not properly scoped" and "wrongly granted" to all organizations reachable by the compromised accounts. Trezor is reevaluating its vendor relationships. The pattern of supply chain compromises targeting crypto users, combined with the irreversible nature of cryptocurrency theft, makes this a high-impact incident for affected users.
