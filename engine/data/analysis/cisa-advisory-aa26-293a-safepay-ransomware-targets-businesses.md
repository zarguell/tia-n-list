CISA advisory AA26-293a, released with the FBI, US Secret Service, Germany's Landeskriminalamt Rheinland-Pfalz, and the UK National Crime Agency and North West Regional Organised Crime Unit, details SafePay ransomware activity observed through FBI, USSS and LKA investigations as recently as August 2026. SafePay has been highly active since August 2024, targeting businesses and critical infrastructure across North America, South America, Europe and Australia, and the advisory states the group has impacted at least 600 separate victims and was among the most active ransomware groups in 2026. Initial access typically comes from credential exposure, brute force password attacks and exploitation of VPN access, and the actors specifically use IP addresses from the VPN provider Mullvad to reach victim networks through phished account profiles.

Once inside, the actors establish persistence by taking over or creating accounts and moving laterally with legitimate tools, using PsExec and WinRM for remote execution and ShareFinder.ps1 and SharpShares.exe to enumerate network file shares for exfiltration. They commonly disable security software, change user passwords to make valid logins impossible, and conceal tooling through packing and virtual machine software protection, and they have been observed using a UAC bypass technique that creates an elevated process through a specified component object model alongside token impersonation. The advisory also documents automated exploitation of internet-exposed systems for initial access.

The advisory's practical emphasis is on unglamorous basics: inventory internet-facing VPN and edge devices, enforce phishing-resistant multifactor authentication so phished credentials alone are not enough, and verify backups against the specific behaviors SafePay relies on, since the actors disable security tools and alter passwords precisely to prevent recovery. The cross-border spread and the 600-victim count make this a prioritization problem rather than a research topic, and defenders should treat the advisory's indicators of compromise as directly usable detections for endpoints, identity and network telemetry.
