SafePay ransomware is not a commodity RaaS affiliate but a self-contained group that has compromised at least 600 organizations since August 2024, according to the joint CISA AA26-293A advisory published October 20, 2026. The actors combine ChaCha20 encryption with deliberate backup-system destruction, use legitimate tools including WinRAR, FileZilla, RClone and WinSCP for exfiltration, and target critical infrastructure across North America, South America, Europe and Australia. Because the advisory confirms SafePay was among the most active ransomware groups in 2026, defenders should treat any unpatched VPN or exposed backup server in these sectors as an immediate exposure.

A successful SafePay intrusion means both data theft and operational paralysis, not just encrypted endpoints, since the group explicitly destroys backups to obstruct recovery. The October 20 advisory notes impacts across multiple critical infrastructure sectors, and the 600-victim count spans more than two years of sustained operations rather than a single campaign spike. That persistence, combined with the joint FBI, CISA, USSS, German LKA and UK NCA attribution, makes SafePay a strategic threat rather than a routine ransomware incident.

Defenders should apply the detection rules and IOC lists from AA26-293A, restrict access to backup systems, segment networks hosting WinRAR and RClone activity, and hunt for unusual WinSCP or FileZilla exfiltration patterns. Patch VPN and remote-access infrastructure immediately, review logs for manipulation of Windows Defender settings as noted in associated research, and assume any unpatched endpoint in critical sectors is a viable target. Monitor the advisory feed for updates to the STIX and PDF releases, since the October 20 release may be revised as new victim telemetry arrives.

The assessment changes if CISA updates AA26-293A with new sectors affected, if a victim count significantly above 600 is confirmed, or if the group shifts to RaaS-style affiliate operations that would expand the attacker pool beyond the core group.
