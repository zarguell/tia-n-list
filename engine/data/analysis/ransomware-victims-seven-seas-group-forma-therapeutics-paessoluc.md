On October 3, 2026 the ransomware.ninja leak-site tracker logged three new extortion claims: SEVEN SEAS GROUP attributed to a brand calling itself spirals, Forma Therapeutics Holdings to nightspire, and Paessolucoes to panzer v. Each entry is a claim posted to a dark-web leak site, not a confirmed breach, and the story carries no CVE because there is no vulnerability to patch. The nightspire attribution carries the most history: SOCRadar profiled the group in May 2025 as a double-extortion operation that emerged in early 2025, Picus Security counted at least 64 organizations across 33 countries hit between March and June 2025, and Proven Data documents its playbook of WinSCP, MEGACmd, or Rclone exfiltration followed by a Go-based encryptor obfuscated with RC4 and XOR, stretched sleep intervals, and log manipulation. Symantec's separate reporting characterizes spirals as a previously unseen family used in a June 2026 double-extortion attack on a South Asian IT services company, delivered through an IIS web shell and PsExec.

The aggregate is the real signal. The update events tacked onto this story add 61 more claims in four days: 1 to lockbit5, 20 in one batch including qilin, safepay, and insomnia, 5 to everest, 2 to endzone and storm, and 33 to the gentlemen, which puts the four-day total at 64 claims across at least 11 distinct ransomware brands. No defender can act on 64 unverified claims, but the batches work as a weekly survey of which affiliate brands are actively monetizing, and that survey is what a flat event list never says out loud: an organization should map its sector and region against the brands currently churning victims, the way everest is working through small firms and the gentlemen just posted 33 names in a single day. The September claims that open this story, the University of San Francisco and PharmaEssentia both attributed to the gentlemen with financial records exposed, show the same brand running for weeks before these October batches.

Defender actions follow from the mechanics rather than from a patch. If your organization is named in a claim, treat it as an incident: assume data left the network before encryption and hunt for the documented nightspire tooling, specifically WinSCP, MEGACmd, or Rclone executions, Go binaries carrying RC4 and XOR obfuscation, unusual sleep intervals around encryption, and log clearing; for spirals-style intrusions, watch for a web shell dropped into IIS followed by PsExec lateral movement. Yazoul's own claim page for Philander Smith University flags EndZone's alleged 500 GB of financial records as unverified, which is the correct posture for every entry in these feeds: verify through the named organization, a regulator, or a second tracker before reacting to extortion pressure, and monitor claim trackers for your sector, your customers, and your suppliers so a claim lands in your queue on day one.

Two developments would change this assessment. A confirmation or denial from any named organization, Forma Therapeutics, PharmaEssentia, or Philander Smith University, converts claim-tracking into incident coverage and re-scores the story. And sustained tempo matters at the brand level: if panzer v or spirals add victims beyond these first claims, or the gentlemen holds a 33-per-day pace, those are emergent brands worth tracking; Huntress's incident analysis is the caution here, since affiliate-driven IOC drift means a brand's current indicators can be stale within weeks.
