Layer3 Intel found residential proxy nodes beaconing out of AS2650, the address block reserved for the Executive Office of the President, between October 5 and November 3. The company counted 236 proxy exits from the White House network in that period. A Reput.io write-up reports the same 236 exits over a month through 28 different proxy providers. Synthient tied the traffic to multiple Windows 11 installations running Infatica, a proxy program often bundled into free desktop and mobile applications. An Aegis Alliance post says strangers could rent the addresses for months through a staff streaming app.

The reporting names a Russian proxy provider and identifies Infatica founder Vladimir Fomenko as the operator of King Servers, the hosting business that held six of the eight addresses used by Russia's state security services in the 2016 compromises of election systems in Arizona and Illinois. No intrusion into White House systems is claimed. The finding is that staff devices made Executive Office of the President exit capacity available for rent, which lets third parties route traffic through an address tied to the White House. Watch for identification of the devices and app involved, whether the AS2650 exits stopped after November 3, and any reported abuse traced to those addresses.
