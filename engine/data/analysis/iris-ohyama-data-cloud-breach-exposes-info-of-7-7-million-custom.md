Iris Ohyama, the Japanese household-goods manufacturer behind the Iris Plaza online store, disclosed that personal information belonging to about 7.7 million people may have been exposed after unauthorized access to a third-party cloud service the company used. The incident was detected on October 28, 2026 when a suspicious file was found in the external cloud service used by the Iris Plaza online store; Iris Plaza and related systems were immediately suspended and an investigation began. Unauthorized third-party access was confirmed and reported to police and Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission on October 29, 2026, in the company's first notice. The scope confirmed in the November 5 second notice covers about 5.8 million registered and former members plus about 1.9 million non-members. Member records may include name, address, phone number, email address, gender, encrypted password (excluding withdrawn members), and date of birth, while non-member records may include name, address, phone number, and email address; credit card data was not stored in the affected cloud service and was not impacted, and no misuse or financial damage had been confirmed as of November 5. Iris Plaza operates as the online storefront for the Iris Ohyama group, and the attacker and the cloud provider have not been publicly identified.

The exposure is larger than an active-customer list because it spans former members and people who never created accounts, which shows a data-retention problem as much as an access-control problem: roughly 5.8 million member records, including withdrawn members, and about 1.9 million non-member records from purchases or campaign entries were sitting in the same external cloud environment. That retention expanded the blast radius well beyond current shoppers and means password reuse is the immediate practical risk, since member passwords may be included even though they were stored encrypted. The two-stage disclosure also matters: the October 29 notice confirmed unauthorized access but left the impact under investigation, while the November 5 notice quantified the scope, so defenders should expect a gap between incident detection and impact quantification when relying on vendor statements. Individual email notices began on November 5, and the company has stated it will never ask for passwords, PINs, or authentication codes through email or SMS links.

For affected customers, the concrete steps are to change any password reused on other services, treat unexpected Iris Plaza-related messages with caution, and verify any notice through the official site or the Iris Plaza contact center at 0120-108-824 or plaza_info@irisplaza.co.jp rather than through links in unsolicited email. For defenders, the hunt centers on the October 28 suspicious file in the external cloud service, the unauthorized access path into that environment, and cloud audit logs around that date for file creation and access events; review third-party cloud access controls and least privilege, monitor for anomalous file placement in shared buckets, and reduce retained data by removing former-member and non-member records that are no longer needed. Organizations should also confirm that passwords are stored with strong encryption and that incident notification timelines are defined before a vendor breach occurs.

The assessment would change if the investigation confirms actual exfiltration rather than possible access, if the attacker is identified or makes a ransom demand, if misuse or fraud tied to the leaked fields appears, or if the scope expands beyond Iris Plaza to other Iris group services such as SIMPLE STYLE or Iris Tech Service, which were suspended preventively on October 28 and reported no compromise as of November 5. A change in the company's handling of the affected cloud provider, or regulatory action by Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission, would also warrant a reassessment. Until the exposed records are fully inventoried, treat any message requesting personal details or credentials as untrusted.
