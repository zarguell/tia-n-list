Japanese manufacturer Iris Ohyama disclosed that personal data for up to 7.7 million people tied to its Iris Plaza online store may have leaked after unauthorized access to a third-party cloud service. The company found a suspicious file on October 28, 2026, suspended the store and related systems the same day, and reported the incident to police and Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission on October 29. The affected population breaks down to about 5.8 million registered and former members plus 1.9 million non-members.

The exposed fields for members include name, address, phone, email, gender, encrypted password, and date of birth; non-members' name, address, phone, and email. Credit card data was not stored in the affected cloud service and was not impacted. Iris Plaza said it would contact affected customers individually by email starting November 5, while the store remained suspended during the investigation.

What to watch: the company is still determining the exact number of records exposed and how many people are impacted, so the 7.7 million figure is an upper bound. The incident highlights third-party cloud supply-chain risk in e-commerce, and the full scope of data access may grow as the investigation continues.
