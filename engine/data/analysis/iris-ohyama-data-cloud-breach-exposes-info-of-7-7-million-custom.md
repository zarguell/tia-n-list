Iris Ohyama, operator of the Iris Plaza online store, suspended the platform on October 28, 2026 after finding a suspicious file in an external cloud service it used. The company confirmed unauthorized access by a third party, reported the incident to police and Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission on October 29, and engaged external experts. The affected service may expose personal data for up to 7.7 million people: about 5.8 million registered and former members whose names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, dates of birth, gender, and encrypted passwords are at risk, plus about 1.9 million non-members whose names, addresses, phone numbers, and email addresses are at risk. Credit card data was not stored in the affected cloud service, and the company says it has blocked the attackers' access.

The incident matters because it is a third-party cloud compromise rather than a breach of Iris Ohyama's own store systems, which broadens the supplier-risk question for retailers that outsource e-commerce infrastructure. The exposed mix of contact details, dates of birth, and password hashes creates phishing and credential-stuffing exposure even without payment data, and the platform remains offline while the investigation continues. The company has stated there are no reports of unauthorized use or financial damage so far.

What to watch next is whether the investigation confirms actual exfiltration or only possible access, how many of the 7.7 million records are ultimately confirmed affected, and whether the encrypted passwords prove recoverable. Regulators and customers should also track the individual email notifications the company promised, any expansion of scope beyond the cloud service, and the timeline for restoring Iris Plaza.
