Iris Ohyama disclosed that personal data of about 7.7 million people may have been exposed after unauthorized access to a third-party cloud service used by its Iris Plaza online store. The affected population splits into about 5.8 million current and former members and about 1.9 million non-members. Member records include name, address, phone, email, date of birth, gender, and encrypted passwords; non-member records include name, address, phone, and email. Credit card data was not stored in the affected cloud service and was not impacted.

The incident was discovered on October 28, 2026 when a suspicious file was found in the external cloud service; Iris Plaza and related systems were suspended the same day. The company reported the incident to police and Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission on October 29. The store remains offline while the investigation continues, and Iris Ohyama says it will contact affected customers individually by email.

The company states there have been no reports of unauthorized use or financial damage so far. The root cause and whether data was actually exfiltrated remain under investigation, so the 7.7 million figure represents potential exposure rather than confirmed theft.
