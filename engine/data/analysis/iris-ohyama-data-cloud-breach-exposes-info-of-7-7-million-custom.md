Iris Ohyama, the Japanese manufacturer behind the Iris Plaza shopping platform, disclosed that personal information belonging to about 7.7 million people may have been exposed after unauthorized access to a third-party cloud service. The company discovered the incident late last month, found a suspicious file in the external cloud service used by the Iris Plaza online store, suspended Iris Plaza and related systems, and confirmed unauthorized third-party access in its first notice on October 29. The impact extends to up to 5.8 million registered and former members and 1.9 million non-members, and Iris Plaza serves as the online storefront for a major household-goods manufacturer spanning storage units, LED lighting, home appliances, and garden accessories.

The company is still determining how many records were actually accessed and which data types were exposed, so the full scope remains unclear; the public disclosure lists the exposed fields only partially. Customers should watch for follow-up disclosures detailing the exposed fields and be alert for phishing or identity-fraud attempts leveraging any leaked contact data. Organizations using similar third-party cloud services should review access controls and monitoring on those platforms, particularly around externally hosted customer databases and suspicious-file detection.

Watch for the company's final tally of affected records and the specific data types confirmed exposed, along with any regulatory or law-enforcement follow-up in Japan. Until the exposed fields are clarified, treat any Iris Plaza-related message requesting personal details with caution.
