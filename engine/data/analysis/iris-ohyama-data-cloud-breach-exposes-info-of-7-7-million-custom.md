Iris Ohyama, the Japanese manufacturer behind the Iris Plaza shopping platform, disclosed that personal information belonging to about 7.7 million people may have been exposed after unauthorized access to a third-party cloud service. The company discovered the incident late last month, found a suspicious file in the external cloud service used by the Iris Plaza online store, suspended Iris Plaza and related systems, and confirmed unauthorized third-party access in its first notice on October 29. The impact extends to up to 5.8 million registered and former members and 1.9 million non-members.

The company is still determining how many records were actually accessed and which data types were exposed, so the full scope remains unclear. Customers should watch for follow-up disclosures detailing the exposed fields and be alert for phishing or identity-fraud attempts leveraging any leaked contact data. Organizations using similar third-party cloud services should review access controls and monitoring on those platforms.
