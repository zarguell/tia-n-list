North Korean threat actors operating under the campaign name "Contagious Interview" have compromised 30,000 devices across more than 100 countries, draining $10.71 million from over 7,000 cryptocurrency wallets. The campaign targets IT professionals and software developers through fake job listings on social media, job platforms, and freelance marketplaces. Victims are lured into downloading a malware-laden file as part of a fake application process.

German intelligence services independently corroborated the campaign, identifying the group as "Waterplum" and noting its dual objectives: generating revenue for the Kim regime through cryptocurrency theft and sanctions evasion, and embedding remote developers at foreign companies under false identities using laptop farms to conceal their location. The social engineering extends to video call behavior where applicants read from second monitors and avoid in-person meetings.

The financial scale ($10.71M from 7,000+ wallets) and geographic breadth (100+ countries) make this one of the larger documented North Korean cybercrime operations. Organizations hiring remote IT staff should scrutinize applicants who resist in-person interaction, request cryptocurrency payment, or show inconsistent behavior during video interviews.
