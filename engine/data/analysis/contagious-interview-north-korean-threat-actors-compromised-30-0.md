North Korean threat actors running the Contagious Interview campaign compromised more than 30,000 devices in over 100 countries and drained $10.71 million from more than 7,000 cryptocurrency wallets, according to reporting by The Hacker News and heise. The operation is built on fake recruitment interviews: IT professionals are approached about jobs, walked through a bogus interview, and then induced to install software that hands the actor a foothold on the candidate's machine.

The scale is the notable part. A recruitment lure that works at this volume does not need a software vulnerability, and it lands on endpoints that belong to employed engineers rather than to a single corporate network, which is why the victim count spans more than 100 countries. The theft is financially motivated and runs through wallets, so the value extracted is liquid and hard to recover once the seed phrases or keys are gone. This is the same lure pattern used by other North Korean fake-recruitment operations tracked separately, where the interview is the delivery mechanism and the payload is a remote access tool or an infostealer.

What to watch: whether the reporting names the malware family and the infrastructure behind the fake employers, since those details are what defenders can block; whether the affected companies are being notified, because most victims are individuals rather than monitored enterprise estates; and whether the wallet count keeps growing, which would show the campaign is still active rather than winding down after exposure.
