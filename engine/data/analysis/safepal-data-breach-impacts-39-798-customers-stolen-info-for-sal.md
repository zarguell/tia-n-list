SafePal, the cryptocurrency hardware wallet maker, disclosed a data breach affecting about 39,798 customers after an attacker exploited a flaw to steal customer order information. A threat actor is now advertising the stolen data for sale on a cybercrime forum. The company describes the incident as an order-tracking flaw rather than a compromise of wallet infrastructure.

The exposed records cover customers who placed orders between March 2, 2025, and April 11, 2026, and include names, email addresses, shipping addresses, phone numbers, and purchase details. SafePal states that wallet seed phrases, private keys, passwords, bank account details, payment card numbers, and government-issued identification numbers were not exposed, and that no evidence links the incident to access of SafePal wallets or funds. The company emailed affected customers on August 16 with the subject "[Important] Your SafePal Order Information Has Been Affected" and published an online tool where customers can check an order number against the stolen set.

The main downstream risk is targeted phishing and social engineering, not direct theft of funds. Customers have reported SafePal-branded phishing emails and phone calls as early as May, indicating the data was already circulating before the public advisory. Watch for follow-on scam campaigns using the exposed shipping and contact details, for details on the underlying order-tracking flaw, and for the seller's asking price and volume as signals of how widely the data will spread.
