The United Kingdom sanctioned Cryptomus, a cryptocurrency payment platform previously known as Certa Payments and Xeltox Enterprises, for processing payments that support ransomware, narcotics, darknet markets, fraud, and Russian financial circumvention. The sanctions freeze Cryptomus assets in the U.K. and prohibit British companies from doing business with it. Related platforms VexPay, TokenSpot, and Tsunami Payments were also targeted. Cryptomus had recently rebranded to Heleket after Canada issued a record CAD 176 million fine for anti-money laundering violations. Chainalysis noted the sanctions aim at the broader infrastructure supporting Russian crypto-to-cash pipelines.

This is significant for cybercrime tracking because disrupting payment rails for ransomware and darknet markets raises the operational cost for attackers. Organizations should not treat sanctions as direct defense, but compliance and threat-intelligence teams should flag any transactions or service interactions linked to Cryptomus, VexPay, TokenSpot, or Tsunami Payments, and ensure their own crypto-related vendors are not indirectly routing through these sanctioned entities.
