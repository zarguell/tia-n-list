The NSA, CISA, and FBI issued a joint cybersecurity advisory on September 8, 2026, accusing Chinese AI companies of running industrial-scale distillation campaigns against U.S. frontier models since at least late 2024. The advisory names DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun, and Z.AI as participants, saying they spent billions of tokens across millions of queries to Anthropic Claude, OpenAI ChatGPT, Google Gemini, and xAI Grok to extract proprietary capabilities into their own domestic models.

DeepSeek is singled out as a primary actor, using distilled outputs from four Claude versions, two Gemini versions, five ChatGPT versions, and Grok 4 to generate synthetic training data for its R1 and R3 models. The agencies describe distillation as "the core, not merely a supplement" of China's AI development strategy, noting it is tacitly encouraged by Beijing even if not directly directed.

The practical fallout is already materializing: Digitimes reports U.S. agencies are asking AI firms to silently degrade responses to traffic suspected of being used for Chinese distillation. That represents a significant escalation from passive monitoring to active countermeasure, with implications for API access policies, rate limiting, and potential collateral impact on legitimate international users of U.S. AI services. Organizations using these models should expect tighter traffic inspection, possible service disruptions, and new compliance requirements flowing from this advisory.
