A joint advisory from the NSA, CISA, and FBI accuses Chinese AI companies of conducting industrial-scale knowledge distillation campaigns against U.S. frontier models since at least late 2024. The agencies name DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun, and Z.AI, stating they spent billions of tokens across millions of exchanges with Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's ChatGPT, Google's Gemini, and xAI's Grok.

DeepSeek alone distilled four versions of Claude, two versions of Gemini, five versions of ChatGPT, and Grok 4 to generate synthetic training data for its R1 and R3 models. The advisory characterizes this as "the core, not merely a supplement" of China's AI development strategy, with tacit encouragement from Beijing though not direct command.

The disclosure reframes the AI competition as an IP theft issue rather than just a market-race narrative. U.S. model providers face a difficult enforcement challenge: their APIs are designed for broad access, making rate-limiting and abuse detection a cat-and-mouse game.
