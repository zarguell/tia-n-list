DreamHost is forcing password resets for email and FTP/SFTP accounts following an incident that began October 23 with connectivity problems to its File Manager interface and quickly expanded to other services. The U.S. hosting provider and registrar, which serves over 400,000 customers in more than 100 countries and supports at least 1.5 million sites, blocked all user access until account owners set new credentials and sent recovery instructions in batches over the weekend. As of the latest update the incident remains active and File Manager access is still unavailable.

The scale of the reset and DreamHost's advice that customers review each email address for unrecognized forwarding destinations suggest some email accounts may have been breached and exposed to external actors, although the company has not confirmed a compromise and describes the mass reset as a precaution. Users cannot log in until their account owner sets new credentials.

Watch for whether DreamHost confirms unauthorized access or data exposure, when File Manager returns, and any customer reports of account takeover. The forwarding-rule guidance is the strongest signal so far that email-level compromise is suspected even if unconfirmed.
