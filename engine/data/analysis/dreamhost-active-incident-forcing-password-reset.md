DreamHost, a U.S. hosting provider and registrar serving more than 400,000 customers across more than 100 countries and hosting at least 1.5 million sites including 750,000 WordPress installations, has been running an active incident since October 23, 2026, when connectivity problems hit its File Manager interface at files.dreamhost.com and quickly spread to webmail and email hosting. The company reset passwords for some email and FTP/SFTP users as a precaution and completed that reset by October 24 at 10:37 a.m. PDT, but as of the latest status update File Manager remains offline and the incident is still active. Account owners must set new credentials in the panel at panel.dreamhost.com before affected mail and FTP/SFTP users can log in; the resets were delivered in batches over the weekend, with all affected account holders notified by October 25 at 9:32 p.m. PDT.

The reset is not a routine outage response: DreamHost told affected holders to inspect every mailbox for unrecognized forwarding destinations and delete them, which is a mailbox-takeover indicator rather than a generic hygiene tip and implies at least some email accounts were reached by an external actor even though the company has not confirmed a compromise. Because shared hosting customers routinely reuse one credential set across email and FTP/SFTP, a File Manager or control-plane exposure can cascade into site defacement, mail-based password resets for third-party services, and abuse of the 750,000 WordPress installs on the platform. The absence of a stated root cause after four days, combined with the decision to invalidate credentials at scale, means defenders should treat this as a credential-exposure event until DreamHost says otherwise, not as an availability incident.

Defenders should not wait for a vendor post-mortem: set new passwords for every mail and FTP/SFTP user in the DreamHost panel under the panel's Mail and Websites sections, allow up to 15 minutes for propagation, and assume any pre-reset credential is burned. Audit each mailbox's forwarding rules and delete destinations not recognized, check FTP/SFTP and webmail login logs for sessions between October 23 and the reset, and verify no unauthorized files or changes landed in hosted document roots during that window. Watch for phishing that spoofs DreamHost password-reset mail: the company states it will never ask for a password or link to a login page, so any such message should be ignored and reported.

The assessment changes if DreamHost publishes a root cause and affected-account count, confirms or rules out unauthorized access to email or FTP credentials, or restores File Manager with a patched build. Until then, the forwarding-rule guidance remains the strongest indicator of suspected mailbox compromise, and the platform's WordPress footprint keeps the downstream risk high even after credentials are rotated.
