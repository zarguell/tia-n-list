FortiGuard Labs attributes a multi-month cyberespionage campaign to the BITTER threat group with high confidence, targeting government, energy, and engineering organizations in South Asia. The campaign delivers a custom remote access trojan called PapRAT through three infection chains: a fileless LNK that abuses ftp.exe and curl to run scripts and scheduled tasks; a VHDX disk image that silently launches an MSI installer to deploy PapRAT behind a decoy spreadsheet; and a RAR archive whose URL shortcut retrieves an LNK over WebDAV to deploy PapRAT, which then downloads the secondary WmRAT implant. PapRAT has both C++ and Rust builds and supports remote shell, file transfer, and screenshots, beaconing to domains such as goodnfortunes[.]com, techtalkhelp.com, and rivingtonclarks[.]com.

Lures include a fake ambassador's letter. FortiGuard based attribution on lure filenames, C2 patterns, file metadata, and malware analysis. The campaign was reported by Infosecurity Magazine, Hackread, GBHackers, and SecurityOnline, with a Fortinet FortiGuard Labs report also circulated via en.hacks.gr.

Defenders should hunt for the three delivery chains and the listed C2 domains, and treat South Asian government and energy targets as at risk. No CVEs are associated; detection hinges on behavioral indicators rather than patches.
