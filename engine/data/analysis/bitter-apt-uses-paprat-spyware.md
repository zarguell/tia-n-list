BITTER APT is running an espionage campaign that delivers a custom remote access trojan called PapRAT to targets in South Asia, using a fake ambassador's letter as a lure. FortiGuard attributes the activity to the BITTER threat group with high confidence, based on lure filenames, command-and-control patterns, file metadata, and malware analysis. Reporting from Infosecurity Magazine and Hackread corroborates the campaign, and a GBHackers write-up describes three infection chains, the first relying on a malicious LNK file.

The diplomatic-document lure is aimed at government, energy, and engineering organizations, and a purpose-built RAT implies sustained access rather than opportunistic crime. No CVE or patchable vulnerability is named in the available reporting, so this is a detection-and-hunting story rather than a patching one. The FortiGuard attribution and three-chain delivery detail give defenders concrete artifacts to hunt for even without a vulnerability identifier.

Watch for diplomatic-document lures and PapRAT payloads in government and energy-sector environments, along with the malicious LNK chain and the group's command-and-control infrastructure.
