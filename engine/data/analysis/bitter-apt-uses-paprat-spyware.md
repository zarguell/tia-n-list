The BITTER APT campaign delivers PapRAT malware through three infection chains targeting South Asian government, energy, and engineering organizations. FortiGuard Labs attributes the multi-month operation with high confidence, based on lure filenames, C2 patterns, file metadata, and malware analysis. The first chain uses a fileless LNK abusing ftp.exe and curl; the second deploys a VHDX disk image launching an MSI installer that opens a decoy spreadsheet; the third uses a RAR archive with a URL shortcut that retrieves an LNK over WebDAV. Both C++ and Rust builds of PapRAT support remote shell, file transfer, and screenshot commands, and beacon to domains including goodnfortunes[.]com, techtalkhelp.com, and rivingtonclarks[.]com.

PapRAT then downloads the secondary WmRAT implant. This is active cyberespionage with multiple delivery mechanisms and multi-language malware builds, indicating a mature, resourced actor. The campaign has been running for months against strategic sectors.

What to watch: whether WmRAT deployments expand beyond the initial South Asian targets, and if new C2 infrastructure is registered. Organizations in government and energy should review LNK and VHDX handling policies.
