The Federal Register website briefly deployed Alibaba's Qwen AI model for search functionality on proposed regulation comments, then removed it on September 18 after social media users noticed the contradiction. The FBI had earlier in September named Alibaba among six Chinese firms accused of "industrial-scale distillation" copying US frontier AI models. The National Archives, which runs the Federal Register, did not respond to requests for comment, and the White House and FBI have not commented on the deployment.

Security experts told Reuters the deployment likely posed minimal risk. The model used was a small open-weight "Qwen3 0.6B level" that runs locally and does not send data to Alibaba or third parties. The Federal Register content is already public, so the model was not processing sensitive government information. Georgetown Law Professor Anupam Chander and ITIF president Daniel Castro both noted the contradiction, with Castro calling it "insane" for a US agency to use an Alibaba model while the FBI pushes American-only alternatives.

The incident highlights tensions in US AI policy. Companies like Nvidia and Meta have pledged to help the US catch up in open-source AI, while researchers warn that China's dominance in small, specialized models could undermine US ability to set technical standards. House China Committee chair John Moolenaar stated that "no federal government entity should use a Chinese AI model," calling it against national interest.
