Microsoft Threat Intelligence detailed DeadLock, an emerging financially motivated ransomware operation first observed in July 2025 that has already listed more than 80 organizations on its leak site, over half in Europe, spanning IT, mining, manufacturing, transportation, logistics, hospitality, and consumer goods. The Rust-based encryptor's pre-encryption routine disables Windows Defender, deletes backups, and clears event logs. It also geofences against Russian, Ukrainian, Belarusian, and several CIS languages plus Iran, Syria, Oman, and Yemen, deleting itself in those locales.

The distinctive feature is the recovery ecosystem. DeadLock combines the Session messaging network with blockchain-backed services for communication, leak hosting, and negotiation, making those parts of the operation resilient to takedown. This is the same decentralization pattern showing up across the ransomware economy, and it shifts the disruption problem from infrastructure to finances and people.

Defenders should focus on the pre-encryption behavior, which is noisy and detectable: Defender tampering, backup deletion, and event log clearing are reliable tripwires that fire before encryption starts. Backups that are offline or immutable are the practical mitigation, since the encryptor explicitly targets them. Watch for DeadLock adopting initial-access tactics already in use by Storm-1175 and related operations.
