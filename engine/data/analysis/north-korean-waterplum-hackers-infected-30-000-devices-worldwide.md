A joint law enforcement advisory from Japan, the United States, Australia, and Germany has quantified the scope of North Korean group WaterPlum's operations for the first time. Between December 2025 and July 2026, WaterPlum compromised at least 30,000 devices across more than 100 countries and exfiltrated funds or credentials from over 7,000 cryptocurrency wallets. The stolen cryptocurrency totaled approximately 1.7 billion Japanese yen, equivalent to $10.71 million USD, transferred back to the DPRK.

WaterPlum operates under the broader "Contagious Interview" campaign, targeting job seekers through fake AI, cryptocurrency, and NFT company recruiting pitches on freelance and job platforms. Victims are directed to download malicious projects, troubleshoot fabricated video-conferencing issues, or execute code that deploys npm-based malware. The advisory links the operation to several known malware families tied to the BeaverTail and Contagious Interview ecosystem.

The scale of this campaign makes it one of the largest financially motivated North Korean operations disclosed publicly. The joint advisory signals increased international coordination on DPRK cyber activity. Organizations should monitor for the identified malware indicators and warn employees about social engineering through job interview pretexts, particularly in the cryptocurrency and AI sectors.
