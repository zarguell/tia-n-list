A joint law enforcement advisory from Japan, the US, Australia, and Germany reveals that the North Korean threat group WaterPlum compromised at least 30,000 devices across more than 100 countries between December 2025 and July 2026. The group exfiltrated funds or credentials from over 7,000 cryptocurrency wallets, transferring 1.7 billion JPY (approximately $10.71 million) back to the DPRK. WaterPlum operates under the "Contagious Interview" campaign, targeting job seekers through fake recruitment drives on freelancing and recruiting platforms.

The attack vector is social engineering combined with malware delivery. Impersonating legitimate AI, cryptocurrency, and NFT companies, the actors instruct victims to download projects, troubleshoot fake video-conferencing issues, or execute malicious code during fake interviews and coding tests. Malicious npm packages are the primary infection vector. The advisory links several malware families to the group and notes the campaign is part of a broader North Korean financially motivated ecosystem funding weapons programs.

This is a significant escalation in scale for the Contagious Interview campaign. The 30,000-device figure and $10.7M theft represent concrete damage from what was previously tracked as a lower-volume operation. The joint advisory from four nations suggests coordinated law enforcement attention. Organizations should warn employees about fake recruitment scams, particularly those involving coding tests or npm package downloads from unknown sources.
