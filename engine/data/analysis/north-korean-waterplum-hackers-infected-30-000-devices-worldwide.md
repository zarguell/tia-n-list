A joint advisory from Japanese, US, Australian, and German law enforcement reveals that North Korean threat group WaterPlum compromised at least 30,000 devices across more than 100 countries between December 2025 and July 2026, transferring over $10.7 million in stolen cryptocurrency to the DPRK.

WaterPlum operates under the broader "Contagious Interview" campaign, impersonating legitimate AI, cryptocurrency, and NFT companies to approach job seekers on recruiting platforms. During fake interviews and coding tests, victims are instructed to download projects, troubleshoot video-conferencing issues, or execute malicious code. The malware ecosystem includes BeaverTail (JavaScript in npm packages), InvisibleFerret (Python backdoor), OtterCookie (JS RAT), and StoatWaffle (modular Node.js malware delivered via VS Code projects).

The advisory links WaterPlum to North Korea's fraudulent IT worker operations, noting shared IP addresses between the two groups. Some WaterPlum hackers also work as remote IT workers, and stolen identity documents from WaterPlum attacks are reused to impersonate victims for job applications. Japanese authorities dismantled a North Korean IT worker "laptop farm" for the first time, finding evidence of hundreds of millions of yen transferred abroad.

The FBI and Japanese police assess that WaterPlum and some IT workers operate under North Korea's 313 General Bureau, part of the Munitions Industry Department responsible for weapons production. Organizations should verify job applicants' identities rigorously, restrict developer access to sandboxes, and inspect provided code for commands that fetch additional payloads.
