Korea Electric Power Corporation confirmed on November 20 that attackers had accessed two of its internal systems: KEPCO ON, the customer platform used to view and pay electricity bills, and SRM, the electronic procurement system. The exposed data covers customers, employees and contractors, with the company reporting 1.52 million affected records and public reports describing more than 1.5 million individuals. KEPCO blocked external IP access and suspended the affected services after detection. It has started notifying exposed individuals, filed a report with data protection authorities and is coordinating with government and financial authorities. KEPCO is state-controlled and operates critical infrastructure, which raises the stakes of an intrusion into its billing and procurement systems.

The public notice, published on November 22 under president Kim Dong-cheol, says the breach resulted from "advanced technologies such as AI" but gives no details on initial access or the attack chain. That claim is unsupported in the available material and should not be read as a confirmed AI-driven intrusion. There is also no attribution, no claim of responsibility and no published indicator. The combination of a billing platform and a supplier system means the exposed fields could include contact and payment-related details, though the notice as reported does not enumerate them.

What to watch is whether KEPCO releases the access vector, the exact data fields and a timeline of how long the attackers had access. Customers should expect phishing and impersonation attempts that use utility-billing context. For defenders, the useful follow-up will be confirmation of whether this was a direct compromise of KEPCO systems or an access-abuse chain through a contractor, since the affected SRM system sits at that boundary.
