Korea Electric Power Corporation detected an intrusion on November 20 and confirmed that an external attacker reached its internal network and accessed two systems: the KEPCO ON electricity billing platform and the SRM procurement system. KEPCO put the total at 1,520,248 affected people. The ON side covers 650,487 customers, or 7.1 percent of the platform's 9.2 million users, with names, addresses, phone numbers, registration numbers, customer numbers, card details, bank names, account numbers and billing records. The SRM side covers 869,761 people, split between 426,827 supplier representatives and 442,934 KEPCO and company employees.

The company blocked external IP access and suspended both systems, notified the Personal Information Protection Commission and opened work with the National Intelligence Service and the climate ministry's cyber safety center. It did not disclose the initial access path. Its statement attributed the incident to advanced technology such as AI, but the reports in the story say there is no evidence that AI was used in this breach. This follows a separate leak disclosed a month earlier that grew from about 24,000 employees to 35,528 people and included coded facial recognition data for 400 headquarters staff.

A DarkForums post dated November 19 and attributed to Noiem claimed 592,585 external user records, 95,054 supplier records, 401,951 bid price records and 26,859 sole-contract records, with the whole set labeled as a 560M CSV. Korean reporting said the categories match SRM work but found no named source system, no verified intrusion path and no proof of transfer. Watch for confirmation that the dark web material overlaps this breach, abnormal queries or bulk exports in SRM, and any statement on whether procurement files with facility or equipment details were exposed.
