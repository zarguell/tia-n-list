KEPCO confirmed on November 20 that an external actor had accessed two internal systems: KEPCO ON, the billing platform used by 9.2 million customers, and SRM, the electronic procurement system. Exfiltration covered 1,520,248 records, split between 650,487 KEPCO ON users and 869,761 SRM users, the latter comprising 426,827 corporate representatives and 442,934 KEPCO or supplier employees. Exposed fields include resident and foreign registration numbers, bank and card numbers, billing records, employee identifiers and mobile phone numbers. KEPCO blocked external IP access, suspended both services and reported to the Personal Information Protection Commission at about 18:00 local time on November 22. It has named no actor, no access path and no patches, and its reference to advanced technologies such as AI remains an unsupported assertion.

The strategic argument is that SRM is not a generic HR file store. Contemporary reporting describes it as the system handling procurement for every power grid construction project in South Korea, so supplier identities, employee numbers and contract-adjacent data sit beside billing and payment data for 650,487 customers. The intrusion was first visible outside KEPCO in a November 19 DarkForums post by Noiem claiming supplier, bid-price and sole-contract files, three days before the company confirmation, and the actor's military and government supplier claims remain unverified. A month earlier KEPCO had already disclosed a separate 35,528-person leak of employee, retiree and contractor data, which makes this a repeat failure of access control at the same utility rather than an isolated event.

With no vendor patch to apply, containment is architectural: keep internet access to procurement and billing portals off unless required, rotate credentials for supplier and employee accounts that used SRM, and treat KEPCO ON notifications as a phishing pretext rather than proof of safety. Hunt in web, authentication and database audit logs around November 19 to 22 for unusual queries, large exports and access to supplier or billing tables from unexpected IPs, using srm.kepco.net and the Noiem DarkForums post as pivots rather than guessing indicators. Since the leaked material includes account holder names, card issuers and bill details, monitor for payment-detail change requests that cite real contract or billing context and verify them through a known phone number. KEPCO's customer channel is 123, and the PIPC and KISA are monitoring online distribution.

The assessment changes if KEPCO or the National Intelligence Service publishes the access path and dwell time, or if the bid-price, sole-contract and military or government supplier files claimed on DarkForums are verified; that would move this from large-scale fraud exposure into power-grid procurement and national-security territory. Also watch whether the PIPC's on-site investigation finds missed reporting deadlines or safeguard failures, and whether the exposed account and card data shows up in fraud or impersonation telemetry.
