KEPCO confirmed on November 20 that an external actor reached its internal network and accessed two systems: KEPCO ON, the billing platform used by 9.2 million customers, and SRM, the electronic procurement system. Exfiltration covered 1,520,248 records, split between 650,487 KEPCO ON users and 869,761 SRM users, the latter comprising 426,827 corporate representatives and 442,934 KEPCO or supplier employees. Exposed fields include resident and foreign registration numbers, bank and card numbers, billing records, employee identifiers and mobile phone numbers. KEPCO blocked external IP access, suspended both services and reported to the Personal Information Protection Commission, with the National Intelligence Service and the climate ministry's cyber safety center also involved. It named no actor and no entry path, and its reference to advanced technologies such as AI remains unsupported.

The strategic argument is that SRM is not a generic HR file store. Contemporary reporting describes it as the system handling procurement for power grid construction projects, so supplier identities, employee numbers and contract-adjacent data sit beside billing and payment data for 650,487 customers. A November 19 DarkForums post by Noiem claimed 592,585 external user records, 95,054 supplier records, 401,951 bid price records and 26,859 sole-contract records, with the whole set labelled as a 560M CSV. DailySecu and ZDNet Korea said the categories match SRM functions but that the source system, authenticity and onward transfer were unverified. KEPCO's own notice did not address the forum post.

This is a repeat failure at the same utility. A month earlier KEPCO disclosed a separate leak of 35,528 people after an initial figure of about 24,000 employees grew to include retirees, 68 contractor staff and coded facial recognition data for 400 headquarters employees. The November 23 BleepingComputer report restates the two-system access confirmation without adding an intrusion path. Watch for forensic findings on SRM query and export activity, whether bid and facility details were actually taken, and any link between the forum posting and the confirmed breach.
