Flight Centre Travel Group disclosed on November 26 that it detected unusual activity in a third-party accommodation booking platform used by several of its leisure travel brands. The company is investigating whether customer records were exposed. ABC described the incident as a significant data breach, and the coverage identifies passport details among the personal data at issue.

Because the affected system is a third-party booking platform, the vendor relationship, the brands that used it and the exact fields exposed are central to scoping the incident. The reports do not establish a victim count, name the booking provider or confirm the full extent of the exposure. Passport information raises the consequence of the incident if access is confirmed, but the event material does not establish that every passport record associated with the platform was taken.

Flight Centre has apologised and says it is investigating the cyber breach. Watch for identification of the booking provider, a confirmed list of affected brands and data fields, and notices to customers. For customers, the practical concern is targeted phishing that references real travel or account details while the company and its provider establish the scope of access.
