The University of New Mexico Health System suffered a cyberattack on Monday, October 26, that disrupted patient care across the state's only academic health system. UNM diverted ambulances to other hospitals, canceled appointments at more than 30 clinics statewide, and suspended outpatient surgeries and appointments, while its hospitals remained open and nonessential staff and students were sent home. Classes at its four health sciences schools were canceled. The network operates New Mexico's only Level I trauma center and handles roughly 900,000 outpatient visits and 100,000 emergency room visits annually.

A ransomware group calling itself BYOD claimed responsibility on its leak site, asserting it compromised 36,000 systems across UNM's campus and hospital environments, including Active Directory forests, Group Policy infrastructure, the entire Microsoft Entra tenant, and two Oracle databases, and exfiltrated student, employee, and patient data. UNM did not confirm ransomware and described the incident as an active law enforcement investigation, with the FBI assisting. Staff reported messages on their computers claiming stolen data.

UNM said hospitals and clinics began resuming operations in phases later the same day, and by Tuesday clinics were resuming scheduled appointments and procedures. Other major New Mexico providers, including Presbyterian Healthcare Services and Lovelace Health System, coordinated to take additional patients during the disruption.
