On Monday, October 26, 2026, the University of New Mexico Health Sciences Center suffered a cyberattack that disrupted operations at New Mexico's only Level I trauma center. UNM limited new patient admissions, diverted ambulances to nearby hospitals, canceled outpatient surgeries and appointments at more than 30 clinics statewide, and canceled classes at its four health sciences schools while hospitals remained open. The health system handles roughly 900,000 outpatient visits and 100,000 emergency room visits annually.

A ransomware group calling itself BYOD claimed responsibility on its leak site, asserting it compromised 36,000 systems across UNM's main campus and hospital environments, including Active Directory forests, Group Policy infrastructure, the entire Microsoft Entra tenant, and two Oracle databases, and exfiltrated student, employee, and patient data. UNM did not confirm ransomware and described the incident as an active law enforcement investigation; the FBI is assisting. Operations began resuming in phases later the same day, with clinics scheduled to reopen Tuesday, October 27.

The incident shows the operational impact of ransomware on healthcare delivery, including ambulance diversion and trauma-center disruption. Defenders should note the claimed scope of compromise across identity infrastructure and databases, and prepare for extended recovery timelines even when services partially resume.
