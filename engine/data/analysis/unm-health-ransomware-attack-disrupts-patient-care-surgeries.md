UNM Health disclosed a ransomware-related cyberattack that disrupted patient care and surgeries. The university began limiting new admissions, diverting ambulances, and suspending outpatient procedures. A ransomware group, Byod, claimed responsibility, stating it compromised 36,000 systems including Active Directory, Group Policy, the Microsoft Entra tenant, and two Oracle databases. All clinics resumed scheduled appointments by Tuesday, October 27, but classes at health sciences schools were canceled and nonessential staff stayed home. The incident underscores how healthcare ransomware hits clinical operations directly, not just back-office IT.

The claim of 36,000 compromised systems and full Active Directory and Entra tenant access is significant if accurate, though the figure comes from the threat actor’s leak-site post rather than UNM’s own disclosure. The organization did not confirm the attack type, but BleepingComputer linked it to Byod. Watch for confirmation of data exfiltration scope, restoration timelines, and whether the Oracle databases involved patient records.
