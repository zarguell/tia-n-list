The University of New Mexico Health Sciences Center disclosed a cyberattack that took systems offline and disrupted patient care, academic activities, and appointments. UNM Health began limiting new patient admissions, diverting ambulances to nearby hospitals, and suspending outpatient surgeries and appointments on Monday. Clinics across more than 30 locations were affected, and the system's Albuquerque hospital hosts New Mexico's only Level I trauma center.

BleepingComputer found that a new ransomware group calling itself Byod claimed responsibility, asserting it compromised 36,000 systems across UNM's main campus and hospital environments, including Active Directory forests, Group Policy infrastructure, the entire Microsoft Entra tenant, and two Oracle databases. UNM did not confirm the attack type or these claims. The organization said UNM Hospital and Sandoval Regional Medical Center remained open, with clinics resuming scheduled appointments and procedures on Tuesday, October 27, while classes at its four health sciences schools were canceled and nonessential staff told to stay home.

UNM Health is a major statewide provider with more than one million patient visits annually, so the disruption carried immediate patient-safety weight. Watch whether UNM confirms ransomware, whether the Byod claims of 36,000 compromised systems hold up, and whether patient data was exfiltrated, which would convert an operational outage into a reportable breach.
