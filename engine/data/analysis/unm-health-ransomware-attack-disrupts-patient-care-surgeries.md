The UNM Health ransomware attack on October 26 forced ambulance diversions and canceled more than 30 clinics, shutting the state’s only Level I trauma center temporarily. Multiple sources, including AP and BleepingComputer, describe the disruption to 900,000 annual outpatient visits and 100,000 emergency visits. A ransomware group claimed data theft, though the health system confirms only that the incident remains an active law enforcement investigation. No CVE applies; the impact is operational and patient-safety risk.

What matters is that critical care infrastructure remains a high-value ransomware target. The attacker’s claim of stolen student, employee, and patient data suggests double-extortion tactics. Administrators should treat any unverified claim of data exposure as a trigger for breach notification planning, but the definitive scope of stolen records is not confirmed in the event content. Watch for confirmation from the FBI or the health system on data access, not just service restoration.
