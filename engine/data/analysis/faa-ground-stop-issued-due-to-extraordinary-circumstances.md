The Federal Aviation Administration issued a nationwide ground stop for all U.S. airports on Friday afternoon, citing "extraordinary circumstances" and "reasons of safety", after a telecommunications outage affected all Air Route Traffic Control Centers nationwide. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said FAA technicians were investigating, and the agency said it halted departures out of an abundance of caution. The FAA directed departing flights to remain on the ground until further notice, handled inbound international flights case by case, and had already halted Chicago traffic before issuing the nationwide order. Flight tracking data put the disruption at nearly 11,000 flights delayed or canceled across U.S. airports.

The cause was later described by the FAA and the White House as a telecommunication outage rather than a hostile act, with the White House saying there was no indication of a "nefarious act" and no indication of a cyberattack. No technical root cause for the communications failure has been released, and the affected equipment and network path have not been publicly identified.

This is an aviation operations story rather than a confirmed cyber incident, but it lands in a period of repeated disruption to critical infrastructure and it shows how little public detail accompanies a nationwide safety order. Watch for the FAA's follow-up findings on the telecommunications failure, whether the underlying circuit or provider is named, and whether the union representing FAA technical workers links the outage to deferred maintenance or staffing.
