The Federal Aviation Administration issued a nationwide ground stop for all U.S. airports on Friday afternoon, citing "extraordinary circumstances" for safety, after a communication issue affected all Air Route Traffic Control Centers nationwide. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said FAA technicians were investigating; the cause of the air traffic control communications disruption was not publicly identified, and it is unclear whether the incident is linked to a cyberattack. The FAA directed departing flights to remain on the ground until further notice, handled inbound international flights case by case, and had already halted Chicago traffic before issuing the nationwide order.

The incident is reported as an operational disruption rather than a confirmed security event, and no attribution to a cyberattack has been made. The available detail is limited to the FAA and Transportation Department statements, with no technical root cause released.

Watch for the FAA's follow-up findings on the communications failure and whether any security component is implicated. Until a root cause is published, treat this as an aviation operations story rather than a confirmed cyber incident.
