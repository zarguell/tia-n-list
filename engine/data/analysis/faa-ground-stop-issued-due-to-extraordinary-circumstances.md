On November 13, 2026, the FAA issued a nationwide ground stop citing extraordinary circumstances, halting all US departures for more than four hours and disrupting roughly 7,000 flights, after a telecommunications outage hit all 22 air route traffic control centers. The order was lifted at 8:15 pm ET. About 4,042 flights were airborne when it was issued, and the stop was only the third nationwide moratorium on flights, after September 11, 2001, and January 11, 2023.

US officials found no evidence of a cyberattack. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no chatter and no footprints, though he did not rule it out entirely; the NSA, DoD, CISA, and FBI were involved in the investigation. Bloomberg reported the FBI does not believe a cyberattack or criminal activity caused the failure and is examining whether a software update was responsible. Multiple sources told CBS News that an update to the private communications network connecting the air traffic control system, operated by L3Harris, caused the system to reboot and crash.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed legacy equipment, specifically switches and routers in the FAA's network, while Air Space Intelligence denied its SMART system, developed under an $875 million contract, was responsible. Senator Tammy Duckworth called for a thorough investigation. Delays were expected to continue for several days. The episode highlights the fragility of legacy aviation telecommunications infrastructure and the difficulty of distinguishing a software-induced failure from an attack while it is still unfolding.
