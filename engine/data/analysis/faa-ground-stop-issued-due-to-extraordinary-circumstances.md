On the afternoon of Friday, November 13, 2026, the FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop for all departing flights at every U.S. airport, citing "extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety." The trigger was a communications failure affecting all 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said technicians were investigating, and the FAA's post on X described a "temporary telecommunications outage," with inbound international flights handled case by case. CBS News, citing multiple sources, reported that an update to the private communications network connecting the air traffic control system caused a reboot that crashed the network, and that the network is operated by L3Harris. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication of a nefarious act and that the grounding was ordered out of an abundance of caution.

The scale is the argument. FlightAware counted roughly 7,000 flights delayed or cancelled in the first hours, Flightradar24 had 4,042 aircraft airborne toward U.S. airports when the order landed, and the New York Times counted more than 30,000 departures and arrivals still scheduled for the rest of the day. Only two comparable nationwide halts exist in recent history: September 11, 2001, and the January 11, 2023 failure of the NOTAM system. The structural lesson the timeline does not spell out is that one vendor-operated private network carries the whole national airspace, so a single pushed update can reboot all 22 centers at once with no independent fallback path between them. The precursor was visible for roughly half an hour before the nationwide order, when the FAA had already stopped flights into Chicago's O'Hare and Midway citing equipment problems and logged equipment-related departure delays at Newark, Las Vegas, Teterboro, Orlando, Houston, and Phoenix.

For operators, the recovery facts matter most: the stop was lifted by late Friday evening Eastern time, so the near-term job is backlog clearance and rebooking rather than outage prevention. The durable item is dependency mapping. Any organization running safety-critical communications on a single private carrier or a single update channel should treat this as a live demonstration of the failure mode and ask whether its own control rooms hold an independent path when the primary network reboots. Two developments would change the assessment: an FBI finding on whether a software update caused the failure, which Bloomberg reported is under examination, and any confirmed link to the Microsoft 365 and Azure outage of November 12, 2026, the day before. Watch also whether the SMART air traffic management system, awarded to Air Space Intelligence under an $875 million contract in June, is ruled in or out, since Mullin said nothing could be ruled out and Senator Tammy Duckworth has demanded a full investigation.
