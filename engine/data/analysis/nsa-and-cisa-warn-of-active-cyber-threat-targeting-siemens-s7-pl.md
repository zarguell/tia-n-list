NSA, CISA, FBI, DOE, and EPA issued a joint advisory on August 19 warning of active exploitation targeting Siemens S7 Series PLCs across U.S. critical infrastructure. Threat actors are using AI-generated scripts disguised as OT monitoring tools to scan for internet-exposed S7 controllers via Censys and ZoomEye, then generate model-specific exploitation code on the fly. The campaign targets S7-200, S7-300, S7-400, S7-1200, and S7-1500 families plus F-series safety PLCs deployed in manufacturing, energy, and water treatment facilities.

The AI angle is the significant detail here. The advisory describes adversaries using LLM-assisted development to produce, debug, and iterate ICS-specific attack tooling faster than manual methods allow. This lowers the barrier for ICS intrusion: operators no longer need deep Siemens protocol expertise to craft working exploits. The targeted products are permissive by design, with limited built-in authentication on communications, making exposure to the internet particularly dangerous.

What to watch: the advisory names specific MITRE ATT&CK techniques and includes IOCs for network detection. Organizations running Siemens S7 controllers should verify these devices are not directly internet-reachable, review segmentation between IT and OT networks, and check for anomalous Modbus/TCP or S7comm traffic patterns. The use of AI for ICS tooling generation is an emerging pattern worth tracking across other PLC vendors.
