An attacker does not need a private key to own a 1024-bit RSA signing identity, only a window on its raw RSA oracle. The paper behind this story, IACR ePrint 2026/2131 by Shea, Haller, Suhl, Heninger and Thome, received September 20, 2026, turned the 2007 Joux-Naccache-Thome e-th root algorithm into a working end-to-end attack: 1,380 CPU core-years of mostly precomputation spread over five calendar months, plus 2^32 queries to a raw PKCS#11 CKM_RSA_X_509 mechanism on Thales Luna K6 and Luna S750 hardware. After that one-time spend, any signature on the key forges offline in about 180 core-years. The key never leaves the device, the modulus is never factored, and the demonstration needed only black-box API access. Ars Technica, which covered the work on September 24, put the economics in scale: roughly 2^65 operations to forge against an estimated 2^80 operations and 500,000 to 1 million core-years to factor the same key.

The dangerous property is conversion of temporary access into permanent capability. Rotation policies, key-compromise detection and factoring-based strength estimates all miss it, because nothing observable about the key changed. There is no CVE and no vendor advisory: the HSM did exactly what its API permits, so exposure is a key-management problem no patch cycle will close. Anyone who has ever held raw signing or decryption rights on a partition, insiders and contractors and compromised build servers alike, must be treated as a permanent holder of that signing identity. The oracle framing also reaches past HSMs into live protocols: Privacy Pass, the blind-RSA scheme used by Apple and Cloudflare, provides the same oracle, and the 2^43 token requests an attacker would need sit on the order of one day of Cloudflare traffic. The result drops RSA's concrete security 15 to 30 bits across 1024-bit to 4096-bit keys in this model, putting even 4096-bit RSA below the 128-bit floor NSA, NIST and ENISA require, and an academic cluster did in five months what 1024-bit factoring was assumed to demand nation-state budgets to attempt.

Inventory every PKCS#11 consumer and forbid CKM_RSA_X_509 and other raw mechanisms, allowing only padded ones such as CKM_RSA_PKCS_PSS and CKM_RSA_PKCS_OAEP. Rotate any key that has ever been exposed to raw or blind signing, and shorten rotation intervals where blind RSA is a protocol requirement. Hunt in HSM audit logs for per-key-handle CKM_RSA_X_509 volumes climbing toward the 2^32 query scale, raw-mechanism calls from unusual client identities, and off-hours raw operations; treat any prior temporary raw access as an incident that retires the key, not as something to watch. Retire 1024-bit raw signers outright, moving them to 2048-bit keys with padded mechanisms or off RSA entirely during the post-quantum transition.

Two developments would change this assessment. Vendor guidance from Thales or a PKCS#11 profile change restricting raw mechanisms would confirm vendors treat the finding as operational rather than theoretical. A demonstrated forgery against a deployed blind-RSA service, most plausibly a Privacy Pass token provider, or a peer-review outcome that breaks the cost numbers, would move this from research result to active exposure.
