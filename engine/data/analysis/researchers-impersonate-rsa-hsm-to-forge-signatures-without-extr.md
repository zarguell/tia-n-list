Researchers Laura Shea, Miro Haller, Adam Suhl, Nadia Heninger, and Emmanuel Thome published "Forging 1024-bit RSA Signatures in Nearly SNFS Time" on IACR ePrint (2026/2131, received September 20, 2026). It is the first end-to-end implementation of a 2007 algorithm by Joux, Naccache, and Thome, and it breaks the usual assumption about HSM key safety: an attacker with temporary access to a raw RSA signing or decryption oracle can later forge signatures on that key forever, without ever extracting the private key or factoring the modulus. The authors drove real HSMs over PKCS#11's CKM_RSA_X_509 raw RSA mechanism, testing Thales Luna K6 and Luna S750 devices with black-box API access only.

The cost profile is the story. A precomputation of 1,380 CPU core-years spread over five calendar months, plus 2^32 oracle queries during the access window, lets any signature on the 1024-bit key be forged offline afterward in about 180 core-years. Forging a 1024-bit signature this way costs roughly 2^65 operations versus an estimated 2^80 operations and 500,000 to 1 million core-years to factor the same key, which is why the authors call it faster than anything previously seen against RSA. Their measurements show raw-RSA-oracle access costs a key 15 to 30 bits of concrete security across 1024-bit to 4096-bit parameters, leaving even 4096-bit RSA below a 128-bit level in this model. Dan Goodin's Arstechnica piece, the first mainstream coverage on September 24, flags Privacy Pass, used by Apple and Cloudflare, as the best-known blind-RSA deployment, where an attacker would need around 2^43 token requests.

Practical exposure requires unpadded raw RSA operations exposed to an attacker, a lunchtime style access window, and a large offline compute budget, so this is not a mass exploitation vector today. What to watch: whether HSM vendors restrict or deprecate CKM_RSA_X_509 by default, guidance from Thales and other token makers on raw signing mechanisms, and any mitigation work in blind-RSA protocols. Anyone operating HSMs that permit raw RSA signing should treat temporary operator access to those functions as equivalent to long-term key compromise.
