Researchers have demonstrated a practical attack that forges arbitrary 1024-bit RSA signatures without extracting or factoring the private key. The work, titled "Forging 1024-bit RSA Signatures in Nearly SNFS Time", applies a two-decade-old cryptanalytic technique to raw RSA signing and decryption oracles. An attacker with temporary access to an HSM's unpadded RSA operations, specifically the PKCS#11 CKM_RSA_X_509 mechanism, performs a large precomputation and query-collection phase and can then generate valid signatures offline for that key indefinitely. The researchers instantiated the oracle on Thales Luna K6 and Luna S750 HSMs using only black-box API access, with no physical compromise, side channels, or firmware modification.

The significance is the permanence of the win. The attack model resembles a lunchtime or non-adaptive chosen-ciphertext scenario: access is needed only once, and the private exponent is never recovered, so key-compromise detection and modulus factoring both miss it. An attacker who briefly touches a raw RSA signing function converts that window into long-term signing and decryption capability. Since 1024-bit RSA remains common in legacy and embedded deployments, any service exposing unpadded RSA operations is a candidate target, and Ars Technica's coverage gave the finding wide reach beyond the research community.

Watch for vendor guidance from Thales and other HSM makers, and for PKCS#11 profile updates that prohibit or restrict CKM_RSA_X_509. Organizations should inventory which applications hold raw RSA signing or decryption rights on their HSMs and treat temporary access to those mechanisms as a serious incident. Migration off 1024-bit keys and toward padded, restricted signing APIs is the durable fix.
