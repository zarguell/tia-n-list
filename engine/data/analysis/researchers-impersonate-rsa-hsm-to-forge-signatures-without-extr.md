A paper by Laura Shea, Miro Haller, Adam Suhl, Nadia Heninger, and Emmanuel Thome, received by the IACR ePrint archive on September 20 and approved September 22, 2026, delivers the first end-to-end implementation of a two-decade-old attack against raw RSA signing oracles at 1024-bit scale. The technique implements the 2007 Joux-Naccache-Thome algorithm: an attacker with temporary access to a raw, unpadded RSA signing or decryption function on an HSM performs a large precomputation and query collection, then permanently forges valid signatures for that key offline. The private key is never extracted and the modulus is never factored.

The cost figures are the story. The authors spent 1,380 CPU core-years over five calendar months and 2^32 oracle queries; after that, any signature on the targeted 1024-bit key forges in about 180 core-years. Ars Technica's coverage, quoting Heninger of UC San Diego and Karsten Nohl of Allurity, frames the comparison: roughly 2^65 operations to forge versus an estimated 2^80 operations and 500,000 to 1 million core-years to factor the same key. The paper concludes RSA loses 15 to 30 bits of concrete security under a signing oracle across 1024-bit to 4096-bit parameters, leaving even 4096-bit RSA below a 128-bit level in that model.

The practical exposure is any deployment that exposes raw or padding-less RSA operations, most plausibly misconfigured HSMs and tokenization services. Privacy Pass, used by Apple and Cloudflare, is identified as the best-known blind-RSA deployment, where an attacker would need on the order of 2^43 token requests. The takeaway for defenders is to audit HSM configurations for raw signing modes, treat temporary oracle access as a permanent compromise event for that key, and watch for follow-on work lowering the precomputation cost.
