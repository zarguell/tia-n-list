Chrome 158 reached desktop Stable on November 17, 2026, with 204 security fixes and six bugs rated Critical. Google's advisory names CVE-2026-130963, CVE-2026-131031, CVE-2026-131060, CVE-2026-131008, CVE-2026-131054 and CVE-2026-130975 across WebView, GPU, Media, GarbageCollection, ANGLE and WebAudio; the three CVEs circulating in coverage are only a subset. NVD records describe the WebView use after free as code execution outside the sandbox through crafted network traffic on Android before 158.0.8127.36, and the GPU and ANGLE out of bounds writes as sandbox escapes through a crafted page. Fixed builds are 158.0.8127.36 on Linux, 158.0.8127.36/.37 on Windows and macOS, and 158.0.8127.42 on Android.

The distinction that matters is the sandbox boundary, not the patch count. Three of the six Critical records describe execution outside the sandbox, and CVE-2026-130963 explicitly puts WebView on Android in scope, so embedded views in apps carry the same exposure as desktop tabs rather than a secondary surface. A crafted page or network response that reaches those paths can yield code execution beyond the renderer on any endpoint still before the fixed builds, and no source reviewed claims in-the-wild use. Google found 145 of the 204 bugs and Xinyang Ge found 15; $11,000 went to five externally reported flaws while 163 drew no reward, so there is little public disclosure to warn defenders ahead of the staged rollout.

Google's advisory lists no workaround, so the control is version enforcement: push 158.0.8127.36/.37 to Windows and macOS and 158.0.8127.36 to Linux ahead of the consumer rollout, and set 158.0.8127.42 as the Android floor. Hunt by checking installed Chrome version strings against those builds, prioritising hosts where WebView, GPU and ANGLE are exercised, and track Chromium issues 562000561, 571153713 and 573128234 as the identifiers for the three sandbox escapes. Google says bug details and links may stay restricted until most users are updated, so the absence of a public repro today is not evidence of low risk.

Watch for a CISA KEV entry or a Google acknowledgement of exploitation for any of the six Critical CVEs, and for the Chromium bugs becoming public with a working chain; either would move this from patch hygiene to incident response.
