Google promoted Chrome 158 to Stable on November 17 for Windows, Mac and Linux, with 158.0.8127.36 on Linux and 158.0.8127.36 or .37 on Windows and Mac, plus 158.0.8127.42 for Android. The release carries 204 security fixes. Six are rated Critical: CVE-2026-130963 (use after free in WebView, code execution outside the sandbox via crafted network traffic on Android before 158.0.8127.36), CVE-2026-131031 (out of bounds write in GPU, outside the sandbox), CVE-2026-131054 (out of bounds write in ANGLE), CVE-2026-130975 (use after free in WebAudio), CVE-2026-131008 (use after free in GarbageCollection) and CVE-2026-131060 (buffer overflow in Media). The last three are described as in-sandbox code execution.

SecurityWeek counted 62 High, 82 Medium and 54 Low severity issues and noted that 163 fixes carry no bug bounty, including all six critical bugs. Use after free is the largest class with 51 instances, followed by incorrect authorization (34) and missing authorization (16). Google paid 11,000 dollars across five externally reported flaws; Anthropic researcher Xinyang Ge is credited with finding 15 issues, two of them critical with AI assistance in later coverage.

No exploitation in the wild is claimed in the advisory or the follow-up reporting. Follow the fixed builds rather than relying on the rolling Stable label, and treat WebView and GPU issues as the highest priority because their NVD records describe escape from the sandbox. Bug details and links may remain restricted until most users have updated.
