On November 12, 2026 the FBI and Department of Justice announced two coordinated actions against North Korea-linked cryptocurrency theft. The first disrupted FaceStealer, a social-engineering campaign that stole more than $400 million over 18 months by hijacking real accounts on Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack, and Discord, inviting targets to fake video meetings on typo-squatted Zoom or Teams domains, and tricking them into installing malware disguised as an audio fix. The FBI seized the cloud infrastructure behind the scheme and more than 900 webcam videos of victims, and published FLASH alert AC-111226-MS describing the chain. The same announcement covered court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion from the October 29, 2026 theft of roughly $6.3 billion from Binance, attributed to the North Korean TraderTraitor group (also called Sapphire Sleet, BlueNoroff, APT38, Stardust Chollima). Investigators and private providers have frozen more than $2.7 billion of those funds; the FBI published 628 attacker wallet addresses and asked crypto firms to block them. The operation also included a supply-chain element: hackers compromised the computer of a developer maintaining widely used software and added malicious code that was detected and stopped before broad distribution.

The two actions share an attribution but differ in method and victim profile. FaceStealer is low-and-slow social engineering against individuals and their contacts, while the Binance theft is a large-scale exchange compromise via compromised developer credentials. Together they illustrate North Korea's continued use of cryptocurrency theft to fund regime programs, spanning both retail social engineering and institutional exchange breaches. The $1.2 billion seizure is the largest single recovery action to date and halts roughly half of the historic Binance breach, but the majority of the $6.3 billion remains at large.

Organizations should alert staff to meeting invites arriving from familiar contacts over messaging platforms and to requests to install software to fix audio problems, verify meeting links against official domains, and review privileged developer workstations for compromise. Crypto firms should screen against the 628 published TraderTraitor addresses. Watch for the FBI's full technical report, the outcome of forfeiture proceedings for the seized $1.2 billion, and any follow-on supply-chain indicators tied to the compromised maintainer account.
