The Justice Department and FBI announced two related North Korea actions on November 12, 2026. One targeted FaceStealer, a social engineering campaign that officials say stole more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over 18 months. The operators hijacked accounts on Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack and Discord, then sent meeting invitations through Calendly links to typo-squatted pages posing as Zoom or Microsoft Teams. During calls they replayed webcam footage of earlier victims, including multiple familiar faces, then claimed an audio failure and offered a malicious fix. The FBI seized the cloud infrastructure and more than 900 webcam videos under a federal warrant.

One intrusion described in the FBI material hit a developer of widely used software. The attackers added code that could have reached people downloading it, but the attack was detected and stopped before wide spread. The agencies also named aliases including Sapphire Sleet, BlueNoroff, APT38 and Stardust Chollima in the infrastructure action. The victim pool described in reporting includes cryptocurrency and Web3 workers and open-source developers.

Separately, the Justice Department obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion tied to the October 29 Binance theft. The FBI attributes that theft of roughly $6.3 billion to $6.4 billion to TraderTraitor and previously published 628 attacker wallet addresses. Investigators and private virtual asset firms have frozen more than $2.7 billion of those funds. What to watch is whether the FaceStealer infrastructure seizure leads to identification of the operators, and whether more of the Binance funds are recovered through the published addresses.
