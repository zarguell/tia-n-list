The Justice Department and FBI announced two actions on November 12, 2026 against North Korean cryptocurrency theft. The first is court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the roughly $6.3 billion taken from Binance on October 29, 2026, a theft the FBI attributes to TraderTraitor. Investigators working with private asset providers have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen funds, and the FBI published 628 attacker wallet addresses and asked exchanges to block them.

The second action is FaceStealer, an 18-month social engineering campaign the FBI says netted more than $400 million. Attackers hijack accounts on Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack and Discord, invite targets to video calls, and send links to typo-squatted domains serving impostor Zoom and Microsoft Teams clients. Victims are shown secretly recorded webcam video of the person they believe they are meeting, then talked into installing software disguised as an audio fix. The FBI named Sapphire Sleet, BlueNoroff, APT38 and Stardust Chollima, said it seized cloud infrastructure and more than 900 webcam recordings, and said it stopped an attempt to add malicious code to widely used software after a developer's computer was compromised.

The dollar figures are the headline; the delivery chain is the lesson. A video call is now a trust primitive that can be counterfeited, and a fake audio-driver install is a plausible pretext for a remote-access tool. Verify out-of-band before installing anything a contact sends during a call, treat unexpected meeting invites as suspect, and watch for the wallet addresses and domains in the FBI flash published as IC3 261112. The forfeiture process will decide who has a legitimate claim to the recovered funds.
