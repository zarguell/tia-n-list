On November 12, 2026 the DOJ and FBI announced two coordinated actions against North Korean cryptocurrency theft: the seizure of cloud infrastructure behind a social engineering campaign called FaceStealer, and court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion from the October 29 Binance theft attributed to the TraderTraitor cluster. FaceStealer stole more than $400 million over 18 months by hijacking real accounts on Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack, or Discord and inviting targets to fake video calls, then tricking them into installing malware disguised as an audio fix. The FBI seized more than 900 webcam videos of victims and issued FLASH report AC-111226-MS; typo-squatted Zoom and Microsoft Teams domains hosted imposter interfaces indistinguishable from the genuine ones, and the actor set includes Sapphire Sleet, BlueNoroff, APT38, and Stardust Chollima.

The Binance side of the announcement is the larger financial hit: about $6.3 billion moved out of hot wallets starting around 4:20 p.m. ET on October 29, 2026, the largest crypto heist on record, with Elliptic tracing 6.43 billion in flows and Chainalysis documenting movement across multiple blockchains. Private providers and the government have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen funds, courts authorized seizure of more than $1.2 billion, and the FBI published 628 attacker wallet addresses for firms to block. The FBI attributed the theft to TraderTraitor and urged cryptocurrency firms to freeze transactions involving those addresses.

Defenders should treat both operations as active and distinct. FaceStealer is an account-takeover and meeting-lure problem: verify meeting invitations out of band, inspect installers pitched as audio fixes, and assume a hijacked contact account can be the lure. The Binance recovery is a laundering problem: the frozen and seized amounts are large but leave billions in motion, and the wallet list is the practical artifact for exchanges and custodians to act on. Together the two actions show North Korean revenue generation running on both social engineering and large-scale exchange compromise in parallel.
