The Justice Department and FBI announced on November 12, 2026 that they disrupted FaceStealer, a North Korean social engineering campaign that stole more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over 18 months. Operators hijacked accounts on Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack and Discord, then sent meeting invitations through Calendly links to typo-squatted Zoom and Teams pages. Reporting on the FBI FLASH AC-111226-MS names the actors publicly as Sapphire Sleet, BlueNoroff, APT38 and Stardust Chollima. While a target waited for the call, the attackers recorded webcam video that was later used to impersonate familiar colleagues, followed by a fake audio error and a malicious fix or installer. The FBI seized cloud infrastructure and more than 900 webcam videos under a federal warrant.

The same announcement covered a separate TraderTraitor operation. The FBI attributes the October 29, 2026 theft of about $6.3 billion from Binance to North Korean military hackers, with more than $2.7 billion frozen by government and private partners and more than $1.2 billion authorized for seizure or restraint. The bureau published 628 attacker wallet addresses in late October. Officials also said the hackers compromised a developer of widely used software and inserted malicious code, which was detected before it spread widely. Those facts belong to the Binance theft, not to the $400 million FaceStealer tally.

The technique is effective because the invitation and the face on the call both come from previously compromised people. Polish coverage on November 18 recaps the same scheme, including fake videoconferences, recordings of real people and deepfakes. Watch for typo-squatted meeting domains, unexpected meeting invites from known contacts, fake audio or video error prompts, and installer downloads offered as a fix. Crypto, Web3 and open-source development teams face the highest exposure, and seized infrastructure does not mean the operator set has stopped using recorded video lures.
