The FBI and Justice Department disrupted FaceStealer, a North Korean social engineering operation tied to more than $400 million in cryptocurrency theft over 18 months. The scheme lured victims into fake video calls with contacts they recognized, then pushed malicious software disguised as an audio fix, yielding account takeover, fund theft, and follow-on approaches to the victim's contacts. Hackers hijacked accounts on Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack, and Discord, used typo-squatted Zoom and Teams domains, and compromised a developer who maintains widely used software, adding malicious code that was detected and stopped before it spread. Authorities seized the cloud infrastructure behind the operation and more than 900 webcam videos of victims.

The same announcement covered a separate action: court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the roughly $6 billion stolen from Binance in October 2026, which the FBI attributes to the North Korean cluster it calls TraderTraitor. Investigators working with private virtual asset service providers have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen funds. The FaceStealer and Binance actions were announced together on November 12, 2026 but involve distinct victim sets and actors.

The case shows North Korean actors combining social engineering with supply chain reach, and it carries a concrete defensive lesson: verify software dependencies and treat unexpected requests to install "audio fixes" during video calls as a social engineering indicator. The scale of the Binance seizure, about half of the stolen funds, shows the same enforcement model can bite even against large state-backed thefts.
