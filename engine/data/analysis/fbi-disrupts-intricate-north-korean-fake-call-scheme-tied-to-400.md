The DOJ and FBI disrupted FaceStealer, a North Korean social engineering campaign that stole more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over the past 18 months. The FBI seized cloud infrastructure behind the operation and more than 900 webcam videos of victims. The scheme starts from a hijacked account on Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack or Discord that sends a routine-looking meeting invitation; the link leads to a typo-squatted domain hosting an imposter Zoom or Teams page designed to be indistinguishable from the real one, and the victim is prompted to install a fake audio fix that delivers malware. Attackers then use the access to steal funds, take over accounts and approach the victim's contacts, and in one case broke into the computer of a developer who maintains widely used software, adding malicious code to a download that was detected and stopped before it spread widely.

The same announcement covered a separate action: court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion from the theft of roughly $6.3 to $6.4 billion from Binance on October 29, 2026, which the FBI attributes to the North Korean TraderTraitor cluster. Investigators and private virtual asset service providers have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen funds, and the FBI published 628 attacker wallet addresses for cryptocurrency firms to block. The two operations are distinct but both feed North Korea's regime and weapons programs.

Watch for further seizures and wallet blocking, any disclosure about the compromised maintainer's project and whether the injected code reached users, and follow-up FBI FLASH alerts detailing indicators for the FaceStealer lure chain.
