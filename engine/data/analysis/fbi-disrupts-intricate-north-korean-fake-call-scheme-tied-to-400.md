On November 12, 2026 the U.S. Department of Justice and FBI announced coordinated actions against North Korean cryptocurrency theft operations. One action targeted FaceStealer, a social engineering scheme that ran for about 18 months and stole more than $400 million. Attackers hijacked real accounts on Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack and Discord, sent meeting invitations that looked routine, and routed targets to typo-squatted Zoom or Microsoft Teams sites. Victims were prompted to install what was presented as an audio fix, which delivered malware; the operators also secretly recorded more than 900 webcam videos and reused that footage to impersonate people in later meetings. The FBI seized the cloud infrastructure behind the scheme and the webcam library, and published a FLASH alert (AC-111226-MS) detailing the mechanics.

The same announcement covered a separate, larger case: the theft of roughly $6.3 to $6.4 billion from Binance on October 29, 2026, attributed to the North Korean cluster the FBI calls TraderTraitor (also tracked as Sapphire Sleet, BlueNoroff, APT38 and Stardust Chollima). Investigators and private virtual asset service providers have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen funds, and courts have authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion. The FBI published 628 attacker wallet addresses and urged cryptocurrency firms to block transactions involving them. Reporting from Elliptic and Chainalysis traced the October 29 movement of about $6.4 billion out of Binance hot wallets across multiple blockchains, and the FBI had earlier attributed the theft to TraderTraitor.

A third element reported in the initial Justice Department announcement is a supply chain compromise: hackers broke into the computer of a developer who maintains widely used software and used that access to add malicious code. Authorities said the attack was detected and stopped before it spread widely. Together the actions illustrate a pattern of North Korean operations that combine social engineering, large scale exchange theft, and attempts to reach downstream users through trusted software.

The investigation remains active and the final disposition of seized and restrained funds will be determined through forfeiture proceedings. Organizations should treat unsolicited video meeting invitations from familiar contacts with caution, verify meeting links and domains, and block the published wallet addresses. Cryptocurrency firms and developers of widely used packages should review access controls and build pipelines for signs of compromise.
