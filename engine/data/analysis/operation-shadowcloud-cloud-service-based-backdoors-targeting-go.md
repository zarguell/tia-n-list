Kaspersky named Operation ShadowCloud in early 2026 after finding an active campaign against government organisations in Asia and Africa. The operators abuse the legitimate MeshAgent utility to deliver two previously undocumented backdoors, GitDoor and GraphDoor. Both hide command and control inside Microsoft SharePoint, GitHub and Ably, download plug-ins on demand and inject shellcode in memory. The toolset includes a modified Erfrp client and Ably messaging plug-ins to proxy traffic, with shellcode families often shipped as COFF objects. Loader DLLs named jli.dll and SbieDll.dll decrypt config.ini payloads.

The second report focuses on GraphDoor, identified by MD5 14ec33ea808e697870d00c5e434c2952. It opens a named pipe built from its process ID in the form \\.\pipe\p%lu, creates a GUID-named directory under C:\ProgramData\Intel_Log, then gathers system information. Observed plug-ins include scr.ts for screenshots, pr.ts for process listing and kl.ts for keylogging. Two observed addresses resolved to twnic[.]top and ekmt[.]tw. Kaspersky reports staging overlaps with FamousSparrow and CopperTurtle on victim systems inside a one-day window, without making a definitive attribution.

A 19 November Aviatrix post turns the reporting into defender guidance: audit MeshCentral deployments for suspicious DLL sideloading, hunt for GitDoor and GraphDoor implants, and inspect east-west traffic. The important signal is the use of trusted cloud services for C2, which keeps implant traffic close to ordinary enterprise activity. Watch for further Plug-in families, new cloud channels and additional government-sector victims in the same regions.
