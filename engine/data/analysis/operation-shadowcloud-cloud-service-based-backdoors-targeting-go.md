Kaspersky's two-part Operation ShadowCloud report covers an active campaign identified in early 2026 against government-sector victims in Asia and Africa. The actor abuses the legitimate MeshAgent utility to drop two new backdoors, GitDoor and GraphDoor, which hide command and control inside Microsoft SharePoint, GitHub issues and repositories, and the Ably messaging service. Both implants download plug-ins on demand and inject shellcode in memory. Loaders named jli.dll and SbieDll.dll decrypt config.ini payloads, and much of the shellcode is delivered as COFF objects.

The second installment analyzes GraphDoor by MD5 14ec33ea808e697870d00c5e434c2952. It opens a named pipe based on its process ID in the form \\.\pipe\p%lu, creates a GUID-named directory under C:\ProgramData\Intel_Log, and collects system information before pulling modules such as scr.ts, pr.ts and kl.ts for screenshots, process listings and keylogging. A modified Erfrp client and Ably channel proxies tunnel traffic, and two observed IP addresses resolved to twnic[.]top and ekmt[.]tw. Kaspersky notes staging overlap with FamousSparrow and CopperTurtle on victim systems within a one-day window but stops short of a definitive attribution. Watch for further reporting on the Ably and SharePoint channels, legitimate-tool abuse through MeshAgent, and whether the staging overlap is confirmed as shared infrastructure or simple co-location.
