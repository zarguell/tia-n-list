Kaspersky describes Operation ShadowCloud as an active intrusion campaign identified in early 2026 and aimed at government networks in Asia and Africa. The operator abuses the legitimate MeshAgent utility to deliver two new backdoors, GraphDoor and GitDoor. Both hide command and control traffic in Microsoft SharePoint, GitHub, and Ably services so that communications blend with ordinary network traffic. The implants can fetch plug-ins on demand and inject shellcode into memory, and the operator uses a modified Erfrp client plus Ably messaging plug-ins to proxy traffic between compromised hosts and C2 servers.

The second research installment focuses on GraphDoor, identified by MD5 14ec33ea808e697870d00c5e434c2952. It opens a PID-derived named pipe in the form \\.\pipe\p%lu, which downloaded plug-ins reuse for status reports. It also creates C:\ProgramData\Intel_Log\ with a GUID-named subdirectory, then gathers system information to send to the attacker. The first installment documented the infection chain, GitDoor's C2 protocol, and several shellcode families delivered mostly as COFF binaries.

The reported toolset is modular and maintained, with multiple observed builds and plug-ins that extend functionality without writing most payloads to disk. The researchers also point to attribution clues linking the activity to Chinese-speaking threat groups. What to watch is further reporting on the MeshAgent delivery path, SharePoint, GitHub, and Ably C2 infrastructure, and whether the GraphDoor artifacts or COFF shellcode families appear in other government intrusions.
