ThreatFabric identified a new Android banking trojan named Hirudo that combines on-device credential theft with live remote control of the infected device. Rather than overlaying fake screens, Hirudo replaces the legitimate banking app with a modified version that disables the bank's internal security checks from inside. The malware is delivered through shopping and entertainment apps that bundle it as a separate installation package, then uses Accessibility-driven techniques to gain control. Once installed, operators can harvest credentials while also manipulating the device remotely to complete fraudulent transactions.

The story is significant because it shows banking malware evolving from overlay fraud to application replacement, which undermines the assumption that genuine banking apps provide trusted execution environments. Financial institutions and mobile security teams should monitor for sideloaded updates to banking apps, unusual Accessibility service usage, and modified app signatures on customer devices.
