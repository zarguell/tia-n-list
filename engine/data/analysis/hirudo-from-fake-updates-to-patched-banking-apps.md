Hirudo is a new Android banking trojan identified by ThreatFabric. It combines on-device credential theft with live remote control of the infected device, delivered through shopping and entertainment apps that install the malware as a separate package. What distinguishes it from typical Accessibility-driven bankers is its method of fraud: instead of overlaying fake screens, Hirudo replaces the legitimate banking app with a modified copy that disables the bank's own security checks internally. The operators target banking apps directly, patching them to suppress verification before stealing account credentials.

The malware is delivered via separate installation packages bundled with benign apps. Once installed, it grants itself remote control and performs app replacement rather than simple overlay attacks. Security teams should watch for modified banking app binaries and unusual Accessibility service usage paired with app replacement behavior. ThreatFabric's reporting notes the campaign distributes through shopping and entertainment apps, indicating broad consumer-facing exposure.
