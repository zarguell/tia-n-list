Nueva EPS, the largest Entidad Promotora de Salud in Colombia's healthcare system, has allegedly been hacked. DataBreaches.net reported on October 5, 2026 that an individual is demanding $15 million not to leak the stolen data. The entity administers healthcare coverage for roughly 11.3 million people, making it the largest regional healthcare administration system in the country. EPS entities sit at the center of Colombian healthcare delivery: they implement objectives set by the Ministry of Health, issue guidelines and protocols, and oversee care delivery, so a compromise touches both member records and the administrative machinery of the system.

The word allegedly matters here. The report is based on the attacker's claims, and neither Nueva EPS nor Colombian authorities have publicly confirmed the breach or its scope as of this report. The $15 million figure is the extortionist's asking price, not a verified measure of what was taken. That leaves open the basic questions: what data classes were exfiltrated, whether clinical or only administrative records are involved, and whether the intrusion is ongoing.

Nueva EPS is a single point of failure for Colombian health data. A compromise at this scale would rival the region's worst healthcare breaches and would feed fraud, identity theft, and further extortion against millions of members whose consents and contact data flow through the entity.

Watch for three things. First, confirmation or denial from Nueva EPS, the Ministry of Health, or Colombia's data protection authority, and any mandate to notify affected members. Second, whether a leak site posting appears and what it actually contains, since staged or partial dumps are common in healthcare extortion. Third, whether Colombian regulators connect this incident to prior compromises of national health infrastructure, which would shift the story from one-off extortion to a systemic exposure problem.
