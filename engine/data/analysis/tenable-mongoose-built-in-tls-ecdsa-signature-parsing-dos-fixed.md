Tenable research advisory TRA-2026-71, posted on November 24, describes a high-severity flaw in Mongoose's built-in TLS stack (MG_TLS=MG_TLS_BUILTIN). CVE-2026-134170 concerns mg_tls_verify_cert_signature() in src/tls_builtin.c, where attacker-supplied DER length fields for the ECDSA signature integers r and s are copied without bounds checks. A remote, unauthenticated peer can declare an oversized INTEGER length during a TLS handshake and trigger an out-of-bounds read and SIGSEGV. Tenable reproduced the crash with AddressSanitizer and in a plain build, which exited with code 139.

The vulnerable parser runs in mg_tls_recv_cert() before the certificate chain or signature is verified, so a self-signed, untrusted client certificate is enough when the server is configured to request one. Tenable rates the flaw CVSS 3.1 7.5 and CVSS 4.0 8.7, credits Ireneusz Pastusiak, and lists Mongoose 7.24 or later as the fix. The fix was merged on November 9, released with Mongoose 7.24 on November 18, and published as an advisory on November 20. The action for embedded users is to move off older Mongoose builds and check whether their device actually parses peer certificates.
