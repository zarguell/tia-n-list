A joint advisory from Japanese, US, Australian, and German authorities details the WaterPlum campaign (also known as Contagious Interview), in which North Korean threat actors compromised at least 30,000 devices across over 100 countries. The campaign targeted software developers, freelancers, blockchain specialists, and cryptocurrency professionals through fake recruitment interviews on job-search and freelance platforms.

The attack chain is straightforward but effective. Actors contact targets via social media, job boards, and gig-work platforms, impersonating AI, cryptocurrency, or NFT companies. Victims are invited to virtual technical interviews or asked to complete coding tasks. During these interactions, targets are instructed to download files supposedly needed to test code or fix development environments. Some interviews reportedly used AI face-swapping software to appear legitimate, with attackers blaming network issues to explain why candidates needed to download special software.

The financial impact is substantial. Investigators estimate the operation generated at least JPY 1.7 billion (approximately $10.71 million) in stolen cryptocurrency for the DPRK between December 2025 and July 2026. Over 7,000 cryptocurrency wallets were compromised. The five-source coverage reflects the multi-government attribution and the scale of the operation. This is a mature, ongoing campaign that leverages the normal job-seeking behaviour of tech professionals as its primary attack vector.
