The North Korean threat group WaterPlum, also tracked as Contagious Interview, has compromised at least 30,000 devices across over 100 countries by targeting software developers, freelancers, and cryptocurrency professionals through fake job recruitment. A joint advisory from authorities in Japan, the United States, Australia, and Germany, issued alongside the FBI and the DoD's Cyber Crime Center, attributed the group to the 313 General Bureau of the Munitions Industry Department under the Workers' Party of Korea.

The campaign works by contacting victims through job-search platforms, freelance marketplaces, and social media, impersonating legitimate AI, cryptocurrency, or NFT companies. Targets are invited to virtual technical interviews or asked to complete coding tasks, during which they are instructed to download and run files disguised as environment setup tools or video conferencing fixes. Some actors reportedly use AI face-swapping during live interviews. The malware deployed steals cryptocurrency and credentials. The joint advisory estimates the operation generated at least JPY 1.7 billion (roughly $10.71 million) in stolen crypto between December 2025 and July 2026, with over 7,000 wallets compromised.

A separate but related campaign from Sandworm-linked UAC-0145 has been using the same fake-recruitment playbook since at least May 2026, targeting system administrators with trojanized WireGuard VPN clients. Ukrainian CERT-UA attributes this activity to APT44 and notes the attackers impersonate companies like Sopra Steria using spoofed domains. The convergence of North Korean and Russian threat groups on the same social engineering vector makes the fake-interview lure one of the most active initial access patterns of 2026.
