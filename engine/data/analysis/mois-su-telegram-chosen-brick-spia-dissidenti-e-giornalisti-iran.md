A joint advisory from NCSC (UK), FBI (US), and AIVD (Netherlands) published September 15, 2026 confirms that CHOSEN BRICK, an espionage campaign attributed to Iran's Ministry of Intelligence and Security (MOIS), remains fully operational. The campaign targets dissidents, journalists, and activists in at least three Western countries, using Telegram and WhatsApp as both the lure channel and the command-and-control infrastructure. The FBI first flagged similar activity in fall 2023, with a further public warning in March 2026 tied to a State Department bounty linked to a July 2025 hack-and-leak operation.

The attack chain relies on social engineering rather than exploits. Operators build trust over weeks on WhatsApp or Telegram before sending a payload disguised as a medical report (including falsified MRI images), or as a cloned legitimate application like Pictory, RunwayML, Norton Antivirus, Adobe Flash Player, or KeePass. Once installed, the malware exfiltrates Telegram and WhatsApp message histories, contacts, screen captures, microphone audio, and email content. The C2 architecture is distinctive: each victim gets a unique Telegram bot ID, routing traffic through api.telegram.org and blending into legitimate service noise. File exfiltration uses low-monitoring cloud providers including Backblaze B2, Vultr Object Storage, and Storj.

Persistence is achieved through Run registry keys (observed values: SMQDService, winappx), with Windows Defender exclusions added for evasion. The operational pattern of convincing targets to move conversations to personal devices, outside employer IT monitoring, is a recurring tactic. For organizations hosting Iranian dissidents or human rights workers, the joint attribution signals a systemic campaign rather than isolated incidents. Immediate priorities include monitoring for unexpected Telegram API traffic from corporate endpoints and connections to the identified exfiltration domains.
