Ahold Delhaize, one of the world's largest food retail groups, took e-commerce systems offline across its U.S. and European brands on October 12, 2026 after a cyberattack. The outage hit websites and apps for Albert Heijn, Etos, Gall & Gall, and Delhaize in Europe and Stop & Shop in the United States, along with employee scheduling and pay systems. Physical stores stayed open throughout. The company has since confirmed the incident was a cyberattack, restored Albert Heijn's site and app with home deliveries resuming October 23, and expects U.S. e-commerce to return within days, while some European systems remain down.

Ahold Delhaize says it has no evidence that customer, associate, or partner data was stolen, and it notified the Dutch National Cyber Security Centre. It has not disclosed the type of attack; when asked whether corporate or store systems were affected and whether devices were encrypted, a spokesperson referred to the company statement. The disruption to online ordering and deliveries across multiple countries shows how a single incident can degrade grocery supply logistics even without store closures.

What to watch: whether the company later confirms ransomware or data theft, the pace of restoring remaining U.S. and European services, and any follow-on disclosures affecting Hannaford or other Ahold Delhaize banners. Customers should monitor brand communications for updates on delivery resumption.
