Ahold Delhaize, one of the world's largest food retail chains, confirmed that a cyberattack disclosed the previous week disrupted services at stores across Europe and the United States. Customers could not use the websites, apps and customer service platforms for Albert Heijn, Etos and Gall & Gall in the Netherlands, or Delhaize in Belgium, while employees at the Stop & Shop chain in the United States reported trouble accessing schedules and pay stubs. The company took systems offline as a containment step and stores remained open to serve customers. In a Monday update, Ahold Delhaize said Albert Heijn's website and app are back online with home deliveries expected to resume from Friday, October 23, the Etos and Gall & Gall sites are available, e-commerce for the US brands is expected back within days, and e-commerce in Belgium is fully operational.

This is the second major cyberattack on the retailer's systems in recent years. In November 2024 the personal, financial and health information of more than 2.2 million individuals was stolen from the company's US systems, a breach claimed by the ransomware group INC Ransom. The multinational retailer employs more than 384,000 people and operates 9,551 stores serving 77 million customers each week across the United States, Europe and Indonesia, so the operational reach of a single incident is large even when the stores themselves stay open.

What to watch: whether the company later confirms ransomware or data theft, the pace of restoring the remaining US and European services, and any follow-on disclosure affecting other banners such as Hannaford. The disruption to online ordering and deliveries in several countries shows how one incident degrades grocery supply logistics even with no store closures, and customers should watch brand communications for delivery resumption times. The pattern of a widely used third-party service platform being a common denominator across recent retail incidents is worth keeping in mind for procurement and vendor risk review.
