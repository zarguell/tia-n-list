Symantec and Carbon Black researchers tie a run of Warlock ransomware intrusions to a China-linked actor they track as Longlegs, the same group Microsoft calls Storm-2603, now confirmed against a water utility, a telecom provider, a regional government body, and a university. The actor emerged in June 2025 and gained notoriety a month later exploiting the ToolShell chain of SharePoint zero-days: CVE-2025-49704, CVE-2025-49706, CVE-2025-53770, and CVE-2025-53771, the same flaws Microsoft saw used by the state-backed Linen Typhoon and Violet Typhoon. Initial access comes through on-premises SharePoint, after which the actor drops a web shell built to work across multiple SharePoint versions and deploys an AV/EDR-killing tool before encryption. In the intrusion that started July 22, that tool disabled protection software on at least 40 hosts within about two hours, after which Warlock encrypted at least 33.

Victim selection looks linguistic rather than sectoral: two months of activity concentrated on Portuguese and Spanish-speaking countries across Europe, Africa, and Latin America. That means exposure tracks the unpatched on-premises SharePoint estate in those regions, not a critical-infrastructure targeting list, and any organization with internet-facing SharePoint is a candidate. For water and telecom operators the damage case is worse than data loss, since the encrypted corporate network is the one that schedules crews, billing, and often supervisory support tooling.

The ToolShell fixes shipped in July 2025 and are KEV-listed, so this is neglect exploitation, but patching alone does not evict this actor: the multi-version web shell and stolen key material survive an update, so previously exposed servers need sweeping, ASP.NET machine keys need rotation, and IIS logs need review for ToolPane.aspx POSTs and shells under LAYOUTS. Because the actor blinded EDR on 40 hosts in two hours, also alert on mass protection-service stop events; once encryption starts, detection time is over. Watch for the language-cluster victim set extending into Iberian or Latin American utilities and government networks, and for the EDR-killing tool moving from selective to routine use, which would compress the two-hour access-to-encryption window further.
