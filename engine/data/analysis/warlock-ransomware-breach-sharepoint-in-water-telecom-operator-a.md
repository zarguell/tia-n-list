The China-linked ransomware group Warlock targeted a water utility, telecom provider, regional government body, and a university by exploiting SharePoint vulnerabilities for initial access. Over two months, the actor focused on Portuguese- and Spanish-speaking countries across Europe, Africa, and Latin America. Warlock emerged in June 2025 and gained notoriety after exploiting ToolShell zero-day chain CVE-2025-49704, CVE-2025-49706, CVE-2025-53770, and CVE-2025-53771.

By August, Microsoft observed state-backed groups Linen Typhoon and Violet Typhoon using the same exploits, along with Storm-2603. The overlap between state actors and ransomware groups increases risk for critical infrastructure operators. Water and telecom sectors must prioritize SharePoint patching, segment operational networks, and review access logs for ToolShell exploitation indicators. The campaign underscores how ransomware actors adopt state-sponsored exploit chains for broader targeting.
