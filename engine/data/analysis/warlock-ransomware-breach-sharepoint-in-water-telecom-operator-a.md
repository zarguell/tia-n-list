The China-linked Warlock ransomware group, also tracked as Storm-2603 and Longlegs, targeted a water utility, a telecom provider, a regional government body, and a university by exploiting on-premises SharePoint vulnerabilities. The campaign ran over roughly two months and focused on Portuguese and Spanish speaking countries across Europe, Africa, and Latin America, deploying ransomware on at least 33 hosts. Initial access relied on the ToolShell chain of SharePoint zero-days CVE-2025-49704, CVE-2025-49706, CVE-2025-53770, and CVE-2025-53771, which Microsoft first saw exploited in July 2025 by state-backed groups Linen Typhoon and Violet Typhoon as well as Storm-2603. The broader CVE list associated with the actor includes additional 2025 and 2026 identifiers.

Warlock emerged in June 2025 and has persisted in exploiting SharePoint flaws well after their disclosure, indicating that unpatched on-premises SharePoint remains a reliable entry point. The targeting of water and telecom operators raises critical infrastructure concerns, and the use of ToolShell provides both initial access and a path to ransomware deployment.

Organizations running on-premises SharePoint should verify patches for the ToolShell CVEs and all subsequent SharePoint updates, restrict external access to SharePoint endpoints, and monitor for the post-exploitation behavior described in the reporting. Critical infrastructure operators in particular should treat unpatched SharePoint as an active ransomware vector.
