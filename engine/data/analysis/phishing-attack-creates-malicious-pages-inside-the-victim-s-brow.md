A new phishing technique abandons traditional hosted credential-harvesting pages in favor of generating the phishing content directly inside the victim's browser using blob URLs. Because the malicious page exists only in the browser's memory during the session, security tools cannot crawl, categorize, or blocklist it before a victim encounters it. The campaign chains through legitimate Microsoft infrastructure: a DocuSign-themed email delivers a calendar invitation file that routes the victim through Microsoft OAuth endpoints and into Microsoft Teams, making the attack chain appear trustworthy at every step.

The campaign was identified by Barracuda and reported across SecurityWeek, GBHackers, and CyberPress. The use of blob URLs represents a meaningful evolution in phishing tradecraft. Traditional phishing kits rely on attacker-controlled hosting that can be taken down or flagged by reputation services. This approach eliminates that dependency entirely. The attacker only needs to deliver the initial lure; the weaponized page assembles itself in the target's browser after they follow the legitimate-looking redirect chain.

Defenders should look for calendar invitation files (.ics) that route through OAuth endpoints to Teams, especially when the originating email appears to be from DocuSign or similar document-signing services. Email filtering rules targeting the initial lure remain the most practical control, since the blob URL technique makes post-delivery detection nearly impossible. Browser-based detection of blob URL content generation is not currently feasible at scale.
