Picus Security researcher Sila Ozeren Hacioglu published an analysis on BleepingComputer arguing that traditional zero-day response playbooks are obsolete. The piece uses PaperCut NG/MF's August 2026 patching saga as its central case study: PaperCut issued an urgent advisory on August 27 with no CVE, no exploit, and no patch. The first emergency patch came a day later and was bypassed the same day. A third patch arrived September 1. Six days elapsed without a patch that held or an exploit to test against, while attackers were already exploiting in the wild.

The core argument is that disclosure-to-exploitation timelines have compressed from an average of 21.5 days to measured-in-hours, making the traditional "wait for patch, test, deploy" cycle dangerously slow. The article walks through a hypothetical CVE-2026-1001 hour-by-hour to illustrate what modern zero-day response actually requires: immediate virtual patching, control validation, and parallel patch testing rather than sequential steps.

This is an analyst-oriented piece rather than a vulnerability disclosure. Its value is in framing the operational reality that defensive teams face as AI accelerates vulnerability discovery and exploitation. The PaperCut example is well-documented and the timeline claims are specific enough to be actionable for security teams building response runbooks.
