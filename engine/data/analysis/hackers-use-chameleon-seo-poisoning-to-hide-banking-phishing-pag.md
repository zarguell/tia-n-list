Fortra Intelligence and Research Experts (FIRE) documented a technique they call "Chameleon SEO Poisoning" that has surged more than 40% in adoption. The approach flips the phishing delivery model from push (email, SMS) to pull: attackers register typo-squatted domains on private second-level domains like .ph.com and .gr.com, then invest in SEO manipulation to rank malicious pages above legitimate results for high-intent queries such as "Bank Name Customer Portal" or "Credit Card Login."

The core evasion is referrer-based cloaking that FIRE terms "Presentation Control." Direct visits from security crawlers, reputation services, or users typing the URL return a dead or 404 page, making the domain appear abandoned. But when the HTTP referrer header confirms a click from Google or Bing, the server deploys a pixel-perfect banking portal clone designed to harvest credentials. This lets phishing pages persist in search rankings while dodging automated scanners that would otherwise flag and block them.

The technique matters because it targets the trust relationship between users and search engines. Victims arrive via a Google or Bing result they perceive as legitimate, bypassing the usual email-security training that teaches skepticism of links. Financial institutions and search providers will need to adapt detection: referrer-aware cloaking defeats standard crawl-based phishing detection, and the use of private SLDs complicates domain-reputation scoring. Watch for adoption beyond banking verticals and for search engines to tighten referrer-based detection signals.
