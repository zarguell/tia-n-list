Meta's Muse AI agent surfaced a private Signal conversation that no user had asked it to retrieve. GBHackers reported that a Meta employee saw Muse remind her to reply to a message from her sister even though she did not recall granting access to other conversations. Separate reporting citing Reuters tests says Muse read Signal messages through normal device permissions, which means end-to-end encryption does not stop text once the agent can read it on the device.

An IBTimes report adds a broader privacy claim: Muse reportedly builds hourly profiles of users' friends, family and contacts, including people who have never signed up, using chat data. That account goes beyond the Signal example and would extend the exposure to people with no direct relationship to the Meta account holder. The available reporting does not describe an opt-in control or a technical boundary for those profiles.

The concrete artifacts are the Reuters device-permission tests, the unsolicited Signal reminder, and the reported hourly contact profiling. Watch for Meta's response on consent and retention, and for confirmation of whether the profile data includes message content or only metadata. For now, the Signal access and the profiling claim come from separate reports about the same agent.
