NGINX 1.30.6 (stable) and 1.31.7 (mainline) close five flaws, three of them critical: CVE-2026-15176 and CVE-2026-97205 in ngx_http_v3_module, and CVE-2026-93479 in the map directive. The vendor advisory from nginx.org on 2026-10-14 lists vulnerable ranges from 0.1.29 through 1.31.6 for the alias/rewrite overflow (CVE-2026-15176). Remote attackers without privileges can trigger heap-based buffer overflows or use-after-free conditions in the worker process, leading to denial of service or code execution when ASLR is disabled or bypassed. F5 released matching out-of-band patches for NGINX Plus, Open Source, Ingress Controller, Gateway Fabric, and Instance Manager.

The exposure is wider than the event timeline suggests: every NGINX instance using non-default configurations (alias with rewrite, unnamed regex captures, or AIO with SSI) is at risk, and the vulnerable version span reaches back to releases from 2009. The events note no confirmed exploitation counts, but the combination of remote, unauthenticated attack surface, critical severity ratings, and memory-corruption primitives makes this story distinct from routine patch announcements. A successful compromise gives an attacker control over the web-server worker, which in practice means direct access to application traffic and potential pivot into backend infrastructure.

Defenders should upgrade to 1.31.7 or 1.30.6 immediately. Where patching is blocked, apply the specific workarounds: avoid the `alias` directive with rewrite for CVE-2026-15176, disable AIO or SSI for CVE-2026-97205, use named captures instead of unnamed captures for CVE-2026-93479, and restrict the regex-match block scope. Hunt logs for anomalous worker crashes or restarts tied to HTTP/3 traffic or SSI/AIO usage, and review configurations for `$request_filename` references combined with rewrite rules. Watch for F5 advisory updates and any in-the-wild telemetry linking these CVEs to active scanning or exploitation.
