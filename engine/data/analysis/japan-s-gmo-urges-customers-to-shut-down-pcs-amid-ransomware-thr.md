GMO Internet pulled the plug on 26,739 FX-trading cloud desktop servers after attackers planted malware that traded on customer accounts. The scale is explicit: all servers in the FX-trading cloud desktop service were shut down. The event ties the response to Japan's National Cybersecurity recommendations regarding ransomware threats, though it does not name a specific ransomware family.

The incident combines malware deployment with direct financial abuse: attackers did not only encrypt or steal data but actively traded on compromised accounts. That operational behavior suggests either insider-level access or a high-privilege compromise of the trading environment, not a simple endpoint infection.

Watch for GMO's post-incident report that should detail the malware entry point, whether customer funds were recovered, and whether trading logs show additional unauthorized transactions. Similar cloud-desktop trading platforms should review isolation between customer trading sessions and server-level access.
