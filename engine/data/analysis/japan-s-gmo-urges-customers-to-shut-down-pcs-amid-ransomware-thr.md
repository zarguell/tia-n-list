Japan's GMO Internet Group urged Onamae.com Desktop Cloud customers to disconnect from networks, shut down computers, and unplug cables after attackers breached the cloud desktop service and threatened ransomware at noon on October 27. The company confirmed unauthorized access and malicious program installation in customer server environments, with progressive server shutdowns starting October 21 at 04:00 JST. The incident affects 26,739 customer servers according to one update. GMO also offered a bounty up to 1 million dollars for information on the perpetrators.

This is an active intrusion with confirmed malicious access, not just a threat. The emergency disconnect instructions indicate the attacker had sufficient foothold to attempt widespread ransomware deployment. Organizations using GMO cloud desktop or similar managed desktop services should treat this as a supply-chain risk indicator and review their own vendor security notifications. Watch for further updates from GMO regarding data exposure and whether the ransomware was executed.
