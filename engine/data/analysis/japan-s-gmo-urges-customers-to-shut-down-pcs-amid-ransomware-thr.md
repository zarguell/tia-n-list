GMO Internet Group's Onamae.com Desktop Cloud service was compromised by a third party who installed malicious programs in some customer server environments, with the intrusion confirmed on October 20, 2026. GMO's first advisory, dated October 21, said the company began progressively shutting down customer servers starting at 04:00 local time that day, affecting 26,739 customer servers. A follow-up on October 22 confirmed ongoing investigation into unauthorized access and potential customer data compromise. The attackers threatened to publish customer information and launch ransomware at noon on October 27, prompting GMO to urge customers to back up data, disconnect computers from networks, shut them down, and unplug cables.

The incident escalated into a $50 million extortion ultimatum, which GMO refused to pay, and the company offered a bounty of up to $1 million for information on the perpetrators via cybercrimebounty.gmo. Reporting also noted a breach exposing remote-access vulnerabilities and that the cloud provider urged clients to disconnect systems amid the threat. The attacker pool and initial access vector are not detailed in the available advisories, and the full scope of customer data exposure remains under investigation.

Customers of Onamae.com Desktop Cloud should follow GMO's guidance to keep affected systems offline until the company confirms remediation, verify backups are intact and not connected to the compromised environment, and monitor GMO's advisories for recovery instructions. Organizations with similar cloud-desktop deployments should review remote-access controls and segmentation, since this incident began with unauthorized access to a shared hosting environment.
