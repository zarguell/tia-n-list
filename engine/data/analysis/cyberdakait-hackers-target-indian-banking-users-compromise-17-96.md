Group-IB has exposed CyberDakait, a financially motivated Android malware operation targeting Indian banking customers since July 2025. Researchers identified at least 17,960 compromised devices and 7,611,443 intercepted SMS messages, with the campaign combining banking credential theft, remote device control, and cryptocurrency mining. Group-IB clusters seven distinct trojans under the CyberDakait umbrella because of overlapping distribution methods and phishing infrastructure rather than shared code.

The scale of SMS interception is the standout figure: over 7.6 million messages gives the operators access to one-time passwords and transaction alerts across a large victim pool, which is what makes the banking fraud viable at this volume. The cryptocurrency mining component suggests the same device fleet is being monetized twice, once through fraud and once through compute.

Defenders should treat Indian banking users as the primary exposure and watch for the phishing distribution channels Group-IB ties to the campaign. Group-IB's clustering is behavioral rather than code-based, so detection engineering should key on delivery and C2 overlap rather than family signatures.
