The Register reported on 2026-11-12 that eMudhra, the Indian certificate authority, blames a buggy script for issuing certificates judged hopelessly weak, and that the script existed because of global supply-chain constraints. The Register's own framing describes eMudhra as a disgraced former CA, which reflects where the incident left the company rather than any new enforcement action detailed in the reporting.

This is a trust-chain problem rather than a single-vendor bug. Certificates that should never have been issued stay valid until browsers and relying parties distrust the issuing chain, and weak keys or predictable material make impersonation feasible for as long as that chain is trusted. The material available here is the headline and its lead paragraph; the specific defect, how many certificates were affected, the key sizes or serial patterns generated, and whether eMudhra's roots have been removed from any trust store are not stated in it.

Watch for the script and its output being described in detail, a count of affected certificates, eMudhra's remediation status against the CA/Browser Forum baseline requirements, and any browser or operating-system trust-store action. Until those are public, treat certificates from this CA in the affected window as suspect and check certificate transparency logs and pinning for unexpected issuance.
