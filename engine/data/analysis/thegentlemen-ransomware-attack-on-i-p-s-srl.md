TheGentlemen ransomware group has now claimed 10 victims across six countries since August 13, up from the original batch of seven. The three newest claims, all posted August 19, add Senvest Capital (international investment firm, USA), Roadvision Systems (trucking management software, USA), and Gfeller Treuhand und Verwaltungs AG (Swiss real estate and fiduciary services, Switzerland, first reported August 14). Every claim follows the identical template: a claim of intrusion plus a threat to publish the full leak unless a company representative opens negotiations through the group's channels.

The expansion from seven to ten victims in under a week, all from the same leak-site batch format, points to a sustained operation rather than a one-off burst. The targets remain overwhelmingly mid-sized companies in non-IT verticals: recycling, HR, food service, auto dealerships, safety equipment, investment, trucking software, and real estate. This pattern is consistent with opportunistic access, likely via commodity RATs or initial access brokers, rather than targeted intrusion campaigns. Senvest Capital and Gfeller Treuhand are the highest-profile names in the updated set because of the financial data they likely hold.

None of the 10 claims have been independently confirmed by the victims. Watch for any victim to disclose what data was taken or for the leak site to actually publish files. The speed of new claims suggests more victims may appear in the coming days. If any victim confirms an intrusion or the group publishes exfiltrated data, the story escalates from allegation to verified breach.
