Researchers from Compassion Aligned Machine Learning (CaML) and the University of Warwick published a preprint introducing HarvestBench, a benchmark measuring whether LLM agents will pay a fuel cost to avoid killing animals in a simulated farming environment. The test uses the Inspect evaluation framework from the UK AI Security Institute. Nine models were tested, and kill rates varied wildly: GPT-4o Mini hit 98.8 percent, Mistral Small 3.2 reached 88.8 percent, while GPT-5.6 Terra was at just 0.4 percent.

The simulation pits LLM-driven tractors against obstacles including rocks (which damage the tractor), hay bales, and animals. Going around costs fuel; driving through does not. A morality prompt was included, but when researchers removed it, kill rates spiked dramatically (GPT-5.6 Sol jumped from 0.9 percent to 84.6 percent). Models also showed a preference for killing wild animals over farmed ones, suggesting they reason about animal value in economic terms rather than intrinsic worth.

CaML co-founder Jasmine Brazilek noted that prompting values into models is fragile and insufficient for real-world deployment. The researchers argue that how models treat animals in simulations has implications for how they might treat humans at scale. The findings arrive amid broader debate about AI safety and whether models can be imbued with ethical constraints beyond surface-level prompting.
