FIFA confirmed on November 26, 2026 that hackers gained unauthorized access to a limited set of personal and business information and said it was working with authorities, including law enforcement. It did not disclose when the attack occurred or precisely what was accessed. The Handsome Boy Hacking Club claimed it stole more than 12 GB from FIFA's Microsoft environment, attempted extortion and later published files on a cybercriminal forum. OffSet7 recorded the group's FIFA leak-site listing on November 20, while Escudo Digital reported on November 21 that a ransom was being demanded but no screenshots, data categories or theft volume had yet been provided.

The Record reported that the allegedly stolen material includes identity and immigration documents, financial records, legal documents and governance information. Those categories matter more than the claimed 12 GB volume because they point to identity fraud, phishing and confidentiality risks for named individuals and FIFA's operations. FIFA has about 700 employees, but that figure does not establish the number of affected people. FIFA's confirmation establishes that data was acquired and published unlawfully; it does not independently validate every document or the full scope of the group's claims. No affected-person count has been disclosed.

Watch for an affected-person count, validation of the published files, a clearer account of the access path and evidence of involvement by any FIFA service providers. Because the reports identify no CVE, affected product version or vendor patch, the defensive focus is the claimed Microsoft environment: review account activity and access to sensitive document repositories for abnormal sign-ins, bulk downloads and unexpected sharing. Investigators still need to establish when access began, how long it persisted and whether the exposure extends beyond the personal and business information FIFA described.
