FIFA confirmed on November 26, 2026 that hackers breached its systems and accessed what it called a "limited set of personal and business information." The soccer governing body said it was working with relevant authorities and law enforcement. It did not disclose the attack date, the precise categories or number of people affected, and did not answer follow-up questions about the incident. FIFA is headquartered in Zurich and has about 700 employees.

The Handsome Boy Hacking Club claimed responsibility and said it stole more than 12 GB of data, later publishing material on a cybercriminal forum. The group claimed access to FIFA's Microsoft environment and described identity and immigration documents, financial records, legal documents and governance information among the stolen files. Those volume and content claims remain the group's assertions, not independently verified findings. OffSet7 recorded an HBHC leak-site countdown on November 20, and Escudo Digital reported the extortion listing on November 21 before FIFA's confirmation.

The confirmed intrusion establishes unauthorized access and publication of personal data, but not the full scope advertised by the extortion group. Watch for FIFA's identification of affected people, regulator or law-enforcement findings, and technical detail on the initial access vector. Authenticity checks on the published files and any link to the claimed Microsoft environment will determine whether the 12 GB figure and document categories can be treated as fact.
