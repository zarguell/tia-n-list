FIFA confirmed that hackers gained unauthorized access to a 'limited set of personal and business information' and said it was working with relevant authorities, including law enforcement. The November 26 confirmation did not identify the attack date, the intrusion method or the precise records involved. FIFA is headquartered in Zurich and has about 700 employees, according to The Record.

The incident began with claims from the Handsome Boy Hacking Club. OffSet7 recorded a FIFA leak-site listing on November 20 with a countdown, and Escudo Digital reported a ransom demand on November 21 while noting that no screenshots, data categories or theft volume had been independently verified. The group later claimed more than 12 GB of stolen data, alleged access to FIFA's Microsoft environment, and publication of identity and immigration documents, financial records, legal files and governance information. Those details remain the group's claims; FIFA's statement confirms unauthorized access and theft of some information but does not validate the volume or categories.

The confirmed exposure of personal data creates privacy and fraud risk, while the alleged governance and legal files could have separate commercial and reputational consequences. Watch for FIFA's notification scope, the number of affected people, law-enforcement findings and independent verification of published samples. The key distinction is between FIFA's confirmed breach and HBHC's unverified 12 GB and data-category claims.
