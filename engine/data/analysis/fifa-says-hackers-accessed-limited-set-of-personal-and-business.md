FIFA confirmed on November 26, 2026 that hackers gained unauthorized access to a limited set of personal and business information. It said it was working with authorities, including law enforcement, but did not disclose when the attack occurred or precisely what was accessed. The Handsome Boy Hacking Club claimed it stole more than 12 GB from FIFA's Microsoft environment, attempted extortion and later published files on a cybercriminal forum.

OffSet7 recorded the group's FIFA leak-site listing on November 20. Escudo Digital reported on November 21 that a ransom was being demanded, but at that point the group had provided no screenshots, data categories or theft volume. The Record later reported alleged identity and immigration documents, financial records, legal documents and governance information among the stolen material. FIFA's confirmation establishes unlawful acquisition and publication of information, but it does not independently validate every document, the claimed volume or the full scope of the group's assertions.

The alleged identity and immigration records create risks beyond routine credential theft, including impersonation and targeted fraud against named individuals. FIFA's roughly 700 employees do not establish the number of affected people, and no affected-person count has been disclosed. Watch for validation of the published files, notices to individuals, a clearer access timeline and evidence of involvement by any FIFA service provider.
