Trezor confirmed that 347,000 of its users were targeted in phishing attacks following a data breach at Brevo, the email service provider Trezor used for marketing communications. Attackers obtained Trezor customer email addresses from the Brevo breach and launched a targeted phishing campaign directing users to fraudulent pages mimicking Trezor's official site. The phishing emails urged recipients to update their wallet firmware or verify their accounts, tactics designed to steal seed phrases and private keys.

The breach highlights the downstream risk of third-party vendor compromises. Trezor itself was not hacked; the exposure stemmed from Brevo's breach, yet the impact falls on Trezor's customer base. Hardware wallet users are high-value phishing targets because a successful seed phrase theft gives attackers direct access to cryptocurrency holdings with no recovery path.

BleepingComputer first reported the story. Trezor users should treat any unsolicited email claiming to be from Trezor as suspicious, never enter seed phrases into web forms, and verify firmware updates only through Trezor Suite or the official hardware interface. Organizations in the cryptocurrency space should audit their third-party email service dependencies and consider whether customer PII stored in marketing platforms warrants the same protections as production databases.
