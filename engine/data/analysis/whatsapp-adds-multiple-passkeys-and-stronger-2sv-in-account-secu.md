WhatsApp is rolling out three account security improvements: support for multiple passkeys, upgraded two-step verification with alphanumeric passwords, and enhanced caller information screens. The passkey update allows users to register separate passkeys for Android and iOS devices, addressing a friction point for users who switch between platforms. WhatsApp reports over one billion passkeys already configured.

The two-step verification change replaces the previous six-digit PIN with full alphanumeric passwords supporting special characters. This significantly increases the entropy of the secondary authentication factor and reduces susceptibility to brute-force or social engineering attacks targeting weak PINs. The call screen update displays additional context for non-contact callers, including country of origin and shared groups, giving users more information before answering potential scam calls.

These changes reflect WhatsApp's continued investment in account security following years of SIM-swapping and social engineering attacks targeting high-profile users. The passkey multi-device support and stronger 2SV directly address known attack vectors, while the caller context feature adds a layer of defense against the platform's persistent scam problem.
