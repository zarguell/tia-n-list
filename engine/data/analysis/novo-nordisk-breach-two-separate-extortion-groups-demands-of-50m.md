Two separate threat actors breached Novo Nordisk, and neither got paid. FulcrumSec demanded $25 million, accessed the Danish pharma giant's systems in March via a GitHub access token, cloned repositories, and harvested further credentials, stealing 1.3TB that included undisclosed drug programs, proprietary compound structures, the Dicerna RNAi pipeline, and private AI models. A second, unnamed actor demanded $50 million. Both demands went unpaid, and FulcrumSec leaked data in June after its deadline passed. Novo Nordisk first disclosed the breach on June 11; the stolen clinical trial data was pseudonymized and not directly linkable to patients by name.

On August 12 FulcrumSec dumped the remainder, publishing Novo Nordisk's complete enterprise HuggingFace AI/ML ecosystem: 30 models, 70 datasets, and half a terabyte of proprietary Cell Painting microscopy images. This is the second dump in the extortion arc, confirming the unpaid demands lead to full disclosure rather than a one-off sample. The pharmaceutical AI/ML stack is the notable exposure: trained models and assay imagery are hard to rotate or invalidate, unlike credentials.

Watch for continued leaks from the second actor, whose $50 million demand has also gone unanswered. The dumped models and RNAi data are research-grade IP; their appearance in downstream analysis or research may surface over time. Pseudonymized clinical data limits direct patient harm, but the scale of the AI/ML release makes this a reference case for how pharma threat models must treat model registries and image repositories, not just source code and credentials.
