The Florida Department of Education disclosed unauthorized criminal access to the Florida Student Financial Aid website, potentially exposing personal information for roughly 4.9 million people. The intrusion was discovered on October 19 and publicly announced on October 29. Affected data may include names, Social Security numbers, dates of birth, home addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, and gender codes, though FLDOE notes not all individuals were necessarily affected and the specifics vary by person.

The site is the online hub for Florida's state-funded scholarship and grant programs, and anyone who has applied for financial aid in the state could be among those affected. FLDOE has not disclosed the intrusion vector or attributed the attack, and no threat group has publicly claimed it. The Florida Digital Service and the Florida Department of Law Enforcement are assisting the response, and some department websites and online services may be disrupted during remediation.

The scale of the exposed identifiers, especially Social Security numbers, makes this a long-tail identity-theft risk rather than a one-time notification event. Affected individuals should monitor credit and expect phishing tailored to financial aid applicants, and institutions should watch for fraudulent aid applications using the leaked data.
