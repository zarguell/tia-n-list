The Florida Department of Education disclosed that unauthorized criminal access to the Florida Student Financial Aid website (FloridaStudentFinancialAidSG.org) may have exposed personal information belonging to approximately 4.9 million people. Staff learned of the intrusion on October 19, 2026, and announced it publicly on October 29. The website is the online hub for Florida's state-funded scholarship and grant programs. Data types potentially accessed include names, Social Security numbers, dates of birth, home addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, and gender codes. The department has not disclosed how the attackers entered or who is responsible, and no threat group has publicly claimed the attack. The Florida Digital Service and the Florida Department of Law Enforcement are assisting with the response, and some department websites may be disrupted during remediation.

The scale of the exposure, roughly 4.9 million records including Social Security numbers, creates long-term identity-theft risk for current and former financial aid applicants across Florida. Because the affected population includes anyone who has applied for state aid in the past, the blast radius extends well beyond current students. The department's statement that not all individuals were necessarily affected and that the specific information varies by person leaves open the possibility of deeper exposure for a subset of records.

Affected individuals should monitor credit and consider fraud alerts or freezes, and watch for official notification from FLDOE. Watch for a root-cause disclosure, whether the intrusion involved a known vulnerability or credential compromise, and whether any stolen data appears for sale. The absence of a claimed attacker so far does not reduce the risk; it may indicate the data is being held for later use.
