SonicWall advisory AV26-1133 covers CVE-2026-19868, an authentication enforcement flaw in the SonicOS SSLVPN service that lets a user with valid credentials for an alternate authentication method bypass SAML controls, including SAML-enforced MFA. SonicWall states the vulnerability is being exploited, and CISA added CVE-2026-19868 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 6, 2026. Affected builds are Gen7 and Gen8 SonicOS at 7.3.3-7015 and older and 8.2.2-8015 and older; fixed builds are 7.3.4-7013 and 8.2.2-8015 or later.

The flaw is tracked as CWE-308 with a CVSS 3.1 base score of 7.5, and NIST published its NVD entry on November 6, 2026 sourcing SonicWall. A separate, older SonicWall SSLVPN issue (CVE-2024-40766, improper access control) was previously added to KEV and was used by Akira affiliates as an initial access vector against devices with MFA disabled and vulnerable firmware, which underscores that SonicWall SSLVPN has been a recurring target.

Administrators should upgrade affected Gen7 and Gen8 appliances to the fixed builds, review SAML-enforced MFA configurations on SSLVPN, and check for indicators of compromise. The combination of an authentication bypass with confirmed in-the-wild exploitation makes this a high-priority patch even at a moderate CVSS score, because it directly defeats MFA on remote access paths.
