SonicWall disclosed CVE-2026-19868, an authentication enforcement flaw in the SonicOS SSLVPN service that lets a user with valid credentials for an alternate authentication method bypass SAML authentication controls, including SAML-enforced multi-factor authentication. The flaw only affects SSL-VPN services configured to use SAML authentication. It carries a CVSS score of 7.1 per SonicWall's advisory (NVD records 7.5), is tracked under CWE-308, and was reported by Ryan Harris of The Electronic Office. SonicWall has released fixed builds 7.3.4-7013 and 8.2.2-8015, which also address six additional vulnerabilities in the same advisory, SNWLID-2026-0014 (Canadian Centre advisory AV26-1133).

SonicWall's product security team investigated a report indicating potential exploitation, and CISA added CVE-2026-19868 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 6, 2026, confirming in-the-wild exploitation. Under Binding Operational Directive 26-04, federal civilian executive branch agencies must remediate by November 20, 2026. Neither SonicWall nor CISA has shared details about the reported attacks, and it is unclear whether the flaw was exploited as a zero-day before the patch.

Administrators should verify SAML is enabled on any SSL-VPN and prioritize patching Gen7 and Gen8 devices running builds 7.3.3-7015 and older or 8.2.2-8015 and older. Because the bypass defeats MFA for an attacker who already holds valid alternate-method credentials, organizations should also review authentication logs for anomalous SAML bypasses and consider restricting alternate authentication paths until patched.
