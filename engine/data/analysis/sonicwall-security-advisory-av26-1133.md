SonicWall released security advisory AV26-1133 (SNWLID-2026-0014) on November 6, 2026, fixing seven SonicOS vulnerabilities. The headline flaw is CVE-2026-19868, an authentication enforcement bug in the SSLVPN service: a user holding valid credentials for an alternate authentication method can bypass SAML authentication controls, including SAML-enforced MFA. It carries a CVSS score of 7.1, affects Gen7 and Gen8 firewalls running SonicOS 7.3.3-7015 and older or 8.2.2-8015 and older, and is only exposed when SAML-based SSLVPN login is enabled. Ryan Harris of The Electronic Office discovered and reported it.

SonicWall PSIRT investigated a report indicating potential exploitation, and CISA added CVE-2026-19868 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day, confirming in-the-wild exploitation. Federal civilian agencies must remediate by November 20, 2026 under Binding Operational Directive 26-04. Neither the vendor nor CISA has published details of the observed attacks, and it remains unclear whether the flaw was exploited as a zero-day before patches shipped. Fixes land in SonicOS 7.3.4-7013 and 8.2.3-8015.

What to watch: the six companion vulnerabilities bundled in the same advisory, including CVE-2026-107155, and whether SonicWall or CISA release technical details or indicators for the exploitation campaign. Organizations using SAML SSLVPN on Gen7 and Gen8 hardware should treat patching as urgent given the MFA bypass and KEV status; devices without SAML login enabled are not exposed to CVE-2026-19868 but still need the other fixes.
