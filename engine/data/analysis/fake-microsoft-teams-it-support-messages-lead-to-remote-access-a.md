Attackers have abused Microsoft Teams external chats in over 400 incidents, impersonating IT support to obtain remote access and deliver malware. The campaigns rely on Teams federation being enabled by default, which lets external organizations message users when both tenants allow it. Researchers tracked recurring tenant names, deceptive display names, remote administration tools, and payload infrastructure across the incidents. Organizations should review federation settings, restrict external access, and train staff to verify IT support requests through separate channels.

The scale of 400 documented incidents shows this is not a one-off phishing trick but a sustained operational technique. Remote access tools deployed after initial contact give attackers direct control of endpoints. Because Teams is a trusted internal channel, users are more likely to comply with requests to run remote assistance software. Defenders should treat unexpected external Teams messages with the same suspicion applied to email attachments.
