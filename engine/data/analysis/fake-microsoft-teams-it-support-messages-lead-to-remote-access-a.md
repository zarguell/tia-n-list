Attackers are abusing Microsoft Teams external chat federation, enabled by default, to impersonate IT support staff and trick employees into granting remote access and installing malware. More than 400 incidents have been recorded, with recurring tenant names, deceptive display names, remote administration tools, and payload infrastructure. The technique relies on social engineering rather than software exploits: victims are lured into accepting external Teams chats or calls that appear to come from their own helpdesk.

Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant tracked threat cluster UNC6692 running such a campaign between March 1 and April 1, 2026, targeting senior employees and leading to malware deployment, data theft, and ransomware. In one documented case the attacker used an external account named helpdesk@Progressive936.onmicrosoft.com, obtained screen control, guided the victim to install HopToDesk and AnyDesk, and delivered a malicious MSI that drops EtherRAT, a blockchain-anchored remote access trojan.

Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 has published guidance on the cross-tenant "external" tag and how screen-control features can be abused. Defenders should restrict Teams federation to trusted domains, train staff to verify IT support through out-of-band channels, and monitor for external helpdesk tenants and unauthorized remote-access tool installations.
