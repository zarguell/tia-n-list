Attackers have abused Microsoft Teams external chats in more than 400 documented incidents, impersonating IT support staff through deceptive display names and recurring attacker-controlled tenants to gain remote access and deliver malware. Teams federation is enabled by default, which allows cross-tenant communication whenever both organizations permit external access; attackers exploit this trust model rather than a software vulnerability. The events reference remote administration tools and payload infrastructure, but the earlier Unit 42 and GTIG reports provide concrete names: the June 28, 2026 Unit 42 disclosure documents EtherRAT delivered via a malicious MSI named v7.msi hosted at camorreado[.]click, while the April 23, 2026 GTIG report on UNC6692 tracks 77% senior-employee targeting between March 1 and April 1, 2026. These are not isolated phishing anecdotes but sustained operational techniques with named malware families, blockchain-anchored C2, and specific attacker accounts such as helpdesk@Progressive936.onmicrosoft[.]com.

The exposure is broader than the event count implies: because Teams is a trusted internal channel with built-in remote screen control, victims are more likely to comply with instructions to download RMM tools such as HopToDesk or AnyDesk than they would be with an unexpected email attachment. A successful compromise grants direct endpoint control, followed by multi-stage loaders that deploy legitimate Node.js runtimes to decrypt embedded payloads. The 400-incident figure represents only documented cases; organizations with default federation settings and no external-chat restrictions remain exposed regardless of whether they have seen an attack yet.

Defenders should disable or restrict Teams external federation, restrict cross-tenant chat to approved domains, and require verification of IT support requests through separate out-of-band channels rather than the same chat thread. Hunt for the virtual-cursor artifact `CtrlVirtualCursorWin_` in endpoint telemetry, audit Microsoft Teams logs for cross-tenant OneOnOne chats with `HasForeignTenantUsers: true`, and inspect Edge download history for HopToDesk or AnyDesk binaries paired with `cmd.exe` invoking `curl.exe` to fetch `v7.msi` from camorreado[.]click. If those indicators are absent, review federation policies anyway, because the absence of telemetry does not mean the absence of exposure.

Watch for updated GTIG or Mandiant tracking on UNC6692 variants, any new blockchain C2 contract addresses associated with EtherRAT, and whether Microsoft adjusts default federation behavior in response to the sustained abuse. A vendor change to external-access defaults would alter the exposure calculation significantly.
