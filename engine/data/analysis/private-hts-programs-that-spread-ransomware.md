AhnLab's security intelligence center (ASEC) identified ransomware being distributed through a private home trading system (HTS) program called "UBP Asset." HTS programs are stock trading platforms used by retail investors in South Korea. The UBP Asset program has been previously linked to online investment scams, and AhnLab found that a version of it was repurposed to deploy ransomware onto victims' machines.

The attack vector is notable because HTS programs require deep system access to function. They install kernel-level drivers, access clipboard data, and run with elevated privileges to execute trades. This makes them an ideal delivery mechanism for malware that needs persistence and high permissions. The ransomware leverages the trust relationship users have with their trading software.

Two sources covered this story: AhnLab's original intelligence report and a follow-up on malware.news. The campaign appears to target South Korean retail investors specifically. Users who install unverified HTS programs from unofficial sources are at risk. This follows a pattern of threat actors abusing legitimate software supply chains in niche verticals to bypass traditional security controls.
