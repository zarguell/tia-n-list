Rhysida, also tracked as Vanilla Tempest, is pressing German public-sector targets. In May 2026 the group listed the city of Stuttgart on its data-leak site with an extortion demand of five Bitcoin, a listing public reporting dates to 19 May 2026. Stuttgart initially said it had no evidence confirming a cyber incident, so the leak-site entry stands as an attacker claim rather than proof of a specific intrusion chain. Berlin is the harder case: the State of Berlin confirmed data exfiltration between 7 and 12 August 2026, before affected Senate administrations were disconnected from the state network on 14 August, and subsequent forensics found additional data loss.

The pattern matters because a ransomware brand that hit healthcare and other sectors elsewhere is now demonstrably working German state and municipal administration, and Berlin's confirmed exfiltration shows these leak-site claims are not all bluster. The public technical record is thin. The analysis behind this story states there are no publicly confirmed Stuttgart- or Berlin-specific Rhysida payload hashes or C2 addresses in authoritative reporting, which limits both attribution confidence and what defenders can actually block on.

Watch for three things: a clearer account from Berlin of what data left the network as forensics proceed, any reversal or confirmation from Stuttgart on whether a real intrusion sits behind the May listing, and whether German authorities or researchers publish usable indicators. New Rhysida leak-site entries naming German public bodies would be the cheapest signal that the campaign is still running.
