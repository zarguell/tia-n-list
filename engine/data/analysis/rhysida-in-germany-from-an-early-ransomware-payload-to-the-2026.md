The Rhysida in Germany reporting pairs two incidents with very different evidence quality. Rhysida listed the city of Stuttgart on its data-leak site on 19 May 2026 with a five Bitcoin extortion demand, but Stuttgart initially said it had no evidence confirming an intrusion, so the listing remains an attacker claim. Berlin is confirmed: the state disclosed exfiltration between 7 and 12 August 2026 from parts of its administration, affected Senate administrations were disconnected from the state network on 14 August, and later forensic work found additional data loss. New coverage from Heise and iX on 17 November examines how forensic analysts are working through the Berlin leak.

The technical analysis notes that no Stuttgart- or Berlin-specific Rhysida payload hashes or command-and-control addresses have been publicly confirmed in the authoritative reporting reviewed, with visibility instead coming from the broader Vanilla Tempest and Rhysida ecosystem. Watch for verified leak contents, host-level indicators tied to the Berlin incident and any correction to the Stuttgart claim. The case is also a reminder that extortion-site listings and confirmed exfiltration need to be kept separate when assessing German public-sector victims.
