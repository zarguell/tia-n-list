State Bank of India disclosed on October 21, 2026 that customer data has appeared on the dark web. The bank was alerted by its threat intelligence provider, the Defence Cyber Agency, and by the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team, CERT-In, and its preliminary analysis of roughly 375,000 records found 3,897 customer records containing personally identifiable information. The exposed fields include customer names, registered mobile numbers, dates of birth, gender, email addresses, residential addresses, branch and Customer Information File identifiers, Lead IDs, and metadata tied to loan and lead records. A threat actor using the name Siriusim has claimed to have released 2,722 PDF documents including loan applications and account opening forms, and SBI has not confirmed that claim.

The bank says it is still investigating the authenticity, completeness, source and extent of the exposure, and it has not confirmed whether the data originated from its own systems or from a third party. It states the incident has no material impact on core business operations, financial performance or business continuity, has activated its cyber incident response and containment protocols, and has engaged an independent cybersecurity agency empanelled by CERT-In to investigate. The number of affected individuals has not been disclosed. A second Dutch-language report frames the same disclosure as a mass breach at India's largest bank by balance sheet and stresses cross-border supply chain exposure, since international banks exchange data daily.

Watch for two things: whether the independent agency determines the data came from SBI systems or from a third-party processor, and whether the regulator or the bank publishes the affected customer count. A second wave of phishing using the leaked names, mobile numbers and addresses is the more likely near-term consequence for customers, who are advised to monitor accounts for unfamiliar activity and never share passwords, PINs or OTPs.
