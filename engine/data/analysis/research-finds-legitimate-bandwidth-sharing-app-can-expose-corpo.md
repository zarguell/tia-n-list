Silent Push research identified that legitimate bandwidth-sharing applications can expose corporate networks to proxy users. Applications like Peer2Profit and AstroProxy allow individuals to monetize unused internet bandwidth by routing third-party traffic through their devices. When employees install these apps on work machines or home devices connected to corporate VPNs, they effectively turn their endpoints into proxy nodes accessible to unknown external parties. This creates a direct path from the public internet into internal network segments.

The risk is that an attacker or investigator using the proxy service could route traffic through an employee's device, gaining access to internal resources, SaaS applications, or network segments that the device is authorized to reach. For organizations, this represents an unmonitored ingress point that bypasses traditional perimeter defenses. The apps are not malware and operate within their terms of service, making them difficult to detect through conventional threat hunting. Organizations should consider blocking bandwidth-sharing application traffic at the network layer and educating employees about the security implications of running such software on corporate-connected devices.
