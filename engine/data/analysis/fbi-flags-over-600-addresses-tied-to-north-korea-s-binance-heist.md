The FBI advisory released October 31 attributes the October 29 Binance intrusion to the North Korean group TraderTraitor, identifying 608 Ethereum addresses, 19 Bitcoin addresses, and one Solana address tied to stolen assets estimated at roughly $6.3 billion. Binance said the intrusion may have started with a compromised DevOps engineer laptop, and blockchain intelligence firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis linked the attack to TraderTraitor within hours of the theft. The attackers emptied hot wallets across multiple blockchains in roughly 20 minutes, making this the largest crypto theft on record.

This is not a routine ransomware bulletin; it is a state-sponsored exfiltration at scale with immediate laundering infrastructure named. A successful continuation of this campaign means any crypto exchange running multi-chain hot wallets with weak endpoint isolation faces comparable loss, and the speed of attribution (two days) suggests law enforcement already had blockchain tracking in place rather than starting from scratch. The exposure is concrete: 608 Ethereum addresses, 19 Bitcoin addresses, and one Solana address are active laundering nodes that defenders must treat as live IoCs, not historical artifacts.

Defenders should hunt for transactions interacting with the named address set, restrict RPC and THORChain node access to transactions from those addresses, and review DevOps endpoint hygiene given Binance's initial compromise vector. The FBI specifically called on THORChain node operators, instant exchanges, DeFi services, and blockchain analytics firms to refuse transactions from the listed addresses, making network-level blocking a directly cited remediation path rather than a general recommendation.

Watch for confirmation of the DevOps laptop vector and any update to the address count or chain coverage, as the advisory notes assets will likely move through DeFi and instant swap services into further laundering stages. Any expansion of the IoC list or identification of additional compromised exchanges would shift the assessment from isolated incident to broader campaign.
