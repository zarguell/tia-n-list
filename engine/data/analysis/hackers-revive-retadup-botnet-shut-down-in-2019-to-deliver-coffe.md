Kaspersky's August 2026 sample is a new CoffeeLoader variant, not a rewritten Retadup worm. An NSIS installer drops a patched 7z.dll into a temporary directory and runs it through rundll32.exe; part of the legitimate 7-Zip library is replaced with self-decrypting Armoury shellcode. The earlier Armoury build decrypted its body with an OpenCL kernel, while this one performs the XOR work through OpenGL compute shaders, and the unpacked downloader injects into dllhost.exe, WerFault.exe, svchost.exe, OpenWith.exe or AtBroker.exe. Payloads are checked with X-Signature and X-Content-MD5 headers.

The distribution channel is the older worm. Kaspersky says expired Retadup C2 names such as alphanoob and radnewage were re-registered, so dormant USB copies from 2017 can still receive commands. Two collector posts in this window add that Kaspersky blocked attempts on about 15,000 systems between January and September 2026, concentrated in Peru, Angola, Russia and Bolivia, and that a 2017 sample was still contacting the servers in 2026. SmokeLoader was served on the channel before CoffeeLoader.

There is no product version to patch here. The risk is a loader that can fetch infostealers or ransomware onto hosts reached by removable media, with persistence under ProgramData and LocalAppData using eight-hex-digit names and a StartupApproved\Run entry that hides the autorun item in Task Manager. Hunt for 7z.dll written to temp by an NSIS installer, rundll32.exe loading a DLL from C:\ProgramData\b79faace\ or C:\Users\[username]\AppData\Local\b79faace\, and Run values named b79faace.dll.

Watch for expansion beyond the four named countries, a new set of re-registered 2019 C2 domains, or a packer revision that drops OpenGL or the payload verification headers. Those checks are why swapping files on the existing servers is not enough for a simple payload replacement.
