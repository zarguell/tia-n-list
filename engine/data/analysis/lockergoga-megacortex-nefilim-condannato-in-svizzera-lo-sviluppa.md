A Zurich district court sentenced a 52-year-old Ukrainian citizen identified through OSINT as Artem Melnik to 12 years and 9 months in prison plus a 10-year ban on entering Switzerland for his role developing code for three of the most destructive ransomware families of recent years: LockerGoga, MegaCortex, and Nefilim. The sentence, one of the longest ever imposed in Europe for cybercrime, followed a five-year prosecution that began with his October 2021 arrest in Basel-Countryside canton, where investigators found ransomware source code and victim extortion messages at his residence.

The trial reconstructed damage affecting over 1,800 individuals and organizations across 71 countries. Notable victims included Norsk Hydro, the Norwegian aluminum giant struck by LockerGoga in March 2019 in one of the most studied ransomware incidents in history. Norsk Hydro was forced back to manual processes and pen-and-paper operations at multiple facilities, with losses estimated in the tens of millions of dollars, and famously chose not to pay the ransom. Swiss rail manufacturer Stadler Rail was hit in May 2020 with a $6 million demand. The group operated as a ransomware-as-a-service operation from July 2020 under the Nefilim brand, with Tymoshchuk as administrator taking a cut from affiliates, explicitly targeting North American and Australian companies with over $100 million in revenue.

The real architect, Volodymyr Viktorovych Tymoshchuk, remains at large. He is on the FBI's most wanted cyber list with an $11 million bounty, one of the highest ever placed on a single ransomware operator. The indictment, filed by the U.S. Department of Justice Eastern District of New York, documents the group's playbook: initial access via brute force, known vulnerability exploitation, and purchases from initial access brokers; lateral movement using PsExec, WMI, and RDP; exfiltration followed by encryption; and double extortion through "Corporate Leaks" portals. The techniques described remain standard in modern ransomware operations, making the case a useful reference for threat hunting against current campaigns.
