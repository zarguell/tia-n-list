Cloudflare patched a cross-tenant data exposure in its Containers platform. The leak came from the Linux device-mapper thin provisioning layer (dm-thin) running with skip_block_zeroing, so storage extents recycled from one tenant's container disks could be reallocated to another tenant without being zeroed first, handing the new tenant the previous tenant's disk contents. A proof of concept demonstrated the leak, and Cloudflare has since patched the issue.

The reason this matters is that it breaks the isolation guarantee a multi-tenant platform sells. Everything a tenant wrote to disk, credentials, application secrets, database files, is exposed when block reuse leaks across tenant boundaries. The flaw also sits in a generic storage layer rather than Cloudflare-specific code, which puts any provider running dm-thin thin provisioning with allocation-time zeroing skipped under the same suspicion.

What to watch: Cloudflare's own writeup of how the exposure was found and closed, whether the proof of concept is published in full, and whether other hosting providers disclose audits of their thin-provisioned allocations. The threadlinqs analysis in this story reports 9 detections and 12 IOCs, a practical starting point for defenders checking their own environments for tenant-boundary leaks in recycled storage.
