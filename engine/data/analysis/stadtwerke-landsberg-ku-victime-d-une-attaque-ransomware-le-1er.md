Stadtwerke Landsberg KU, a German municipal public utility, disclosed a ransomware attack that hit on September 1, 2026. The attackers encrypted central IT systems, forcing the organization to sever all internet connections and shut down systems to contain the incident. Critical infrastructure including the electrical grid, water supply, wastewater treatment, district heating, and fiber network remained unaffected.

The company published a GDPR Article 34 notification on September 7, warning customers that personal data including names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, and banking information may have been exfiltrated. Stadtwerke Landsberg activated its crisis plan, brought in external cybersecurity specialists and IT forensic investigators, notified authorities, and filed a criminal complaint. MITRE ATT&CK techniques observed include T1486 (Data Encrypted for Impact) and T1657 (Financial Theft).

No threat actor has been claimed responsibility. The incident follows a pattern of ransomware groups targeting European municipal utilities and critical infrastructure operators, though this particular attack appears contained to IT systems rather than OT networks. The delayed public disclosure seven days after the attack is typical for German organizations coordinating with data protection authorities.
