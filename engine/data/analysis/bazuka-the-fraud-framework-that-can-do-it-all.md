ThreatFabric found Bazuka in late October 2026 while investigating an incident with an NFC card relay application and published the research on 18 November. This is a framework with a buyer, not a one-off stealer: a dropper or downloader, a core, and 23 modules, with two WebSocket channels named Control and Data carrying ChaCha20-Poly1305 encrypted frames. Published samples include dropper SHA256 f046b50166c8229b49839eeebcc7fd14bf3f198e4ec1bfde8a5b271e1407dec5, downloader SHA256 4d572669d6000304a692874c1583021dfdb4f2f113ea487e470f7e4c486e503a, and core SHA256 3cb8711a51331123247476eef768c435d6286a21cd8ac5479a6276349632c82b. The downloader pulled the core and tapread-nfc_module as DEX files loaded in memory, and the operator panel offers LLM chats that score victims and suggest permission-click strategies.

The 23 modules cover credential capture, SMS and 2FA interception, screen control, crypto clipboard swapping and contactless card relay, so one infection spans most of a mobile fraud chain short of automated payments. The more consequential detail is the ADB bridge: the core can enable Wireless Debugging, intercept the pairing PIN and grant permissions through shell commands, while modules are loaded from byte arrays rather than disk, which means a clean uninstall may not remove the capability. Dropper builder settings deliberately keep manifest permissions minimal to ease Google Play review, and the panel includes traffer accounts, so app-store scale is a design goal even though ThreatFabric observed no real Play distribution. Only two sources carry the story at score 5.0, with no victim count, no loss figure and no CVE; the unobfuscated modules and publicly reachable panel frontend are an unusual chance to hunt this tooling before a measured outbreak exists.

Block egress to c2ws.brokerok[.]org, c2data.brokerok[.]org, c2proxy.brokerok[.]org, c2api.brokerok[.]org, gargona[.]cash, 95.181.213[.]21 and 170.168.61[.]117, and watch ports 8443 and 8444 for bot C2 plus 8082 and 8445 for the panel. Hunt for installation of package com.tiktok.premium.pro, PackageManager session side-loads, InMemoryDexClassLoader use, the self_adb rebind_accessibility command, the com.guidocool.cryptomanager.action.A11Y_REBIND broadcast, writes to enabled_accessibility_services, and Wireless Debugging or ADB pairing activity on managed devices. Alert on the three published hashes and module names such as tapread-nfc_module.dex, smsmessage.dex, twofa.dex and clip_monitor.dex; the bot's first C2 message carries a ThreatLevel score and encryption keys rotate through SharedPreferences, both useful telemetry anchors.

Two findings would change this assessment: evidence that the minimal-permission droppers reached Google Play, which would turn a sideloading problem into store-scale exposure, or confirmation that ADB deployed modules survive removal of the host application, which would make device rebuilds the baseline response. Absent those, the right posture is targeted blocking and hashing now rather than a broad campaign assumption.
