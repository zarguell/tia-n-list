ThreatFabric's Mobile Threat Intelligence team found Bazuka in late October 2026 while investigating an incident involving an NFC card relay application. The researchers describe a modular fraud framework rather than a single stealer: a dropper or downloader, a core component, and 23 modules covering overlays, keylogging, two-factor authentication interception and SMS interception. The framework also integrates large language models for managing and controlling victims, which points to automation of victim interaction alongside the device-side tooling.

That module set maps to mobile banking fraud and account takeover. Overlay and keylogging components capture credentials, while SMS and 2FA interception target the second factor that banks and payment apps rely on. The NFC relay connection from the original incident suggests the operators combine card-relay activity with on-device credential theft. No CVE, victim count, geographic scope or financial loss figure appears in the available write-ups, so the reporting supports a capability assessment, not a claim about campaign scale.

Watch for detection coverage around overlay abuse, SMS interception and accessibility-style keylogging, and for infrastructure or dropper samples tied to the NFC relay case. The LLM integration detail is worth tracking because it may change how victims are engaged once a device is compromised, but the published material does not name a model or describe a specific lure workflow. Given the two-source footprint, additional research from ThreatFabric or incident reports from affected banks would be needed before assigning the framework broad prevalence.
