ThreatFabric's Mobile Threat Intelligence team found Bazuka in late October 2026 while investigating an incident involving an NFC card relay application. The company describes a complete Android fraud framework rather than a single trojan: a dropper or downloader, a core, and 23 modules covering screen overlays, keylogging, 2FA and SMS interception, remote device control, crypto-address swapping and contactless card interaction. The operator panel includes large language model integration for managing victims at scale. The Hacker News and other outlets picked up the research, and this window added another write-up describing the same combination of data theft, phone control and AI-assisted fraud.

The report gives concrete artifacts to hunt. A TikTok 18+ themed dropper is tracked at SHA-256 f046b50166c8229b49839eeebcc7fd14bf3f198e4ec1bfde8a5b271e1407dec5, and the downloader at 4d572669d6000304a692874c1583021dfdb4f2f113ea487e470f7e4c486e503a. Control infrastructure sits under brokerok[.]org and gargona[.]cash. Fake screen overlays hide credential theft from the device owner while the framework keeps remote access, so a victim can be drained without an obvious prompt.

The significance is the packaging. Overlay banking trojans, SMS interception and NFC relay usually appear as separate tools; Bazuka puts 23 modules behind one panel and adds LLM-driven victim handling. Watch for reuse of the two hashes and domains, for further module disclosures from ThreatFabric, and for copycat frameworks borrowing the same operator-panel design. No CVE, victim count or attribution to a named actor appears in the reporting so far, so those claims should stay open.
