ThreatFabric published its Bazuka research on 18 November 2026 after finding the framework in late October while investigating an incident involving an NFC card relay application. The report describes an Android fraud platform rather than a single banking trojan: a dropper or downloader, a core, and 23 modules covering screen overlays, keylogging, 2FA and SMS interception, remote device control, crypto-address swapping and contactless card interaction. The operator panel integrates large language models for victim management and control at scale. Later posts from The Hacker News, GBHackers and DroidWatch restate that structure without adding a victim count or a named actor.

The research carries usable indicators. A TikTok 18+ themed dropper is tracked at SHA-256 f046b50166c8229b49839eeebcc7fd14bf3f198e4ec1bfde8a5b271e1407dec5, with a downloader at 4d572669d6000304a692874c1583021dfdb4f2f113ea487e470f7e4c486e503a. Control infrastructure sits under brokerok[.]org and gargona[.]cash. Fake overlays hide credential capture while the framework retains remote access, so SMS codes and banking sessions can be taken without an obvious prompt on the device.

The concern is packaging. Overlay theft, SMS interception and NFC relay often appear as separate tools; Bazuka bundles 23 modules behind one panel and adds LLM-assisted handling of compromised devices. Watch for reuse of the two hashes and domains, further module disclosures from ThreatFabric, and copycat panels adopting the same design. No CVE, victim count or actor attribution appears in the reporting so far, and none should be inferred from the AI framing.
