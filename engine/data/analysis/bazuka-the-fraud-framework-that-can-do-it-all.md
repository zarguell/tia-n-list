ThreatFabric found Bazuka in late October 2026 while investigating an incident involving an NFC card relay application. Its report describes a complete Android fraud framework rather than a single banking trojan: a dropper or downloader, a core, and 23 modules for screen overlays, keylogging, 2FA and SMS interception, remote device control, crypto-address swapping and contactless card interaction. The operator panel integrates large language models to help manage victims at scale. Reporting from The Hacker News and GBHackers in this window restates those findings without adding new artifacts or victim counts.

The research names usable indicators. A TikTok 18+ themed dropper is tracked at SHA-256 f046b50166c8229b49839eeebcc7fd14bf3f198e4ec1bfde8a5b271e1407dec5, with a downloader at 4d572669d6000304a692874c1583021dfdb4f2f113ea487e470f7e4c486e503a. Control infrastructure sits under brokerok[.]org and gargona[.]cash. Fake overlays mask credential theft while the framework keeps remote access, so SMS codes and banking sessions can be taken without an obvious prompt on the device.

What makes this notable is packaging. Overlay theft, SMS interception and NFC relay are often separate tools; Bazuka bundles 23 modules behind one panel and adds LLM-assisted victim handling. Watch for reuse of the two hashes and domains, further module disclosures from ThreatFabric, and copycat panels adopting the same design. No CVE, victim count or named actor attribution appears in the reporting so far, and none should be inferred from the AI framing.
