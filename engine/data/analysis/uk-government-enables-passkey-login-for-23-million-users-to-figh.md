The UK government has deployed passkey authentication across GOV.UK One Login, covering more than 23 million users. The rollout, announced September 14, enables citizens to authenticate using device-bound cryptographic keys verified through fingerprint, Face ID, or local PIN instead of passwords and SMS codes.

Passkeys eliminate the reusable secrets that phishing kits, credential-stuffing attacks, and adversary-in-the-middle proxies target. They are cryptographically bound to both the user's device and the legitimate website, making stolen credentials useless. During an initial trial, over 300,000 users adopted the technology, and nearly one in ten daily GOV.UK One Login authentications now use passkeys. The government reports that passkey logins are up to eight times faster than traditional password-plus-2FA flows.

Beyond security, the shift reduces operational costs by cutting dependence on SMS authentication codes. The deployment covers tax management, State Pension services, childcare platforms, and driver's license renewals. This represents one of the largest government passkey deployments globally and signals that passwordless authentication is moving from enterprise pilots to national-scale consumer systems.
