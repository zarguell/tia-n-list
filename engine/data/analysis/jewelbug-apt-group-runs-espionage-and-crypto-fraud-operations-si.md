Jewelbug, also tracked as Earth Alux, REF7707, and CL-STA-0049, is a China-based hackers-for-hire group running espionage and cryptocurrency fraud as parallel operations from one control panel. Symantec Threat Hunter's investigation, published August 13, 2026, documents campaigns against government and military targets across the Middle East, Southeast Asia, and South Asia, including more than 90 police and government email addresses in South Asia, alongside a for-profit crypto fraud business aimed at Chinese-speaking victims. BleepingComputer coverage of the same research details the webmail operation: Jewelbug gained write access to a shared government webmail installation and inserted a malicious script into its common template, which then ran on login pages and mailbox views across 15 government tenants in a Middle Eastern country.

The toolset spans five generations of C2 code feeding one victim database. The main Windows implant is Antino, delivered via HTA downloaders and fake Adobe Flash or installer executables, alongside the XG-Web remote-access tool. A malicious Chrome and Firefox extension named PDF Viewer requests nearly every dangerous browser permission and pairs with a native messaging helper disguised as com.microsoft.runedge to reach a command shell on the host. ClientKing, a Rust implant with 37 builds, extends the group to Linux servers and ASUS consumer routers, with DNS tunneling, SOCKS pivoting, and kernel module loading among its transports. Jewelbug also abuses public Google Docs as a C2 delivery channel, embedding freshly obfuscated payloads in documents that victim implants retrieve and execute so traffic resolves through Google-owned infrastructure and blends into legitimate web activity.

In the webmail campaign, the script established a WebSocket connection to the attacker's C2, exfiltrated webmail cookies, and checked the user's email address to identify targeted government domains; valuable targets received a fake Adobe Flash update prompt that installed Antino and browser tooling. The victim database recorded more than one million implant check-ins and more than 580,000 stolen browser cookies in less than three months, plus thousands of captured credentials and more than 2,300 exfiltrated email bodies. One implant build was configured to beacon through the internal proxy of a major U.S. aerospace and industrial manufacturer. Operators are tied to a registered Changsha company, with the fraud arm run under the handle paopaodada.
