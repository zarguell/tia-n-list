The attack that disrupted refrigeration at hundreds of French supermarkets now spans Copeland and Dixell controllers in addition to Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC equipment, per France's ANSSI CERT-FR. The agency first warned on October 12 and added the Copeland XWEB and Dixell XWEB systems a day later. Copeland initially declined to confirm but has since acknowledged a vulnerability and stated it is working with impacted customers to mitigate the risk. The entry path remains unknown, and CERT-FR has not tied the compromises to any specific vulnerability, including the 23 flaws in Copeland's XWEB Pro controller that Claroty disclosed this summer and that Copeland patched. Most affected Danfoss devices are older AK-SM 800 and AK-SC 355 systems that reached end of life in 2021 and 2016; some current AK-SM 800A controllers running outdated firmware were also hit. CERT-FR urges operators to take affected controllers off the internet.

This is an industrial control system incident with broad physical impact: refrigeration failure in supermarkets directly affects food supply. The lack of a linked CVE or confirmed entry vector limits defensive guidance, but the vendor acknowledgment and widening scope confirm active, widespread compromise rather than a single-site failure. Watch for CERT-FR to publish IOCs or link the incident to a known vulnerability. Operators running end-of-life Danfoss AK-SM 800 or AK-SC 355 controllers should treat them as exposed and isolate them immediately, since no patches are available.
