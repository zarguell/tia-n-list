CERT Polska warned of active exploitation of a chain of critical MikroTik RouterOS vulnerabilities collectively dubbed "MikroTrick." The attack chain combines two flaws: CVE-2026-67276, an SSH authentication-bypass vulnerability in RSA public key handling, and CVE-2026-86060, a privilege-escalation flaw involving a specially crafted username during SSH login. Together they allow unauthenticated remote attackers to seize full control of exposed routers.

A compromised router sits at the network edge, giving attackers the ability to change DNS settings, redirect or capture traffic, create remote-access tunnels, alter firewall rules, or use the device as a pivot point into the internal network. Six vulnerabilities were disclosed in total; two form the MikroTrick chain, while the remaining four provide additional attack paths.

MikroTik routers are deployed worldwide across ISPs, enterprises, and home networks. Organizations should ensure SSH management interfaces are not exposed to the internet, apply the latest RouterOS patches immediately, and audit configurations for unauthorized admin accounts or firewall rule changes. The active exploitation warning from a national CERT indicates this is not theoretical.
