CERT Polska disclosed MikroTrick, an attack chain combining two critical RouterOS vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-67276 and CVE-2026-86060, both CVSS 9.2) that allows unauthenticated full administrative takeover of MikroTik routers via SSH. The first flaw bypasses SSH authentication by comparing only the RSA public modulus instead of the full key, letting an attacker construct a different key sharing that modulus. The second escalates privileges through a crafted SSH username. Together they grant complete control without any credentials, as long as SSH is reachable from the network. MikroTik shipped patches on September 3, and CERT Polska published full details on September 5 after confirming active exploitation since at least September 2.

Shadowserver Foundation scans found approximately 122,500 MikroTik devices with SSH exposed to the internet as of September 5. CERT Polska traced successful compromises to IP address 82.192.72.4, where attackers created unauthorized admin accounts. A second address (103.102.31.18) was observed attempting exploitation. The patched releases are RouterOS 6.49.21, 7.23.5 (not 7.23.4 due to an IPv6 DHCP regression), 7.24.2, and 7.25beta3. Devices are only vulnerable if SSH management is exposed; MikroTik's default firewall blocks this, but ISP and enterprise deployments commonly open it for remote management.

The discovery method is notable: CERT Polska found the vulnerabilities with AI assistance from GPT-5.5-cyber and GPT-5.6-sol through OpenAI's Government and Trust Agency Collaboration program. This is one of the more concrete public examples of AI-assisted vulnerability research producing a critical finding in widely deployed infrastructure. The implication for defenders is clear: the pace of vulnerability discovery in mature network code is increasing, and organizations running MikroTik should patch immediately or disable SSH from untrusted networks as a temporary measure.
