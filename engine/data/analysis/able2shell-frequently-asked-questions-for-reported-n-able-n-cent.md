A researcher publishing under the GitHub account Oracle-Security disclosed 33 alleged zero-day vulnerabilities in N-able N-central under the name Able2Shell, complete with proof-of-concept code; the earliest README commit is dated November 9, 2026. The researcher claims one attack chain requires no credentials and lets an unauthenticated attacker run commands on the server. N-able published seven CVE records and released N-central 2026.4 Hotfix 2 on November 10 to address them.

Security firms reported exploitation attempts against N-central honeypots, but as of November 10 no confirmed compromise of a customer server had been reported. The social echo from SecurityWeek added that exploitation of the chain was observed within hours of the PoC publication, with 21 servers running XMRig miners, though that claim is unverified and should be treated cautiously.

Defenders should apply Hotfix 2 and review N-central instances for unauthorized command execution or miner activity. Given the PoC is public and the product is used by managed service providers, the window for opportunistic exploitation is short; the absence of confirmed customer compromises as of November 10 is not assurance of safety.
