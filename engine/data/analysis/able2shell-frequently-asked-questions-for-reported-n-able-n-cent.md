A researcher operating as "Oracle-Security" (Sean Bales) published the Able2Shell collection, roughly 33 alleged N-able N-central zero-days with proof-of-concept code, on November 9, 2026. N-able responded by taking its hosted N-central environments offline across the Americas, Europe, and Asia-Pacific, then shipped N-central 2026.4 Hotfix 2 fixing seven CVEs. The most serious is CVE-2026-86218, a CVSS 10.0 pre-authentication remote code execution flaw; CISA added it to the KEV catalog and Shadowserver tracks nearly 1,500 exposed N-central instances, most in the US and Europe.

N-able says it has no confirmed exploitation of CVE-2026-86218 in production, but Huntress flagged it as a potential zero-day and found an already-patched customer, while security firms reported scanning and exploitation attempts against N-central honeypots. The researcher claims one attack chain needs no credentials. Because N-central is an RMM platform used by MSPs and IT teams to manage client networks, a pre-auth RCE on the management server is a supply-chain risk that can span administered environments, not just the server itself.

Defenders running on-premises N-central should upgrade to 2026.3 Hotfix 4 / 2026.4 HF2 immediately, restrict or remove internet exposure, and hunt for post-exploitation activity. N-able described the disclosure as non-responsible and has not confirmed advance notice, so the full scope of the 33 alleged flaws remains unclear; only the seven patched CVEs are confirmed. Treat unpatched internet-facing instances as actively at risk regardless of the vendor's no-confirmed-exploitation statement.
