N-able N-central, the RMM platform used by MSPs and IT teams to administer client networks, faced a mass disclosure on November 9, 2026 when a researcher operating under the GitHub account "Oracle-Security" published the "Able2Shell" collection describing 33 alleged vulnerabilities with proof-of-concept code, including an unauthenticated command-execution chain tested against N-central Appliance 2026.4.0.31. The earliest README commit was dated 04:14 UTC on November 9, and the disclosure was made without prior notice to the vendor. N-able took its hosted N-central environments offline across the Americas, Europe, and Asia-Pacific within hours and advised on-premises customers to shut down their servers until further notice.

N-able responded on November 10 with N-central 2026.4 Hotfix 2, build 2026.4.1.26, which fixes seven CVEs including CVE-2026-125883 (multiple XXE issues, CVSS 9.3), CVE-2026-125878 (XSS, CVSS 8.7), and CVE-2026-125879 (privilege escalation). The company stated that all vulnerabilities exploitable without authentication were resolved, while authenticated flaws requiring elevated privileges would follow in later hotfixes. Beazley Security reported exploitation attempts against N-central honeypots after the PoC surfaced, and N-able reported scanning activity tied to the published exploit code, though no confirmed compromise of a customer server had been reported as of November 10. Canadian Centre advisory AV26-1143 states that open-source reporting indicates CVE-2026-125883 is exploited in the wild, so that CVE should be treated as actively exploited.

This incident is separate from an earlier N-central emergency: CVE-2026-86218, a pre-authentication remote code execution flaw rated CVSS 10 that was patched in N-central 2026.3 Hotfix 4 and added to the CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, with Huntress reporting an already-patched customer compromise. Together these events show N-central's outsized value as a target, since a single compromised management server can span administered infrastructure. Administrators should apply 2026.4 HF2 immediately, review authentication activity from November 8 onward, check for unrecognized accounts, and watch for the remaining authenticated-flaw patches.
