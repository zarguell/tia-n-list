Cybercriminals are distributing Vidar infostealer malware through fake Rockstar Games websites advertising a non-existent GTA 6 demo, timed to coincide with leaked footage and maps circulating online. The sites copy Rockstar artwork and the legitimate Extended Look promotion (scheduled for August 27, 2026) but add "Play Now" buttons that deliver a 1.1 MB executable named gta6_installer.exe. That file size alone is a red flag, as a modern AAA game cannot fit into such a small package. Rockstar has not released any demo, beta, or downloadable PC build. The game launches November 19, 2026 for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.

The downloaded Vidar sample targets saved passwords, browser cookies, session tokens, browsing history, autofill data, and credentials stored by FTP clients. Critically, it extracts browser session data that can let attackers access accounts even after victims change passwords, since stolen cookies preserve authenticated sessions. The malware uses victims' own browsers to extract data that browser encryption would normally protect, because the running browser process holds the decryption keys.

This is a credential-hijacking campaign, not just a password stealer. Users who downloaded GTA 6 "early access" installers should assume their browser sessions are compromised, log out of all web sessions, and rotate credentials. The attack leverages gaming hype as luring bait, a pattern likely to repeat with other anticipated game releases.
