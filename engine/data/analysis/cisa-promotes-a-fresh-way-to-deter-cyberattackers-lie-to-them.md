CISA has published new guidance on deploying cyber decoys to strengthen detection and response capabilities in critical infrastructure environments. The 22-page document, "Using Cyber Decoys to Strengthen Detection and Response," provides practical frameworks for creating fake systems, accounts, and data designed to lure and identify attackers already inside networks. Chris Butera, CISA's acting executive director for cybersecurity, emphasized the approach is particularly valuable for resource-constrained organizations.

The guidance covers different types of decoys including honey tokens, fake credentials, and decoy systems, with deployment scenarios tailored to various infrastructure environments. CISA positions this as complementary to zero-trust and assume-compromise security models, offering high-fidelity detection at relatively low cost compared to traditional monitoring solutions.

This marks CISA's first formal endorsement of active deception techniques for critical infrastructure defenders. Organizations with limited security budgets should evaluate decoy deployment as a force multiplier that can detect adversaries who have bypassed perimeter controls.
