Microsoft disclosed CaptiveCrunch, an active campaign by Storm-2945, an operational sub-cluster of the SVR-linked Midnight Blizzard group, that compromises captive portal networks at hotels, conference centers, and other shared venues. Since early May 2026 the group has manipulated DNS and HTTP traffic on guest networks so that browsers' automatic connectivity checks deliver malware disguised as fake browser or OS updates, with ClickFix prompts pushing victims to execute the payload. The goal is access to corporate travelers' accounts.

Microsoft documented the full toolset: CornFlake, a Go RAT with keylogging, screen and webcam capture, credential theft, and a persistence watchdog; ChocoShell, an in-memory PowerShell infostealer that targets Microsoft 365 SSO tokens and Wi-Fi credentials with three silent UAC bypasses; and FruitStone, the operator panel that compiles CornFlake payloads on demand. The group uses AI to support a significant portion of operations, and ChocoShell's code style suggests AI-assisted generation. Travelers should treat hotel Wi-Fi as hostile: use cellular or SSE connections, avoid reusing corporate credentials on registration portals, and enforce phishing-resistant MFA with device-code flows blocked.
