The chief technology officer and co-founder of ransomwarehelp.com, 52-year-old Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar of Miami and Medellin, was indicted in Rhode Island on three wire fraud counts. The six-year-old Florida firm advertised advanced cryptographic tools, 100 percent data recovery and a success rate above 99 percent, and said it never paid ransoms. Prosecutors say the company instead bought decryption keys from ransomware groups and charged victims a markup for recovery work that was not performed.

The case came from an FBI sting in which one agent played a Rhode Island ransomware victim and another played the attacker. Reported figures include a 2,750 dollar assessment, a 13,260 dollar quote and a 6,003 dollar bitcoin payment for the key. A Kansas business was billed 720,000 dollars. The account, first reported by 404 Media with Court Watch and detailed by Krebs on Security, describes a data-recovery market where clients cannot easily tell whether a vendor is paying extortionists.

The indictment is an allegation, not a conviction. What to watch is whether the case produces further charges against recovery firms, and whether victims and insurers start demanding disclosure of ransom payments in recovery contracts. The parallel October case against the owner of MonsterCloud suggests prosecutors are looking at the same business model across more than one vendor.
