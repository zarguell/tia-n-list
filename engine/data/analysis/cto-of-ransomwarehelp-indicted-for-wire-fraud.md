Federal prosecutors charged Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, the 52-year-old co-founder and CTO of ransomwarehelp.com, with three wire fraud counts in a Rhode Island indictment returned this week. The Florida firm, in business for six years, marketed itself as a cryptographic recovery service that could restore 100 percent of data without paying attackers. Its site claimed a success rate above 99 percent, 28 years of experience and a team of 80 experts, and stated that it never paid ransoms on clients' behalf.

An FBI undercover operation tested those claims by playing both a Rhode Island ransomware victim and the gang demanding payment. According to 404 Media and Court Watch, the firm charged a $2,750 assessment and quoted $13,260 for recovery, then paid about $6,003 in bitcoin for the decryption key while presenting the work as its own. The indictment also describes a Kansas business billed $720,000 for similar work.

The case matters because data-recovery firms sit between victims and ransomware operators and can profit from secrecy around ransom payments. Prosecutors say the markup paid for work that was never performed rather than genuine decryption expertise. Watch whether the separate October case against the owner of MonsterCloud is joined to this one or proceeds on its own, and whether the charging papers identify victims beyond the Kansas company.
