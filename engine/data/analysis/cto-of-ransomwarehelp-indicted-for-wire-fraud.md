Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, the 52-year-old chief technology officer and co-founder of ransomwarehelp.com, was indicted in Rhode Island on three wire fraud counts. The six-year-old Florida firm advertised proprietary cryptographic tools, 100 percent data recovery and a success rate above 99 percent, while saying it never paid ransoms. Prosecutors allege that it instead bought decryption keys from ransomware groups, marked them up and charged victims for recovery work that was not performed.

The FBI sting had one agent pose as a Rhode Island ransomware victim and another pose as the attacker. Reports describe a 2,750 dollar assessment, a 13,260 dollar recovery quote and about 6,003 dollars in bitcoin paid for the key. A Kansas business was billed 720,000 dollars. The operation tested the central claim made by recovery firms: that their tools, rather than payments to criminals, restored the victim's files.

The indictment is an allegation, not a conviction. The case puts scrutiny on how recovery vendors negotiate and account for ransom payments, and on what victims and insurers can verify before hiring one. Watch for additional defendants, restitution figures and evidence that the same business model was used at other firms. The separate October case involving MonsterCloud's owner shows prosecutors are examining more than one recovery provider.
