The chief technology officer and co-founder of ransomwarehelp.com, a six-year-old Florida company, was charged with wire fraud in an indictment returned this week in Rhode Island. Krebs on Security and malware.news report that 52-year-old Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, of Miami and Medellin, Colombia, routinely paid ransomware groups for decryption keys and then billed victims well above what he had secretly paid. An FBI sting operation found the company was charging a markup for recovery work it never performed.

RansomwareHelp's site told victims "Do not pay the ransom!" and advertised "advanced cryptographic tools" with 100 percent data recovery, a success rate over 99 percent, 28 years of experience, and a team of 80 experts in cryptography, cybersecurity, forensics and reverse engineering. It also said it never paid threat actors on clients' behalf. The charge puts that claim in front of a court and gives victims of the now-defunct service a concrete case to follow. 404 Media reported the indictment earlier in the week.

The available reports do not state the counts or the period of charged conduct. The docket detail and any charges against other RansomwareHelp staff are the next markers. The case also matters for incident responders who outsource decryption: a vendor's recovery claim can conceal a ransom payment and a markup, and the defense promised to victims was the opposite of what the indictment describes.
