Researchers from Hunt.io, working with Guy Yasur, mapped a command-and-control infrastructure cluster linked to SpiceRAT targeting government, energy, and telecommunications organizations across Central Asia. The investigation tracked servers active from late 2025 through August 2026, with five SpiceRAT C2 servers appearing across several European hosting networks in March 2026 within days of one another. The servers were connected through reused hostnames, TLS certificates, and an identical cloned webpage resembling RTX Corporation's website, which appeared as default content on 13 servers.

The infrastructure overlaps with indicators from Bitdefender's August 2026 SilkParasite report, which documented suspected China-nexus activity in the region. Three malware families previously treated as separate, SpiceRAT, NodeEdgeRAT, and NomadRAT, share infrastructure at the registration and certificate level. A TLS certificate from a Chinese state-funded certificate authority, impersonating Uzbekistan's state railway, ties much of the cluster together. Domains in the infrastructure spoof named government and energy entities across five Central Asian countries, and passive DNS analysis dates the activity back to at least mid-2022.

Researchers notified affected organizations and relevant national CERTs with a TLP:AMBER advance copy ahead of the September 9 publication. The domains and certificates are attacker-controlled impersonations; organizations named in the findings are apparent targets of spoofing and are not confirmed to have been compromised. The findings extend the picture of state-sponsored espionage infrastructure operating across Central Asia's energy and government sectors.
