Researchers from Hunt.io and Bitdefender have mapped a SpiceRAT command-and-control infrastructure cluster targeting government, energy, and telecommunications organizations across Central Asia. The investigation, building on Bitdefender's August 2026 SilkParasite report, revealed that three malware families previously treated as separate (SpiceRAT, NodeEdgeRAT, NomadRAT) share infrastructure at the registration and certificate level. A TLS certificate from a Chinese state-funded Certificate Authority, impersonating Uzbekistan's state railway, ties much of the cluster together.

The domains and certificates are attacker-controlled impersonations of named government and energy entities across five Central Asian countries. Passive DNS analysis dates the activity back to at least mid-2022. Researchers identified five SpiceRAT C2 servers appearing within days of each other across European hosting networks in March 2026, connected through reused hostnames, identical TLS certificates, and a cloned webpage resembling RTX Corporation's site. All findings were reconstructed from internet scan data without touching compromised systems.

The convergence of SpiceRAT, NodeEdgeRAT, and NomadRAT under shared infrastructure suggests a single operator or coordinated group managing multiple toolsets for long-duration espionage campaigns. The targeting of energy and government sectors in Central Asia aligns with known China-nexus strategic interests in the region's resources and political landscape. Organizations in Central Asia should treat these indicators as active threats and review network logs for the identified C2 domains and certificate patterns.
