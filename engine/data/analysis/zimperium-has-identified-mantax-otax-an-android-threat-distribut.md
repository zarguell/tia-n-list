Zimperium identified a new Android threat it calls Mantax Otax, distributed through unofficial app stores, Telegram channels, forums, and social media. The malware combines ransomware with spyware capabilities in a single implant. It is distributed as standalone APK files via third-party file-sharing services, relying on victims to sideload the package outside Google Play. Once installed and granted permissions, the malware can record the screen, intercept one-time passwords from SMS and notification content, activate the camera covertly, and encrypt local files for extortion. Researchers linked the campaign to Indonesian threat actors targeting Indonesian victims. The spyware component is the more dangerous half: OTP interception can defeat two-factor authentication on banking and messaging accounts, while screen recording exposes conversations and financial details. Camera activation adds a physical privacy dimension, potentially capturing the victim's surroundings, identity, or sensitive documents. The ransomware portion encrypts files but operates after the data has already been exfiltrated, meaning backup restoration does not eliminate the exposure. Distribution relies entirely on social engineering through fake app names, update notices, or messages from compromised contacts. After installation, the malware guides victims through permission prompts or abuses accessibility services to expand control. Organizations should enforce app installation policies through managed enterprise catalogs, block sideloading where possible, and treat unexplained battery drain, camera indicators, or data usage as investigation triggers. Infected devices should be isolated from corporate networks immediately.
