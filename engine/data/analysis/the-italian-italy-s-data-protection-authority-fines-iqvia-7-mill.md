Italy's Data Protection Authority has fined IQVIA Solutions Italy Srl 7 million euros over GDPR violations in its handling of healthcare data. Per the Garante's press release, the case centers on data covering roughly one million patients of 800 family doctors that the company treated as anonymous when it was not. IQVIA is a major health and pharma data broker, so the ruling lands on its core business of building analytics from prescriber and patient records.

The fine matters because it targets the pseudonymization shortcuts common in health data monetization. Data derived from GP records can still single out individuals, and the regulator treated that gap as a violation rather than a technicality. The 7 million euro figure also signals the Garante's willingness to price enforcement against a large processor's revenue, following its pattern of substantial fines in the health sector.

Worth watching: whether the decision orders deletion or mandatory rework of IQVIA's anonymization pipeline, not just payment, and whether IQVIA appeals. Any published remedy will set a template other EU regulators can cite against health data brokers. Secondary coverage so far is thin, coming mainly through the Garante's own release, so the full decision text is the document to check for the specific GDPR articles cited.
