Apollo Global Management disclosed a data breach involving social engineering attacks that hit the financial sector in July. Attackers accessed Apollo's cloud platforms between July 6 and 10, 2026, using social engineering as the initial access vector. The company filed a breach notification in California but has not named the threat actor or detailed how the social engineering was conducted. Apollo is the first victim in a broader wave of attacks targeting large private equity firms, law firms, financial rating agencies, and medical technology companies to formally confirm sensitive personal data was compromised.

The exposed data categories include names, birth dates, addresses, and Social Security numbers. This combination creates material identity theft risk: the four fields together can support fraudulent account applications and credit openings. Apollo's notification language ("potentially obtained") suggests the company has not confirmed exactly what was exfiltrated, which may indicate incomplete forensic visibility into the cloud environment during the July 6-10 access window.

This breach sits within a coordinated social engineering campaign against financial services, not an isolated incident. The lack of attribution and the breadth of targeted sectors suggest a financially motivated actor running a sustained operation against high-value targets. Organizations in private equity, legal, and financial services should review social engineering controls, particularly around cloud platform access. The wave likely has additional undisclosed victims given the range of sectors affected.
