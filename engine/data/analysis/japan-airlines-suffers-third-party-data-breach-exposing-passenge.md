Japan Airlines disclosed on October 28, 2026 that unauthorized access to Amadeus's Passenger Service System exposed reservation data for about 92,000 customers. Amadeus notified JAL and Japan Transocean Air on October 23, 2026 that access occurred between August 10, 2023 and September 23, 2026. About 90,000 customers had reservation codes (PNRs), names, flight dates, routes and travel classes exposed; about 2,000 had those fields plus email addresses, phone numbers, passport numbers, nationalities, frequent flyer numbers and flight, ticket and seat numbers. Credit card numbers and passwords were not compromised and JAL reports no evidence of misuse. Separately, Amadeus confirmed to heise online on October 13, 2026 that a display-environment vulnerability it was made aware of on September 21, 2026 exposed an unauthenticated Spring Boot heap dump containing access credentials and administrator passwords, which Amadeus says it has fixed. Korean Air and Asiana disclosed a parallel Amadeus credential-misuse exposure on October 23, 2026, and no CVE or affected counts have been published for any of these.

The 2,000 passport-and-contact records are the high-value subset because passport numbers combined with contact details and frequent flyer numbers support identity fraud and travel-document abuse, while the 90,000 PNR-only records remain dangerous because a booking reference plus surname is commonly enough to open manage-booking flows, so a message quoting a real PNR will look credible. The multi-year dwell time from August 2023 to September 2026 means the exposed data is stale but still usable for targeted phishing and account takeover attempts long after the access ended. The shared-platform factor is the real multiplier: Amadeus serves over 400 airlines, 210 airport operators, 34 cruise and ferry lines and 2 million hotels, so JAL customers are not the only exposed population even though only some carriers have confirmed impact so far. The assessment should treat confirmed counts as a floor, not a total.

Defenders should verify whether their own Amadeus integrations or booking-display environments exposed Spring Boot Actuator endpoints, especially `heapdump`, and block or remove any unauthenticated access to them; move management endpoints to a separate non-public port, require authentication and a specific role, or disable `heapdump` entirely. Hunt web and proxy logs for successful (HTTP 200) requests to `heapdump` or custom actuator base paths such as `/admin-api/actuator/heapdump`, and treat any such hit as a credential-exposure incident requiring rotation of database passwords, API tokens, OAuth secrets and administrator passwords held in memory. Affected passengers should be warned to verify any message quoting a real PNR through official channels and not to click links or provide passport or payment details. Organizations using Amadeus should review service-account permissions, rotate shared credentials, and confirm contractually how the provider will support breach-notification clocks.

The assessment changes if Amadeus confirms whether the Korean Air credential misuse and the heap-dump exposure share a cause, or if additional carriers such as All Nippon Airways or Japan Transocean Air confirm impact beyond JAL. Publication of affected counts, indicators of compromise, or a formal vendor advisory would replace current inference with evidence. A wave of phishing or account-takeover attempts quoting real PNRs would confirm the data is in active use and raise the priority from exposure to active exploitation.
