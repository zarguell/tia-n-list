Japan Airlines (JAL) disclosed a data breach at Amadeus's Passenger Service System that exposed data of about 92,000 customers. Amadeus notified JAL on October 23, 2026 that unauthorized access to its systems occurred between August 10, 2023 and September 23, 2026. The threat actor accessed reservation codes (PNRs), names, flight dates, routes, and travel classes for roughly 90,000 customers; an additional 2,000 customers had those data types plus email addresses, phone numbers, passport numbers, nationalities, frequent flyer numbers, and flight, ticket, and seat numbers exposed. JAL states credit card numbers and passwords were not compromised and says it has no evidence of customer information being misused. Amadeus has implemented security measures and flight operations are unaffected. Amadeus is a major travel IT supplier used by over four hundred airlines, 210 airport operators, 34 cruise and ferry lines, and 2 million hotels, and published a statement on October 1, 2026 about a vulnerability in its travel booking display environment.

The breach is notable for its multi-year dwell time, with access spanning from August 2023 to September 2026, and for the passport and contact data exposed for the 2,000 higher-risk customers. Although JAL reports no evidence of misuse, the combination of PNRs with passport numbers and contact details creates identity and travel-fraud risk for affected passengers, including potential phishing or account takeover attempts using reservation codes. Organizations relying on Amadeus should review their own exposure and access logs, since the same platform serves hundreds of airlines and the full scope of the Amadeus incident may not be limited to JAL.
