Japan Airlines disclosed that a data breach at Amadeus's Passenger Service System exposed information for about 92,000 customers. Unauthorized access ran from August 10, 2023 to September 23, 2026, and Amadeus notified JAL on October 23, 2026. Roughly 90,000 customers had reservation codes, names, flight dates, routes, and travel classes exposed, while about 2,000 had those fields plus email addresses, phone numbers, passport numbers, nationalities, frequent flyer numbers, and flight, ticket, and seat numbers. Credit card numbers and passwords were not compromised, and JAL reports no evidence of misuse so far.

The breach persisted for more than three years inside a system that handles reservations, ticketing, and check-in for over 400 airlines, which shows how a third-party travel IT provider can become a long-lived blind spot. Reporting indicates the Amadeus exposure involved access credentials visible in plain text via an unprotected heap dump, and Korean Air and Asiana Airlines reported similar leaks of customer reservation data through the same provider. The scope is personal and travel data rather than payments, but passport numbers and frequent flyer details are enough to support targeted phishing and account takeover.

Affected passengers should monitor for phishing referencing their itineraries and consider passport and frequent flyer account protections. Watch for Amadeus's full technical write-up and for whether additional airlines beyond JAL, Korean Air, and Asiana confirm exposure. The case raises questions about how long heap-dump credential exposure went undetected and whether travel IT providers will face new audit requirements.
