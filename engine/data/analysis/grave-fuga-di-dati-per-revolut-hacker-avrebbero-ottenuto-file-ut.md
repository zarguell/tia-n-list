Revolut disclosed a security incident in which an unauthorized third party obtained customer records by sending fraudulent requests from a legitimate government agency's email domain. The request passed domain authentication checks and was fulfilled, handing over complete KYC files including identity documents, verification selfies, residential addresses, IBANs, account statements, and full bitcoin transaction histories. On-chain investigator ZachXBT circulated the customer notification on September 11, and Revolut confirmed the incident publicly on September 12.

By September 13, the group behind the theft escalated from private extortion to public leaks. They published passport selfies and identity documents of named individuals including tennis player Alexander Shevchenko and Gamdom crypto casino CEO Felix Romer. The hackers stated they would release customer data daily until Revolut paid. The combination of real names, documented crypto holdings, and home addresses creates elevated physical robbery risk, the so-called "wrench attacks," along with fraud and targeted phishing.

Revolut said the number of affected users is limited but has not given a figure or named the agency whose domain was abused. The company stated its systems and customer funds were unaffected, though security researchers noted the attackers never needed to break into anything since Revolut handed the data over itself. The incident lands as Revolut holds conditional OCC approval for a US national bank charter and is reportedly weighing an IPO at around $200 billion valuation.
