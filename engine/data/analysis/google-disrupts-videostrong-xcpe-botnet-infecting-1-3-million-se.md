Google, working with Plume, TrendAI, Lumen, HUMAN Security, Synthient, and Japan's National Police Agency, took down the XCPE botnet hidden in the firmware of more than 1.3 million Android TV set-top boxes sold across over 200 countries. The backdoor was embedded by Chinese manufacturer Videostrong Technology Co. before the devices reached consumers, and the operation severed command-and-control servers used by more than a dozen related malware families, cutting access to at least 10 million additional compromised devices including retail set-top boxes, karaoke machines, and telehealth hardware. Google revoked Videostrong's license to manufacture certified Android TV devices and banned the company and its affiliates from producing certified Android hardware.

The case is notable because the malware was pre-installed at the factory rather than delivered after purchase. Videostrong slipped past Google's pre- and post-launch detections by loading the malicious code after the device's first boot, then sold proxy and ad-fraud access to criminal affiliates through the infected boxes. That makes XCPE a supply-chain compromise rather than a user-driven infection, and it turns consumer set-top boxes and connected healthcare devices into residential proxy nodes without the owner's knowledge.

Owners of affected boxes are unlikely to receive a clean firmware fix, so the practical remediation is replacement or network isolation of suspicious devices. Watch for whether Google or partners publish a device identification list, whether additional OEMs are implicated, and whether the residential proxy market shifts to other pre-infected hardware. The legal action against Videostrong's domains also sets a precedent for treating a certified manufacturer as a botnet operator.
