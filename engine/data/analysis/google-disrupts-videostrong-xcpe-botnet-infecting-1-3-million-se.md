Google and industry partners dismantled the XCPE botnet, a network of more than 1.3 million Android TV set-top boxes that Google attributes with high confidence to Chinese manufacturer Videostrong Technology Co. The company says Videostrong deliberately pre-installed a persistent backdoor in devices it manufactured and sold access to criminal affiliates, who used the devices for ad fraud, residential proxy services, and installation of additional malware. Google terminated its relationship with Videostrong, revoked its Android TV certification, and banned the company and its affiliates from producing certified Android devices.

As part of the disruption, Google took down XCPE command-and-control servers used by more than a dozen related malware families, cutting off access to at least 10 million additional compromised devices. Those devices were not infected through XCPE itself but through other malware and include retail set-top boxes, karaoke machines, and telehealth hardware, not all of which run Android.

What to watch: whether the takedown produces lasting effects on the residential-proxy market that relied on XCPE, and whether other device makers face similar action. Owners of affected set-top boxes should treat the devices as compromised and replace or reflash them, since the backdoor was installed at the firmware level by the manufacturer.
