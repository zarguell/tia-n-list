Google and a coalition including Plume, TrendAI, Lumen, HUMAN Security, Synthient, and Japan's National Police Agency disrupted the Videostrong XCPE botnet that infected over 1.3 million set-top boxes across more than 200 countries. The backdoor was embedded in device firmware before sale, turning the devices into a residential-proxy service sold to cybercrime affiliates. The takedown targeted affiliate command-and-control domains and was expected to cut access to at least 10 million additional compromised devices.

The botnet's scale makes it one of the larger IoT proxy networks publicly disrupted. Firmware-level infection before sale points to supply-chain compromise rather than post-purchase exploitation, which means patches through normal user updates may not fully remove the implant. Coordinated international action with Japan's National Police Agency involved suggests the network had significant domestic impact in Japan and possibly other Asia-Pacific regions.

What to watch: whether manufacturers release firmware updates that actually scrub the preinstalled backdoor, how quickly affiliates rebuild C2 infrastructure, and whether Google or partners publish IOCs or device-model lists so network defenders can scan for residual infection. The mention of 10 million additional devices implies a much larger latent pool still reachable by remaining affiliate nodes.
