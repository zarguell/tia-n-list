Google and a coalition of industry partners and law enforcement dismantled the XCPE botnet, a firmware-level backdoor that Chinese Android device maker Videostrong Technology shipped in more than 1.3 million Play Protect certified set-top boxes across over 200 countries. Google Threat Intelligence Group assesses with high confidence that Videostrong built and operated the backdoor for profit, selling access to criminal affiliates who used the devices for ad fraud, data theft, and residential proxy resale. The takedown of command-and-control domains also cut off access to at least 10 million additional compromised devices, including other IoT products such as karaoke machines and telehealth hardware.

The backdoor was embedded before sale and loaded after first boot, evading Google's pre- and post-launch detections by constantly changing configurations. Because the malicious code lives in device firmware, a factory reset does not remove it; infected boxes remain compromised. Google terminated its relationship with Videostrong, revoked its license to manufacture Android TV devices with preloaded Google apps and services, and banned the company and future affiliates from producing certified Android devices or supplying software to certified vendors.

The operation highlights a supply-chain failure in certified consumer hardware: an approved OEM abused its position for years across multiple device models and brands. Users who suspect an affected box should disconnect it and seek a fix or replacement from the seller, since the backdoor persists in firmware and cannot be cleared by a reset.
