Google dismantled the Videostrong XCPE botnet that infected 1.3 million Android set-top boxes across over 200 countries. The backdoor was embedded in firmware before sale and sold residential proxy access to cybercrime affiliates. The coordinated takedown involved Google, Plume, TrendAI, HUMAN Security, and Japan's National Police Agency, severing command-and-control servers and cutting access to at least 10 million additional compromised devices that included retail set-top boxes, karaoke machines, and telehealth hardware.

This is a significant supply-chain compromise because the manufacturer intentionally infected devices. The botnet spans 1.3 million devices, with at least 10 million more tied to related malware families, making it one of the larger IoT botnet disruptions reported recently. Google terminated its manufacturing relationship with Videostrong Technology Co.

The concrete artifacts include the XCPE malware family, the 1.3 million infected Android TV boxes, the 10 million additional devices at risk, and the list of affected hardware categories. Watch for follow-up attribution reports linking the backdoor installation to the manufacturer and any further law enforcement action against Videostrong.
