Google and industry partners dismantled the XCPE botnet, which infected more than 1.3 million Android TV set-top boxes. Google attributes the botnet with high confidence to Chinese manufacturer Videostrong Technology Co, accusing it of deliberately installing a persistent backdoor into devices it manufactured and selling access to criminal affiliates. Those affiliates used the backdoored devices for ad fraud, turned infected devices into residential proxies and installed additional malware.

As part of the disruption, Google took down XCPE command-and-control servers used by more than a dozen related malware families, cutting off access to at least 10 million additional compromised devices. Those devices were compromised through malware distributed by other means and include retail set-top boxes, karaoke machines and telehealth hardware, not all of them Android devices. Google also terminated its relationship with Videostrong, revoked its license to manufacture Android TV devices with preloaded Google apps and services, and banned the company and future affiliates from producing certified Android devices.

The action removes a residential proxy pool that fed cybercrime operations, but the scale of the 10 million additional compromised devices shows the problem extends well beyond XCPE. Operators of consumer and small-business IoT hardware should treat uncertified Android TV boxes as untrusted endpoints and segment them from sensitive networks.
