Google and industry partners have dismantled the XCPE botnet, a fleet of more than 1.3 million Android TV streaming boxes. Google Threat Intelligence Group assesses with high confidence that China-based device maker Videostrong Technology Co. deliberately built and operated the backdoor for profit, implanting it in devices it manufactured and selling access to criminal affiliates. The malicious code loaded only after the device's first boot, which helped it evade Google's pre-launch and post-launch detection.

Affiliates used the backdoored boxes for ad fraud, data exfiltration, and residential proxy services, and loaded additional malware onto them. Google took down the botnet's command-and-control servers, which also supported more than a dozen related malware families, cutting access to at least 10 million additional compromised devices including retail set-top boxes, karaoke machines, and telehealth hardware. Google terminated its relationship with Videostrong, revoked its license to build Android TV devices with preloaded Google apps, and banned it from the certified device program.

The backdoor remains in infected boxes' firmware, so affected owners should disconnect the boxes and ask the seller for a fix or replacement. Administrators should treat pre-installed malware in certified devices as an ongoing supply-chain risk and verify the firmware and vendor status of any Android TV hardware they deploy.
