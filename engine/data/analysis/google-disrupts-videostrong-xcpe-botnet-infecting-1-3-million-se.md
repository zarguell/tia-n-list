Google and a coalition including Plume, TrendAI, Lumen, HUMAN Security, Synthient and Japan's National Police Agency disrupted the XCPE botnet hidden in the firmware of more than 1.3 million Android set-top boxes sold across over 200 countries. The backdoor was embedded by device manufacturer Videostrong Technology before sale and loaded after first boot to evade Google's security checks, establishing XCPE as a residential-proxy and malware-distribution platform for criminal affiliates.

The operation severed command-and-control servers used by more than a dozen malware families, cutting off access to at least 10 million additional compromised devices ranging from retail set-top boxes to karaoke machines and telehealth hardware. Affiliates used the botnet for ad fraud, data theft and malicious traffic routing through victims' home connections, while Google terminated Videostrong's Android certification and banned the company and future affiliates from producing certified Android devices.

Watch items include how many already-deployed boxes receive remediation, whether residual XCPE modules survive on non-Android hardware, and whether certification gaps let similar pre-installed backdoors reach consumer devices again.
