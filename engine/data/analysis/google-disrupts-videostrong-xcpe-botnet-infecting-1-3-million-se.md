Google and industry partners dismantled the XCPE botnet, which enrolled more than 1.3 million Android TV set-top boxes shipped with a firmware-level backdoor. Google Threat Intelligence Group attributes the backdoor with high confidence to Videostrong Technology Co., a China-based Android OEM/ODM that Google says deliberately installed the persistent backdoor and sold access to criminal affiliates. Those affiliates used the backdoored devices for ad fraud, residential proxy traffic, data theft, and loading additional malware. Videostrong evaded Google's pre- and post-launch security checks by loading the malicious code after the device's first boot and constantly evolving configurations.

The takedown cut off access to at least 10 million additional compromised devices from other manufacturers, reached through more than a dozen malware families that ran as modules via the XCPE backdoor. Those devices include retail set-top boxes, karaoke machines, and telehealth hardware, not all of them Android. Google terminated its relationship with Videostrong, revoked its license to manufacture Android TV devices with preloaded Google apps and services, and banned the company and its future affiliates from producing certified Android devices or supplying software to certified vendors.

What to watch: set-top boxes and IoT devices already in homes remain at risk until firmware is updated or replaced, and residential proxy abuse from the broader module ecosystem may persist. The case also raises questions about OEM vetting in Google's certification process, since a licensed manufacturer operated a botnet across multiple device brands over several years.
