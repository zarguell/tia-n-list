Google and a coalition of partners including Plume, TrendAI, Lumen, HUMAN Security, Synthient, and Japan's National Police Agency dismantled the XCPE botnet, a network of more than 1.3 million Android TV set-top boxes spread across more than 200 countries. Google Threat Intelligence Group attributes the backdoor with high confidence to Videostrong Technology Co., a China-based OEM and ODM that embedded it in device firmware before sale and sold access to criminal affiliates. The affiliates used the devices for ad fraud, residential proxying, data exfiltration, and loading additional malware. Google also took down command-and-control domains used by more than a dozen related malware families, cutting off access to at least 10 million additional compromised devices from various manufacturers, including retail set-top boxes, karaoke machines, and telehealth hardware, not all of them Android.

Google revoked Videostrong's license to manufacture Android TV devices with preloaded Google apps and services and banned the company and its future affiliates from producing certified Android hardware. The backdoor evaded Google's pre-launch and post-launch detections by loading its malicious code only after the device's first boot, which let it pass security review. The backdoor remains in the firmware of infected boxes, so owners of suspect devices should disconnect them and seek a fix or replacement from the seller.

Operators and defenders should treat any low-cost Android TV box of uncertain provenance as potentially compromised, since the malware ships in the firmware and requires no user interaction. The takedown removed the botnet's revenue and access, but the installed base of 1.3 million boxes is not automatically cleaned, and the same module operators had already diversified beyond XCPE to other device types.
