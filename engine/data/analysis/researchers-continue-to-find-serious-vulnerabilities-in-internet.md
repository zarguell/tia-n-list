VulnCheck disclosed 14 vulnerabilities in Dixell refrigeration controllers made by Copeland, all affecting the XWEB EVO supervision system. Lab testing on rebuilt firmware showed each flaw could grant an attacker root or administrative access, with severity scores ranging from 8 to 9.8, and at least two are known to be under active exploitation in the wild. These devices were among those hit in the October attack on refrigeration controllers across roughly 700 supermarkets in France.

Why it matters: internet-connected industrial and retail refrigeration is a physical-world target, not just a data one. The affected supervision systems sit in environments where tampering can spoil inventory and disrupt cold chains, and the flaws are severe and already being exploited. The XWEB EVO issues add to a pattern of weak access controls in these products, with the researcher describing the login handling as worse than a trivial daily password.

What to watch: patching and network segmentation for exposed XWEB EVO deployments, monitoring for exploitation of the two in-the-wild flaws, and whether additional vendors in the same sector disclose similar weaknesses as scrutiny continues.
