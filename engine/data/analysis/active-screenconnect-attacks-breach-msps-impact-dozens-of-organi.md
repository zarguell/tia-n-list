Attackers used access to cloud-hosted ConnectWise ScreenConnect panels belonging to managed service providers on November 11, pushing commands through Run Command to install rogue remote-access clients. Huntress counted at least 6 compromised MSPs, 288 endpoints across 48 organizations, roughly one-third of the hosts within the first 10 minutes and all of them within about 90 minutes. The SCKit toolkit gives persistent access, intercepts session cookies and spreads when a technician connects to an infected endpoint, then moves through that technician's other sessions and panels. Microsoft confirmed the activity and described a batch launcher, a PowerShell decoder, scheduled tasks and registry Run entries, plus a RelayTrap component aimed at connecting technicians.

Huntress traced initial access to RMM+, a third-party extension whose versions 1.0.31 and 1.0.32 defaulted its shared access key to blank, bypassing ScreenConnect accounts, roles and MFA. ConnectWise disabled five extensions across about 400 partner environments and said it found no compromise of its own infrastructure or flaw in ScreenConnect itself. A separate line of reporting covers CVE-2026-84869, scored 9.9, which the SCKit code tries to use against technician clients; Huntress and Blackpoint did not confirm successful exploitation and a patched client resisted the exploit in testing. Affected builds named in reporting include 26.6.6.9747 and 26.7.6.9783.

Later reporting described more than 2,200 fake, always-online computers with crypto-themed names on over 100 ScreenConnect servers and 688,000 malicious file requests tied to the CVE-2026-84869 activity. ConnectWise has recommended technician clients at version 26.6.5 or later, but disabling extensions does not remove existing persistence. Watch for further on-premises cases, cookie-theft abuse and any confirmation of successful technician-client exploitation.
