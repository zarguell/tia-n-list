An attacker abused access to multiple cloud-hosted ConnectWise ScreenConnect panels on November 11, 2026 to push rogue ScreenConnect clients to at least 288 endpoints across 48 organizations and 6 managed service providers, hitting all 288 hosts within roughly 90 minutes. The payload is SCKit, a worm-like toolkit providing persistent backdoor access, session-cookie interception, and self-replication: it waits for a technician to connect to a compromised endpoint, infects that workstation, then spreads to other sessions and panels. Huntress traced initial access to RMM+, a third-party extension that manages ScreenConnect sessions without the console and authenticates with a shared access key; in versions 1.0.31 and 1.0.32 that key defaulted to blank, so the authentication check did nothing.

ConnectWise said it found no evidence of compromised ConnectWise infrastructure or a vulnerability in ScreenConnect itself, attributing the activity to abuse of independent third-party extensions. It disabled five third-party extensions across roughly 400 partner environments and advised on-premises customers to disable them manually, noting that disabling extensions does not remove already-established malware or persistence. The recovered toolkit also carries exploit code for CVE-2026-84869 (CVSS 9.9), a previously patched ScreenConnect flaw, but Huntress and Blackpoint Cyber report they have not observed successful exploitation during these incidents and the exploit failed against a patched client in testing.

Separately, Huntress reported more than 2,200 fake always-online computers with crypto-related names on over 100 ScreenConnect servers. Affected MSPs should treat every endpoint that received a rogue client as compromised, reimage those hosts, and audit ScreenConnect server logs for Windows Script Host or PowerShell execution, Run Command history, extension installs, and authentication activity. Because SCKit can ride a technician session into new panels, any technician who connected to an infected endpoint during the window needs their workstation checked before further remote work, and RMM+ should be removed or updated.
