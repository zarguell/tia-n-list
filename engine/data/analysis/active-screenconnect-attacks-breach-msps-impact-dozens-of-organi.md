An attacker abused access to cloud-hosted ConnectWise ScreenConnect panels on November 11, 2026 to push rogue ScreenConnect clients to at least 288 endpoints across 48 organizations and 6 managed service providers, hitting all 288 hosts within roughly 90 minutes. The payload, a worm-like toolkit Huntress calls SCKit, provides persistent backdoor access, intercepts session cookies, and self-replicates by waiting for a technician to connect to a compromised endpoint before infecting that technician's workstation and spreading to other sessions and panels. Huntress traced the initial abuse to RMM+, a third-party extension that lets tools manage ScreenConnect sessions without the console.

ConnectWise said it found no evidence of compromised ConnectWise infrastructure or a vulnerability in ScreenConnect itself, attributing the activity to abuse of independent third-party extensions. As a precaution it disabled five third-party extensions across roughly 400 partner environments and told on-premises customers to disable them manually, warning that disabling extensions does not remove already-established malware or persistence. The recovered toolkit also carries exploit code for CVE-2026-84869 (CVSS 9.9), a previously patched ScreenConnect flaw, but Huntress and Blackpoint Cyber say they have not observed successful exploitation of it during these incidents and the exploit failed in testing against a patched client.

Affected MSPs should assume every managed endpoint that received a rogue client is compromised, reimage those hosts, and audit ScreenConnect server logs for Windows Script Host or PowerShell execution, Run Command history, extension installs, and authentication activity. Because SCKit can ride a technician session into new panels, any technician who connected to an infected endpoint during the window needs their workstation checked before further remote work.
