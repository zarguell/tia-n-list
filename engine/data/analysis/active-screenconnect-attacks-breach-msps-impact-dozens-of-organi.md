Huntress began tracking an active campaign on November 11, 2026 after attackers used cloud-hosted ScreenConnect panels belonging to managed service providers to push commands to managed endpoints. The intruders installed rogue ScreenConnect clients and the SCKit toolkit on at least 288 hosts across 48 organizations and six MSPs. Roughly a third of the hosts were hit in the first ten minutes and all of them within about 90 minutes. Microsoft later confirmed the activity and described SCKit persistence through scheduled tasks and registry Run entries, plus a RelayTrap component that delivers a payload to a connecting technician and can spread through that technician's management console.

The access path in the November wave was RMM+, a third-party ScreenConnect extension that bypasses ScreenConnect accounts, roles and MFA by authenticating with a shared access key. Huntress says versions 1.0.31 and 1.0.32 defaulted that key to blank. ConnectWise disabled five third-party extensions across roughly 400 partner environments and said it found no evidence of a compromise of its own infrastructure or a defect in ScreenConnect itself. A related, month-long wave exploits CVE-2026-84869, a CVSS 9.9 missing-authorization bug patched on September 8 and added to KEV on September 11. Huntress counted more than 688,000 guest run-file requests for update*.bat against 42 MSP servers from 29 IP addresses since October 20; a November 12 compromise used a batch file that called a Telegram bot and produced a rogue client connection in about nine seconds.

Earlier August incidents in the same story used a four-stage VBScript chain that spread over ScreenConnect sessions, and ConnectWise recommended reimaging affected hosts. The November 19 situation update indicates the MSP targeting is still active. Watch third-party extension inventories, authentication and extension-installation records, Run Command and Toolbox history, and technician workstations that connect to suspect endpoints. ConnectWise's latest guidance is to ensure the technician client is at 26.6.5 or later; disabling extensions does not remove established persistence.
