A suspected Chinese-speaking threat actor is actively exploiting CVE-2026-59310, a critical directory-traversal vulnerability in VMware vCenter Server's Syslog Server component (CVSS 9.8, disclosed July 29, 2026). The attacker writes a malicious cron job file to /etc/cron.d/ via the vulnerable Syslog Server, triggering a root-level command that downloads and executes a payload. From that initial foothold, the actor establishes multiple backdoors, steals Active Directory credentials via directory-service access, and deploys Babuk-derived ransomware on ESXi hypervisors. QUIRSO security researchers identified 361 unique victim IP addresses across 47 countries. Germany, the United States, Turkey, Iran, and France are the most affected. The first compromised systems were observed phoning home to attacker infrastructure on August 3, just five days after disclosure.

A second track involving CVE-2026-59309, another vCenter RCE, appears to be operating in parallel, possibly by the same or a related actor. The CVE-2026-59309 exploitation chain also turns a single exposed vCenter appliance into a launch point for broad virtual-infrastructure compromise. Both vulnerabilities are in the same VMware vCenter product family, meaning organizations that patch one and not the other remain exposed. The five-day window between disclosure and first exploitation confirms this is a fast-moving campaign, not opportunistic scanning.

This is a high-priority story. VMware vCenter is ubiquitous in enterprise virtualization environments, and the combination of unauthenticated RCE, credential theft, and ransomware deployment in one chain makes this immediately exploitable at scale. The 361 victim IPs across 47 countries suggest broad reach already. Organizations running vCenter should verify they have applied the July 29 patches for both CVE-2026-59309 and CVE-2026-59310, and check logs for the zz-poc59310-syslog.log indicator and the linuxFile payload.
