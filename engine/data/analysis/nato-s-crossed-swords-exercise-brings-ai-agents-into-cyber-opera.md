NATO's CCDCOE kicked off Crossed Swords 2026 in Tallinn, Estonia, with an execution phase running through Friday and more than 250 participants from over 40 NATO and non-NATO countries. The exercise trains national cyber forces and supporting units, including military cyber operators, threat hunters, and digital forensics experts, on planning cyber operations and timing them with kinetic military action. Exercise Director Jozef Kostelansky framed the core challenge as synchronisation measured in minutes rather than hours.

New this year is a dedicated track on AI agents, in which participants build, deploy, and assess agents and operate alongside them. The environment runs on locally hosted AI and, for the first time in Crossed Swords, also uses OpenAI's Daybreak cyber defense offering. The stated rationale is that AI is now in the loop on both sides, making the timing problem harder. The exercise is also tied into special operations forces training.

The event matters as an indicator of how military cyber doctrine is absorbing autonomous tooling. Observers should watch for follow-up reporting on agent performance, rules of engagement for AI-assisted effects, and whether Daybreak-style tooling appears in allied defensive baselines.
