The investigations team at Duel made contact with the hacker behind the Revolut incident, uncovering details of a sophisticated attack blending infostealer compromise with social engineering. The attacker initially gained access to Italian government employee accounts (pec.interno.it addresses from the Ministry of the Interior) through infostealer malware. Hudson Rock's database identified approximately 300 compromised pec.interno.it webmail logins from already-infected machines.

Once inside a government employee's email, the attacker added a recovery email under their control, silently monitored all communications, and deleted outgoing fraudulent emails before the account owner noticed. They downloaded replies as .eml files and removed them in real time. The hacker admitted to initially trying forged court orders before pivoting to impersonation of Italian government officials in correspondence with Revolut, using the compromised government email addresses to lend credibility.

The social engineering was sophisticated enough to convince Revolut to hand over sensitive customer data, including passport copies, KYC selfies, and financial information. The attack demonstrates how infostealer malware feeds into downstream social engineering campaigns that target third parties, not just the original victim. Financial institutions should verify identity documents and data requests through out-of-band channels, not just because the email appears to come from a legitimate government domain.
