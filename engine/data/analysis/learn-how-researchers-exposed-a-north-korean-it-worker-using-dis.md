Researchers at Silent Push exposed a suspected North Korean IT worker operating on Discord to recruit foreign intermediaries for fraudulent remote employment. The operator, using the alias "tecguru113," posted in the Mouse Review Discord community targeting individuals in the US, EU, and Latin American countries including Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, and Chile. The scheme offered intermediaries 35% of the salary in exchange for lending their identity during job interviews while the actual developer worked remotely.

The investigation revealed the operator recommended Astrill VPN, a service previously linked by Silent Push to North Korean IT worker infrastructure. When researchers coaxed the operator into a video call, he refused to share his screen and abruptly terminated the feed when North Korea was mentioned. Silent Push assessed with high confidence that the operator is a North Korean citizen, though this remains an attribution rather than a law enforcement determination.

The scheme mirrors tactics documented in a July 31, 2026 joint advisory from the US, UK, Germany, France, the Netherlands, Japan, and South Korea. Those nations detailed widespread use of forged documents, intermediaries, VPNs, remote access tools, and proxy bank accounts. In April 2026, the US DOJ dismantled a scheme that helped North Korean workers infiltrate over 100 companies using at least 80 American identities, with illicit proceeds exceeding $5 million. Two operators managing "laptop farms" received sentences of 108 and 92 months. The FBI has warned that employed North Korean IT workers can access source code and internal systems, then extort employers after termination.
