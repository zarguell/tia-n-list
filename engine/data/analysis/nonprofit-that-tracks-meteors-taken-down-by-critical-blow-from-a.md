The International Meteor Organization (IMO), a nonprofit founded in 1988 that coordinates global amateur and professional meteor observations, has been taken largely offline by a cyberattack. The organization's website displays a message calling it a "critical blow to aging infrastructure" and says it expects several weeks of partial downtime while transitioning to new infrastructure.

The IMO maintains databases of meteor and fireball observations including text, photos, and video, publishes the bimonthly WGN journal, and has helped forge unified standards for reporting meteor observations. Fireball reporting is currently being prioritized through a separate portal at fireball.imo.net.

No group has claimed responsibility and there are no reports of a ransom demand. The motive is unclear: the IMO's data is largely public, and attacks on space-observation nonprofits are rare. Previous incidents include a 2023 ransomware attack on the American Meteorological Society, a breach of the National Science Foundation's Noirlab facility, and an attack on Chile's Atacama Large Millimeter Array observatory. US agencies warned in 2023 that space-related organizations may be targets of nation-state hackers.
