The International Meteor Organization (IMO), a nonprofit founded in 1988 that coordinates amateur and professional meteor observations worldwide, suffered a cyberattack that took most of its infrastructure offline. The organization described the incident as a "critical blow" to aging infrastructure and expects several weeks of partial downtime while transitioning to new services. Fireball observation reporting remains functional through a separate portal.

Ars Technica reported the story on September 16, noting that the IMO's databases of meteor and fireball observations (text, photos, videos) are considered essential by many in the field. No group has claimed responsibility and no ransom demand has been reported. The motive remains unclear: the IMO's data is mostly public, and attacks on space observation organizations are rare.

While the direct security impact is limited (this is a niche scientific nonprofit), it fits a pattern of increasing attacks on research and scientific organizations. The American Meteorological Society was hit by ransomware in 2023, and US agencies warned in 2023 that space-related organizations may be nation-state targets. The IMO incident is notable primarily as a reminder that even small, low-profile scientific bodies are not immune to cyberattacks.
