Citizen Lab published the first public technical confirmation of zero-click mercenary spyware delivered through targeted ads, a delivery technique it calls AdFection. Devices at Al Jazeera were infected with Candiru spyware, also rebranded as Integrity Labs, after malicious ads rendered in a browser or app; no click was needed. One follow-up ties the attacks to at least 45 devices at Al Jazeera, and another reports that Google cut off the ad accounts involved.

Israeli ad-tech firm Discover-Tech bought placements through mainstream ad networks and real-time bidding exchanges and used its Controlium platform to de-anonymize, fingerprint and target devices. Citizen Lab describes the firm as a bridge between mercenary spyware vendors and the advertising ecosystem. Amnesty International documented a similar mechanism called Aladdin in late 2025 involving Predator spyware and leaked Intellexa documents, but AdFection is the first case caught in real attacks.

On November 18, NRC reported that computer networks at the EU, NATO, the UN and governments worldwide may have been infected with Israeli spyware through ads, extending the suspected victim set beyond Al Jazeera. Watch for further victim notifications, confirmation of which agencies were affected, and ad-platform controls on device fingerprinting and code embedded in ad creatives.
