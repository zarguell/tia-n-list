CISA advisory ICSA-26-323-02, published 19 November 2026, covers Elecbrakes EB2 controllers through 3.0.4.2.0, VM1 below 1.3.4.2.1 and all ELBC2000 units. Missing authentication and replayable Bluetooth commands are tracked as CVE-2026-96805 and CVE-2026-125273 at CVSS 9.6, and an unprotected firmware-update channel as CVE-2026-124281 at CVSS 6.1. Any Bluetooth device in range can apply trailer brakes with any force without pairing, PIN or authentication; iTnews puts the range at 10 to 100 metres and names jackknife or loss of control as the risk.

Patrick Forck and Jeremy McCarthy of Element Digital wrote that a four-byte write to the brake control characteristic set force through a byte ranging from 0x00 to 0xFF, with notification feedback confirming brake state and no sequence numbers, nonces or replay protection. Their closed-road test produced stronger deceleration at higher force values. They also found an over-the-air update channel reachable after Just Works pairing with firmware integrity checked only by CRC32. GBHackers reported on 24 November that the researchers decompiled the Elecbrakes Android app with AI assistance and mapped 47 Bluetooth characteristics.

The vendor shipped fixes on 6 July 2026 for EB2 3.0.4.4.3 and VM1 1.3.4.2.1, but installation is user-initiated through the Elecbrakes app. Elecbrakes told iTnews that 60 to 70 percent of connecting controllers were then on current firmware, and the discontinued ELBC2000 remains without a fix. CISA reported no public exploitation at disclosure. The consequence here is physical force on a moving trailer rather than data theft, which makes the remaining unpatched units the key question.

Owners should install the fixed EB2 and VM1 builds and verify the version in the app, and treat any powered ELBC2000 as unmitigated. Watch for unexpected brake writes and state notifications, and for any field report of unexplained braking, which would change the assessment from a safety hazard with no reported exploitation to an incident.
