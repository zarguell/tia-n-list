CISA advisory ICSA-26-323-02, published 19 November 2026, covers Elecbrakes EB2 controllers through version 3.0.4.2.0, VM1 below 1.3.4.2.1 and all ELBC2000 units. Missing authentication and replayable Bluetooth commands are tracked as CVE-2026-96805 and CVE-2026-125273 at CVSS 9.6, and an unprotected firmware-update channel as CVE-2026-124281 at CVSS 6.1. CISA says exploitation could let a nearby attacker control trailer brakes, change firmware or disable brake lights and other safety features.

Patrick Forck and Jeremy McCarthy of Element Digital describe four-byte unauthenticated writes to a brake-control BLE characteristic, with no pairing, PIN or replay protection and notification feedback on brake state. Their closed-road test produced stronger deceleration at higher force values. They also found an over-the-air update path reachable after Just Works pairing, with firmware integrity checked only by CRC32. GBHackers reported that the researchers decompiled the Elecbrakes Android app with AI assistance and mapped 47 Bluetooth characteristics. iTnews puts the attack range at 10 to 100 metres and quotes the jackknife risk when trailer brakes engage while the tow vehicle continues.

Elecbrakes shipped fixes on 6 July 2026 for EB2 3.0.4.4.3 and VM1 1.3.4.2.1, but installation is user-initiated through the vendor app. The company told iTnews that 60 to 70 percent of connecting controllers were then on current firmware, and the discontinued ELBC2000 still has no fix. The devices are used mainly in Australia and New Zealand. CISA reported no public exploitation at disclosure. Watch for field reports of unexplained braking, for the remaining unpatched EB2 and VM1 units, and for any decision on the unsupported ELBC2000 fleet.
