Fandango is notifying customers that payment card details entered on its checkout page were exposed between October 8 and 11, 2026. The company learned of a problem with the page, brought in external cybersecurity experts and determined on October 30 that customer information may have been affected. One account of the incident describes card skimmer code injected into the checkout page that intercepted details as orders were placed and sent them to the attackers. Fandango sells tickets for 31,000 U.S. cinema screens including AMC, Regal and Cinemark and also operates Rotten Tomatoes, so the pool of potentially affected transactions is broad.

The exposed fields are the card number, CVV, expiration date and billing ZIP code, along with the customer's email address. Fandango says names, Social Security numbers, government-issued identification and other account data were not affected, and that it is not aware of fraudulent use of the information so far. The combination is still enough for unauthorized purchases on online stores. The company says the problem has been fixed and its security processes strengthened, but it has not disclosed how the skimmer reached the checkout page.

Affected customers are offered two years of IDX credit and dark web monitoring plus identity theft insurance and recovery support. What to watch is whether Fandango publishes the initial access path, whether other ticketing or checkout pages show the same skimmer, and whether card issuers see fraud tied to the October 8 to 11 window. The incident is another client-side payment skimming case where the compromise sits in the transaction flow rather than in a stolen account database.
