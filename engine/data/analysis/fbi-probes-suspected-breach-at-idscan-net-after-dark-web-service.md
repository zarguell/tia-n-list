A dark web identity-theft service called Nexus surfaced on September 1, 2026, advertising searchable access to over 153 million scanned U.S. and Canadian driver's licenses, plus 10 million ID cards, 3 million travel documents, and 579,000 medical cards. Brian Krebs traced the database to IDScan, an identity verification company whose systems are used by car rental firms, retailers, gun shops, financial institutions, and cannabis dispensaries across the U.S. Krebs confirmed the data by searching for his own records and those of individuals who consented to checks.

The FBI's New Orleans field office opened a formal investigation the same day the service appeared. IDScan has not published any statements about the allegations and did not respond to media inquiries. It remains unclear whether IDScan's systems were directly compromised or how many individuals are ultimately affected.

Multiple law firms, including Markovits, Stock & DeMarco and Hall Attorneys, have filed lawsuits and launched investigations into potential class-action litigation. The sheer scale of the exposed data, spanning government-issued identity documents across two countries, makes this one of the largest identity document breaches on record. Organizations using IDScan for identity verification should assume their customer data may be in the hands of threat actors.
