Volt Typhoon, the China-linked actor long associated with burrowing into US critical infrastructure, has been running a framework that Black Lotus Labs (Lumen) calls "Silent Train": rotating chains of compromised SOHO routers, IoT devices, and enterprise infrastructure that are assembled and dismantled daily, sometimes in under two minutes, to reach high-value targets. Researchers tracked the framework for three years and confirmed Volt Typhoon exploited at least four zero-day vulnerabilities in edge and management infrastructure. Targets include telecom providers, government entities, defense contractors, universities, utilities and critical infrastructure, satellite service providers, IT firms, MSPs, transportation, aviation, and emergency services, with California, Texas, and Hawaii the most targeted regions, pointing at Indo-Pacific priorities.

Silent Train partly replaced KV-Botnet after the FBI disrupted that infrastructure, and it is not a standalone network but a continuously refreshed chain of short-lived nodes. The most useful detection signal is the churn itself: rapid rotation of compromised edge devices, near-zero persistent nodes, and zero-day exploitation of network edge gear. Defenders should prioritize patching edge and management infrastructure and monitor for ephemeral proxy chains rather than static command-and-control.
