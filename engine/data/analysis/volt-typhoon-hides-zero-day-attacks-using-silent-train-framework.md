Volt Typhoon, a China-backed threat actor, has been running an ephemeral relay infrastructure dubbed "Silent Train" that is assembled and dismantled daily rather than operated as a standing botnet. Lumen's Black Lotus Labs tracked the framework for three years and reported that the group builds fresh chains of four to six hops for each operation, mixing non-U.S. VPS providers such as Ucloud, Alibaba Cloud, and Huawei Cloud with compromised SOHO and IoT devices from vendors including Intelbras, TP-Link, Ruckus, Zyxel, and Cambium. The group can rebuild a new chain in under two minutes, keeping the number of live nodes near zero at any moment and frustrating takedown efforts.

Black Lotus Labs confirmed that Volt Typhoon used Silent Train to exploit at least four zero-day vulnerabilities in edge and management platforms, including VeloCloud Orchestrator, Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN components, N-central, Versa Director, and KACE SMA. Targets span telecom firms, government agencies, defense contractors, educational institutes, utilities, satellite providers, IT firms, MSPs, transportation, aviation, and emergency services, with the most targeted regions being California, Texas, and Hawaii. The framework's deployment was partly fueled by the FBI's disruption of the KV-Botnet in December 2023, which had previously served as Volt Typhoon's staging ground for concealing intrusions into U.S. critical infrastructure.

Defenders should confirm that every VeloCloud Orchestrator, Catalyst SD-WAN Controller/Manager/Validator, N-central, Versa Director, and KACE SMA instance they or their MSPs operate is on the fixed builds published alongside the report. Because Silent Train is rebuilt continuously and individual chains are short-lived, network-level blocking alone is insufficient; patching the named edge and management platforms is the priority.
