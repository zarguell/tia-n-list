Volt Typhoon is using a framework called Silent Train to rotate compromised devices for selective zero-day attacks. Black Lotus Labs tracked the framework for three years and confirmed at least four zero-day exploits against edge and management infrastructure. Targets span telecom, government, defense contractors, utilities, transportation, and emergency services, with California, Texas, and Hawaii most targeted. The framework is refreshed after each operation, and its use was partly fueled by the FBI disruption of the KV-Botnet that Volt Typhoon previously relied on. CISA has previously warned that Volt Typhoon seeks persistent access to U.S. critical infrastructure for potential disruption during a crisis.

This is significant because Silent Train shows a sustained, state-backed capability to exploit zero-days at scale against high-value systems while cleaning up traces after each use. The focus on Indo-Pacific regions and critical sectors aligns with prior CISA warnings about pre-positioning for future conflict. Defenders should assume continued targeting of edge devices, management interfaces, and critical infrastructure, and prioritize visibility into rotating compromise chains rather than single-point indicators.

Watch for additional Black Lotus Labs reporting on Silent Train tactics, any CISA or FBI updates on Volt Typhoon activity, and patch cycles for the zero-day vulnerabilities used. Monitor infrastructure in California, Texas, and Hawaii for unusual edge-device behavior and rapid infrastructure rotation.
