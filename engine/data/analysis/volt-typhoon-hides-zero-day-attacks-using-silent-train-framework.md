Volt Typhoon continues to build ephemeral infrastructure under the framework researchers call Silent Train. Black Lotus Labs and Cisco Talos describe a rotating chain of compromised SOHO routers, IoT devices, and enterprise gear used to hide access and launch zero-day attacks against edge and management platforms. The group targets telecoms, government agencies, defense contractors, utilities, satellite providers, and IT firms, with the heaviest activity in California, Texas, and Hawaii. Black Lotus Labs has tracked Silent Train for three years and confirmed at least four zero-day exploits in this chain, including attacks on VeloCloud, SD-WAN controllers, and IT management platforms. The FBI previously disrupted KV Botnet, but Volt Typhoon rebuilt quickly, demonstrating it can assemble a new chain in under two minutes.

The framework is not a persistent botnet but a rapidly refreshed relay network assembled for each operation. Attackers filter targets through exposed services and use short-lived hops to evade detection. Victims include hundreds of notified entities across critical infrastructure sectors. Defenders should verify that VeloCloud Orchestrator, Catalyst SD-WAN controllers, N-central, Versa Director, and KACE SMA instances run fixed builds, because these are among the platforms targeted through Silent Train. The ongoing refresh of infrastructure means detection based solely on IP indicators will have a short shelf life.
