Volt Typhoon, the China state-sponsored group previously tied to pre-positioning in U.S. critical infrastructure, has been running a covert operational framework that Black Lotus Labs (Lumen) calls Silent Train. Rather than a standing botnet, the framework assembles and dismantles fresh relay chains of four to six hops each day: two non-U.S. VPS hosts, then compromised overseas SOHO and IoT gear, then compromised U.S. devices, with infrastructure refreshed continuously. Black Lotus Labs has tracked it for three years and says Volt Typhoon can rebuild a new chain in under two minutes, keeping the number of live nodes close to zero.

The framework has been used to exploit at least four zero-day vulnerabilities in centralized management platforms, including VeloCloud Orchestrator, Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN controllers, N-able N-central, Versa Director, and Quest KACE SMA. Targets span telecom providers, government agencies, defense contractors, universities, IT and managed service providers, transportation, aviation, utilities, and emergency services, with the most targeted regions reported as California, Texas, and Hawaii, pointing to an Indo-Pacific focus. Black Lotus Labs has notified hundreds of victims since it first observed the activity.

Silent Train partly emerged after the FBI disrupted Volt Typhoon's earlier KV-Botnet in late 2023, and researchers assess it as a repeatable capability to burrow into edge and management infrastructure for persistent access. CISA has previously warned that Volt Typhoon seeks to maintain access that could be used for disruption during a future crisis or conflict, so the rotated infrastructure model complicates detection and victim notification.

Defenders are urged to verify the specified patched builds on all VeloCloud, Catalyst SD-WAN, N-central, Versa Director, and KACE systems they or their MSPs operate, since the four zero-days were aimed at IT-management and SD-WAN platforms.
