Researchers have identified Aeternum, a C++-based botnet loader that abuses Polygon blockchain smart contracts as a decentralized C2 infrastructure. Instead of contacting traditional attacker-owned servers, infected machines send JSON-RPC requests to public Polygon RPC endpoints, read a contract value, and retrieve C2 domains or instructions from the chain. This dead-drop resolver pattern makes takedowns significantly harder: there is no single domain to seize or server to sinkhole, and the contract data lives on a public, immutable ledger.

The technique is not entirely new (blockchain-based DNS resolution has appeared in prior malware families), but Aeternum's implementation is notable for its operational simplicity and the fact that it targets a mainstream botnet loader rather than an APT toolkit. Operators can rotate C2 domains by updating the smart contract, giving them resilience against conventional infrastructure disruption at near-zero hosting cost.

Watch for other loader and RAT families adopting the same pattern. The barrier to entry is low: any attacker with a small amount of MATIC can deploy a contract. Defenders should monitor for anomalous JSON-RPC traffic to Polygon public endpoints from non-crypto software on enterprise networks.
