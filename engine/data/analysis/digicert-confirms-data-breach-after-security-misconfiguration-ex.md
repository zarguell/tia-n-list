DigiCert confirmed unauthorized access to an internal business system holding archived customer information, exposed to the internet by a February 2025 server migration. Attackers abused a self-registration feature in the system's account manager, built for other purposes and not requiring MFA, to create a read-only account that could reach archived data between June 27 and July 2; DigiCert blocked the account on July 2 but only determined in October that archived contact data had been accessed. The exposed data covers names, business email addresses, phone numbers and business addresses, and in some cases identity documents such as driver's licenses, passports and utility bills. DigiCert stresses that CA systems run on separate network segments and were unaffected: no CertCentral, DigiCert ONE, login credentials or issued certificates are in scope. The incident is also tracked as an Mozilla Bugzilla report after DigiCert disclosed it there in October.

The story advanced on November 6 with wider corroboration and customer impact detail. SecurityWeek's feed report matches the BleepingComputer account of the migration error and the June 27 to July 2 access window, and The Register summarized the same exposure of contact data plus identity documents. An IT-Connect writeup adds that DigiCert has begun notifying affected customers, with cloud architect Fabian Bader posting his notification on X and objecting that the leak covered his full PII plus submitted verification documents. DigiCert told customers it is still reviewing identification documents to determine who is affected.

Customers who received notifications should treat any verification-document request with suspicion until DigiCert completes its review, since exposed IDs enable follow-on fraud and social engineering against its customer base. Watch for the Bugzilla review to expand the affected count and for confirmation of how many identity documents were actually accessed.
