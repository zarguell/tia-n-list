DigiCert confirmed a data breach of an internal business system that archived customer information. A February 2025 server migration moved the system to a different network segment but inadvertently exposed it to the internet. Attackers abused a self-registration feature in the account manager, which did not require MFA, to create a read-only account that accessed archived customer contact information between June 27 and July 2. DigiCert blocked the unauthorized account on July 2 and began notifying affected customers once its review of identification documents was complete.

The exposed data included names, business email addresses, phone numbers, and business addresses. For some customers the records also contained personal identification documents such as driver's licenses, passports, and utility bills. DigiCert said the access could not write records, act as a validation approver, or issue certificates, and that its certificate authority systems, including CertCentral and DigiCert ONE, run on separate secure network segments and were not affected.

The exposure dates back to the February 2025 migration, though DigiCert only started suspecting archived data access on October 12, 2026, when it launched a formal investigation. An incident report on Mozilla's Bugzilla platform documents the migration error. The breach affected a small portion of DigiCert's archived customer base, with customer login credentials and issued certificates out of scope.
