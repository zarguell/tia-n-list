DigiCert confirmed a data breach after a February 2025 server migration mistakenly exposed an internal business system to the internet. Attackers abused a self-registration feature in the system's account manager, which did not require multi-factor authentication, to create a read-only account that accessed archived customer data between June 27 and July 2, 2026. DigiCert blocked the unauthorized account on July 2 but only began suspecting broader data access on October 12.

Exposed information includes names, business email addresses, phone numbers and business addresses. In some cases the records also contained identity documents such as driver's licenses, passports and utility bills. DigiCert began notifying affected customers. The company stressed that its certificate authority systems, including CertCentral and DigiCert ONE, run on separate secure network segments and were not affected, and that the intruder could not write records, act as a validation approver, or issue certificates.

Customers should watch for breach notification emails and treat any exposed identity documents as a risk for identity theft or impersonation. Organizations using DigiCert should confirm their account credentials and review any issued validation records.
