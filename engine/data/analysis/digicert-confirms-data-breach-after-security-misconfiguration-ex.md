DigiCert confirmed unauthorized access to an internal business system holding archived customer information after a security misconfiguration exposed the application to the internet. Exposed data included business contact details and, in some cases, identity documents. DigiCert's statement covered its certificate authority services, including CertCentral and DigiCert One, customer login credentials, and issued certificates.

The incident matters because identity documents and business contact details of customers were exposed even though the certificate authority infrastructure was not the system that was accessed. Reporting from GBHackers and BleepingComputer covers the confirmation, and Mozilla's Bugzilla references the incident as well, so browser and certificate communities are tracking the disclosure. Attackers holding contact details and identity documents can run convincing phishing or social-engineering campaigns against DigiCert customers.

DigiCert customers should check whether their identity documents were among the exposed data and expect follow-up notices from DigiCert. Watch for phishing messages that use stolen business contact details to appear legitimate, and monitor for further disclosure about which internal system was exposed and how the misconfiguration was introduced.
