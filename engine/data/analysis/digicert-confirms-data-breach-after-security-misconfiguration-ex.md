DigiCert confirmed unauthorized access to an internal business system holding archived customer information, caused by a security misconfiguration that exposed the application to the internet. The exposed data included names, business email addresses, phone numbers, and business addresses; for some affected people it also included identity documents such as driver's licenses, passports, and utility bills. DigiCert said the breach touched only a small portion of its archived customer data and that its certificate authority services, CertCentral, DigiCert ONE, customer login credentials, and issued certificates were not affected.

DigiCert has begun sending breach notifications to affected customers and is still reviewing identification documents to determine the full scope. Cloud architect Fabian Bader shared a copy of the notification on X, describing the leak as limited to his full PII. A Mozilla Bugzilla incident report traces the exposure to a February 2025 server migration that mistakenly put an internal business system on the internet.

Affected customers should expect breach notifications and treat exposed identity documents as a fraud risk. The main thing to watch is DigiCert's completed review of which customers were affected. No CA compromise or certificate impact was reported.
