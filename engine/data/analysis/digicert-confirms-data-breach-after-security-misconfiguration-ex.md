DigiCert confirmed that a security misconfiguration exposed an internal business system to the internet, allowing unauthorized access to archived customer information. Exposed data included names, business email addresses, phone numbers, and business addresses, and for some individuals also personal identification documents such as driver's licenses, passports, and utility bills. DigiCert said certificate authority services, including CertCentral and DigiCert ONE, customer login credentials, and issued certificates were not affected, and it has begun sending breach notifications to affected customers.

A Mozilla Bugzilla incident report places the exposed archive access at June 27 through July 2 after a server migration exposed a business system, while another report describes a February 2025 server migration as the root cause. The discrepancy in dates is unresolved in the material reviewed. Affected customers should expect breach notifications and treat exposed contact and identity-document data as usable for phishing or social engineering.

DigiCert said it was still reviewing identification documents to determine exactly who was affected and would notify them once the review is complete. DigiCert's guidance to customers is to confirm whether their organization received a breach notification and to enforce MFA on DigiCert accounts; until the review finishes, treat any unexpected message referencing the incident as a potential phishing pretext and verify through DigiCert's own channels.
