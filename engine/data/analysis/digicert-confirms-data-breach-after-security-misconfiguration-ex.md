DigiCert confirmed that a security misconfiguration exposed an internal business system holding archived customer information to the internet, resulting in unauthorized access. The exposed data covered names, business email addresses, phone numbers, and business addresses; for some individuals, records also included personal identification documents such as driver's licenses, passports, and utility bills. DigiCert said its certificate authority services, including CertCentral and DigiCert ONE, plus customer login credentials and issued certificates, were not affected.

According to an incident report on Mozilla's Bugzilla platform, the exposure stemmed from a server migration in February 2025 that mistakenly placed the internal business system on the internet. DigiCert said only a small portion of its archived customer data was affected and that it was still reviewing identification documents to determine the full scope. It has begun sending breach notifications to affected customers, and cloud architect Fabian Bader shared a copy of the notification on X.

What to watch: the Bugzilla review may expand the affected-customer count, and exposed identity documents raise follow-on fraud and social-engineering risk against DigiCert's customer base. The case shows how migration-driven exposure of archived systems can go undetected for a long time; customers who received notifications should treat verification-document requests with suspicion until DigiCert completes its review.
