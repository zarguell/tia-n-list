OpsShield disclosed a supply-chain breach of the cPGuard update channel: an unidentified attacker used compromised credentials to access the company's update distribution infrastructure on November 2, 2026 at 02:06 UTC and pushed malicious cPGuard versions 5.88.12 through 5.88.19 to customers. The malicious updates installed a backdoor and ran as root on Linux servers that polled the update infrastructure during the compromise window; reporting puts the scale at about 2,950 affected Linux servers, with stolen data including root credentials such as /etc/shadow uploaded to OpsShield's licensing server.

OpsShield said it first detected the intrusion after the attacker registered a free trial account and studied how cPGuard talks to the licensing system, then obtained command execution on the billing and licensing server and uploaded a fake update using a stolen access key. The vendor detected the compromise and stopped distribution within 12 hours, though the fake updates were live for roughly 70 minutes and not every customer polled during the window. cPGuard is a Linux web security suite used alongside hosting panels such as cPanel, DirectAdmin, and Plesk, and Namecheap offers it on VPS and dedicated servers, so the blast radius spans hosting infrastructure rather than end-user workstations.

The vendor released cPGuard 5.88.25 to remove the backdoor and files left by the script, and revoked and cleared the compromised licensing account and infrastructure credentials. Customers should verify they are not running versions 5.88.12 to 5.88.19, upgrade to 5.88.25, and inspect affected servers for unauthorized root shells and credential access. Watch for follow-on reporting on how many servers actually executed the script and whether stolen licensing data surfaces in further attacks.
