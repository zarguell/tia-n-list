The FBI's FLASH advisory (published Friday, November 13, 2026) warns that multiple threat actors have weaponized ARTEX, an open-source, AI-driven penetration testing framework, to automate intrusions against organizations worldwide. The agency reports the framework has been in active criminal use since at least August 2026, hitting financial, government, technology, defense, and healthcare targets. ARTEX is written in Go and orchestrates AI agents to handle reconnaissance, vulnerability discovery, exploitation, privilege escalation, and data theft with minimal human input.

The framework does not ship its own model. It plugs into external large language models including Anthropic Claude, OpenAI GPT, DeepSeek, GLM, Grok, and Kimi, converting an operator's stated goals into commands executed by specialized agents. The FBI says this collapses the intrusion timeline, with some attacks moving from initial targeting to privileged access in under three hours, and lets low-skill actors run complex, multi-target operations simultaneously. The advisory ties ARTEX to the ConnectWise supply-chain breach that compromised Microsoft 365 customer environments.

Independent reporting extends the picture. GMO Cybersecurity by Ierae found ARTEX, described as a China-origin tool, on attack servers aimed at roughly 20 Japanese companies, giving a concrete victim count and a regional footprint beyond the US advisory. What to watch: whether cloud and identity defenders get usable detection guidance, since the advisory frames cloud compromises as leaving defenders only hours to react, and whether more vendors confirm ARTEX-attributed intrusions as the tool's operator kit spreads.
