Coles says a cyber security incident in its IT environment is under investigation, with stores and online outlets operating normally and no evidence so far that customer, team or supplier data was compromised or removed. The company employs more than 115,000 people, runs over 1,800 outlets and has more than nine million active Flybuys members; it has brought in global security experts and is working with the Australian Cyber Security Centre. Liquorland click-and-collect is failing for a small number of customers, but Coles has not linked that to the incident. Woolworths acknowledged its own issue within hours: WISH eGift card balances drained, with Nine reporting at 19:32 UTC on 15 November that dozens of customers found cards already redeemed and funds spent at Dan Murphy's and Big W, often interstate.

These are two different harm models arriving together, and folding them into one breach would be as wrong as ignoring the coincidence. Coles puts a potential confidentiality problem across systems holding team, supplier and loyalty records at national scale, while the Woolworths side is money already gone from customer wallets: one shopper bought a $500 WISH card, spent about $130 in Melbourne and lost the remaining $367 in New South Wales three days later. The reason this outranks a routine advisory is that the no-data-taken language is being used before scope is known, while a loyalty base above nine million and an active redemption fraud channel are live at competing chains within hours.

There is no patch, hotfix, CVE, actor name or published IoC to act on, so the work is containment and evidence preservation. Hold changes to identity, loyalty and gift-card platforms until Coles can name the systems it isolated; restrict privileged and third-party access to those planes to known accounts and networks, and keep Flybuys, Coles Plus, Coles Plus Delivery, QuiteLike and Liquorland click-and-collect records under retention. On the Woolworths side, hunt WISH redemptions with a short purchase-to-spend gap, especially interstate Dan Murphy's and Big W charges, cards bought through Qantas Marketplace and accounts where the card is already marked redeemed; route those customers to giftcard@customerservice.woolworths.com.au for freeze and replacement.

The assessment changes if Coles withdraws the no-evidence finding, if notifications go to staff, suppliers or Flybuys members, or if Liquorland click-and-collect faults and WISH redemptions trace back to a shared provider. Confirmation of a common managed service or payments processor would turn two simultaneous retail disclosures into one supply-chain incident; a clean forensic result and restored click-and-collect would lower the urgency.
