Coles Group is investigating what it calls a cyber security incident. The retailer employs more than 115,000 people, runs more than 1,800 outlets and operates Flybuys with over nine million active members. Coles says stores and online outlets are unaffected, and that Flybuys accounts, Coles Plus, Coles Plus Delivery and QuiteLike subscriptions are still working. It has brought in external cyber security experts and is working with the Australian Cyber Security Centre and regulators.

The company said on 16 November it had found no evidence that customer, team or supplier data was compromised or removed. That finding is early and limited to what has been checked so far. ABC reported Coles telling customers to remain vigilant, while the company acknowledged Liquorland click-and-collect problems for a small number of customers without linking them to the incident. Woolworths disclosed its own cyber incident within hours of Coles, and the first reporting raised the question of a shared provider, but no common cause has been confirmed.

Watch for a reversal of the no-data-taken assessment, notifications to affected employees or suppliers, and any attribution of the Liquorland disruption. Scope and impact remain undisclosed, so the current statement should not be read as final.
