Coles disclosed a "cyber security incident" on November 16 while saying that stores, the website and app, Flybuys and gift cards remained usable. The company said it had found no evidence that customer, team or supplier data had been compromised or removed, and that it was working with the Australian Cyber Security Centre and global cyber security experts to contain and secure its IT environment. It acknowledged Liquorland click-and-collect issues for a small number of customers, without giving a detection time, attack method or affected system. Coles operates more than 1,800 outlets, employs over 115,000 people and runs Flybuys with more than nine million active members.

Woolworths disclosed a separate problem on the same day: unauthorized transactions against digital WISH eGift cards. Nine reported dozens of customers whose card balances were drained, with purchases often at Dan Murphy's or Big W in another state. One shopper bought a $500 card, spent about $130 in Melbourne and found the remaining $367 spent in New South Wales three days later. The Daily Mail reported similar interstate drains, including cards bought through third-party platforms such as Qantas Marketplace. Woolworths said it was looking into the matter as a priority and that replacement cards were among the next steps.

The two disclosures landed within hours of each other, which prompted a social post asking whether the competitors shared an MSP. No event in the store confirms a shared cause, and Coles said it had no evidence of data theft. Earlier reporting from June 2026 also documented physical gift card tampering at both retailers: a Coles customer reported being $2,000 out of pocket over four $500 cards with scratched redemption details, while Woolworths said a very small number of cards were affected. What to watch is whether either company links the gift card fraud to the Coles incident, and whether any customer data exposure is later confirmed.
