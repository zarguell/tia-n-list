Coles disclosed a cyber security incident on 16 November 2026 while saying that stores, the website and app, Flybuys and gift cards remained usable. It said it had found no evidence that customer, team or supplier data was compromised or removed, acknowledged Liquorland click-and-collect problems for a small number of customers, and gave no detection time, attack method or affected system. The company is working with the Australian Cyber Security Centre and global cyber security experts. Coles runs more than 1,800 outlets, employs over 115,000 people and operates Flybuys with more than nine million active members.

Woolworths disclosed a separate problem the same day: unauthorized transactions against digital WISH eGift cards. Nine reported dozens of customers with balances drained, often through purchases at Dan Murphy's or Big W in another state. One shopper bought a $500 card, spent about $130 in Melbourne and found the remaining $367 spent in New South Wales three days later. The Daily Mail reported similar interstate drains, including cards bought through Qantas Marketplace. Woolworths said it was looking into the matter as a priority and that replacement cards were among the next steps.

The two disclosures landed within hours, which prompted a social post asking whether the competitors shared an MSP. Nothing in the collected reporting confirms a shared cause, and a common incident remains speculation. Earlier reporting from June 2026 also documented physical gift card tampering at both retailers, including a Coles customer $2,000 out of pocket over four $500 cards with scratched redemption details and a Woolworths statement that a very small number of cards were affected. Watch for a Coles update on scope, any later confirmation of customer data exposure, and whether Woolworths links the WISH card fraud to a broader intrusion.
