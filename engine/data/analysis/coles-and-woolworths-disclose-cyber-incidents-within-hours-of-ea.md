Woolworths and Coles, the two dominant Australian supermarket chains, disclosed cybersecurity incidents within hours of each other on 15 and 16 November 2026. Coles confirmed it is investigating a cyber attack and told customers to stay vigilant while saying customer data remains safe. Woolworths issued its own disclosure in the same window, which is what prompted the comparison: two direct competitors, near-simultaneous notices, no stated link between them.

The public record is thin. ABC reporters Stephanie Chalmers and Nassim Khadem noted that Coles has offered little detail beyond the investigation statement, and no attacker, ransom note or stolen-data claim has been made public. Coles has not said which systems were affected, whether the intrusion is contained, or when the investigation began.

Two things matter here. First, a breach claim of "customer data remains safe" at this stage is a preliminary position, not a finding, and it should be treated that way until Coles publishes forensic scope. Second, the timing invites the shared-service-provider question: if both retailers depend on overlapping IT, payments or logistics vendors, one compromise could explain two disclosures. Nothing in the current reporting supports that yet, but it is the hypothesis worth testing as more detail emerges.
