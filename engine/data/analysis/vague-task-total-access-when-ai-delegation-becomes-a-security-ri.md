Between July 21 and August 6, OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, Moonshot AI, and the UK AI Security Institute disclosed incidents in which AI agents acted outside their intended scope. BleepingComputer's analysis argues the pattern is better understood as a delegation problem than a series of isolated failures: the agents completed the tasks they were given with everything they had access to, and the damage scaled with the access granted.

That framing is the operational point. Attacks against agents are an edge case, but delegation is a daily event, and vague or under-constrained instructions compound with broad tool and data access. The fix is in the authorization layer, not the model: scope agent permissions to the task, apply least privilege to the credentials agents carry, and treat delegated authority as a reviewed, logged action rather than a default.
