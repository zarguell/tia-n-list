ShinyHunters claimed on September 22, 2026 that it breached FBI systems, telling 404 Media it holds data on all FBI employees and applicants and shipping a sample of roughly 5,000 records with names, home addresses, phone numbers, and spouse details. The group claims 2 to 3 terabytes in total, taken through a second, still unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft zero-day RCE, with lateral movement into FBI-managed AWS GovCloud and access to Criminal Justice, HR, and Medlink services, and it defaced apply.fbijobs.gov with the message "THIS SITE HAS BEEN SEIZED BY SHINYHUNTERS. rooting your systems since '19". The FBI confirmed it is investigating unauthorized activity affecting FBIjobs.gov and pulled the job portal to a maintenance page, without confirming that data left its networks. The motive is coercive rather than financial: one week to retract IC3 PSA I-051526-PSA, the May 15, 2026 advisory that accused the group of exaggerated access claims, harassment of victims' relatives, swatting, and fabricated compromising material.

The demand structure matters more than the volume. This breaks the group's own playbook: no ransom, no leak-site countdown, a political demand addressed by name to Director Kash Patel and Cyber Division Assistant Director Brett Leatherman, which means the usual negotiation and takedown calculus does not apply. Even if the reported figure of roughly 38,000 staff and applicants is inflated, the verified 5,000-record subset reportedly includes hundreds of intelligence analysts covering Russia, China, Hezbollah, and cartels, plus members of the Remote Operations Unit, the FBI's secretive exploit-development team. A name, home address, and family map of those people is a targeting package for foreign intelligence services and for the harassment ecosystems the FBI itself catalogued in the May PSA, and criminals in this scene have already used stolen telecom data to track and intimidate agents investigating them. That is why this story outranks the other thirty from today: it converts a personnel-data breach into a standing counterintelligence exposure that does not expire when the news cycle does.

PeopleSoft operators should treat the June campaign as precedent for the current claim. CVE-2026-35273, a CVSS 9.8 RCE in the Environment Management component, was exploited as a zero-day from May 27 to June 9, 2026 before Oracle's June 10 advisory, and Mandiant notified over 100 organizations, 68 percent of them higher education. Apply Oracle's CVE-2026-35273 patch, disable the EMHub service or remove PSEMHUB in single-server deployments, and block external access to /PSEMHUB/* and /PSIGW/HttpListeningConnector at the perimeter. Hunt WebLogic PIA access logs for POST /PSEMHUB/hub from external source IPs, outbound TCP 445 from PeopleSoft hosts, rogue .jsp files under PSEMHUB.war, modified .xml under envmetadata that could trigger XMLDecoder execution on restart, and the campaign artifacts README-IF-YOU-SEE-THIS-YOUVE-BEEN-HACKED.TXT and fanout.sh. Mandiant's published indicators cover staging hosts 142.11.200.186 through 142.11.200.190 and the C2 wss://azurenetfiles.net:443/agent.ashx. ShinyHunters says it is already reusing the alleged new bug against Fortune 500 targets, so patching and perimeter blocks cannot wait for Oracle to confirm a second vulnerability.

Two developments would move this assessment. Independent confirmation from Oracle, Mandiant, or the FBI that a second PeopleSoft zero-day exists would turn an unverified claim into an active federal-network exploitation case with a live unpatched vector. The one-week retraction deadline set on September 22 lands around September 29, and what happens there is diagnostic: a data release would suggest GovCloud-resident records, a quiet deadline extension or edited PSA would suggest the group holds little more than the portal export it already sampled.
