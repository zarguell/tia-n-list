WorkOS, a developer infrastructure provider used by at least 3,000 companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, Cursor, Perplexity, Snowflake, Plaid, Webglow, and Netlify, disclosed unauthorized access to an internal sales application detected on October 30, 2026. The intruder used an AI-powered offensive security framework called ARTEX to automate reconnaissance and exploitation, reaching business contact records and summaries of customer communications held in the sales tool. WorkOS says no app data or production services were accessed or affected, and it disabled external access to the affected application, reviewed internal access controls, and engaged an independent forensics firm.

The exposed material is CRM and support correspondence rather than production identity data, but it is still useful for targeted phishing and social engineering against WorkOS customers and their users. The company is notifying affected customers directly and has warned users to stay alert for phishing leveraging the exposed information.

The notable element is the use of an autonomous AI exploit framework against a vendor's internal tooling, which lowers the cost of reconnaissance and initial access. Watch for follow-on phishing campaigns referencing WorkOS, and for whether the forensics review changes the scope of exposed data beyond business contact records and communication summaries.
