The breach targets Educational Testing Service (ETS), a nonprofit that operates California's CAASPP and ELPAC assessment programs under contract with the California Department of Education (CDE). An unauthorized party used a compromised login credential to access an ETS secure file transfer protocol (SFTP) site between May 22 and September 18, 2026. ETS noticed unauthorized activity on September 25, but the earliest access dates back to late May. The CDE breach notice states files containing student information were copied, covering all students enrolled in the previous three school years plus former students whose transfers were not confirmed as far back as 2006.

The exposed data includes student names, Statewide Student Identifiers (SSIDs), dates of birth, home addresses, demographic information, disability and program participation details, and statewide assessment scores including test results. CDE enrollment figures show more than 5.7 million students in California public schools for 2025-2026. The impacted population is not limited to current enrollees, and not every affected student had every data category exposed. CDE is distributing the ETS breach notice and has provided an incident FAQ with available resources.

This is a real, large-scale education-sector breach tied to a state assessment contractor, not a vendor marketing claim or generic guide. The timeline spans roughly four months before detection, and the access method was a single compromised login rather than a software vulnerability. The story has been covered by multiple outlets including securityweek, malware.news, and the LA Times, with CDE and ETS confirming the incident details. Watch for any update on victim counts, regulatory notifications, or whether the compromised credential was reused across other ETS clients.
