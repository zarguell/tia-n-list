A hacktivist group calling itself the Department of Peace released internal data on more than 41,000 Department of Homeland Security employees and contractors through a transparency site, with Distributed Denial of Secrets hosting the dataset. The data includes staff in covert, undercover, and surveillance roles, plus leadership from CISA and ICE, and dates back to at least 2023. CyberScoop verified current CISA employees in the dataset, and Reuters, NBC News, Wired, and Politico covered the disclosure. The group linked the breach to DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin's July 17th statement about turning up enforcement operations.

The exposed database covers nearly a third of ICE's workforce and includes group names tied to COVID-19 planning and internal summits. Multiple outlets reported DHS acknowledged the potential breach and said it is actively working to address it. The same group previously published DHS contract records through DDoSecrets, covering over 6,000 entities including Anduril, Palantir, Microsoft, and Raytheon. No CVEs or technical exploits are tied to this event; it is a data-exfiltration and leak claim rather than a vulnerability.

Watch for official DHS confirmation of scope, any CISA KEV or advisory tied to the identity service involved (reportedly an Okta single sign-on), and whether the Department of Peace releases additional internal datasets. The leak's simplicity makes it hard to fully verify chain of custody, but the breadth of verified employee records makes it a substantive breach claim worth tracking.
