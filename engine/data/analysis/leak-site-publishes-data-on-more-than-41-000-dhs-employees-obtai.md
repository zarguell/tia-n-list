A hacktivist outfit calling itself the Department of Peace claims to have breached an identity service used by the Department of Homeland Security and handed the resulting data to the transparency nonprofit Distributed Denial of Secrets, which published a searchable dataset on October 28, 2026 covering more than 41,000 DHS employees and contractors. The material reportedly includes undercover ICE agents, surveillance staff, CISA employees, and personnel tied to deportation programs, with records stretching back years to COVID-19 planning and internal summits in 2023. The group framed the leak as retaliation for DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin's promise of stepped-up ICE operations. DHS has acknowledged a potential breach and says it is actively working to address it.

The exposure of covert and undercover personnel is the sharp edge here: DDoSecrets founder Emma Best described the leak as identifying officials responsible for covert operations, the government's hunt for leakers, and the BlueLeaks investigation, alongside key staff in rendition and deportation programs. CyberScoop verified that numerous current CISA employees appear in the data, along with some former leaders who left years ago, but also noted that the origin, chain of custody, and verification of the underlying claim remain unclear. The same group earlier distributed a separate "DHS Contracts" dataset through DDoSecrets covering more than 6,000 entities, including defense and technology vendors such as Anduril, L3Harris, Raytheon, Palantir, Microsoft, and Oracle. Coverage spanned WIRED, NBC News, Reuters, Politico, CNBC, and Rappler, and Bluesky blocked links to the leak.

What to watch: whether the Okta single sign-on compromise claim is independently confirmed, since that is the load-bearing assertion and so far rests on the hacktivists' word. Also watch for operational fallout for exposed undercover staff, doxxing or targeting of named individuals, and any legal pressure on DDoSecrets or hosting platforms. The earlier contracts dataset suggests this group has sustained access to DHS-adjacent systems, not a one-off scrape.
