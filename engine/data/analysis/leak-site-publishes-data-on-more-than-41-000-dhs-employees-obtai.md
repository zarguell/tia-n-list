A hacktivist group calling itself the Department of Peace published a leak site containing data on more than 41,000 Department of Homeland Security employees and contractors, including undercover ICE agents and CISA staff. The group said it obtained the database by breaching an identity service used by the agency, specifically an Okta single sign-on system. Distributed Denial of Secrets and the hacker maia arson crimew received and published the data.

The leaked records span years and include references to COVID-19 planning and internal summits from 2023. CyberScoop verified that numerous current CISA employees and some former leadership members appear in the data. DDoSecrets founder Emma Best described the leak as identifying officials responsible for overseeing covert and undercover operations, the government's hunt for leakers, and the failed BlueLeaks investigation, plus key personnel in DHS deportation programs.

The hacktivists framed the breach as a response to DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin's promise of stepped-up ICE operations. The exposure of undercover and sensitive personnel identifiers raises physical-security and operational risks for the named individuals and their agencies.
