A leak site published data on more than 41,000 Department of Homeland Security employees and contractors, obtained by the hacktivist group Department of Peace and distributed through Distributed Denial of Secrets. The dataset includes names tied to CISA, ICE, and other DHS components, with references to undercover roles, surveillance programs, and internal summits going back years. DHS acknowledged a potential breach and said it is actively working to address it. The leak was described by the hacktivists as a response to stepped-up ICE operations promised by DHS leadership.

The data comes from an identity service used by the agency, with verification from CyberScoop confirming current CISA staff and former leadership listed. The same group previously distributed DHS contract records covering over 6,000 entities, including defense contractors and technology vendors. The event reinforces the risk of centralized identity and HR systems being targeted for both espionage and activist exposure. Organizations with similar centralized access should review access controls and data exposure limits for employee directories.
