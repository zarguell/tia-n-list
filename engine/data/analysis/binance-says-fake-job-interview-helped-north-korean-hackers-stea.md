Binance attributes the theft of $6.3 billion from its digital wallets to a rigged recruitment coding task that compromised a software engineer's work computer, combined with abuse of an AI tool for initial access. The FBI attributed the October 29 theft to North Korea and asked cryptocurrency firms to block transactions involving the stolen funds; the DPRK denied involvement through state media. Binance traced the campaign back to June, when a team member downloaded a tampered software installer while setting up an AI coding tool.

The intruders were caught after the theft, but the campaign's dwell time of months shows how a single compromised developer endpoint can cascade into a multi-billion-dollar loss. The case underscores the risk of unsolicited coding assignments and unverified AI tooling in engineering workflows.

Cryptocurrency firms and other high-value targets should restrict developer tooling to approved sources, segment build environments and monitor for unusual outbound connections from engineering workstations.
