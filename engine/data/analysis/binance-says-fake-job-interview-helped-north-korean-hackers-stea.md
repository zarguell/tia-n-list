Binance disclosed on November 3, 2026 that North Korean state-sponsored hackers (TraderTraitor, also tracked as Lazarus Group, Jade Sleet, and APT38) stole roughly $6.3 to $6.4 billion from its hot wallets across about ten blockchains on October 29, 2026, in the largest cryptocurrency theft on record. The attackers moved most of the funds within about 20 minutes, using an EIP-7702 delegation to make Binance's own withdrawal infrastructure sign and send transactions to attacker-controlled addresses. The FBI attributed the theft to North Korea and asked cryptocurrency firms to block transactions involving the stolen funds, while North Korea denied involvement.

The intrusion was a months-long campaign, not a single breach. It started in June 2026 when a Wallet DevOps engineer downloaded a tampered Homebrew installer while searching for a popular AI coding tool; Binance detected it, replaced his device, and reset credentials, but the attackers had already learned about the wallet infrastructure team. In September a second engineer was lured through a fake job-hunting platform by an impersonated project director, put through two interviews with fake HR and a fake CEO, and tricked into downloading a coding assignment that delivered malware. In total three engineers' computers were compromised. The attackers then reached Binance's internal wallet signing service and issued unauthorized withdrawals, with Binance confirming at 00:10 UTC on October 30 that a new authorization from the Binance14 hot wallet cleared the EIP-7702 delegation code. Binance says it found no evidence of leaked wallet private keys and no individual employee is blamed.

Why it matters and what to watch: about $2.8 to $3 billion of the stolen assets were frozen or intercepted by stablecoin issuers and the Arbitrum Security Council, leaving roughly $2.4 billion under attacker control, and Binance pledged to reimburse users from company funds and its SAFU reserve. The case shows developer laptops and fake recruitment as an entry path into financial infrastructure. Watch for further Binance post-mortem details, additional frozen funds, and whether other exchanges or wallet providers see similar EIP-7702 delegation abuse.
