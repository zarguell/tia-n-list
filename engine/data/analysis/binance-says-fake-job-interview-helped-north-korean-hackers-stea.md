Binance disclosed that North Korean hackers stole approximately $6.34 billion in digital assets on October 29, 2026, by compromising the exchange's wallet infrastructure. The FBI attributed the theft to North Korean state-sponsored hackers known as TraderTraitor, also tracked as Lazarus Group, Jade Sleet, and APT38; the DPRK denied involvement. Binance said the intrusion began with a social-engineering campaign in September: an attacker posing as a Project Director at a fictitious company contacted a software engineer through a legitimate job-hunting platform, then held interviews with fake HR and CEO personas, including a live video call with a social media profile to build trust.

During the second interview, conducted over a social chat application, the employee was instructed to download a coding assignment that installed malware on his work computer. Binance also traced the campaign to June, when a team member downloaded a tampered software installer while setting up an AI coding tool. The compromised endpoint gave attackers a foothold that ultimately reached Binance's internal wallet signing service, enabling unauthorized blockchain transactions that bypassed normal withdrawal controls.

The FBI has asked cryptocurrency firms to block transactions involving the stolen funds. The case highlights how fake recruitment flows and malicious developer tooling can bridge into high-value financial infrastructure, and how a single compromised engineer endpoint can escalate to billions in unauthorized transfers.
