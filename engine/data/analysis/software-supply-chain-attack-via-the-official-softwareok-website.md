AhnLab's ASEC confirmed that installers for Q-Dir and DesktopOK distributed through SoftwareOK's official website were tampered with. The malicious files were produced both by direct downloads and by the update process for previously installed programs, while the utilities continued to appear functional. A summary of the findings identifies Q-Dir 12.83 through 12.85 and DesktopOK 12.82 through 12.85 as affected ranges.

The payload was a Python-based remote access trojan. Scoop maintainers recorded SHA-256 mismatches for the Q-Dir 12.84 portable archive on November 13 and 15, then reported a trojanized, unsigned executable. Their incident report described dsv2_py39_agent.7z installed under C:\ProgramData\Microsoft\DeviceSync\b2, persistence, remote shell, file transfer, screenshots and tunneling, with TCP traffic to 8.210.69.51:9876. Those indicators tie the delivery problem to observable endpoint activity rather than a generic warning about corrupted downloads.

Compromise of the vendor's own distribution and update paths breaks a core trust assumption: users can be infected while installing or updating software from the expected source. Watch for SoftwareOK's incident statement, the exact tampering window, clean replacement hashes and evidence of additional victims. Administrators should check Q-Dir and DesktopOK installations in the stated ranges, hunt for the device-sync directory and network indicator, and treat affected hosts as remotely controlled until cleaned and rebuilt.
