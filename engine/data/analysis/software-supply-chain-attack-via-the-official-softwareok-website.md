AhnLab's ASEC confirmed that Q-Dir and DesktopOK installers distributed through SoftwareOK's official website were tampered with. Malicious files were produced both by direct downloads and by the update process for previously installed programs, while the utilities continued to appear functional. Reported affected builds include Q-Dir 12.83 through 12.85 and DesktopOK 12.82 and 12.85.

Scoop Extras recorded SHA-256 mismatches for the unversioned Q-Dir 12.84 portable archive on November 13 and in a later report, with the expected and served hashes reversed between checks. Its user report described dsv2_py39_agent.7z installed under C:\ProgramData\Microsoft\DeviceSync\b2, persistence, remote shell, file transfer, screenshots and tunneling, with TCP traffic to 8.210.69.51:9876. Those artifacts give defenders concrete endpoint and network checks for the delivery chain.

BleepingComputer reports that the trojanized installers were digitally signed with a certificate issued to legitimate developer Nenad Hrg. The vendor's own download and update paths were the delivery mechanism, not third-party mirrors or advertisements. A valid signature alone therefore does not establish that a SoftwareOK package is clean.

Administrators should inventory the affected builds, compare downloads with known-good hashes and hunt for the DeviceSync directory and network indicator. Treat hosts that ran a suspect installer or update as remotely controlled until cleaned and rebuilt. Watch for SoftwareOK's incident statement, the exact tampering window, clean replacement packages and evidence of additional victims.
