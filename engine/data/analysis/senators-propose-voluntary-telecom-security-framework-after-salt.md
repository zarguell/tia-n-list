Sens. Mark Warner (D-Va., Senate Intelligence vice chair) and Ted Cruz (R-Texas, Senate Commerce chair) introduced the Telecommunications Cybersecurity and Resilience Act. The bill creates a voluntary framework for telecom security practices, developed jointly by government and industry, with an independent assessment process. It responds to the Salt Typhoon espionage campaign, in which Chinese hackers compromised systems handling lawful surveillance requests, targeting communications providers and political figures globally. The FBI reports the campaign spread to over 80 countries.

The event cites no CVEs or specific versions, but the timeline is concrete: the bill renews efforts nearly two years after Salt Typhoon became public. The shift from mandatory FCC rules to voluntary third-party certification is the central policy change. Watch whether telecom operators adopt the voluntary assessments or resist them, and whether the framework gains bipartisan support beyond Warner and Cruz.
