Security researchers physically dismantled a Flock Safety surveillance camera and published their findings on Hackaday. Flock cameras are automated license plate readers deployed by law enforcement and private entities across the US. The teardown revealed how the system actually works under the hood, including its data collection and transmission architecture.

Wired independently covered the same story, reporting that hackers got inside a Flock camera and extracted data showing how the system operates in practice. The combined coverage from Hackaday, Wired, and fediverse security discussions indicates significant interest in the security posture of these widely deployed surveillance devices. Three sources picked up the story within hours.

Flock Safety cameras are proliferating in municipalities and neighborhoods as part of automated policing programs. The fact that researchers could extract operational data from a physical device raises questions about device hardening, data-at-rest protections, and whether the cameras could be tampered with or redirected by someone with physical access. This matters for anyone relying on Flock deployments for security or living in areas where these cameras operate.
