Ahold Delhaize estimated that its October cyberattack will wipe 200 million to 250 million euros, about 224 million to 280 million dollars, from fourth-quarter net sales. The Dutch-Belgian grocery giant published the figure with its third-quarter results. The attack hit after the quarter closed and disrupted operations in the US and the Benelux. Its brands include Albert Heijn, Delhaize, Food Lion and Hannaford.

The company also expects 75 million to 100 million euros, roughly 84 million to 112 million dollars, of pressure on fourth-quarter underlying and IFRS operating income, on top of incident response and remediation expenses. It revised its 2026 guidance downward, now projecting diluted underlying earnings per share growth in the low single digits instead of the mid to high single digits previously forecast, and free cash flow of about 2.1 billion euros versus at least 2.3 billion euros before. Ahold Delhaize first reported the incident in mid-October after disconnecting some systems, and confirmed on October 19 that it had been hit by a cyberattack.
