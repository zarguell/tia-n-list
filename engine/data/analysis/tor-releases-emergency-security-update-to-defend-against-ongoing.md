Tor 0.4.9.17 is an emergency release answering an ongoing DDoS attack that has overwhelmed relays across the network. Developer David Goulet said relays are being flooded with client circuits and singled out a fix in relay-side IPv6 handling as especially important for DoS defenses. Relay operators were told to upgrade immediately, with no further action required; the project said more defenses are being evaluated and additional releases should follow.

The release on November 13 followed 0.4.9.15 on October 28, which added three DoS protections against what the project called a thunderstorm of directory requests. Goulet said a single relay could receive hundreds of circuits per second at that stage. Version 0.4.9.16 followed on November 5 as a directory-authority fix for older clients stuck in restart loops repeatedly fetching directory information.

Availability, not compromise, is the concern here. Reports after 0.4.9.17 say the disruption has not ended: one operator reported two relays losing HSDir status before the update, and another remained affected. Watch for further emergency versions, recovery of HSDir and other relay flags, and publication of the ticket details withheld from the earlier 0.4.9.14 security release.
