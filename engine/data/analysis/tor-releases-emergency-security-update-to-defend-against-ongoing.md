Tor 0.4.9.17 is an emergency release issued in response to an ongoing DDoS attack that has overwhelmed relays across the network. Developer David Goulet said relays are being flooded with client circuits and called one IPv6-related fix especially important for relay DoS protections. The project asked relay operators to upgrade immediately and said more defenses are being evaluated, with further releases expected. Reports after the November 13 release indicate the disruption has not ended; one operator said two relays lost HSDir status before the update and another remained affected.

The release follows a fast sequence. Tor 0.4.9.14 addressed high-severity issues in clients, onion services, directory authorities and relays, with ticket details withheld under project policy. Version 0.4.9.15 on October 28 added three DoS defenses against a thunderstorm of directory requests; Goulet said a single relay could see hundreds of circuits per second. Version 0.4.9.16 on November 5 was a directory-authority-only fix for older clients stuck in restart loops repeatedly fetching directory information, which overloaded fallback directories and authorities.

The operational concern is network availability rather than a bug being exploited for compromise. Relay operators are the main upgrade population, and the 0.4.9.17 changes specifically touch relay-side DoS handling and IPv6. Watch for subsequent releases, whether HSDir and other flags recover, and whether the project eventually publishes the withheld ticket details from the earlier 0.4.9.14 fixes.
