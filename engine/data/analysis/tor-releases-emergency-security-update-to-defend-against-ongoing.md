The Tor Project released Tor 0.4.9.17 on 13 November as a rapid response to the distributed denial-of-service attack that has affected most relay operators. The release fixes several security issues, and the project singled out one concerning how relays' built-in DoS protections deal with IPv6. Developer David Goulet described the build as a response to relays being overwhelmed by a massive flood of client circuits and said more defenses are under consideration with new versions to follow shortly. Relay operators were urged to update without delay.

This is the third build in a fast sequence. Tor 0.4.9.15 introduced three DoS protections against what the project called a thunderstorm of directory requests, after which Goulet warned operators that a single relay could be hit with hundreds of circuits per second and would stay under heavy load for as long as the attack lasted. Version 0.4.9.16 followed on 5 November. Operator reports suggest 0.4.9.17 has not ended the disruption: one operator posted on 14 November that two of its relays had lost HSDir status to attacks before the update and that another was still struggling.

For relay operators the action is the upgrade itself, followed by confirming the running build, since a relay still on 0.4.9.16 remains exposed to the circuit flood. The visible casualties are directory authorities and relays carrying the HSDir flag, so a drop in consensus weight or a lost HSDir flag is the symptom to track rather than relay CPU alone. Watch for the next release in the series and for whether the IPv6 DoS handling fix holds under a sustained flood, given that the project has shipped three times in two weeks without the attack subsiding.
