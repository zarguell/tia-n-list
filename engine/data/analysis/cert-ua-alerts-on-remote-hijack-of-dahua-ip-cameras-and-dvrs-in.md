CERT-UA warned that attackers are remotely hijacking Dahua IP cameras and DVRs in Ukraine by abusing the Easy4IP peer-to-peer access feature and authentication weaknesses. The attackers obtain access through P2P serial numbers, which follow a predictable pattern, then pull live streams and manipulate settings on the devices. The campaign targets infrastructure monitoring and physical security cameras, turning consumer and small-business surveillance gear into an intelligence collection platform.

The hijacking relies on the cameras' own convenience features rather than a single patched vulnerability, which makes remediation harder than a routine firmware update. Device owners may not realize P2P access is enabled or that serial numbers are guessable.

Owners of Dahua cameras and DVRs should disable Easy4IP and P2P access where not needed, change default credentials, segment surveillance devices from production networks, and update firmware to the latest release. Organizations in Ukraine and neighboring regions should treat exposed cameras as potential surveillance assets for adversaries.
