Boston Scientific is notifying individuals after a voice phishing intrusion gave a hacker access to its cloud infrastructure. The exposure of cloud-based applications and cloud-hosted data began May 7. The company learned of the unauthorized access on May 12 and determined on November 5 that a breach had occurred. It filed a sample notification with the California Attorney General on November 25, with notices going to affected individuals in multiple U.S. states.

The Texas attorney general's breach portal puts the affected population at 117,789 people. The exposed material included personal details and medical information. Boston Scientific says patient medical records and data from remote monitoring systems, connected medical devices, and clinical case management or workflow applications were not exposed. It also reports no disruption to manufacturing, ordering, distribution or service to patients. Those exclusions narrow the operational and clinical impact, but do not remove the privacy consequences of exposed cloud data.

Watch for the complete exposure end date, the final affected count and any changes to the disclosed data categories as notifications and regulatory filings develop. The key distinction is between the exposed cloud information and the medical-record and device systems the company says remained outside the breach.
