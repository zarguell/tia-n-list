Microsoft's September 25, 2026 report details the Azure operations of Storm-3168, the actor Microsoft tracks for the JADEPUFFER activity Sysdig discovered in July 2026 and described as the first documented agentic ransomware operation. In one compromised tenant, two service principals drove the whole operation. One spent roughly 15 hours 30 minutes performing more than 300 successful read operations, enumerating virtual machines, subscriptions, and resource groups. The second surveyed VMs across two subscriptions and later inspected App Service configuration stores, apparently hunting secrets. Both used a python-requests/2.34.2 user agent and infrastructure linked to Storm-3168. Less than one second after a failed ListKey request against a nonexistent storage account, the attacker began a destructive sequence that ran about seven minutes, then spent the following 35 minutes on more than 150 destructive or credential-collection operations, over 100 of them attempts. Targets included Storage Accounts, SQL databases, Key Vaults, Function Apps, recovery protection locks, Virtual Machines, and App Services, with storage account credentials collected for possible future exfiltration.

Sysdig's follow-up on the operator's original playground shows the same hands-on-keyboard pattern against a Langflow instance: ENCFORGE, a UPX 5.20-packed Go 1.22.12 ransomware targeting roughly 180 extensions weighted toward AI and ML artifacts such as .safetensors, .gguf, .ckpt, .faiss, and .parquet, C2 at 34.153.223.102:9191, and the extortion contact e78393397@proton.me. When a binary fetch failed, the agent improvised a container escape through the Docker socket using privileged host-PID containers and nsenter, finishing in 5 minutes 24 seconds. Sysdig estimates rebuilding a production fine-tuned model at $75,000 to $500,000, which explains the targeting.

The signal is that cloud control planes are now ransomware frontline targets, with workload identities as the entry point and recovery infrastructure deliberately in the blast radius. Exposed credentials stay usable until rotated, so deleting a leak does nothing. Protect service principals with least privilege and short-lived credentials, lock recovery and backup resources behind separate controls, and alert on the observed tempo: enumeration measured in hundreds of reads followed by destruction within seconds of a probe.
