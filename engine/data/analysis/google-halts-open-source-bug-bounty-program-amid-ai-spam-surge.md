Google suspended product-vulnerability submissions to its Open Source Software Vulnerability Rewards Program (OSS VRP) effective October 1, 2026, after an influx of automated reports, the vast majority invalid. The program, launched in August 2022 with rewards ranging from $100 to $31,337, covers Google-maintained open-source projects including Golang, Angular, Bazel, Protocol Buffers, Fuchsia, and critical third-party dependencies as well as repository settings such as GitHub actions, configurations, and access controls. Supply chain reports and the Patch Rewards Program (bounties up to $15,000) remain open, and Google says it is restructuring the OSS VRP with an update expected in the first quarter of 2027.

This is a signal about attacker economics, not a patch release: the spam surge shows AI-generated vulnerability reports have become cheap enough to overwhelm a major vendor program, which means any organization relying on open-source dependency scanning or bug-bounty triage is now competing with noise at scale. Only two sources (bleepingcomputer.com and gbhackers.com) covered the pause within hours of the story's first appearance on October 5, 2026, and no CVE was assigned, so defenders should treat this as an operational warning rather than an immediate technical exposure. The exposure is broad, covering the full Google-maintained open-source footprint plus critical dependencies.

Defenders should verify whether any internal tools rely on OSS VRP disclosure timelines for dependency tracking, since submissions are paused through at least early 2027. Monitor for supply chain reports, which remain open, and continue submitting patches through the Patch Rewards Program. Watch for restructuring details in Q1 2027, particularly any new validation gates or automated-submission filters, and track whether other vendors mirror this suspension.

A change in assessment would come if Google publishes specific restructuring rules or if telemetry shows attackers pivoting from spam reports to exploitation of the unpatched dependency classes the OSS VRP previously covered. The absence of a CVE and the exact October 1 suspension date are the concrete anchors; any shift in those facts would alter the risk profile.
