Google paused product vulnerability submissions to its Open Source Software Vulnerability Reward Program effective October 1, 2026, citing a significant rise in automated submissions, the vast majority of them invalid. The OSS VRP launched in August 2022 with rewards from $100 to $31,337 and covers Google-maintained open source including Golang, Angular, Bazel, Protocol Buffers, and Fuchsia, plus repository settings like GitHub Actions configurations and access rules.

The pause is scoped: supply chain reports and everything submitted before October 1 are still accepted, and researchers still have outlets through the Cloud VRP for Google Cloud repository issues and the Patch Rewards Program, which pays up to $15,000 for proactive fixes. Google committed to an update in Q1 2027.

This is the third such retreat in under a year. curl shut its HackerOne program in early 2026 after low-quality, often AI-generated reports overwhelmed a small team, and Intel's Intigriti program dropped bounties, reportedly for the same reason. The pattern is generative AI collapsing the cost of polished-looking but hallucinated findings until triage becomes the bottleneck, and programs respond by gating or suspending intake for everyone.

Watch what Google's Q1 2027 relaunch requires, since mandatory proof-of-concept thresholds and rate limits there will likely become the template for other large programs, and whether legitimate low-resource researchers get squeezed out by the same controls that filter the noise.
