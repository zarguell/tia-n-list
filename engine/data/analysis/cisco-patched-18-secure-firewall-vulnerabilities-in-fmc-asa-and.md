Cisco disclosed 18 vulnerabilities across its Secure Firewall product line on September 16, covering FMC, ASA, and FTD. The most urgent is CVE-2026-20242, a CVSS 9.8 flaw in Secure Firewall Management Center that lets unauthenticated remote attackers execute code as root. A second critical bug, CVE-2026-20341, targets the sftunnel protocol in FMC software, allowing administrators who already have a foothold to escalate to root through insecure deserialization. Both are unauthenticated or require only low-privilege access, making them prime targets for initial access.

This batch is significant because Cisco firewall infrastructure sits at the perimeter of countless enterprise networks. The combination of unauthenticated RCE and privilege escalation in the same product line within one disclosure cycle means organizations running FMC need to patch immediately, not wait for the next maintenance window. ASA and FTD devices also need attention, though those flaws appear less severe.

Watch for exploitation within days. Cisco perimeter gear is a perennial target for both APT groups and ransomware operators, and unauthenticated RCE in management interfaces historically gets weaponized fast. The sftunnel flaw could be chained with other Cisco bugs for deeper access into managed devices. Verify that FMC instances are not exposed to untrusted networks, and audit sftunnel configurations.
