Trezor warned customers after a credible report of a suspected hardware implant in a Trezor Safe 5 purchased from reseller CryptoBilis in Indonesia via Tokopedia in August 2026. The reported device contained an extra circuit board fitted between the main board and the screen, carrying a cellular module and antenna; the implant appears capable of reading the wallet recovery phrase from the screen and transmitting it over a mobile network when the device is plugged in. Trezor has not yet examined the device and has not independently verified the implant, and no affected customers have reported fund losses so far. As a precaution, the company advised anyone who bought a Trezor through CryptoBilis since March 2026 to move funds to a new wallet with a new seed, and offered replacement devices or vouchers.

CryptoBilis is the same Southeast Asian reseller implicated in a confirmed Ledger hardware implant incident earlier in October. In that case, Ledger confirmed that devices sold through CryptoBilis were compromised with a physical supply-chain implant that intercepted screen output to capture the 24-word recovery phrase, with onchain estimates of $86 to $93 million drained across Bitcoin, Ethereum and Tron. Ledger asked resellers and distributors in Southeast Asia and Hong Kong to halt sales. Trezor removed CryptoBilis as an authorized reseller on October 9 following those reports. The two implants appear similar in form and function, suggesting a shared supply-chain compromise at the reseller rather than a flaw in either vendor's manufacturing.

The risk here is physical supply-chain tampering of hardware wallets, not a remote vulnerability. Users who bought through CryptoBilis should treat the device as potentially compromised even if no funds are missing, because a captured recovery phrase allows later drainage. Buying only from official stores or verified channels, and inspecting devices for unexpected components, remains the practical mitigation.
