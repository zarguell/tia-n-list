Trezor disclosed on October 30, 2026 that its security team received a responsible disclosure on October 28 of a single hardware implant in a Trezor Safe 5 purchased through reseller CryptoBilis via Tokopedia in August 2026. The report describes an extra circuit board fitted between the main board and screen, carrying a cellular module and antenna, that appears able to read what the screen shows including the wallet recovery phrase and transmit it over a mobile network whenever the device is plugged in. Trezor has not examined the device and has not confirmed the implant, and no CryptoBilis buyer has reported a loss of funds to the company. CryptoBilis was an authorized Trezor reseller in Southeast Asia until October 9, 2026, when Trezor removed it following the related Ledger implant incident, and the two implants appear similar in form and function.

The mechanism matters more than the single device because it converts the wallet's trusted display into the exfiltration channel: a seed that is never typed on a keyboard and never leaves the device is still captured at the moment it is shown on screen, so conventional transaction monitoring and address allowlisting provide no signal. Exposure is defined by purchase channel and date rather than by model, since Trezor's guidance applies to every Trezor bought from CryptoBilis since March 2026, and the number of affected buyers is unknown. The absence of confirmed losses is not reassuring, because a seed-harvesting implant can be silent for weeks before any funds move, and the same reseller channel was already tied to more than $86 million in confirmed Ledger losses. This is a provenance failure, not a firmware bug, and it undermines the core claim that an air-gapped signer protects the seed from remote theft.

Anyone who bought a Trezor from CryptoBilis since March 2026 should stop using the device, move funds to a new wallet generated with a fresh seed, and never enter an existing backup on the suspect device; if no secure hardware wallet is available, a temporary move to a familiar software wallet or exchange is acceptable, but the receiving address must be verified in full. Buyers who have not set up their device should not initialize it. Trezor is offering a replacement device shipped directly or a voucher for purchases since March 2026, and affected customers should keep the CryptoBilis device and contact Trezor Support; SEAL 911 is assisting the response. Hunt focus belongs on provenance and seed reuse rather than endpoint telemetry, since the implant is physical and has no firmware signature, and any device that used a CryptoBilis-purchased seed should be treated as compromised even if no transaction has occurred.

The assessment changes if Trezor's lab examination confirms or refutes the implant, or if the same implant design is found in devices sold outside the CryptoBilis channel, which would expand a single-reseller compromise into a broader supply-chain incident. A second trigger would be confirmed fund losses tied to Trezor buyers, which would move this from a precautionary advisory to an active theft case, or evidence linking the Trezor and Ledger implants to the same operator.
