Trezor warned that Safe 5 hardware wallets sold through the Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis contained a suspected supply-chain implant. The implant is an extra circuit board fitted between the wallet's main board and screen, carrying a cellular module and antenna; it reads the 24-word recovery phrase from the screen and exfiltrates it over the mobile network when the device is plugged in. Trezor has not independently verified the device and no customers have reported lost funds, but it treated the single responsible-disclosure report as credible. The same reseller was implicated earlier in a Ledger implant incident that drained roughly $86 to $93 million across Bitcoin, Ethereum and Tron.

The risk is a physical supply-chain compromise of a hardware wallet rather than a firmware or remote exploit, which makes detection by end users unlikely. Trezor removed CryptoBilis as an authorized reseller, advised buyers since March 2026 to move funds to a new wallet with a new seed, and offered replacement devices or vouchers. Ledger confirmed tampered devices through the same channel and halted reseller sales in Southeast Asia and Hong Kong.

Watch for Trezor's lab verification of the implant, the full scope of affected Safe 5 units sold via Tokopedia and other channels, and whether the CryptoBilis channel served additional wallet brands. Any confirmed verification would move this from suspected to confirmed exploitation of recovery phrases.
