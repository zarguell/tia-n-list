Trezor has confirmed that three Safe 5 hardware wallets sold by the Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis carried an implant designed to steal recovery phrases. The company examined the first device, reported by an Indonesian customer in October 2026, in its own laboratory and identified two more compromised units from photographs supplied by their owners. All three were bought from CryptoBilis between August 20 and September 23, 2026. Trezor's engineers analyzed the implant firmware and confirmed that the added board can capture the information the wallet screen displays.

The implant is an extra circuit board fitted between the wallet's main board and its screen, carrying a cellular module and antenna. It reads the recovery phrase off the display and sends it over the mobile network when the device is plugged in. Forty-eight CryptoBilis customers have contacted Trezor since the October warning, with devices being examined in the laboratory or through photographs of their internals. Trezor also inspected units from other resellers in the region and found no evidence of tampering, and no affected customer has reported losing funds. The update lands alongside a fix release from Trezor, and CryptoBilis had already been removed from the authorized reseller list.

This is the second confirmed hardware supply-chain compromise traced to CryptoBilis, after the Ledger incident earlier in October that drained roughly $86 to $93 million across Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Tron. Because the implant sits in the device rather than its firmware, a firmware update cannot make an already-shipped unit trustworthy, and a buyer cannot see the extra board without opening the case. Watch for how many of the 48 contacted customers turn out to hold implants, whether Trezor widens the review to other models sold through the same channel, and whether any theft is eventually confirmed.
