Trezor moved from a credible but unverified report to confirmation of physical tampering in its Safe 5 line. The first report came from an Indonesian buyer who purchased a Safe 5 through CryptoBilis on Tokopedia in August 2026 and found an extra circuit board between the main board and screen, with a cellular module and antenna. The board reads information displayed on the screen, including the recovery phrase, and transmits it over a mobile network. Trezor removed CryptoBilis as an authorized reseller on October 9 after related Ledger cases, and the same channel has been tied to more than $86 million in drained Ledger funds.

On November 13 Trezor said it had examined the original device in its lab and identified two more compromised wallets from photographs, all bought from CryptoBilis between August 20 and September 23, 2026. Forty-eight CryptoBilis customers had contacted the company, devices from other regional resellers showed no tampering, and no affected customer had reported stolen funds.

The confirmed artifact is a three-device hardware implant limited so far to Safe 5 wallets from one reseller, not a Trezor firmware or manufacturing compromise. Trezor's advice is to move funds to a new wallet with a new seed, with replacement devices or vouchers offered for purchases since March 2026. A fresh Hacker News link on November 18 kept the confirmed finding in circulation, but the underlying response remains reseller-focused.
