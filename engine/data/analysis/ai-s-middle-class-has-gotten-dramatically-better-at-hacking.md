XBOW research covered by CyberScoop argues the industry's mid-tier AI models have crossed a practical threshold for offensive security work. Models such as Z.ai's open-weight GLM-5.2, xAI's Grok 4.5, Anthropic's Opus 4.7 and Meta's Muse Spark 1.1 now perform strongly on hacking and exploitation tasks that worry policymakers, according to Albert Ziegler, XBOW's head of AI. Six months ago mid-tier models struggled with moderately complex agentic tasks; today they largely succeed.

The cost angle is the strategic point. Cheaper models can be run repeatedly at many times the resource budget, which lets them come from behind and beat frontier models on the same challenge. Ziegler notes this did not work half a year ago because running an open-source model on a complex agentic task was not economical.

Why it matters: the offensive capability curve is no longer only about frontier labs. Open-weight and mid-tier models put exploitation-grade capability within reach of a much wider set of actors, and at a price that makes brute-force repetition viable. Watch for this class of model showing up in agentic attack tooling and in the next wave of AI-enabled intrusion reporting.
