Security firm Calif has built and demonstrated WeWorm, described as the first zero-click worm capable of spreading across both iOS and Android devices through WeChat calls. The attack exploits a vulnerability in WeChat's VoIP technology that grants elevated privileges to stored contacts. A worm leveraging this flaw would spread between contacts, with each infection triggering calls to the victim's own contacts, making the chain appear to come from known people. The infection occurs regardless of whether the call is answered or ignored, and can be re-triggered if initially declined.

Calif says the vulnerability was discovered by its AI system in July 2026. The exploit took two days to develop with AI support, and the full worm took about a week, whereas a comparable effort by a traditional team would have taken months. Calif reported the bug to Tencent on July 24. After initial account blocks were reversed, Tencent released patches on August 21. Calif estimates the worm could have compromised more than a billion WeChat accounts given the app's user base. Full device takeover would require additional vulnerabilities, but Calif recently demonstrated OEMpocalypse, a technique for gaining root access on Android devices from multiple manufacturers without special permissions, which would pair dangerously with WeWorm.

The timeline is the critical detail here: between July 24 and August 21, every WeChat user on both platforms was theoretically exposed. The combination of zero-click infection, cross-platform operation, and contact-trusted propagation makes this one of the more concerning mobile worm demonstrations in recent memory. Organizations with WeChat-heavy user bases in Asia-Pacific markets should verify all devices are running the patched version and consider the implications of AI-accelerated exploit development in their threat modeling.
