GhostMap is an Android availability flaw in how two system libraries parse ZIP compression in APK files. The libraries disagree on the compressed size, so a malformed APK bypasses size checks and gets mapped beyond its real contents; any process that reads the entry takes a SIGBUS and the kernel kills it. Bitdefender built the proof of concept as an app with no executable code, no requested permissions, and no launch requirement: installation alone starts a persistent boot loop. The researchers found it on Aug. 12, 2026 after a banking dropper collected from the wild crashed their analysis emulators, reproduced it on a Pixel 10 running Android 17 with the Aug. 5, 2026 security patch, and say it affects Android 10 and above. Google was told in August, closed the report as not reproducible, and has shipped no patch; no CVE has been assigned.

A successful hit does not steal data; it bricks the device until the malicious app is removed, which is hard when the phone will not finish booting. Exposure is every Android 10 and up device reachable by an app installed from any channel, with no user interaction past installation. This matters more than most app bugs because the delivery mechanism is already proven in the wild: Bitdefender says roughly 9,200 samples in a single year used the same corrupt-ZIP-header packing, tied to banking trojans including Anatsa, Cerberus, and BianLian. The cloak is common; the crash loop is new, and with no vendor fix there is nothing to deploy.

Defenders should treat this as an availability incident with no patch yet. Block sideloading and installs from outside trusted stores, and flag any app that requests no permissions yet crashes system services during installation. Hunt install-time SIGBUS crashes and tombstones referencing APK resource reads, and compare ZIP central directory entries against declared sizes in submitted APKs. Prioritize Android 10 through 17 fleets, since the researchers reproduced the fault across that range.

Watch for Google to reopen the issue or address GhostMap in a future Android security bulletin, which would be the first real fix, and watch for the crash loop to appear in actual banking-trojan samples rather than only in analysis emulators. Either development moves this from a local denial of service to an active availability threat.
