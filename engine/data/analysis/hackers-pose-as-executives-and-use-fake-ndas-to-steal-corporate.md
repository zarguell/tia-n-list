A fraud campaign dubbed Phantom Deal targeted Gen, a consumer technology company, using impersonation of real executives and consulting firms to steal €626,735.45. The attackers contacted a Gen legal employee via WhatsApp, impersonating a real Gen executive in Dublin using the executive's name, photograph, and an Irish phone number. A phone call confirmed the fraud when the caller's voice did not match the real executive, but the employee continued the conversation as part of a controlled investigation.

The attackers then introduced a second fake identity, impersonating a professional connected to PwC. They sent a professional-looking non-disclosure agreement branded as a PwC document, claiming Gen was involved in a confidential acquisition. The NDA instructed the employee not to discuss the deal internally and to use WhatsApp and personal email for all communication. This isolation tactic prevented the employee from verifying the request through normal corporate channels. The campaign ultimately pressured the company into transferring €626,735.45 to a Hong Kong entity.

This is a targeted BEC operation that abuses legitimate M&A processes and corporate hierarchy. The use of real executive identities, forged NDAs, and deliberate isolation of the target employee shows sophistication beyond mass phishing. The financial loss is significant but the technique is the real concern: attackers are weaponizing corporate secrecy norms and legal document workflows to bypass internal controls. Organizations with cross-border M&A activity or high-value transfer authorization processes should review their verification protocols.
