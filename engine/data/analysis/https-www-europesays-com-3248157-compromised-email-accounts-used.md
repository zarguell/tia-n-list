Singapore police issued a crime advisory on September 12 reporting an increase in cases where compromised email accounts were used to access cryptocurrency exchanges since mid-August. Investigations found that affected email accounts had previously appeared in data breaches on other platforms, and victims had reused passwords across multiple services. The attack chain is straightforward: attackers search compromised inboxes for cryptocurrency platform notifications, set up forwarding rules to intercept password resets, and use the same breached credentials to log into crypto accounts.

The attackers exploit email inbox rules to archive, forward, or delete messages from exchanges to avoid detection while they drain accounts. The advisory notes that while multiple security layers exist between an attacker and a cryptocurrency account, credential reuse combined with email compromise eliminates most of them. The police recommend unique passwords, authenticator-based 2FA, and regular review of email forwarding rules and login activity.

This pattern is not new but the scale reported by Singapore authorities suggests an organized campaign targeting cryptocurrency holders through credential stuffing. The attack does not require sophisticated tools, only access to breached credential databases and basic email manipulation. Cryptocurrency exchanges should monitor for unusual login patterns and alert users when email-linked accounts show suspicious access.
