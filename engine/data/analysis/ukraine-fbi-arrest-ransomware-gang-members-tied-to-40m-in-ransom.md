Ukrainian authorities working with the FBI arrested four suspected members of an international ransomware organization after a two-year investigation. Ukraine's National Police describes a group of at least 27 people from Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Russia and other countries, connected to a wider cybercrime network of more than 60 members across eight jurisdictions that included former Conti ransomware operators. Victims were in oil and gas, pharmaceuticals, manufacturing, logistics, finance and critical infrastructure. The attackers researched financial standing, assets, employees and IT weaknesses before stealing data, encrypting systems and demanding cryptocurrency.

Acting National Police chief Maksym Tsutskiridze told Censor.NET that investigators identified at least 28 companies that paid more than $40.5 million, about UAH 1.8 billion, and said the full damage figure is still being established. The arrests matter less for the number of people detained than for the visibility into payment totals and the cross-border structure behind the extortion operation.

Watch for formal charging documents and any seizure figures that confirm or revise the ransom tally, plus whether the eight-jurisdiction network produces further arrests. The public account does not name the ransomware brand or individual victims, so attribution claims beyond the police description should be treated as unresolved.
