Ukrainian police and the FBI detained four suspected key members of a ransomware operation after a two-year investigation. Prosecutors describe a core group of at least 27 people from Ukraine, Kazakhstan and Russia, linked to a wider network of more than 60 individuals in eight jurisdictions that included former Conti members. Investigators identified at least 28 companies that paid more than $40.5 million, about UAH 1.8 billion, and say the full damage figure is still being established.

The group researched victims' finances, assets, employees and IT weaknesses before deploying data theft and encryption. Operators used AI-generated voice messages while calling staff as IT support, persuaded employees to hand over credentials or install remote-access software, then demanded cryptocurrency and threatened to publish stolen data. One documented US victim lost 1.4 TB, faced a $10 million demand and paid $5 million after negotiation. Targets included oil and gas, pharmaceuticals, manufacturing, logistics, financial institutions and critical infrastructure.

Authorities conducted 66 searches in Kyiv and 11 regions, seizing computers, phones, bank cards, about $370,000 and EUR 20,000. The four detainees were served notices under Articles 255(2) and 361(5) of Ukraine's Criminal Code and face up to 15 years in prison. Watch for further arrests across the eight jurisdictions, publication of the named victim counts, and any seizure of cryptocurrency wallets or decryptors that could change the recovery picture.
