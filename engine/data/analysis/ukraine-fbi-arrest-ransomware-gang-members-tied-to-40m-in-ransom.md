Ukrainian police and the FBI exposed a ransomware operation after a two-year investigation, detaining four suspected key members. Prosecutors describe a core group of at least 27 people from Ukraine, Russia and Kazakhstan, linked to a wider network of more than 60 individuals across eight jurisdictions that included former Conti members. Investigators identified at least 28 companies that paid more than 40.5 million dollars, about UAH 1.8 billion, and say the true damage total is still being established. Recorded Future's November 19 follow-up names oil and gas, pharmaceuticals, manufacturing, logistics, finance and critical infrastructure among the targeted sectors.

The group researched victims' finances, assets, staff and IT weaknesses, then called employees while posing as IT support, using AI-generated voice messages to automate the calls. Victims were pushed to hand over credentials or install remote-access software before data was stolen and systems encrypted. One US company lost 1.4 TB of data, faced a 10 million dollar demand and paid 5 million after negotiation, according to Ukraine's National Police.

Officers carried out 66 searches in Kyiv and 11 other regions, seizing computers, phones, bank cards, about 370,000 dollars and 20,000 euros. The four detainees were charged under Articles 255(2) and 361(5) of Ukraine's Criminal Code and face up to 15 years in prison. Authorities say they are still identifying other members. Watch for further arrests, leak-site activity from the wider network, and IT-support voice phishing that uses generated audio.
