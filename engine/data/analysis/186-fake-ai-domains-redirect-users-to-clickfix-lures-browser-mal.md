Researchers at Island have tracked a wave of lookalike domains that imitate popular AI tools and turn a routine attempt to reach a familiar service into a malware delivery path. The pattern, which Island calls TypoPrompting, abuses typosquatted AI-app domains to serve ClickFix-style lures, browser-hijacking malware, and a set of extensions that quietly redirect a victim's searches. The first batch described covers 186 fake AI domains.

The mechanism is social-engineering heavy. A user who mistypes a URL or is redirected to a fake AI domain is shown a prompt framed as a verification step, and completing it runs the attacker's payload rather than opening the real tool. Because the lure rides on trust in well-known AI brands, it sidesteps the suspicion a user might apply to an unknown download or an unfamiliar sender.

Defenders should treat lookalike AI-tool domains as a live phishing vector and block or monitor newly registered domains that imitate major AI products. Watch for ClickFix clipboard-page patterns reaching users through search results, and for browser extensions that change default search behavior after an install. Reporting and sinkholing the typosquatted domains cuts the delivery path, but user-facing guidance against completing verification prompts on non-official domains matters as much as the blocks.
