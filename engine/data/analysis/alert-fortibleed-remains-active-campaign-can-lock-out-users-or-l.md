The FBI said on October 7 that FortiBleed attacks against exposed Fortinet FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN gateways are still ongoing, with a twist that complicates response: intruders create their own administrator accounts and delete or lock out existing ones, so victims can find themselves locked out of the very devices they need to remediate. Per the FBI, attackers get in with previously leaked credentials or logins harvested from infostealer logs, credential stuffing, and password spraying, then pull additional authentication data off compromised devices and crack the stolen password hashes offline using Hashcat and Hashtopolis on a distributed GPU cluster. The bureau states the FortiBleed chain has been observed as an initial entry point for ransomware affiliates, naming INC/Lynx and Payload.

The scale is established: FortiBleed surfaced in June as a leak of usernames and plaintext passwords tied to 73,932 firewall URLs across 194 countries, and SOCRadar's latest count puts confirmed compromises at 86,644 devices. This sits on top of the patched Fortinet bugs the campaign abuses, CVE-2025-59718 and CVE-2025-59719 (FortiCloud SSO login bypass, patched December 9, 2025) and CVE-2026-24858 (FortiCloud SSO cross-account bypass, KEV-listed January 27, 2026). Patching closes the door but not the vault, because credentials and configuration hashes taken before remediation remain crackable, and the FBI's new detail about admin account takeover means standard patch-and-reset guidance can fail if attackers have already locked operators out.

Response should assume credential compromise, not just vulnerable software: terminate all SSL VPN and admin sessions, rotate credentials from a trusted path, enforce phishing-resistant MFA, pull management interfaces off the public internet, and hunt for the campaign's named artifacts such as the backdoor account adminin, sniffer logs named fg_capture.log, and AD enumeration scripts ad_enum.py and ad_full_audit.py. Watch for ransomware affiliate activity citing FortiGate access, since the FBI's confirmation of INC/Lynx and Payload as consumers of this access chain means successful logins are likely precursors to deployment, and watch for any Fortinet guidance forcing legacy salted SHA-256 config hashes off devices, which would reveal how much crackable residue remains across the fleet.
