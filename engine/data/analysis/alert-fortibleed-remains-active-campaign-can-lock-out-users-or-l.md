The FBI and Secret Service warned on October 6 that FortiBleed, a credential compromise campaign against Fortinet firewalls and VPN gateways, is still active. The joint alert says attackers with access can disable accounts or change passwords, locking defenders out and forcing remediation beyond standard patching and password resets. It also confirms the attack chain has been observed as an initial entry point for ransomware affiliates, naming INC/Lynx and Payload.

The campaign has been tracked since June 2026. SOCRadar first verified 86,644 compromised devices across 194 countries and later sized the operation at roughly 400,000 to 450,000 firewalls targeted, with a Go-based tool called FortigateSniffer deployed on about 19,000 devices to capture authentication traffic across 24 protocols including RADIUS, NTLM, Kerberos, LDAP, and SMB. Cracking ran on a 36-GPU cluster rented on Vast.ai for about $350 a day, reaching 720 billion hashes per second against legacy salted SHA-256. Hudson Rock confirmed a leaked dataset covering 73,932 firewall URLs across 21,632 domains, including Chevron, Samsung, Comcast, AT&T, and Toyota. SOCRadar later linked the operation directly to the INC and Lynx ransomware platforms, and an initial access broker (SantaAd) sold bulk Fortinet access on Russian-language forums.

CISA issued a formal warning in June urging session termination, password resets, phishing-resistant MFA, and removal of management interfaces from the public internet; most affected devices had exposed management planes. Qualys notes a patched device can remain compromised when credentials or configuration material were stolen before remediation. Watch for new victim disclosures, ransomware posts advertising Fortinet access, and whether the FBI alert adds IOCs or victim counts.
