The FBI and U.S. Secret Service issued a joint cybersecurity advisory on October 6, 2026 warning that the FortiBleed credential compromise campaign remains active against internet-facing Fortinet FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN gateways. Attackers reuse leaked or infostealer-derived credentials, credential stuffing, and password spraying to gain access, then extract password hashes from compromised devices and crack them offline using a distributed GPU cluster running Hashcat and Hashtopolis. SOCRadar first documented FortiBleed in June 2026, when a server exposed usernames and plaintext passwords for 73,932 firewall URLs across 194 countries; later counts cite 86,644 compromised devices, and SOCRadar's CISO told CyberScoop the wider operation may have targeted 400,000 to 450,000 firewalls. In some incidents the actors create new administrator accounts and delete or change passwords on legitimate accounts, locking defenders out of their own FortiGate devices while retaining control and pivoting internally. The alert also notes the campaign has served as an initial access vector for ransomware affiliates, including INC/Lynx and Payload.

The lockout behavior is the operationally significant shift. Standard patching and password resets are insufficient once an attacker has removed or altered original administrator accounts, so remediation must include account inventory, credential rotation, and verification of administrative access. Because initial access brokers are feeding ransomware affiliates from this campaign, the exposure window is not just a credential hygiene problem but a precursor to encryption and extortion.

Defenders should treat any internet-facing FortiGate or FortiVPN as in scope, hunt for unauthorized administrator accounts and changed passwords, and review logs for credential-based access and lateral movement. Organizations that cannot confirm clean administrative state should assume compromise and rebuild access rather than rely on resets alone. Watch for follow-on ransomware activity attributed to INC/Lynx and Payload in environments with Fortinet exposure.
