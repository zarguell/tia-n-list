The FBI and Secret Service said on October 6 that FortiBleed, the credential compromise campaign against Fortinet firewalls and VPN gateways that surfaced publicly on June 18, is still running and still feeding ransomware affiliates, including INC/Lynx and Payload. The operation had a long private runway before that disclosure. Fortinet patched CVE-2025-59718 and CVE-2025-59719 on December 9, 2025 (FG-IR-25-647), an unauthenticated FortiCloud SSO login bypass via crafted SAML response hitting FortiOS, FortiProxy, FortiSwitchManager, and FortiWeb, and Arctic Wolf saw malicious SSO logins on FortiGate appliances by December 12. CVE-2026-24858, a FortiCloud SSO cross-account authentication bypass in FortiAnalyzer, FortiManager, FortiOS, and FortiProxy (FG-IR-26-060), followed on January 27, 2026, and entered the KEV catalog the same day. What runs on top of these bugs is a pipeline rather than a payload: curated password lists, the Go-based FortigateSniffer abusing the FortiOS diagnose sniffer packet command to capture authentication traffic across 24 protocols, then offline cracking on a 36-GPU cluster rented on Vast.ai for roughly $350 per day.

The decisive argument is that patching closes the door but not the vault. Qualys states plainly that a patched device can remain compromised if credentials or configuration material were stolen before remediation, and the cracking economics explain why: the rented cluster does 720 billion guesses per second against legacy salted SHA-256 config hashes but only 180 to 360 million against PBKDF2, a gap of three orders of magnitude. Whether an organization completed the PBKDF2 migration decides whether the June leak of 73,932 firewall URLs across 21,632 domains is a closed incident or a live breach still in progress. Fortinet characterized the dataset as re-shared material from earlier incidents, yet SOCRadar dates the campaign to at least February 2026 and sized it at 400,000 to 450,000 firewalls targeted, with the sniffer deployed on about 19,000 devices, 86,644 confirmed compromised machines across 194 countries, and victims including Chevron, Samsung, Comcast, AT&T, Toyota, and a Turkish NATO defense contractor that allegedly lost classified documents. The October alert adds a twist that makes routine remediation dangerous: operators with this access can disable accounts or change passwords, locking defenders out of the very devices they need to clean.

CISA's June directive still defines the floor: terminate all SSL VPN and administrative sessions, rotate credentials, enforce phishing-resistant MFA, and pull management interfaces off the public internet, since a majority of affected devices had exposed management planes. Then hunt, because the artifacts have names. Look for the backdoor account username adminin, sniffer logs named fg_capture.log, Active Directory enumeration scripts ad_enum.py and ad_full_audit.py, Hashcat and Hashtopolis orchestration files, and log-clearing markers, plus infrastructure at 85.11.187.8 from Recorded Future's reporting. The behavioral signature Unit 42 describes is spray-then-login: a successful admin authentication on an edge device within minutes of a burst of password failures, so alert on that gap rather than on volume alone. Validate PBKDF2 migration and configuration integrity on every FortiGate even where patches are confirmed, and treat Qualys QID matches across the eight mapped CVEs, including CVE-2026-24858 and CVE-2025-59718/59719, as investigation seeds.

Two developments would move this assessment. Ransomware victim posts or new affiliate advertising from Payload, INC, or Lynx citing FortiGate or FortiCloud SSO access would confirm the December 2025 SSO bypass as a working entry vector at fleet scale, converting this from credential hygiene story to exploitation campaign. Any forced or accelerated Fortinet migration of legacy password hashes off salted SHA-256 would reveal how large the crackable residue is, and that number, not the leak size, is what determines whether 400,000 targeted firewalls stay a standing risk.
