FortiBleed is an ongoing credential compromise campaign targeting Fortinet firewalls and VPN gateways that the FBI and Secret Service warned remains active. Affected organizations risk being locked out of systems if attackers disable accounts or change passwords, and the campaign has served as an initial access vector for ransomware affiliates. When first uncovered, SOCRadar verified more than 86,644 compromised devices across 194 countries, with later investigation identifying 400,000 to 450,000 targeted firewalls. The campaign traces back to FortiCloud SSO authentication bypasses in FortiOS, FortiProxy, FortiSwitchManager, and FortiWeb.

The underlying flaws are CVE-2025-59718 and CVE-2025-59719, an improper verification of cryptographic signature allowing unauthenticated FortiCloud SSO login bypass via crafted SAML responses, rated CVSS 9.8, affecting multiple FortiOS, FortiProxy, FortiSwitchManager, and FortiWeb versions. CVE-2026-24858 is a separate cross-account authentication bypass in FortiAnalyzer, FortiManager, FortiOS, and FortiProxy, CVSS 9.8, allowing an attacker with one registered FortiCloud device to access other tenants where SSO is enabled. CISA added CVE-2025-59718 to KEV on 16 December 2025 and CVE-2026-24858 on its 27 January 2026 publication date.

Arctic Wolf observed intrusions using malicious SSO logins on FortiGate appliances beginning 12 December 2025, three days after Fortinet's disclosure, showing exploitation predated public FortiBleed attention. Fortinet published vendor guidance on SSO abuse in January 2026. Organizations should patch all affected versions, audit FortiCloud SSO usage, reset credentials, and review for unauthorized account changes and ransomware staging.
