FortiBleed remains an active global credential-theft campaign against internet-facing Fortinet FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN gateways, with the FBI and Secret Service confirming ongoing attacks in an October 6 advisory. The attackers use leaked and infostealer credentials, crack legacy salted SHA-256 hashes on rented GPU clusters, and deploy a Go-based FortigateSniffer tool to intercept authentication traffic across 24 protocols. The campaign has targeted approximately 430,000 firewalls, compromised roughly 86,644 devices, and operates as an initial-access broker feeding ransomware affiliates including INC/Lynx and Payload.

Key changes since June disclosure include administrator lockouts and account deletion as post-compromise actions, not just credential theft. The attackers build AI-assisted management scripts and use 36-GPU clusters at $350 per day to crack hashes at 720 billion per second. Defenders should treat patched devices as potentially still compromised if pre-remediation credentials were stolen, rotate all credentials, enforce phishing-resistant MFA, restrict management interfaces from the public internet, and hunt for successful admin logins followed by account removal events.
