The US Supreme Court issued a ruling blocking the Trump administration's efforts to have the United States Postal Service restrict mail-in voting procedures ahead of the midterm elections. The order prevents USPS from implementing new policies that critics argued would have delayed or complicated ballot delivery in key states.

This is not a cybersecurity story in the traditional sense, but it intersects with election security infrastructure. Changes to USPS handling of mail-in ballots would have had downstream effects on election administration systems, voter notification processes, and the chain of custody for physical ballots. The ruling preserves the status quo for mail-in voting procedures.

The Wired reporting indicates the ruling was issued without public dissent. Election security practitioners should note that the procedural status quo remains in effect, but the underlying political dynamics around mail-in voting continue to create uncertainty for election administrators.
