GreyNoise researchers identified a campaign in which threat actors impersonate web crawlers from OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepSeek, Google, Perplexity, Amazon, and other companies to scan internet-facing servers for exposed secrets. The activity ran from late July through late August 2026 across 824 IP addresses, using forged user-agent strings to request .env files, AWS credential stores, private keys, and password databases. Six of the impersonated names, tied to Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and Perplexity, appeared at nearly identical volumes, suggesting coordinated infrastructure.

The campaign exploits a fundamental weakness in how websites identify bots. User-agent headers are self-reported and trivially spoofed. AI companies publish crawler names and sometimes IP ranges, but websites that rely only on the header string are easily deceived. GreyNoise counted 13 forged crawler identities across eight companies in this cluster alone.

This matters because the attack vector is low-effort and high-yield. Exposed .env files and cloud keys are among the most common paths to full cloud compromise. Organizations should validate crawler identity against known IP ranges rather than trusting user-agent strings, and audit their public-facing servers for accidentally exposed configuration files.
