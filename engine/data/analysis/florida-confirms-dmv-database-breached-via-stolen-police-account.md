Florida's Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (FLHSMV) has confirmed a data breach of its DAVID driver database after the ShinyHunters extortion group claimed to have compromised the system. FLHSMV discovered the breach on September 4, 2026 and says its investigation determined the attacker used compromised credentials belonging to a single Plant City Police Department employee. The credentials were improperly stored on the employee's personal electronic device.

ShinyHunters claimed a different access method than what FLHSMV disclosed. The group said they exploited a password reset flaw to gain access to multiple DAVID accounts, including accounts belonging to DMV employees and an FBI agent. ShinyHunters claims to have stolen more than 200,000 driver records. FLHSMV says the breach was quickly mitigated and no further breach is ongoing.

The discrepancy between FLHSMV's account and ShinyHunters' claims is notable. FLHSMV attributes the breach entirely to stolen credentials from a single user, while ShinyHunters describes a broader exploitation of a password reset flaw affecting multiple accounts. This gap suggests either FLHSMV's investigation is incomplete or ShinyHunters is exaggerating their access. The involvement of an FBI agent's account, if true, would elevate the severity significantly. FLHSMV has notified the Florida Office of the Attorney General and is working with the Florida Digital Service and Florida Department of Law Enforcement. Watch for further technical details from the ongoing criminal investigation and any confirmation or denial of the ShinyHunters' specific claims about the password reset flaw and FBI agent account.
