Florida's Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles confirmed its DAVID driver database was breached after the ShinyHunters extortion gang claimed compromise. The agency's investigation determined the attacker used compromised credentials belonging to a single Plant City Police Department user whose credentials were improperly stored on a personal electronic device. The breach was mitigated on September 4, 2026, and no ongoing access was detected.

ShinyHunters had previously claimed a different access method, stating they exploited a password reset flaw to gain access to multiple DAVID accounts, including DMV employee and FBI agent accounts. The discrepancy between FLHSMV's findings and ShinyHunters' claims suggests either the attackers used multiple access vectors or are inflating their capabilities for extortion leverage.

The DAVID database contains driver records, license information, and personal data for Florida residents. The breach affected the database accessed by law enforcement and government agencies. FLHSMV has notified the Florida Office of the Attorney General and is working with the Florida Digital Service and Department of Law Enforcement. The incident highlights the risk of credential reuse and improper credential storage on personal devices, particularly for accounts with access to sensitive government databases.
