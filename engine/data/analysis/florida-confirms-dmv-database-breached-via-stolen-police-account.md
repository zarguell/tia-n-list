The Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (FLHSMV) confirmed on September 11 that its DAVID driver database was breached. The agency's investigation determined the attacker used compromised credentials belonging to a single Plant City Police Department employee whose credentials were improperly stored on a personal electronic device. FLHSMV said the breach was quickly mitigated and is no longer ongoing. The agency notified the Florida Office of the Attorney General and is working with the Florida Digital Service and Florida Department of Law Enforcement.

The disclosure followed claims by the ShinyHunters extortion gang that it had compromised the DAVID database and stolen more than 200,000 driver records. ShinyHunters described a different access method than FLHSMV's findings, claiming they exploited a password reset flaw to gain access to multiple DAVID accounts, including accounts belonging to DMV employees and an FBI agent. The group said it began iterating through DAVID record IDs and downloading associated HTML pages and images beginning September 3. As proof, ShinyHunters shared a screenshot of a DAVID record belonging to Jeffrey Epstein containing sensitive personal and vehicle information.

FLHSMV has not disclosed how many records were accessed or stolen, and has not confirmed ShinyHunters' claim that over 200,000 records were taken. The discrepancy between the agency's attribution (stolen police credentials) and ShinyHunters' description (password reset flaw) leaves open questions about the full scope of the intrusion. ShinyHunters told BleepingComputer it had lost access to the system and believed the flaw was being patched. The DAVID database contains driver's license records, vehicle registrations, and personal identification data for Florida residents.
