SETTRA is a new ransomware variant first observed in mid-2026 that abuses the legitimate MeshAgent remote monitoring and management (RMM) platform for persistence and uses Bring Your Own Vulnerable Driver (BYOVD) techniques to disable security controls before encrypting Windows systems. Huntress investigated two SETTRA incidents in July and September 2026, finding a repeatable pattern: the operator deploys victim-specific ransomware binaries, clears Windows event logs, and uses MeshAgent for persistent remote access. The BYOVD component targets vulnerable kernel drivers to gain SYSTEM-level privileges and terminate security processes before encryption.

The malware targets retail and manufacturing sectors. SETTRA's use of MeshAgent, a legitimate open-source RMM tool, makes detection harder because the traffic blends with normal IT management activity. The variant appeared in early summer 2026 and has since been observed in at least two confirmed investigations. GHBackers and OSINTsights both reported on the campaign, with Huntress providing the most detailed technical analysis including indicators of compromise.

Organizations should audit MeshAgent deployments across their networks, monitor for unauthorized RMM tool usage, and ensure vulnerable kernel drivers are patched or blocked via driver blocklists. The BYOVD technique is increasingly common in ransomware operations, and SETTRA's reliance on legitimate tools for persistence represents a continuation of the trend toward living-off-the-land tactics in ransomware attacks.
