Amazon Threat Intelligence reports that a Chinese-nexus actor used Cairn, an open-source autonomous penetration-testing framework, to find and exploit a zero-day in a medical imaging application during attacks on East Asian universities between 8 and 10 September 2026. The operator supplied IP addresses and the goal "obtain shell access", and the agents planned and adjusted the steps themselves. Amazon found the campaign because the attackers left their Cairn instance publicly accessible without authentication, exposing the platform's operational history, prompts written in Simplified Chinese and timestamps set to China Standard Time. CJ Moses of Amazon Integrated Security described the investigation, which began on 10 September.

The reporting also shows where the automation failed. One attempt spent 16 hours and took 148 actions trying to gain control of a Redis server, including an unsuccessful try at Log4Shell exploitation. Another ran more than 30,000 credential combinations over six hours against a university GPU server and did not get in. The reported success was the medical imaging application: the agents found a previously unknown flaw there and took complete control of a server. Neither account gives a CVE or names the affected product.

Cairn has appeared in other intrusions. Gambit Security documented its use in a large-scale credit card theft operation in September, and Google reported it in an attack on a US aerospace organization. The useful artifacts here are the unauthenticated Cairn control server, the Chinese-language prompts and China Standard Time settings, and the scale and speed of the agent actions rather than a conventional malware family. Watch for legitimate pentesting frameworks being repurposed as attack infrastructure and for fast, unexplained vulnerability discovery against unexamined application stacks.
