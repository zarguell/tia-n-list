China-aligned threat actor FamousSparrow has deployed a new backdoor called SparroWocky in attacks targeting government entities across Latin America since at least August 2025. ESET researchers Alexandre Cote Cyr and Romain Dumont reported the activity, noting that 90% of the group's recent targets are located in the region. Affected countries include Argentina, Ecuador, Guatemala, Honduras, Panama, Peru, Puerto Rico, and Venezuela. FamousSparrow shares overlap with Earth Estries and Salt Typhoon, and has been active since at least 2019.

SparroWocky is a modular C++ backdoor that replaces the group's previous SparrowDoor implant. It uses DLL sideloading for initial execution: a legitimate executable loads a loader DLL, which decrypts and launches the main payload. The backdoor can execute arbitrary files, act as a TCP proxy, run commands, collect system information, exfiltrate files, take periodic screenshots, and delete itself. Early samples contained the first stanza of Lewis Carroll's "Jabberwocky" poem, giving the malware its name.

The malware integrates several open-source projects for defense evasion: Mbed TLS for encrypted C2 communication (to 216.238.110.120), MinHook to hide thread start addresses, COFF Loader for in-memory plugin execution, and a SilentMoonwalk variant to spoof call stacks from MinHook routines. The initial access vector remains unknown. Organizations in Latin America, particularly government entities, should monitor for DLL sideloading indicators and anomalous network connections to the identified C2 infrastructure.
