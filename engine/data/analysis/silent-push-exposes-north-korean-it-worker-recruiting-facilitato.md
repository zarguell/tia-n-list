Silent Push researchers identified a fraudulent hiring scheme operating through a Discord server dedicated to computer hardware reviews, where a suspected North Korean IT worker recruits proxy candidates to secure remote developer positions at Western tech companies. The operation targets firms across the United States, European Union, and Latin America (Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, Chile), with claimed salaries of $130,000 to $180,000 per position.

The scheme works through a structured revenue-sharing model: the proxy candidate receives 35% of earnings while the North Korean operative keeps 65%. During technical interviews, the operative provides live answers through messaging platforms and offers to take remote control of the proxy's computer during coding challenges. Silent Push's investigator, operating under a cover persona on Telegram, documented the handler's instructions to use AI assistants for generating technical responses and routing payments through cryptocurrency wallets.

Multiple indicators point to North Korean attribution. The operative immediately recommended Astrill VPN, a tool commonly used by North Korean network operators. During a direct video call, the operative abruptly terminated the feed when analysts mentioned regional politics and refused to criticize the regime. The technical infrastructure, language patterns, and financial routing align with documented Pyongyang front operations. The U.S. Department of Treasury enforces strict OFAC sanctions against such activities, and the Department of State has issued formal warnings about these deceptive practices. Hiring managers should require in-person video interviews with active identity checks, prohibit screen sharing via third-party remote desktop software, and cross-reference applicant IP addresses with corporate application portals.
