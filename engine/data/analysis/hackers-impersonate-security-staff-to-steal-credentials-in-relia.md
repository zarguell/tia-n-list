ReliaQuest confirmed that an attacker targeted its employees on August 22, 2026, using a social engineering campaign attributed to the ShinyHunters extortion group. The attacker registered the domain reliaquest.claims and deployed a counterfeit ReliaQuest single sign-on page behind a CDN. Multiple employees received phone calls from someone impersonating a named member of ReliaQuest's security team, directing them to the fake SSO portal. One employee entered credentials and approved a multi-factor authentication push notification, granting the attacker a short-lived session with view-only access to ReliaQuest's identity dashboard.

The breach did not reach customer data, business applications, or internal systems. ReliaQuest's layered identity and device security controls enabled rapid containment. The company publicly denied claims of a broader ransomware compromise. After ReliaQuest's threat research team posted on X about ShinyHunters registering .claims domains to impersonate help desks, a newly created account linked to the attackers replied with screenshots of what appeared to be a compromised Okta SSO session for a ReliaQuest employee. ShinyHunters subsequently published the same screenshots on their data leak site. Both posts were later removed from X.

The incident highlights the .claims TLD as an emerging phishing infrastructure choice and the effectiveness of vishing combined with MFA push fatigue. ShinyHunters demonstrated willingness to publicly retaliate against a cybersecurity firm that called out their campaign, using the confrontation itself as a extortion lever. Organizations should audit lookalike domain registrations under newer TLDs and consider phishing-resistant MFA methods like FIDO2 keys that cannot be approved via push notification.
