ReliaQuest disclosed a targeted social engineering attack detected on August 22, 2026. Threat actors registered a domain mimicking a legitimate ReliaQuest property and deployed a counterfeit SSO page behind a CDN to obscure hosting infrastructure. They then called multiple employees, impersonating ReliaQuest security team members by name, directing targets to the fake authentication portal.

One employee entered credentials on the fraudulent page and approved an MFA push notification, giving the attackers a short-lived session with view-level access to ReliaQuest's identity dashboard. Layered identity and device security controls limited the scope and enabled rapid containment. No customer data, business applications, or internal systems were accessed. ReliaQuest explicitly stated that claims of a ransomware compromise or broader breach are false.

The attack highlights the continued effectiveness of MFA push fatigue combined with phone-based impersonation. Even organizations with strong defensive postures remain vulnerable when employees can be socially engineered into approving authentication prompts. The use of CDN-backed disposable phishing infrastructure and employee-specific targeting (by name, by role) suggests reconnaissance prior to the attack.
