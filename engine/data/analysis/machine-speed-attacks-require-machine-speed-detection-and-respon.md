This story tracks the shift toward machine-speed attacks and the matching need for machine-speed detection and response. Recent entries include a China-linked multi-agent system called CARLIY0S that coordinated attacks against more than 400 financial institutions and payment providers across Latin America using commercial AI models such as Claude, Grok, and Kimi, plus automation and shared infrastructure. Other entries cover GitLab CVE-2026-110264 (CVSS 10.0), a critical flaw in self-managed GitLab, and ShinyHunters exploiting Oracle PeopleSoft CVE-2026-35273 as a zero-day for two weeks before adapting to bypass a WAF rule.

The Oracle E-Business Suite CVE-2026-101767 (CVSS 9.8) also appears in this bucket: threat actors began probing for the SQL injection flaw in the Application Object Library after Oracle's October CPU, with Censys identifying 2,246 exposed instances. Separately, the story includes actively exploited older flaws such as CVE-2024-9537 in ScienceLogic SL1 and CVE-2024-40711, a deserialization bug in Veeam Backup and Replication, plus CISA and FBI warnings about SafePay ransomware actors targeting critical infrastructure and Operation IXIM, which prevented a botnet of millions of Popcorn Time users.

Taken together, the entries show attackers compressing reconnaissance, exploitation, and lateral movement into automated pipelines while defenders face unpatched edge cases, ransomware pressure, and high-severity flaws in widely deployed platforms. The common thread is that patching and detection windows are shrinking, so prioritized remediation of internet-facing and management-plane systems matters more than broad but slow patch cycles.
