A.P. Moller-Maersk confirmed on November 11, 2026 that an attacker gained unauthorized access to one of its systems and extracted data from it. The affected system was inherited through an earlier acquisition, which Maersk has not named publicly. Ransomware group WhiteArmor had listed Maersk on its leak site on November 7, demanding $5 million to withhold roughly 100GB of data. The alleged haul is tied to LF Logistics, the Hong Kong logistics firm Maersk acquired in 2022, and is claimed to include about 29,000 driver records, of which 16,093 contain plaintext passwords, plus 16,123 staff accounts.

Maersk says its cybersecurity team found the intrusion, launched an investigation, and is securing the affected system. Its public position is that findings so far strongly suggest no sensitive data has been disclosed, that the incident is isolated to a local legacy system, and that there is no indication of impact on other Maersk systems or on customer and partner systems connected through APIs and EDI. The company does not expect operational disruption, and its ocean, terminal, and booking platforms remain available.

The tension worth watching is between Maersk's containment language and WhiteArmor's claim of 100GB including tens of thousands of records with plaintext credentials. If the plaintext-password claim holds, driver and staff accounts inherited via LF Logistics are exposed regardless of Maersk's "no sensitive data disclosed" framing. Watch for whether Maersk names the acquired entity, whether WhiteArmor publishes a sample as proof, and whether credential-stuffing or phishing against the exposed accounts follows.
