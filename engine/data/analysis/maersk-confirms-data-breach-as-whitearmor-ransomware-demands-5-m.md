Maersk confirmed a data breach affecting systems belonging to a previously acquired company after the WhiteArmor ransomware group claimed an attack over the weekend of November 7, 2026 and threatened to release roughly 100 GB of data allegedly exfiltrated from Maersk's logistics and supply-chain platform. The group demanded $5 million in bitcoin with a 30-day deadline. Maersk states the incident is isolated to a local system tied to the former LF Logistics business (OCF platform), that it has found no indication of impact on other Maersk systems or customer and partner integrations such as APIs and EDI, that no sensitive data has been disclosed, and that operations and booking are unaffected. The claimed stolen data, per ransomware.live and f4n6, includes 16,093 driver records with plaintext passwords, 16,123 staff accounts including 33 SUPER_ADMIN accounts, and 27,842 mobile device logs, though CyPro noted the claim remained unverified and unsupported by public evidence as of November 7. A.P. Moller-Maersk operates across roughly 130 countries with 700 container ships and about 100,000 employees.

The exposure of plaintext passwords and privileged SUPER_ADMIN accounts, if the leaked sample is genuine, would give attackers direct access to logistics workflows and potentially to connected partner systems, making credential rotation and privileged-account review urgent regardless of Maersk's assurance that the breach is isolated. The incident also illustrates third-party and acquired-entity risk: a localized legacy platform became the entry point for an extortion claim against one of the world's largest container carriers.

Customers and partners should treat any credentials tied to the OCF/LF Logistics platform as compromised, force password resets including on the reported SUPER_ADMIN accounts, and review access logs for the affected window. Maersk should publish which data types were accessed once the investigation concludes. Watch for WhiteArmor follow-through on the threatened 100 GB release and for independent verification of the claimed record counts.
