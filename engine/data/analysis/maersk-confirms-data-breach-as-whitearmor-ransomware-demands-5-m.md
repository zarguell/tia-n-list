Maersk confirmed on November 11, 2026 that an intruder gained unauthorized access and extracted data from a local legacy IT system inherited through a previous acquisition. The WhiteArmor ransomware group had listed Maersk on its leak site on November 7, demanding a $5 million ransom and threatening to publish roughly 100GB of allegedly exfiltrated data. WhiteArmor attributes the data to a platform associated with LF Logistics, a Hong Kong-based logistics company Maersk acquired in 2022, though Maersk has not identified the acquired business. Maersk states the incident is isolated to the local system, with no indication of impact on other Maersk systems or on customer and partner systems connected through APIs and EDI, and says it does not anticipate disruptions to booking or shipment management.

WhiteArmor claims the haul includes about 29,000 driver records, of which 16,093 contain plaintext passwords, plus 16,123 staff accounts. Maersk counters that its findings so far strongly suggest no sensitive data has been disclosed, while emphasizing that the investigation is ongoing and that it has not detailed the affected data types. The gap between the attacker's claims and the company's assessment remains unresolved, and Maersk has not confirmed whether the published figures are accurate.

What to watch is Maersk's final determination on the scope of exposed data and any affected individuals, whether WhiteArmor publishes the claimed 100GB, and whether the alleged plaintext passwords enable credential-stuffing against driver and staff accounts. Customers, partners, and former LF Logistics users should treat Maersk-linked credentials as potentially exposed and watch for follow-up disclosures or required password resets.
