A.P. Moller-Maersk confirmed on 2026-11-11 that a threat actor gained unauthorized access to a local legacy IT system inherited from a previous acquisition and extracted data. The company says the incident is isolated to that system, that it sees no impact on other Maersk systems or on customer and partner integrations such as APIs and EDI, and that current evidence indicates no sensitive data was disclosed. The investigation is ongoing and Maersk says it does not expect operational disruption; booking and shipment management continue through its normal systems.

The claim came from the WhiteArmor ransomware group, which listed Maersk on its leak site on 2026-11-07, seven days earlier. The group alleged roughly 100 GB stolen from the OCF / LF Logistics platform and demanded USD 5,000,000 in bitcoin within 30 days. Backfill records attribute to f4n6 a sample of the claimed data: 16,093 driver records with plaintext passwords, 16,123 staff accounts including 33 SUPER_ADMIN accounts, and 27,842 mobile device logs. DeXpose, CyPro and HIFY Radar tracked the same listing. None of the claim details are independently verified by Maersk, which has only confirmed access and extraction from a legacy system.

Why it matters is the target, not the technical novelty. Maersk operates about 700 container ships, roughly 13.5% of global fleet capacity, 60 terminals in 35 countries, and reported $54 billion in revenue and $2.9 billion net profit last year. Logistics and freight systems are attractive for extortion because downtime is expensive and customer integrations are broad, and a legacy acquisition is a plausible blind spot in the security program. The plaintext driver passwords and SUPER_ADMIN accounts in the claimed dump, if real, point at credentials that would be reusable against related platforms.

What to watch is whether WhiteArmor publishes a sample and whether the stolen data traces to the LF Logistics acquisition, which Maersk has not named. The 30-day deadline from 2026-11-07 runs to early December. Maersk's statement that no sensitive data was disclosed is preliminary and may change if the leak site sample matches real records.
