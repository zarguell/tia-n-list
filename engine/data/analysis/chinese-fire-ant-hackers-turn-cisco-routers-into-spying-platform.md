Sygnia researchers disclosed that the Chinese-nexus threat actor Fire Ant has shifted tactics from targeting VMware hypervisors to compromising Cisco IOS XR routers. The discovery came after an active GRE tunnel interface was found on a router that had no corresponding entry in the running configuration or commit history. The attackers deployed custom malware that created a fake system service running the implant only during alternating hours, and selectively suppressed syslog messages to hide tunnel activity from administrators.

Fire Ant also compromised TACACS authentication servers and Linux management hosts to support the router operations. The malware established outbound Telnet connections to attacker-controlled infrastructure and provided interactive shell access with no logging. The group captured traffic from multiple routers and exfiltrated PCAP files to external FTP servers, exposing internal topology, authentication flows, and routing relationships.

This represents a significant escalation in network infrastructure targeting. Routers are typically treated as trusted transit devices and receive far less security scrutiny than endpoints or servers. Fire Ant's ability to maintain persistent access on Cisco IOS XR devices, suppress logging, and capture network traffic gives them deep visibility into victim environments. Organizations running Cisco IOS XR should audit configurations for unexplained GRE tunnel interfaces, review TACACS server integrity, and inspect router logs for gaps that might indicate log suppression.
