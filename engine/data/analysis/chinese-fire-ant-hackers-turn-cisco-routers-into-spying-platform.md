Chinese state-sponsored group Fire Ant has expanded from VMware hypervisor targeting into Cisco IOS XR router compromise, according to Sygnia's investigation. The actor plants GRE tunnel interfaces on routers with no matching configuration or commit history, then deploys custom malware that persists through a fake system service running only during alternating hours. The implant selectively suppresses syslog messages to hide tunnel activity, opens outbound Telnet connections to Fire Ant infrastructure, and provides interactive shell access without logging.

The operation extends beyond routers. Fire Ant also compromised TACACS authentication servers and Linux management hosts, gaining access to the trusted infrastructure layer. Attackers used their administrative access to capture router traffic and exfiltrate PCAP files to external FTP servers, potentially exposing internal topology, admin connections, authentication flows, and routing relationships. This transforms routers from transit devices into persistent espionage platforms.

First reported in 2025 targeting VMware environments, Fire Ant's pivot to network infrastructure represents a significant escalation. The ability to suppress logs and manipulate router output means defenders cannot rely on standard syslog or administrative visibility to detect compromise. Organizations running Cisco IOS XR should audit for unexplained GRE tunnel interfaces and review TACACS server logs for anomalous authentication patterns.
