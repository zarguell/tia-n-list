Microsoft Threat Intelligence documented two human-operated intrusions that began with cross-tenant Microsoft Teams messages and calls impersonating IT or help desk staff, socially engineering users into granting interactive remote access through Quick Assist. No vulnerability in Teams was exploited; the decisive transition was the user-approved remote session. Operators then staged portable Python, Bun, or Java runtimes in KB-prefixed directories under the user profile, ran host and network discovery, and assessed Active Directory Certificate Services for certificate-based authentication paths. The same initial access diverged at the certificate layer: in Case 1 an ESC1-vulnerable template allowed certificates for privileged accounts and DCSync, while in Case 2 PetitPotam coerced a domain controller over MS-EFSRPC and the relayed NTLM authentication was abused via ESC8 against AD CS Web Enrollment to obtain a Domain Controller certificate used for PKINIT. Microsoft published the research on October 30, 2026 and does not attribute the activity to a specific actor.

The argument of the case is not the certificate technique but the containment gap. Case 1 first yielded certificates for three highly privileged accounts, and Defender attack disruption restricted those identities plus the initial user, but one privileged account was excluded from automated response and stayed usable. About 90 minutes later the operator ran a larger Teams-phishing wave against 16 users, compromised a second workstation, and pulled certificates for eight more privileged accounts; more than 1,000 SMB file-access attempts and additional logons were blocked. Within seconds of obtaining one certificate the operator authenticated to a domain controller and started DCSync before containment took effect. The excluded identity then carried the actual damage: network and remote-interactive logons to a file server, followed by a renamed Rclone binary (SearchApp.exe) recursively copying data to an external SFTP destination with 20 concurrent transfers, files up to three years old, and exclusions for executable, database, virtual-disk, media, temporary, and log files. Case 2 shows the opposite outcome: Defender generated ESC8 alerts, the customer isolated the workstation tied to the enrollment and Kerberos activity, and no further progression was observed. The lesson is that the durable harm came from an identity left out of automated containment, not from the absence of a patch, and that certificate issuance outlives both password resets and workstation reimaging.

Defenders should treat unsolicited help desk contact followed by Quick Assist as a high-risk pattern and harden both the collaboration and certificate paths. Audit AD CS templates for requester-supplied subject (CT_FLAG_ENROLLEE_SUPPLIES_SUBJECT), broad enrollment rights, and weak issuance requirements; remove unnecessary Web Enrollment and Certificate Enrollment Web Service endpoints and enforce Extended Protection for Authentication and channel binding on the remaining IIS endpoints per KB5005413. Use Defender for Identity AD CS security posture assessments to surface ESC1 template exposure and insecure enrollment IIS endpoints. Apply App Control for Business to block unapproved portable runtimes and script execution from user-writable paths, and test attack surface reduction rules since Bun and Java execution is not covered by Windows script host controls. Hunt for Bun or Java launching scripts from AppData\Roaming\KB[0-9]+ with connections on ports 80, 445, 636, and 88; correlate CloudAppEvents Teams external chats with Quick Assist process starts. If an unauthorized certificate was issued, locate its serial number and thumbprint in CA records, revoke it, publish updated revocation information, and separately review active Kerberos TGTs and service tickets because revocation does not invalidate tickets already issued. Preserve CA security events, IIS and Web Enrollment logs, endpoint process lineage, identity alerts, and Teams interaction records; known SHA-256 indicators include 85b6c424117494d3056e88689dbc94c75e9afdb187a339714043aec0da109f17, f326201269f1ee2f803ae435317507263bb6e0dbfdeb70bd1bb5446e2e46f001, and 06c5c14f043aee3bcc5843b5f50909623d5c9a6239a7c1fba9cddad4111469e7.

The assessment would change if Microsoft closes the containment-exclusion gap by default in attack disruption, if automated certificate revocation and Kerberos ticket invalidation become part of the response playbook, or if the same help desk impersonation pattern migrates to other approved support channels beyond Quick Assist. It would also worsen if operators begin chaining ESC9 through ESC16 variants or targeting certificate renewal and CA key material, which would extend persistence beyond the single issued certificate.
