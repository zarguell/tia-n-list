Trezor disclosed that its third-party email provider was breached and used to send phishing emails to cryptocurrency users. The attacker sent messages from help@trezor.io with the subject "Critical Security Alert: STM32 Entropy Vulnerability," claiming that a hardware flaw in the STM32 microcontrollers used by Trezor wallets could expose seed phrases to brute-force cracking. Trezor took down the compromised domain and said it is investigating how attackers gained access to its legitimate sending infrastructure.

The phishing campaign extended beyond Trezor. Users of BitBox and CoinTracking also reported receiving similar "Critical Security Alert" emails, suggesting the breach hit a shared email service provider. Security researcher Zack Whittaker and others identified Brevo as the likely email platform involved, though neither Trezor nor Brevo has confirmed the specific provider.

The incident compounds Trezor's recent security troubles. In August, the company disclosed that attackers compromised ShipMonk, its shipping and logistics provider, stealing order data for nearly 14,000 customers. A subsequent investigation expanded that figure to 81,000 affected individuals across the US, Brazil, Colombia, and Italy. The combination of a supply-chain data breach and a compromised email channel puts crypto holders on high alert for targeted social engineering.
