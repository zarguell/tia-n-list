Queens Public Library, which serves roughly 2.3 million residents across 66 branches, was hit by a ransomware attack on November 4, 2026, that encrypted systems and took down the library's website, public Wi-Fi, mobile app, on-site computers, checkout and return machines, and digital media services. Branches stayed open during the outage, late-return fines were suspended, and no restoration date was given. No ransomware gang had claimed the attack as of Thursday evening, and the library had not disclosed whether patron data was stolen.

The scope of disruption across all 66 branches at once shows how a single ransomware incident can shut down both digital and physical services for a public institution, since checkout and return machines were affected alongside online services. The library issued multiple alerts on Wednesday, November 4, and continued urging patrons to visit branches in person while systems were restored.

What to watch: whether a ransomware gang eventually claims responsibility and publishes data, which would clarify whether this was a data-theft extortion or encryption-only incident. Restoration progress and any disclosure of patron data exposure matter for 2.3 million residents. Public libraries remain attractive targets because of limited security budgets and broad public access, so the recovery approach here may inform peer institutions. No ransomware group claim as of the last update means attribution and motive remain open.
