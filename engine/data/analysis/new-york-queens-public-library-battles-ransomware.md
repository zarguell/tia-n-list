Queens Public Library, serving 2.3 million residents across 66 branches in New York City, was hit by a ransomware attack on November 4, 2026. The library's website, public Wi-Fi, mobile app, on-site computers, and checkout and return machines were knocked offline, while physical branches stayed open and late-return fines were waived. The library issued multiple alerts on Wednesday, and The Register reported the outage on November 5.

Why it matters: a public library system is critical civic infrastructure, and the outage cut off internet access, digital media, and basic circulation for millions of residents who rely on it for connectivity and services. No ransomware gang had claimed responsibility as of Thursday evening, and no restoration date has been provided, leaving the recovery timeline and any data-exfiltration scope unknown.

What to watch: whether a ransomware group publicly claims the attack and posts stolen data, the restoration timeline for Wi-Fi, checkout systems, and the mobile app, and whether any personal data of patrons or staff was exfiltrated. The lack of a named actor and no provided recovery date suggest the investigation is still early.
