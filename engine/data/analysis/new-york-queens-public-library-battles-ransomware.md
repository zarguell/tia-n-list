Queens Public Library, which serves roughly 2.3 million residents across 66 branches, was hit by a ransomware attack that encrypted systems and took down its website, public Wi-Fi, mobile app, on-site computers, checkout and return machines, and digital media services. Branches stayed open during the outage, late-return fines were suspended, and no restoration date was given in the early notices. The Record reported that no ransomware gang had claimed the attack as of Thursday evening, and the library had not said whether patron data was taken.

The disruption crossed the digital and physical boundary at once: checkout and return machines failed alongside online services, so the incident affected library operations even where buildings remained open. The library's own November 23 update says most services are back online and cites encouraging indicators that no personal information was accessed. That is not a final all-clear, and the update does not name a ransomware group or describe the entry path.

What to watch now is whether a gang claims the attack or publishes data, which would settle the question of data-theft extortion versus encryption-only impact. Confirmation of the personal-data assessment and any incident report from the library matter for the 2.3 million residents it serves. The update from the library itself is the first concrete recovery signal since the November 4 outage.
