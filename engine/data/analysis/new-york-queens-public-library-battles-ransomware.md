Queens Public Library (QPL) confirmed on Wednesday, November 4, 2026 that a ransomware gang had encrypted its systems, knocking out the website, on-site computers, checkout and return machines, public Wi-Fi, mobile app, and digital media services across all 66 branches. The disruption continued into Thursday, and the library said it would take time before the network and systems are restored. No ransomware group has claimed responsibility, and QPL has not said whether patron or employee data was accessed. Chief Executive Dennis Walcott said the library is working with the NYPD, the FBI, City Hall, and external cybersecurity experts.

The outage affects the borough's 2.3 million residents, who lose free access to more than 5 million books in 50 languages and to dozens of learning centers in public housing complexes plus several mobile libraries. Branches stay open and late fines are suspended, but anyone reliant on the catalog, public computers, or Wi-Fi is cut off until the network is rebuilt. This is not a one-off event: public libraries are repeat ransomware targets that hold significant population data, and recoveries are slow and costly. The British Library's 2023 Rhysida incident was still incomplete as of its 2025-26 annual report, and QPL faces the same rebuild with no named attacker and no stated ransom.

Defenders should treat this as an active branch-network incident with no restoration date and no patch to apply, since no vulnerability or CVE is involved. QPL has moved to manual, in-person checkouts and suspended late fines, which is the correct containment posture for public-facing services. The hunt should focus on encryption activity, ransom notes, and unauthorized remote access across the branch network's endpoint and authentication telemetry, with logs retained from branch infrastructure and the integrated library system. Staff and patrons should be warned about follow-up phishing that pretends to be a restoration notice.

Watch for a ransomware claim or a leak-site post naming QPL, which would shift the incident from encryption-only to data-theft extortion. Also watch for restoration timing and for any spillover to the Brooklyn or New York Public Library systems, which joined QPL in ending late fines in October 2021 and may share vendors or infrastructure.
