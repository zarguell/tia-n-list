The DOJ unsealed a 14-count indictment charging 17 Iranian nationals linked to the Mabna Institute with conducting cyber theft campaigns targeting American and foreign institutions on behalf of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. The indictment, announced Tuesday, names the defendants as members of an Iran-based company that has allegedly conducted sustained intrusions against universities, corporations, and government agencies for years. Five of the defendants have $10 million rewards posted for information leading to their arrest.

The Mabna Institute has been previously identified in connection with the "Alan Turing" and "Operation APT35" campaigns. The indictment covers a broad range of activity including credential theft, spear-phishing, and network intrusions across multiple countries. The reward amounts signal the FBI considers these individuals high-priority targets and that attribution is firm.

This is a continuation of the US government's strategy of indicting foreign hackers even when arrest is unlikely. The indictments serve as a deterrent signal and provide legal basis for asset seizures and travel restrictions. The $10 million bounties on five defendants match the amounts offered for top-tier nation-state hackers, suggesting the FBI believes these individuals are actively conducting operations or have critical knowledge of IRGC cyber capabilities.
