Denmark's Central Person Register (CPR) has now publicly confirmed unauthorized access to personal data covering roughly 8.8 million of the 11 million registered individuals, about 80 percent of the registry, including people living in Denmark, Danes who moved abroad, and deceased persons. Exposed data includes names, addresses, CPR numbers, and other registry content. Danish authorities are now actively warning citizens about phishing and fraud attempts in the wake of the breach, advising people never to give out passwords or confidential information on request.

The attack path is unusual and instructive: threat actors misused a private Danish company's legitimate access to the registry. The Data Protection Agency says the attack involved brute-forcing to enumerate valid CPR numbers and then extracting the related entries. The incident occurred in September 2026; the CPR administration noticed irregular behavior on the evening of October 2 and sized the impact over the weekend. The company's access has been blocked and police are investigating. Denmark's minister informed Parliament's Business and Digitalization Committee.

A national registry compromised through a third party's lawful access channel is a supply-chain breach of identity data at near-total population scale. CPR numbers underpin Danish banking, healthcare, and tax interactions, so the fraud exposure will play out for years, and the brute-force detail implies the lookup interface lacked adequate rate limiting or anomaly controls. The public fraud warnings indicate authorities expect abuse to start quickly.

Watch for identification of the company and how its credentials were compromised, the timeline of the September activity versus the October detection, measurable phishing and fraud waves against CPR holders, and regulatory consequences for both the company and the registry's access controls.
