Microsoft Teams suffered two distinct service disruptions in 2026 that this story tracks. On August 26, 2026, maintenance activity had an unintended impact on the Teams service, degrading meeting creation, joining, and screen sharing for users in the Asia-Pacific region while one-to-one calls stayed available; Microsoft halted the maintenance and reported the issue largely mitigated by 7:26 a.m. IST, monitoring for another 15 to 30 minutes under incident TM1461801. On November 12, 2026, a wider Microsoft 365 outage tracked as advisory MO1550821 began at 1:26 p.m. ET after earlier Exchange Online mailbox connectivity problems in the Americas, affecting Exchange Online, Teams, SharePoint Online, Microsoft 365 Copilot, and Microsoft Defender XDR across multiple regions.

The August event was a scoped meeting-plane degradation with a known trigger, maintenance, while the November event was a multi-service cascade in which servers became unexpectedly unavailable and strained the remaining infrastructure without any increase in traffic. That difference matters for defenders because the November blast radius couples email, collaboration, and security tooling in one window, meaning detection and response capability can be impaired at the same moment the channels used to coordinate response are down. Microsoft reported no evidence of a cyberattack in the November advisory, so the priority is availability and resilience rather than an intrusion hunt, though the combination of failed automated health checks and elevated processor usage on affected servers points to a failure mode worth baselining. The story is worth reading among routine outage noise because it documents that health-check component as the suspected choke point rather than capacity.

For now there is no customer patch for either incident. Actions are continuity and verification: monitor the Microsoft 365 admin center service health and status page for advisories TM1461801 and MO1550821, keep backup conference bridges and alternate channels ready, and treat a broad collaboration outage as a continuity event for SOC huddles, incident bridges, and executive escalations. During the August incident, users and administrators were advised to restart clients and switch between desktop, web, and mobile rather than assume local configuration issues, and to verify meeting creation and joining across all platforms after mitigation. In the November incident, Microsoft was testing a workaround that bypasses the component processing automated health checks while investigating elevated processor usage, so the practical hunt is service-health telemetry and user-reported meeting and mailbox failures rather than a specific indicator of compromise.

What to watch is Microsoft's post-incident explanation of why the health checks failed and why processor usage rose, whether the November root cause is eventually linked to the Windows updates released two days earlier, and whether the server-unavailability pattern recurs. Any disclosure of malicious activity would change the assessment from an availability problem to an incident response problem, but at present the evidence supports only the former.
