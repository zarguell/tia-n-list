Microsoft 365 suffered a widespread outage on Thursday, November 12, 2026, that disrupted Exchange Online, Microsoft Teams, SharePoint Online, Microsoft 365 Copilot, and Microsoft Defender XDR across multiple regions. The incident was tracked as advisory MO1550821 and began at 1:26 PM ET, after earlier Exchange Online mailbox connectivity problems in the Americas. Downdetector showed a surge of user reports across Outlook, Teams, Copilot, Azure, OneDrive, and the Microsoft Store. Microsoft determined that the elevated load was not caused by increased traffic but by a number of servers becoming unexpectedly unavailable, straining the remaining infrastructure. Affected servers were failing automated health checks, and Microsoft was testing a workaround that bypasses the component processing those checks while also investigating elevated processor usage.

Microsoft said it had found no evidence of a cyberattack at the time of publication, and its service advisories did not report malicious activity. Some Google Workspace users also reported trouble accessing Google-hosted email through Outlook desktop and mobile clients. The disruption came two days after Microsoft released recent Windows updates, though the company has not linked the two. Microsoft has not yet explained why the health checks failed or why processor usage rose, and the root cause of the server unavailability remains under investigation.

What to watch is Microsoft's post-incident explanation and whether the health-check component proves to be a recurring failure mode. Organizations that depend on Microsoft 365 for incident bridges, SOC huddles, and executive escalations should treat a broad collaboration outage as a continuity event, keep backup conference bridges and alternate communication channels ready, and monitor the Microsoft 365 admin center and status page for further updates.
