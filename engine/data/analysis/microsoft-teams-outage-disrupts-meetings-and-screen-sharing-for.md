Microsoft 365 has had two outages in three months that this story tracks together. On August 26, 2026, maintenance activity degraded Teams for users in Asia-Pacific. Microsoft's status account pointed to incident TM1461801, said the maintenance was halted, and reported the service back to standard availability. Meeting creation, joining meetings and screen sharing failed while one-to-one calls kept working, and customer notes posted at 6:56 and 7:26 a.m. IST described the issue as largely mitigated with 15 to 30 minutes of monitoring to follow.

The November 12, 2026 incident was wider. Advisory MO1550821 lists Exchange Online, Teams, SharePoint Online, Microsoft 365 Copilot and Defender XDR as services customers may be unable to connect to, starting at 1:26 PM ET, with Downdetector complaints across Outlook, Teams, Copilot, Azure, OneDrive and the Microsoft Store. Microsoft first attributed the disruption to elevated infrastructure load, then said servers had unexpectedly become unavailable and were straining the remaining infrastructure, that affected servers were failing automated health checks, and that it was testing a workaround that bypasses the component processing those checks. It also reported elevated processor usage on the affected servers and said it had no evidence of a cyberattack. The Register tied the same outage to email and Intune, and Reuters reported that the AI tools plugged into Microsoft 365 went down with it and that one publication slipped as a result.

Both events are continuity problems rather than intrusions, but the blast radius is the story. A tenant that cannot reach Exchange Online, Intune or Defender XDR loses its management plane at the moment its incident-response tooling is degraded. Watch for Microsoft's postmortem on why the health checks failed and why processor usage rose, and for whether the same server-eviction pattern repeats. Teams-dependent organizations should keep an out-of-band conference bridge for incident calls, and IT should confirm that Intune and Defender policy enforcement survives a cloud-side authentication failure.
