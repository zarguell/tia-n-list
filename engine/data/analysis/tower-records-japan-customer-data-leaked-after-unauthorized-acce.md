Tower Records Japan disclosed the incident on 21 November 2026 after confirming external unauthorized access to Tower Records Online at 17:10 on 19 November. The company's notice describes about 11 million affected records, broken out as 10,214,652 login records, 2,784,514 member records and 776,953 delivery records. Exposed fields include email addresses, member IDs, password hashes, names, dates of birth, gender, delivery addresses and phone numbers. Former members who left within the previous three years are included. The company said credit card data was not involved and that no secondary damage had been confirmed as of 21 November.

Coverage from Nikkei, Bloomberg, Japan Times and local outlets repeat the 11 million figure and note the online service was suspended. The reviewed notice does not state how attackers got in. Password hashes combined with names, addresses and phone numbers create phishing and credential-reuse exposure for affected members, and the inclusion of former members widens the notification problem.

Watch for an update on the intrusion route, any revision to the record counts, and whether Tower Records offers password resets or monitoring to the roughly 11 million people in the affected sets. The absence of confirmed secondary damage on 21 November is a point-in-time statement, not a finding that abuse stopped.
