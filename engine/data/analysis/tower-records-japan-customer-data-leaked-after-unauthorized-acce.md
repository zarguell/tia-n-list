Tower Records Japan confirmed that external unauthorized access to Tower Records Online was detected around 17:10 JST on 19 November 2026, with the company disclosing the leak on 21 November. The exposed material was split into 10,214,652 login records (email addresses, member IDs and password hashes), 2,784,514 member records (names, furigana, nicknames, dates of birth, gender and postal codes), and 776,953 delivery records (addresses, recipient names and phone numbers). The company said no credit card information was included and reported no confirmed secondary damage as of 21 November; no CVE, intrusion method, patch version or IoC list has been published.

The 11 million headline undersells the credential problem. More than 10 million of those records are login material, so the first-order risk is password reuse against other services, not card fraud; hashed passwords still support offline cracking, and the accompanying names, birth dates and delivery addresses make phishing look like a real order notification. Former members who left within three years are in scope, which extends exposure past the current customer base. The three category counts sum to more than 11 million, so they cannot be treated as unique victims, and the two-day gap between detection on 19 November and broad reporting on 22 November matters because credential testing does not wait for a press cycle.

Reset Tower Records passwords and any password reused with the same email address, then enable multifactor authentication on the email account and other reuse targets. Watch authentication logs for failed or successful logins from new locations against the affected addresses and rate-limit login endpoints where Tower Records customer emails are used as identifiers. The disclosure names no log path, port or IoC, so the usable hunt keys are the published fields themselves: member email addresses, member IDs, names, dates of birth and delivery addresses; use them to find phishing and account-takeover attempts rather than waiting for a malware signature. The online service was suspended after the 19 November detection, and the company's notice at tower.jp is the place to check for restoration and updated instructions.

Watch for two developments that would change this assessment: disclosure of the intrusion route and the password-hash algorithm, which would set the real cracking timeline, and a revised unique-victim count or confirmed secondary damage after 21 November. Evidence of credential-stuffing against the exposed email addresses would move this from a contained retail breach to an active identity-feeder event.
