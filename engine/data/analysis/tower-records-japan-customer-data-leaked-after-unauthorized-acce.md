Tower Records Japan disclosed on 21 November 2026 that customer data from Tower Records Online was exposed after unauthorized access around 17:00 on 19 November. Company notices give 10,214,652 login records, 2,784,514 member records and 776,953 delivery records, roughly 11 million records in total. The affected fields include email addresses, member IDs, password hashes, names, dates of birth, gender, delivery addresses and phone numbers. Former members who left within the previous three years are included. Credit card data was not part of the set, and the company reported no confirmed secondary damage as of 21 November.

The intrusion route was not stated in the company notice, and the service was suspended after discovery. Nikkei reported the same counts and timing on 22 November, while Bloomberg and other outlets framed the disclosure as the latest breach at a Japanese retailer. The detailed per-category counts are more useful than the rounded headline because they show which systems were touched: login records, member profiles and delivery records, rather than a payment system.

Password hashes are the immediate concern, especially for customers who reused Tower Records passwords on other services. The exposure of names, birth dates, addresses and phone numbers also gives phishers enough context to make contact appear legitimate. Watch for an explanation of how the attacker got in, whether the hashes were salted, and any later report of credential stuffing or fraud tied to the leaked accounts.
