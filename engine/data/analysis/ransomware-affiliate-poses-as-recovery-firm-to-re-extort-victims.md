A threat actor operating under the name "Ransom Busters" is posing as an independent data recovery service to contact ransomware victims, often before the underlying attack has been publicly disclosed. GuidePoint Security's Research and Intelligence Team (GRIT) assesses with moderate confidence that this operation is not a legitimate recovery firm but rather a ransomware affiliate running a diversion scheme. The actor reaches out to victims offering to retrieve stolen files, destroy backups held by criminal infrastructure, and provide decryption keys, presenting itself as a helpful third party that has supposedly breached the ransomware gangs' servers.

The scheme targets victims of DragonForce, Settra, and Anubis ransomware families. Instead of relying solely on a standard ransom note, the suspected affiliate intercepts the extortion process by inserting itself as an intermediary. By claiming to have compromised the original ransomware operation's infrastructure, the actor attempts to redirect extortion payments away from the primary ransomware group and toward itself. This represents a novel double-extortion layer where victims already facing data theft and encryption are approached with a second, conflicting solicitation that exploits their urgency to resolve the incident.

The tactic is notable for how it weaponizes the victim's own crisis. A company mid-incident, with encrypted systems and stolen data on the line, is in no position to verify whether a cold outreach from a "recovery firm" is legitimate. GRIT's assessment carries moderate confidence rather than definitive attribution, suggesting the affiliate's operational infrastructure overlaps with known ransomware ecosystem patterns but hasn't been conclusively mapped to a single group. Security teams should treat unsolicited recovery offers during active ransomware incidents as highly suspicious and cross-reference any such contact with incident response timelines and known threat intelligence on the specific ransomware family involved.
