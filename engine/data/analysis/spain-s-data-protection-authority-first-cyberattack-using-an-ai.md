Spain's data protection authority (AEPD) has received what it describes as the first breach notification involving an AI agent as the primary attack tool. The incident, reported by an affected organization, involved an AI agent powered by a known large language model that autonomously searched for vulnerabilities, logged into systems, probed applications for additional weaknesses, modified personal data, and accessed financial invoices. The AEPD has not yet completed its investigation but says the notification demonstrates that AI-driven data breaches are no longer theoretical.

The agency emphasized that this is qualitatively different from AI-assisted attacks like deepfakes or automated phishing. Here, a third party deployed an AI agent that independently planned intermediate tasks, used tools, executed code, interpreted results, and adapted its approach based on findings. The attacking agent chained together multiple phases of the intrusion autonomously, with the speed and adaptability that characterizes agentic AI systems. The AEPD's description suggests the agent operated without continuous human direction once given an initial goal.

AEPD deputy director Francisco Perez Bes wrote that organizations must now explicitly account for AI-driven attacks in risk assessments, noting that generic references to malware or unauthorized access are insufficient. He warned that incident response procedures designed for manually executed attacks may be too slow against AI-driven ones, and that compromised digital credentials and API keys become critical risk factors since they allow attackers to move at machine speed. This incident will likely influence how European regulators approach AI-related threat modeling.
