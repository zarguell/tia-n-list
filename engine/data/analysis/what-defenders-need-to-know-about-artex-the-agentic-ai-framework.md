Microsoft Threat Intelligence published a November 25 report on ARTEX, a publicly available AI-powered offensive security framework released in July 2026. Microsoft says state-backed and financially motivated actors had adopted it by November 2026, naming Storm-0940, Storm-3358, Onyx Sleet, Pearl Sleet, Crystal Typhoon, and Salt Typhoon. The reported uses are network reconnaissance, credential attacks, and web application exploitation. Operators define scope and targets, and the framework's agents set goals, plan tactical steps, call tools, track discovered systems in graphs, and report results back.

The report describes recent campaigns that used ARTEX to search public repositories for exposed secrets, support supply chain compromise, and scan internet-facing systems for exploitation. The Record's summary says Chinese and North Korean state-backed groups are using the platform and quotes Microsoft on Crystal Typhoon and Salt Typhoon integrating it into their tooling. That coverage appeared hours after reports from The New York Times and NextGov alleging ARTEX use against the CIA, the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency, and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence. Salt Typhoon is the group linked by US agencies to the 2024 breach of at least nine telecommunications companies.

The practical signal is not a new vulnerability but the automation of tasks that defenders already associate with human operators: secret discovery in public repos, external scanning, and follow-on exploitation. Microsoft's article includes ARTEX network and system activity indicators, Defender detections, hunting guidance, and MITRE ATT&CK mappings. Watch for reporting on exposed secrets being found faster, agent-driven scanning patterns against internet-facing systems, and whether the named actor campaigns produce additional victim disclosures.
