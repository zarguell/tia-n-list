Malware-Traffic-Analysis.net published an 2026-08-12 campaign write-up tracking a ClickFix infection chain run through SmartApeSG infrastructure that drops two remote access trojans. The post includes the HTTPS traffic capture, a pcap of traffic from both RATs, and the files recovered from the infection, so the delivery mechanics and post-exploitation behavior are fully documented for defenders to replay.

ClickFix continues to be a high-volume initial access technique: fake CAPTCHA or verification pages push users to paste a PowerShell payload, which pulls the next stage. The SmartApeSG naming tracks the campaign's command infrastructure, and the two RATs give the operators persistent interactive access rather than a single commodity payload, which raises the stakes for any environment that gets hit.

Watch for follow-on coverage naming the specific RAT families and any overlap with other ClickFix lures reported this week. Organizations should treat browser-based fake verification prompts as a reportable security event and review endpoint logs for PowerShell execution triggered outside normal admin workflows. The artifacts are password-protected on the site, per MTA's usual practice.
