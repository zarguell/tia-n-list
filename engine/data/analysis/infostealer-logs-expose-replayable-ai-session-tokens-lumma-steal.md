Okta's threat intelligence team analyzed a 7 GB infostealer dump released on Telegram on August 2, 2026, and found data from 5,871 infected machines in 162 countries. The dump held 44,791 unique JSON Web Tokens, of which 555 were authentication tokens for AI services including Google, Microsoft, Anthropic, Amazon, Cursor, Notion, and Character.ai, plus 2,937 encrypted JWE structures set mostly by OpenAI through NextAuth.js. 1,843 JWTs and JWEs were still unexpired the day the dump was published, and 17.7 percent of the JWTs contained plaintext PII such as names, phone numbers, and email addresses. A TruffleHog pass surfaced 24 still-valid API keys for Google Gemini, OpenAI, Groq, and OpenRouter.

The reason these tokens matter is that replaying a stolen session token or API key bypasses both passwords and MFA entirely. Okta and The Hacker News describe a ready market for this access: Telegram vendors selling accounts for Claude, Cursor, ChatGPT, and Gemini with support and refund guarantees, and services like Poison Claude reselling Anthropic model access, all served by anti-detect browsers such as Camoufox that load stolen session data and defeat impossible-travel checks. Google's GTIG separately reports rising buyer demand for AI accounts and intrusions staged to hijack enterprise compute. The new BleepingComputer report on SOCRadar findings widens the aperture: AI logins harvested by infostealers are tied to more than 80,000 corporate domains, connecting shadow AI usage to LLMjacking risk at organization scale.

Defenses exist but are uneven: IP allowlisting breaks replay, Google's Device Bound Session Credentials in Chrome cryptographically bind sessions to hardware, and scoped, short-lived OAuth tokens shrink the replay window. Watch how fast AI providers adopt device-bound sessions, whether enterprises inventory and monitor AI accounts the way they do SaaS identities, and whether token-bill fraud starts appearing in cloud cost anomalies rather than incident reports.
