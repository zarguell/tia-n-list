CISA's Continuous Diagnostics and Mitigation (CDM) program needs to move faster, according to Richard Grabowski, the program's acting branch chief and deputy program manager. Speaking at the Elastic Federal Cyber Defense Breakfast on September 15, Grabowski said current collaboration speed "wasn't fast enough for the threats of yesterday" and won't work for tomorrow's either. The CDM program, which provides cybersecurity tools and capabilities to federal agencies, is built around three core goals: velocity, unification, and data-driven risk management.

The velocity push centers on responsible automation of tasks at scale, so analysts can focus on novel threats and tuning advanced technology rather than triaging routine alerts. Unification means breaking down data silos across agency deployments to enable reusable, actionable lessons learned. Data-driven risk management aims to give agencies "timely, accurate, and trustworthy data" during crisis-level events so CDM can serve as a first-response tool.

The CDM program is one of CISA's largest operational programs, distributing security tools like Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) capabilities and endpoint detection to civilian federal agencies. A speed-up in deployment and automation could accelerate security posture improvements across the federal civilian enterprise, though the program has historically struggled with adoption and integration challenges at individual agencies.
