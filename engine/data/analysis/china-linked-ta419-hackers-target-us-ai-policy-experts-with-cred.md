China-aligned actor TA419 ran customized credential-phishing campaigns against U.S. AI policy experts at think tanks, universities, and law firms, per Proofpoint tracking reported by GBHackers and The Hacker News. The operation used adversary-in-the-middle infrastructure, a modified Browser-in-the-Browser toolkit, and reply-triggered lures to steal Microsoft 365 sessions. Targets work on issues that shape U.S. AI regulation and export controls, which points to intelligence collection rather than financial motive.

Fresh reporting on October 4 and 5 filled in the social engineering. Attackers impersonated economists, policymakers, a senior Anthropic staffer, and former government officials, including ex-White House OSTP official Lynne Edwards Parker. Lures included invitations to a fabricated AI Policy Advisory Committee and requests to contribute to a fictional Senate Foreign Relations report. One email used the subject line Request for Feedback on Military Integration of Claude. After a few exchanges, targets received malware-laced documents meant to reach their cloud accounts. Proofpoint researcher Mark Kelly assessed the cohort as Chinese government-aligned based on targeting, infrastructure, technical artifacts, and industry corroboration.

Expect continued impersonation of AI-industry identities in lures, since vendor names carry trust with policy audiences. Watch for the campaign moving from session theft toward document access inside policy organizations, and for TA419 infrastructure reuse across new target sets. Organizations in the AI policy space should enforce phishing-resistant MFA, shorten M365 session lifetimes, and verify unusual requests through a second channel.
