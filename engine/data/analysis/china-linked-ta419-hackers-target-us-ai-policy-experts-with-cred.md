TA419, a China-aligned espionage actor attributed by Proofpoint, is running a focused credential-phishing operation against U.S. artificial intelligence policy specialists. The mechanism relies on impersonation emails, reply-triggered adversary-in-the-middle infrastructure, and a modified Browser-in-the-Browser toolkit built to intercept Microsoft 365 login flows and steal active sessions. No CVEs are involved; this is pure social-engineering and session-hijacking, with three source reports (gbhackers.com, 2026-10-02; thehackernews.com; cyberworldops.eu) tracking the same campaign over a 48-hour window.

The exposure is narrow but high-value: the targets are the individuals who shape U.S. AI regulation and export controls, meaning a single compromised M365 session could yield draft policy language, meeting notes with government contacts, or pre-public analysis on technology restrictions. The story carries a 5.7 peak score with zero Reddit uptake and zero CVE linkage, which means it is flying under automated threat-intelligence feeds that rely on vulnerability identifiers; a defender skimming CVE dashboards will miss it entirely, which is exactly why this actor prefers it.

Defenders should hunt for adversary-in-the-middle login flows rather than malware artifacts: inspect proxy and mail-gateway logs for deceptive Microsoft sign-in redirect chains, look for Browser-in-the-Browser iframe injections against login.microsoftonline.com, and audit conditional-access events for session cookie replay from unexpected geographies. Patch and harden M365 with phishing-resistant authentication and restrict legacy authentication protocols that this toolkit is known to abuse; network-level blocking should target the impersonation domains referenced in the GBHackers and The Hacker News coverage rather than broad IP lists.

Watch for Proofpoint to release IOCs or a full threat research brief, and for any shift from AI-policy targets to defense-industrial or semiconductor-policy circles, which would confirm strategic collection priorities beyond the current narrow focus.
