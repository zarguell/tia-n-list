Attackers remotely manipulated internet-exposed Danfoss refrigeration controllers to force defrost cycles at hundreds of European supermarkets, in some cases raising temperatures to 50 degrees Celsius and spoiling food. The October 2026 incident affected chains including Auchan, Carrefour, E.Leclerc, Intermarche, and Monoprix, with more than 300 French locations and over 115 stores across Belgium, the Netherlands, Sweden, Luxembourg, Ireland, and Estonia affected. Cooperative U CEO Dominique Schelcher called the targeting of food-supply equipment unprecedented and unacceptable.

The underlying weaknesses were known before the attack: Claroty Team82 disclosed three vulnerabilities in the Danfoss AK-SM 800A controller (CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451, CVE-2025-41452) in August 2026, including an authentication bypass via a hidden code-of-the-day mechanism and command injection through the SMTP configuration page. Danfoss issued advisory DSA-2025-03-01 in March 2025. IPinfo's scans found 2,528 exposed Danfoss StoreView dashboards across 73 countries before the attack, with 1,331 in France alone, and 85 percent of French units running software with known flaws. Whether the disclosed CVEs were the actual exploit path remains unconfirmed.

What to watch: whether the attacker is identified and whether more exposed controllers are taken offline. IPinfo found that France reduced its exposed dashboard count from 1,331 to 149 after the attack, while Belgium, Sweden, Portugal, and Estonia saw smaller drops, leaving many dashboards still reachable. The attack illustrates how internet-facing OT systems with default or weak credentials can be turned against physical supply chains.
