Attackers remotely forced Danfoss refrigeration controllers at hundreds of French supermarkets into defrost overnight October 11-12, 2026, raising temperatures to about 50 degrees Celsius and spoiling fresh and frozen stock. Affected chains included Intermarche, Cooperative U, Carrefour, E.Leclerc, Monoprix, Netto, Auchan and Super U, with French outlets reporting between 250 and 350 stores affected and losses running into millions of euros. Le Monde reported that attackers extended defrost cycles from one hour to eight hours, delaying alarms and giving staff less time to intervene. Danfoss said most affected equipment was legacy AK-SM800 and AK-SC355 gear no longer receiving updates, and the attack reused a vulnerability previously exploited against US Department of Defense commissaries in August 2026. The Paris prosecutor's office opened an investigation.

The underlying weaknesses predate the attacks. Claroty Team82 disclosed CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451 and CVE-2025-41452 in the Danfoss AK-SM 800A controller in August 2026, including an authentication bypass via a hidden code-of-the-day mechanism and command injection through the SMTP configuration page. Danfoss published a March 2025 advisory for CVE-2025-41450. IPinfo scans found 2,528 internet-exposed StoreView dashboards across 73 countries on October 6-7, 1,331 of them in France, with roughly 85 percent running software with known flaws and only 195 fully patched. France took most of its dashboards offline after the attack, dropping from 1,331 to 149 by October 27. CERT-FR initially linked the compromises to Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC series controllers, then expanded its alert to cover Copeland and Dixell XWEB systems.

A second wave hit overnight November 1-2, with more than 100 stores in Belgium, Estonia, Ireland, Luxembourg, the Netherlands and Sweden reporting fridges and freezers pushed to 50 degrees Celsius, and further problems in Finland, Italy and Portugal. Affected retailers included ICA, Coop, Ahold Delhaize, Spar under Colruyt Group, Carrefour Belgium and at least 17 Dutch butcher shops, plus an Ikea superstore in Mons. Sweden's national cybersecurity center is responding to multiple cooling-system incidents, and no group has claimed responsibility. Investigators have not established whether the two waves are connected. Stephan Moelans, CEO of Belgian refrigeration firm Sabcobel, told Belga that attackers forced units' evaporators into defrost mode. IPinfo scans showed exposed dashboards falling in Belgium (85 to 51), Sweden (73 to 64) and Portugal (92 to 37) between late September and late October, while three of four Estonian dashboards still online were an older model.

Defenders should treat exposed refrigeration controllers as operational-technology assets: remove legacy controllers from networks, patch supported AK-SM firmware, restrict StoreView dashboards, and monitor for defrost-cycle changes and temperature setpoint tampering. The November wave shows the French incident was not isolated, and unpatched controllers remain reachable across Europe.
