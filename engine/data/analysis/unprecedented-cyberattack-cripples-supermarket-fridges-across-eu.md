Attackers remotely manipulated internet-exposed Danfoss refrigeration controllers to force defrost cycles at hundreds of European supermarkets, in some cases raising temperatures to 50 degrees Celsius (122 Fahrenheit) and spoiling food. The initial October 11-12, 2026 incident affected chains including Auchan, Carrefour, E.Leclerc, Intermarche, Monoprix, and Cooperative U, with more than 300 French locations hit and losses estimated in the millions of euros. The campaign then expanded on November 2, 2026 to Belgium, Luxembourg, and Sweden, where ICA, Coop, Delhaize, and Spar stores saw freezers reach 55 degrees Celsius and more than 115 stores across Belgium, the Netherlands, Sweden, Luxembourg, Ireland, and Estonia were affected. No group has claimed responsibility and no authority has named a suspect or entry point.

The weaknesses were known before the attack. Claroty Team82 disclosed three vulnerabilities in the Danfoss AK-SM 800A controller (CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451, CVE-2025-41452) in August 2026, including an authentication bypass via a hidden code-of-the-day mechanism and command injection through the SMTP configuration page. Danfoss issued advisory DSA-2025-03-01 in March 2025 for CVE-2025-41450. IPinfo scans found 2,528 exposed Danfoss StoreView dashboards across 73 countries before the attack, with 1,331 in France alone and 85 percent of French units running software with known flaws. Danfoss said most affected equipment was legacy (AK-SM800, AK-SC355) no longer receiving updates, and whether the disclosed CVEs were the actual exploit path remains unconfirmed.

What to watch: attribution and further expansion. France cut its exposed dashboard count from 1,331 to 149 after the attack, while Belgium, Sweden, Portugal, and Estonia saw smaller drops, leaving many controllers still reachable. The incident shows internet-facing OT equipment with weak or default credentials can be turned against physical supply chains, and the November 2 spread suggests the same playbook is being reused across borders.
