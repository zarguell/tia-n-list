A cyberattack on supermarket refrigeration systems has caused food spoilage and store closures across Europe in two waves. The first wave struck overnight October 11-12, 2026, affecting roughly 700 French stores including Intermarché, Carrefour, Coopérative U, Monoprix, Leclerc, and Netto, with some units reaching 50°C (122°F) and an estimated several million euros in losses for Les Mousquetaires alone. A second wave hit overnight November 2, 2026, affecting more than 100 stores in Belgium, Luxembourg, Sweden, the Netherlands, Ireland, Estonia, and others, including Delhaize, Carrefour Belgium, Spar, and ICA/Coop. Attackers remotely manipulated refrigeration controllers, forcing extended defrost cycles and raising temperatures, with some freezers reported at 55°C.

The affected equipment is primarily Danfoss refrigeration controllers, including legacy AK-SM800 and AK-SC355 models that Danfoss says no longer receive updates, with CERT-FR later expanding its alert to Copeland and Dixell XWEB systems. Claroty's Team82 disclosed three vulnerabilities in the Danfoss AK-SM 800A in August 2026 (CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451, CVE-2025-41452), including an authentication bypass via a hidden code-of-the-day mechanism; Danfoss published advisories months earlier, but no link between those flaws and the exploit chain has been confirmed. The Paris prosecutor's office opened an investigation; no group has claimed responsibility and no entry point has been officially identified.

The incident shows how attacks on connected operational-technology equipment can cause physical-world harm. Retailers discarded large quantities of food, some stores closed temporarily, and authorities condemned targeting food-preservation infrastructure. Owners of Danfoss, Copeland, and Dixell controllers should verify firmware levels, segment OT networks, and remove or upgrade legacy units that no longer receive security updates.
