On the night of October 11, attackers hijacked Danfoss refrigeration controllers at hundreds of European supermarkets and used the controllers' built-in defrost function to push fridge and freezer temperatures to 50C. French outlets count 250 to 350 affected stores, BFM TV reports more than 300, and the damage lands hardest in France: Intermarché, Coopérative U, Carrefour Market, Monoprix, Leclerc, Auchan, and Super U all confirmed or were named as victims. Food retailers in Belgium, Germany, the Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, and Spain were also hit. Losses run to millions of euros in spoiled stock; a Gradignan store manager described staff finding a cold room at 50C at 4am with sprinklers triggered. Coopérative U president Dominique Schelcher called the attack on food-supply equipment unprecedented, EU agriculture commissioner Christophe Hansen condemned it, and the Paris prosecutor's office is involved.

The technical root is unpatched operational technology. Danfoss confirmed the attackers exploited a known vulnerability, and reporting notes the same flaw was used against US DoD commissary refrigeration in August. Most affected stores had simply never patched or updated their controllers, and ITPro reports many run controller versions that no longer receive security updates at all. One outlet, cyberworldops.eu, links the flaw to CVE-2025-41450, though that attribution is the weakest link in the reporting chain.

This is the clearest recent case of criminal capability against physical food-supply infrastructure without ransomware involvement: no encryption, no extortion note, just cold-chain destruction at scale. Watch for the Paris prosecutor's attribution, whether Danfoss names the specific CVE and issues forced updates, and whether regulators treat retail refrigeration fleets as critical infrastructure requiring patch SLAs. The August DoD commissary precedent suggests the technique is being passed between groups, not used once.
