Attackers remotely manipulated refrigeration controllers at hundreds of European supermarkets, forcing units into defrost mode and raising temperatures to around 50 degrees Celsius, spoiling chilled and frozen stock and causing losses estimated in the millions of euros. The first wave struck overnight October 11 to 12, 2026, hitting chains including Intermarché, Netto, Coopérative U, Carrefour, E.Leclerc, and Monoprix, with roughly 700 French stores affected and reports of damaged equipment and fires. The Paris prosecutor's office opened an investigation, and EU agriculture commissioner Christophe Hansen condemned the targeting of food-supply infrastructure.

The attack chain ran through connected refrigeration management equipment, notably Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC series controllers, with Danfoss stating that most affected units were legacy products no longer receiving updates. Claroty Team82 had disclosed three vulnerabilities in the Danfoss AK-SM 800A in August 2026, including an authentication bypass via a hidden code-of-the-day mechanism and command injection through the SMTP configuration page, mapped to CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451, and CVE-2025-41452. Danfoss had published advisory SA-24:20 in March 2025 for CVE-2025-41450. French reporting indicated attackers extended defrost cycles from one hour to eight hours, delaying alarms and reducing staff intervention time.

A second wave hit overnight November 1 to 2, 2026, with more than 100 stores across Belgium, the Netherlands, Sweden, Luxembourg, Ireland, and Estonia showing sudden temperature rises, some units set to 50 degrees Celsius. CERT-FR had expanded its alert to cover Copeland and Dixell XWEB systems alongside Danfoss. Sweden's national cybersecurity center responded to multiple incidents, and no group has claimed responsibility; investigators have not confirmed whether the two waves are connected. Operators of retail refrigeration should patch or retire legacy controllers, block management interfaces from the internet, and verify alarm and defrost-cycle integrity.
