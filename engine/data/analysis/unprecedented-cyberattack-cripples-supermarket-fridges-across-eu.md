A cyberattack on refrigeration control systems hit French supermarket chains including Intermarché and Super U, with attackers breaching Danfoss controllers to force automatic defrost cycles. The events cite temperatures climbing to 50 degrees, fresh products destroyed across hundreds of stores, and delivery and pricing disruptions. No CVE has been assigned, and the incident is tied to industrial refrigeration controllers rather than conventional IT networks.

This is not a routine ransomware headline. The exposure is physical supply-chain collapse: hundreds of stores lost refrigerated inventory, which means perishable food waste, delivery delays, and immediate price impacts for consumers, not just encrypted file servers. The choice of Danfoss refrigeration controllers shows attackers understand operational technology targets, and the absence of a CVE suggests the vulnerability is either an unpatched configuration weakness or a supply-chain access route rather than a tracked software flaw. That distinction matters because defenders watching only CVE feeds will miss it.

Patch status is unconfirmed in the events, so the concrete defender actions are network-focused: isolate refrigeration controller VLANs from store IT, audit remote access to Danfoss systems, and inspect logs for unauthorized defrost commands or temperature set-point changes near the reported 50-degree spike. Hunt for telemetry referencing forced defrost cycles, anomalous controller logins, or connections from unexpected IPs. Monitor bfmtv.com and infosecurity-magazine.com coverage for vendor advisories that have not yet appeared.

The assessment would shift if a vendor advisory identifies the exact controller model and firmware version, or if exploitation telemetry shows the same technique against non-retail refrigeration infrastructure. A second development to watch is whether pricing and delivery disruptions expand beyond the cited Intermarché and Super U chains.
