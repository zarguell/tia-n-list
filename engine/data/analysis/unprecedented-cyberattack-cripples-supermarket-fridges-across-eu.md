Attackers remotely forced Danfoss refrigeration controllers at hundreds of French supermarkets into defrost on October 11-12, 2026, raising temperatures to about 50 degrees Celsius and spoiling fresh and frozen stock. Affected chains included Intermarche, Cooperative U, Carrefour, E.Leclerc, Monoprix and Netto, with estimates of 250 to 350 stores and losses running into millions of euros. Danfoss said most affected equipment was legacy AK-SM800 and AK-SC355 gear no longer receiving updates, and the attack reused a vulnerability previously exploited against US Department of Defense commissaries in August 2026.

The incident has since spread beyond France. Overnight into November 2, refrigeration systems at dozens of stores in Belgium, Luxembourg and Sweden showed sudden temperature rises, with some freezers set as high as 50 degrees Celsius, forcing retailers to discard stock and delay openings. Sweden's national cybersecurity center is responding to multiple cooling-system incidents, while no group has claimed responsibility and authorities have not named an entry point.

The underlying weaknesses predate the attacks. Claroty disclosed CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451 and CVE-2025-41452 in the Danfoss AK-SM 800A controller in August 2026, including an authentication bypass via a hidden code-of-the-day mechanism and command injection through the SMTP configuration page. Danfoss published a March 2025 advisory for CVE-2025-41450, and IPinfo scans found thousands of internet-exposed StoreView dashboards, most running unpatched or end-of-life software.

Defenders should treat exposed refrigeration controllers as operational-technology assets: remove legacy controllers from networks, patch supported AK-SM firmware, restrict StoreView dashboards, and monitor for defrost-cycle changes and temperature setpoint tampering. The November wave shows the French incident was not isolated, and unpatched controllers remain reachable across Europe.
