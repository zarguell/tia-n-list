Hackers disrupted supermarket refrigeration systems across Europe by exploiting internet-exposed refrigeration controllers, activating automatic defrosting that spoiled products and raised store prices. The incident spans multiple countries and has been described as unprecedented in scale for this sector. Danfoss published a security advisory on March 24, 2025 addressing CVE-2025-41450, an improper authentication vulnerability in the AK-SM8xxA Series prior to release R4.2, noting the flaw allows an attacker to generate a web report disclosing sensitive information. The broader campaign targets refrigeration infrastructure that should not be exposed to the public internet, and the impact includes delivery disruptions, price increases, and direct food-safety concerns.

This event highlights a structural problem in OT and industrial IoT deployment rather than a novel exploit technique. The authentication weakness in Danfoss controllers is a known vulnerability from March 2025, yet attackers found exposed devices and weaponized them at scale. The automatic defrost activation is a simple but effective sabotage method that does not require ransomware or data exfiltration to cause economic damage. Supermarket chains and refrigeration vendors need to audit all internet-facing controllers, apply the R4.2 release, and segment these devices from public networks.

Going forward, expect more targeting of food-supply infrastructure as attackers recognize the low complexity and high impact of disrupting cold-chain systems. The European response includes Europol coordination and national cybersecurity alerts, which suggests authorities treat this as critical infrastructure rather than a routine IT breach. Monitor for follow-on advisories from Danfoss, CISA, and European national CERTs regarding additional affected models or new exploitation patterns.
