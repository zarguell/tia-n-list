Overnight on Sunday, October 11, attackers hijacked Danfoss refrigeration controllers running older control software at French supermarkets and used them to trigger the built-in automatic defrost system, raising refrigerator temperatures and destroying fresh product. French reporting puts temperatures as high as 50 degrees in affected cases.

Victims include Intermarche with 150 to 200 shops and Cooperative U with almost 100 outlets, and the attack reportedly also reached Carrefour Market, Monoprix, Leclerc, and Super U, for 250 to 350 stores in total. Cooperative U president Dominique Schelcher confirmed the incident and called targeting equipment essential for food supply extremely serious and unprecedented. EU agriculture commissioner Christophe Hansen condemned the attack and named Leclerc as a victim. Paris prosecutors confirmed the attack, and AFP reported significant financial losses and food waste. A separate cybersecurity incident the same weekend disrupted Albert Heijn in the Netherlands, though a link is unconfirmed.

This is a physical-consequence attack on OT-adjacent systems in civilian infrastructure, notable for scale and stupidity rather than sophistication: the capability came from legitimate defrost functions in aging controller software reachable over the network. Watch for the Paris prosecutor's investigation findings, whether Danfoss issues advisories on the affected controller versions, and copycat activity against other food retail cold chains.
