Check Point Research identified Volant Manticore as a previously undocumented Iranian group linked to the Ministry of Intelligence and Security. Check Point assesses with medium confidence that it is a spin-off of former MuddyWater operators, and calls it the first Iranian group to make Cyprus a primary espionage target. Targets included telecommunications companies, airlines and critical infrastructure. Activity goes back to September 2025, and Check Point observed a pivot to Saudi healthcare in September 2026. Lures included an AI-generated CV for a persona named Athena Marinou and an earlier Word document in January 2026.

The group's KuboShell backdoor uses IPFS for command and control, with Pinata-pinned files acting as a command mailbox. Check Point describes host registration through MAC-derived files in C:\Users\Public and polling every 60 seconds. KuboShell deploys the Homa Framework, a reimplementation of MuddyWater's MuddyC2Go client. Three Homa stages were mapped to 185.205.187[.]109 on port 8000; the January Word lure pointed to 185.205.187[.]108 on port 25498.

Alongside custom malware, Volant Manticore used NetLock RMM, N-able RMM and MeshCentral, ran credential-harvesting campaigns and used Fortinet SSL VPN clients that suggest network access through VPN. Check Point documented operator development, testing and production setups on-premises and in the cloud, including antivirus testing before deployment.

What to watch: Pinata-pinned IPFS paths, the two 185.205.187 addresses, the named RMM tools and the persona-based lures. Check Point published file hashes and domains with the report, and the Saudi healthcare spike is the clearest indicator of a change in targeting priority.
