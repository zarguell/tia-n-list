Operation ASTERIX is a multi-stage cryptocurrency fraud campaign uncovered by Rapid7 after researchers found an exposed Asterisk telephony server containing the operator's full operational toolkit. The campaign chains account enumeration, branded phishing emails, automated voice calls, and trojanized wallet apps to steal seed phrases, then exfiltrates recovery phrases via Telegram bots. Fake Ledger, Trezor, and Exodus wallet applications were recovered from the server alongside dialer scripts, phishing panels, and lead databases.

What makes this operation notable is the targeting precision. Attackers used bulk phone-number validation against a Crypto.com endpoint, submitting 316,002 German mobile numbers through 300 concurrent threads with rotating residential proxies. The tool confirmed 43,066 crypto-linked accounts, a 13.6% hit rate, letting the operator focus vishing on confirmed crypto holders rather than random numbers. The exposed server also showed the operator actively using AI coding tools during live fraud development, suggesting the campaign's technical infrastructure was being iterated in real time.

Rapid7 notified Apple and relevant authorities while infrastructure was still active. The campaign is significant because it demonstrates how commodity telephony platforms and automated enumeration can be combined to produce high-confidence targeting for financial fraud at scale. Crypto wallet users receiving unexpected support calls or branded emails should treat them as suspicious regardless of how legitimate they sound.
