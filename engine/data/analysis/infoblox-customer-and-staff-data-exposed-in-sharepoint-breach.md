Infoblox confirmed that attackers reached internal files through a compromised third-party data migration vendor with access to its SharePoint environment. The Register's November 16, 2026 report, relayed through r/cybersecurity and Mastodon, describes exposure of customer and staff data. A later write-up says the stolen material includes some customers' NIOS configuration snapshots, which describe DNS and DHCP setup, Grid databases and reporting logs, plus employee contact, payroll and identity records. Infoblox said its products, cloud services, updates and build process were not involved. The third-party vendor's name and the number of affected customers are not stated in the available reports.

NIOS snapshots are valuable even without account passwords because they map internal domains, server names, IP ranges and DHCP scopes. For banks, telecom operators and government networks running Infoblox DDI, that is reconnaissance material for a targeted intrusion. The entry path also matters: trusted vendor credentials into Microsoft 365 and SharePoint were enough to reach the data, with no need to exploit an Infoblox appliance. The reporting does not confirm extortion demands or publication of the files.

Watch for direct notification from Infoblox about whether a Grid snapshot was taken, and rotate secrets held in the Grid if exposure is confirmed or suspected. Review vendor accounts with SharePoint access, their M365 audit logs and any bulk file access around the November 16 disclosure. Also check for follow-on phishing that uses leaked internal domains and network details. No CVE is attached to this incident; it is a vendor-access breach with downstream network-mapping risk.
