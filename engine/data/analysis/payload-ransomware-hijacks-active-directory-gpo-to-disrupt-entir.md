A ransomware operation tracked as PAYLOAD has adopted a novel technique that hijacks Active Directory Group Policy Objects to disrupt entire Windows domains without deploying traditional file encryption. According to Kaspersky's Securelist analysis, the attackers modify Group Policy settings to push destructive commands across all domain-joined machines, achieving domain-wide disruption without the ransomware binary itself encrypting files. This approach evades encryption-focused detection and leaves traditional ransomware indicators absent from the environment.

The attack chain begins with compromise of a domain controller or Group Policy management console. From there, the attackers modify existing GPOs to execute destructive commands at the next policy refresh cycle. Because GPOs are a legitimate administrative mechanism, the malicious policy changes blend with normal administrative activity. The lack of encryption means endpoint detection tools looking for ransomware behavior may not trigger, and the domain-wide scope means recovery requires restoring GPO backups rather than decrypting individual files.

Organizations should monitor Group Policy changes on domain controllers, implement GPO backup procedures, and restrict who can modify policy objects. The technique represents a shift toward "wiper-style" ransomware operations where the goal is disruption rather than encryption, complicating incident response playbooks built around traditional ransomware scenarios.
