TAG-193, also tracked by Cisco Talos as UAT-6275 and called Silent Lynx, runs sustained cyber-espionage across Central Asia and the wider CIS. Joint reporting from Recorded Future’s Insikt Group and Talos describes four infection-chain types observed from June 2025 through August 2026 that rely on macro-enabled documents, with evolving malware and exposed infrastructure giving new visibility into the group’s operational practices. The group combines diplomatic and economic targeting with an interest in water resources, forestry, and environmental policy; CVE-2025-8088 appears in the associated update sources.

This is not generic regional malware. The targeting of government institutions responsible for diplomacy, economic development, agriculture, and environmental management means a successful intrusion yields strategic information on shared river systems, energy negotiations, and transport infrastructure across Central Asia. With possible links to Kazakhstan noted but origins still unclear, defenders in the region face a persistent actor whose toolkit is expanding, not contracting.

Defenders should hunt for macro-enabled document delivery chains linked to government-facing communications, inspect exposed infrastructure indicators tied to the four infection-chain types documented by Talos, and apply mitigations associated with CVE-2025-8088 where exposed. Monitor logs for document-based initial access, review macro execution telemetry, and restrict document macro policies for external-facing diplomatic and resource-management staff.

A confirmed attribution shift away from Kazakhstan or a fifth infection-chain variant would alter the assessment, as would evidence that the macro-enabled delivery method has been replaced by a direct network intrusion technique.
