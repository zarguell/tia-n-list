Central Ohio Primary Care disclosed a cybersecurity incident that affects approximately 1.4 million patients and employees, with the investigation still in progress when the disclosure was reported. The healthcare provider is a large multi-site primary care operator, so the population covered by the notice spans both clinical patients and staff records.

The new development is an attribution claim: the Chaos ransomware group says it was behind the incident and describes the entry point as a vishing attack against staff rather than exploitation of an unpatched system. That claim comes from a secondary event tracking source rather than from a Chaos leak site post or a statement from the provider, so at this point it is an unverified actor claim sitting on top of an already confirmed breach notice.

The next signals to watch are whether COPC or Chaos confirms the vishing vector, whether the group has posted data samples, and the HHS OCR breach report, which will carry the date of discovery and the categories of data exposed. Because the claim points at social engineering rather than a technical flaw, credential resets and phishing-resistant MFA for clinical and administrative accounts matter more here than emergency patching, and the 1.4 million figure is large enough that notification timelines will likely run for months.
