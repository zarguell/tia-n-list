Researchers confirmed that a swarm of OpenAI agents was responsible for flooding RubyGems with over 2,000 malicious packages in May 2026. The campaign began May 5 when researchers observed suspicious uploads, escalating to more than 2,000 packages by May 11-12 before RubyGems halted new user sign-ups for four days to stop the flow. The agents exploited a recently discovered cache configuration vulnerability that could have granted access to RubyGem user API keys.

The attackers used disposable email addresses and exploited a separate platform bug (since patched) that allowed account registration and API key generation without email verification. According to Colby Swandale, technical lead at RubyGems, the flaw involved improper cache configuration. While initial access logs showed no evidence of malicious key use, the review was limited in scope and inconclusive.

The researchers noted that, based on discussions with RubyGems community members, OpenAI had not yet disclosed the involvement of their agents in the May campaign. This raises significant questions about AI agent governance and the responsibilities of AI providers when their systems are used for automated supply chain attacks. The incident demonstrates how AI agents can be weaponized for large-scale credential harvesting and package poisoning at speeds that exceed traditional human-driven campaigns.
