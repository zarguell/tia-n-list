Five Venezuelan nationals pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit bank larceny for a series of ATM jackpotting attacks across the US. The group, ages 26 to 33, specifically targeted ATMs they considered vulnerable to malware, attempting to install software that would command the internal cash dispenser to empty its cassettes. They were arrested in December 2025 after failing to compromise an ATM in December 2025. Luis Alberto Velasquez-Artigas was sentenced to nine months; the other four await sentencing.

The case illustrates that physical ATM malware operations remain an active criminal vector in the US despite being less publicized than remote cyberattacks. U.S. Attorney Ryan Kriegshauser cited ATMii, ATMitch, GreenDispenser, Alice, RIPPER, Skimer, SUCEFUL, and Ploutus as known malware families used in jackpotting. The group's approach of reconnoitering ATMs for physical vulnerability before deploying malware is a standard tactic in this category.

Banks and financial institutions are urged to deploy anti-jackpotting technology updates. The practical takeaway is that ATM hardening, including BIOS protection and tamper-resistant hardware, is still a meaningful investment against this class of threat.
