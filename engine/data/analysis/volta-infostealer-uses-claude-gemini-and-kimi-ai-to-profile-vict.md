A new Windows infostealer called VOLTA, self-named VOLTA-AUTODOXER, harvests credentials, sessions, and cryptocurrency data and outsources analysis to commercial AI models. The malware uses Claude Opus 4.6 via OpenRouter, with fallbacks to Kimi K2 and Gemini 2.5 Flash, to profile victims, determine identity and value, and rank stolen data. The malware is self-deleting, uses extensive collection, and performs automated identity enrichment by feeding stolen evidence bundles to large language models.

This is significant because it demonstrates operational integration of LLM pipelines into malware workflows, not just marketing claims. The concrete artifacts include the malware families Claude Opus 4.6, Kimi K2, and Gemini 2.5 Flash; the OpenRouter relay; the self-deleting design; and the evidence bundle categories (geolocation, employer, card last-4 digits, seed phrases, Gmail receipts). Talos discovered the malware, and WIRED reported it exclusively on October 31, 2026. Fetterman told WIRED that Volta has not been tracked in active campaigns yet.
