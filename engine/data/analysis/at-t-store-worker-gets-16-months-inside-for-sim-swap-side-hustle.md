A former AT&T retail worker in Portland, Oregon, Kenneth Carter, 44, has been sentenced to 16 months in federal prison for carrying out SIM-swap attacks on AT&T customers. Carter was an employee at an AT&T store and used his authorized system access to hijack customers' phone numbers. The SIM swaps allowed cybercriminals to intercept SMS authentication codes and drain victims' bank accounts. Carter claimed he was paid less than $4,000 for his role in the attacks.

The case illustrates the insider threat problem in telecom: employees who can access customer accounts can facilitate large-scale financial fraud. SIM-swap attacks often target crypto wallets, banking apps, and email accounts that rely on SMS-based two-factor authentication. Even with limited access, a store employee can execute the swap with minimal effort, causing significant financial harm to victims before the bank can respond. The sentence highlights the federal government's continued focus on prosecuting SIM-swap facilitators.

This sentencing follows a broader pattern of telecom insiders being caught for SIM-swap operations. Reported by The Register and the Databreaches.net on September 13, 2026, the case reinforces the industry argument for moving away from SMS-based 2FA toward passkeys or hardware tokens. For organizations that still rely on SMS for authentication, the risk of insider-facilitated SIM swaps remains a credible threat model.
