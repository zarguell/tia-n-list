Wired reviewed the System76 Pangolin Pro, a Linux laptop positioned as a high-performance machine for developers and power users. System76 is one of the few remaining manufacturers shipping laptops with Linux preinstalled and firmware (Open firmware/Open EC) that avoids proprietary BIOS dependencies. The Pangolin Pro targets users who want a Linux-native experience without the compatibility headaches of running Linux on Windows-first hardware.

The review positions the device as competitive on processing power, port selection, and build quality against mainstream Windows laptops. System76's value proposition for security-conscious users includes the open firmware stack, which allows independent auditing of the boot process and hardware initialization, reducing trust in opaque proprietary UEFI implementations. The company also ships with coreboot-compatible firmware on select models.

For the security community, System76 represents a practical alternative to repurposing corporate ThinkPads or running Linux on consumer hardware with incomplete driver support. The open firmware approach has implications for supply chain security and remote attestation, though the Pangolin Pro specifically uses AMD processors with its own security features. The laptop is priced competitively with mid-range business ultrabooks and is available direct from System76.
