Trung Nguyen Van, a 37-year-old Vietnamese national, was charged with money laundering for his role in a pig-butchering operation. Per the DOJ and court documents reported by BleepingComputer, Van entered the US through the San Ysidro, California pedestrian crossing on September 22 and was arrested at Los Angeles International Airport on September 24 while boarding a flight to Taiwan. One victim transferred roughly $16 million in cryptocurrency to wallets directly traceable to Van between June and August 2024 after being drawn into a fake investment platform called Triangle; the funds then moved to an unhosted wallet outside the centralized chain.

The charging documents frame Van as a laundering node in a much larger operation. His wallets received approximately $53,275,939 in crypto from wire-fraud schemes targeting US citizens between February 9, 2018 and December 17, 2024, of which at least $24 million is linked to known pig-butchering scams, and pushed about $53,188,466 back out to other off-chain accounts. The broader scheme cited in the filings involves more than $125 million.

The case matters because it shows law enforcement working the cash-out tail of pig butchering rather than the scam farms themselves: identifying wallet clusters that consolidate victim funds, then catching a courier in transit. Tracing on centralized exchanges plus border arrests is becoming a repeatable pattern, and each laundering wallet identified exposes more of the cash-out graph.

Watch for the indictment's full wallet list once unsealed, whether Van's cluster ties to previously charged pig-butchering networks, and any forfeiture actions against the $16 million traceable to the Triangle victim.
