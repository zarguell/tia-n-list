Cisco Talos published a detailed report on ransomware activity in Japan during the first half of 2026, attributing a significant share of incidents to The Gentlemen, a relatively new ransomware group that emerged in early 2025. The group's infrastructure investigation revealed connections to Qilin, another active ransomware operation, suggesting possible collaboration or shared resources between the two. The report also noted a broader 4.7% increase in ransomware attacks targeting Japanese organizations across H1 2026, with The Gentlemen accounting for a disproportionate share of reported incidents.

The Gentlemen's operational profile is notable for its targeting of mid-sized Japanese enterprises across manufacturing, logistics, and services. The group uses double-extortion tactics, exfiltrating data before encryption and threatening public release. Talos identified infrastructure overlap between The Gentlemen and Qilin at the network and hosting level, including shared bulletproof hosting providers and certificate patterns. This clustering suggests the groups may share initial access brokers or operational support, though Talos stopped short of declaring a formal alliance.

For defenders in Japan and the Asia-Pacific region, the key takeaway is that The Gentlemen should be treated as an active, well-resourced threat rather than a minor player. The group's rapid growth and infrastructure ties to Qilin mean that indicators of compromise from one operation may be relevant to the other. Organizations should prioritize patching internet-facing systems, enforcing MFA on all remote access, and monitoring for the specific TTPs Talos documented in its report.
