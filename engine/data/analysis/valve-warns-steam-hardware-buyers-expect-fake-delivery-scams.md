Valve warned Steam hardware buyers on August 10 to expect fake delivery scams after customers began receiving phishing emails that reference real purchase details. The messages use the recipient's name, address, and the specific hardware product ordered, which makes them far harder to dismiss than generic account alerts.

The campaign targets European Steam hardware buyers and likely relies on order data to build convincing lures, either from a separate data source or from information the scammers assembled about recent purchases. Delivery-themed phishing works because shipping notices are expected, so users click links and enter credentials or payment data without suspicion. Watch for lookalike carrier domains, fake tracking pages, and follow-on credential theft, and treat any delivery email carrying an embedded link as a verification case rather than a click.
