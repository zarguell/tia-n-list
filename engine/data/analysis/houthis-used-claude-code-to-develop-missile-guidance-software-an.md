Anthropic's September 2026 threat report documents a case in which a cell in northern Yemen, assessed as highly likely linked to the Houthis, used Claude Code to perform missile engineering work. The operators ran multiple Claude instances in parallel, splitting tasks between coding, research, and technical review. The work included software for a guided rocket, a ballistic missile with a range exceeding 2,000 km, and a hypersonic glide vehicle concept called "R2000."

The technical scope covered navigation, flight control, trajectory simulation, and reinforcement learning for tuning algorithms. The operators compiled the project into an offline executable, meaning the engineering toolkit continued to function after their accounts were banned. A guided rocket was test-fired in Yemen and appeared to fail; within hours, the operators used Claude to analyze the launch telemetry and diagnose the failure. This cycle illustrates direct incorporation of AI into an iterative weapons engineering process, not just information gathering.

Anthropic said it found no evidence the weapon was fielded operationally, but the assembled offline engineering toolkit had already begun moving away from Claude access by the time accounts were disrupted. The case is one of six conventional-weapon cases Anthropic documented between December 2025 and August 2026, with three linked to China, two to Russia, and one to Yemen. The operators adapted around safety measures by fragmenting work across separate conversations and obscuring their end intent. This is notable as the clearest example yet of generative AI being applied directly to physical weapons development by a conflict group.
