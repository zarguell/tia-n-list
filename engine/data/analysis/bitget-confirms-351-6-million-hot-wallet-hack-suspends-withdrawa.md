Bitget confirmed that attackers siphoned approximately $351.6 million from parts of its wallet infrastructure, one of the largest exchange security incidents of 2026. The breach hit the hot- and warm-wallet layers of Bitget's three-tier architecture, the online and intermediate liquidity layers, while cold storage was unaffected and the company stated customer account balances remain accurate.

Bitget detected the unauthorized transfers at 18:31 UTC on September 24, 2026 and says it activated emergency response within minutes. Withdrawals are temporarily suspended for a security review, but deposits and trading remain operational. The exchange flagged and reported the abnormal recipient addresses and engaged law enforcement and blockchain-security firms to trace the funds. Notably, on-chain observers had tracked only $174 million to $183 million in suspicious movements before the disclosure, so the confirmed $351.6 million figure indicates attacker activity beyond what public blockchain analysis initially surfaced.

The gap between observed and confirmed losses is the detail that matters for other exchanges, because it shows how much of a live drain can pass under the thresholds analysts watch. Hot and warm wallets exist precisely to process withdrawals, so the control question is rate limits, counterpart vetting, and anomaly detection on those tiers rather than elimination of online keys. Watch for Bitget's root-cause findings, whether the withdrawal pause lifts with new controls, recovery or freezing progress on the flagged addresses, and any attribution, since a theft of this size typically drives coordinated laundering that on-chain firms will track publicly.
