Bitget confirmed attackers stole about $351.6 million in crypto from its hot and warm wallet layers on September 24, 2026, with unauthorized transfers detected at 18:31 UTC and emergency procedures activated within minutes. Withdrawals were suspended while deposits and trading continued. Cold wallets and the self-custodial Bitget Wallet were not affected. The stolen assets initially spanned Ethereum, XRP Ledger, Arbitrum, and Avalanche, including ETH, XRP, BNB, AVAX, USDT, and USDC, with XRP the largest single-chain loss; the CEO later put the affected chains at Ethereum, XRP Ledger, Arbitrum, Avalanche, Optimism, BSC, and Base.

The intrusion path stands out: the attacker compromised a critical backend wallet-service system and abused it to forge transfer information and trigger Bitget's own authorization-signing process, rather than brute-forcing keys. Early on-chain observers only tracked $174 to $183 million in movements, meaning roughly half the loss came from flows invisible to public analysts. Some blockchain foundations have frozen attacker-linked addresses.

CEO Gracy Chen attributes the attack to North Korean hackers based on IP behavior patterns and on-chain analysis, consistent with Lazarus Group methods, though no specific group was named and the evidence is not public. Mandiant, SlowMist, and law enforcement are tracing funds, and the company's User Protection Fund, holding 5,500 BTC or about $464 million, covers losses. Bitget says the exploited vulnerability is addressed, no further unauthorized transfers are possible, and Bitcoin withdrawals have resumed, with a published schedule for the rest: ETH and EVM-chain assets on September 29 at 08:00 UTC, USDT on September 30, and other tokens, fiat, and P2P assets from October 2.

The forged-transfer technique against a signing backend is the detail other exchanges should study. Watch for the technical post-mortem explaining how the backend was reached, whether frozen funds get clawed back through mixer tracing, and any OFAC action against attacker wallets.
