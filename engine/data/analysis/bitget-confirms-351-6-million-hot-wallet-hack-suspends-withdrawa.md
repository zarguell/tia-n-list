Attackers took about $351.6 million from Bitget's hot and warm wallet tiers on September 24, 2026, with the first unauthorized transfers flagged by Bitget's own security systems at 18:31 UTC and emergency response initiated within minutes. The company says the attackers reached its key backend wallet-service system and used it to forge transfer information and trigger the authorization-signing process, which means they obtained the ability to initiate properly signed withdrawals rather than breaking any on-chain cryptography. Cold storage was untouched, customer balances are stated as accurate, and the self-custodial Bitget Wallet ran on separate infrastructure. BleepingComputer's reporting attributes the theft to suspected North Korean hackers, and Bitget has suspended withdrawals while working with Mandiant, SlowMist and law enforcement to trace funds across chains CEO Gracy Chen named as Ethereum, XRP Ledger, Arbitrum and Avalanche.

The number that should worry every exchange operator is the gap between what public on-chain observers saw and what Bitget confirmed. Before disclosure, analysts tracked only $174 million to $183 million in suspicious movement, roughly half the final figure, so a live drain of nine figures can pass under exactly the telemetry most of the industry watches. The claimed mechanism compounds this: once the signing pipeline is compromised, wallet-tier segmentation does not contain the event, because the attacker no longer needs keys from any individual tier, only continued access to the service that authorizes transfers. The User Protection Fund holds 5,500 BTC, about $464 million, which absorbs this loss with roughly $112 million of headroom, meaning one incident consumes most of the backstop Bitget advertises to its customers.

Keep withdrawals halted until the signing path is re-secured: exchanges should treat the authorization-signing service as tier-zero, require out-of-band approval for outbound transfers above per-chain velocity baselines, and audit every identity that can call the wallet-service backend. On-chain teams should screen deposits against the recipient addresses Bitget has flagged and reported to law enforcement, and expect laundering movement on Ethereum, XRP Ledger, Arbitrum and Avalanche. Bitget has published no technical IoCs beyond those recipient addresses, so address screening and audit logs from your own signing and treasury services are the concrete artifacts available; hunt for outbound transfer bursts to freshly created addresses on the four named chains and for any counterparty touching the flagged addresses. Users have no defensive action beyond refusing any direct "recovery" or "compensation" request while withdrawals are suspended, since the fund is the stated recourse.

Two developments would move this assessment. The first is Bitget's root-cause statement on how the backend wallet-service was accessed, which it has not yet shared, and which determines whether other exchanges share the exposed component. The second is the pace of consolidation of the stolen funds on the four named chains, since DPRK-attributed thefts typically route through mixers and bridges within days. The loss figure itself may still move, because the confirmed $351.6 million against the $174 million to $183 million observers saw on-chain shows the accounting was still converging at disclosure.
