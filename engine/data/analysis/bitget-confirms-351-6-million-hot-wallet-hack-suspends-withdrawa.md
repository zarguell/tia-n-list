Bitget, a centralized crypto exchange founded in 2018, confirmed that attackers stole approximately $351.6 million from part of its three-tier wallet architecture, hitting only the internet-connected hot and warm wallet layers. Security systems flagged unauthorized transfers at 18:31 UTC on September 24, 2026, and the exchange activated emergency response within minutes, suspending withdrawals while keeping deposits and trading running. On-chain observers had tracked only $174 million to $183 million in suspicious movement before disclosure, meaning the confirmed total covers activity beyond what public analysts could see.

The intrusion path ran through a critical backend wallet-service system: the attacker compromised it, forged transfer information, and pushed the spoofed data through the exchange's own authorization-signing flow. Bitget has not yet explained how that backend was accessed. Stolen assets span Ethereum, XRP Ledger, Arbitrum, Avalanche, BNB Chain, plus USDT and USDC, with XRP the largest single-chain loss. Cold wallets and the self-custodial Bitget Wallet, which runs on separate infrastructure, were untouched.

CEO Gracy Chen attributed the operation to North Korean hackers, citing IP behavior patterns and on-chain analysis that she described as highly consistent with known North Korean threat actors. Bitget did not name a specific group, though coverage has pointed at Lazarus, and has not published the evidence behind the assessment. The exchange engaged Mandiant, SlowMist, law enforcement, and on-chain security partners; some blockchain foundations have frozen attacker-linked addresses. The User Protection Fund, holding 5,500 BTC worth about $464 million, is expected to cover losses, and customer balances are reported accurate.

Watch for a post-incident report explaining the backend compromise, whether the initial access technique is disclosed or stays vague, and how much of the $351.6 million is recovered or frozen before laundering completes. North Korean exchange heists historically move funds through mixers and cross-chain bridges over weeks, so the freeze announcements so far are early. The incident is also a test of the User Protection Fund's coverage claims at scale, and of whether hot and warm wallet signing flows adopt stricter out-of-band verification after this forgery pattern.
