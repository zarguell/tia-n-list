Crypto exchange Bitget confirmed that attackers stole approximately $351.6 million from its hot and warm wallet layers on September 24, 2026, in one of the largest exchange incidents of the year. Security systems flagged unauthorized transfers at 18:31 UTC and triggered emergency response within minutes; withdrawals are temporarily suspended while deposits and trading continue. Bitget attributes the attack to suspected North Korean hackers and says cold wallets, the self-custodial Bitget Wallet, and customer balances were unaffected.

The breakdown of internal wallet-service controls is the notable part: the company has not yet explained how attackers reached its key backend wallet system to forge transfer information and trigger the authorization-signing process. On-chain observers first tracked only $174 to $183 million in suspicious movements, so the confirmed $351.6 million indicates activity beyond what public analysts could see. CEO Gracy Chen said the incident spans Ethereum, the XRP Ledger, Arbitrum, and Avalanche, and Bitget has engaged law enforcement plus Mandiant and SlowMist for tracing. The User Protection Fund, holding 5,500 BTC worth about $464 million, is earmarked to cover losses.

A nine-figure hot-wallet drain attributed to DPRK actors will renew scrutiny of exchange custody architectures and signing controls. Watch for the post-mortem on how transfer forgeries passed authorization, whether the attackers' asset-swapping frustrates freezing efforts, and any regulatory response toward exchanges' warm-wallet exposure.
