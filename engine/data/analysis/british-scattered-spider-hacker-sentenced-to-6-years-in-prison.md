Tyler Robert Buchanan, 24, of Dundee, Scotland, received six years in a US federal prison for conspiracy to commit wire fraud and aggravated identity theft, the last sentence among five defendants in the case. Between September 2021 and April 2023 he and co-conspirators sent hundreds of SMS lures that impersonated employers or contracted IT and BPO suppliers, harvested credentials and personal data on lookalike sites, and routed the logins through a Telegram channel he ran with an accomplice. They used SIM swaps to defeat two-factor authentication and reach individual victims' crypto accounts and wallets. The theft figure grew from $8 million at the April 2026 plea to at least $11.6 million at sentencing; he was arrested in Spain in May 2024, held in US custody since April 2025, and faces a restitution hearing on December 11.

A sentence closes a prosecution, not a workflow. The chain documented here, SMS lure to harvested login to carrier-level SIM swap to wallet drain, turns one employee's response into irreversible loss, and the revision from $8 million to $11.6 million shows victim losses were still being counted after the plea. The victim set spans at least a dozen companies across telecommunications, technology, BPO, entertainment, IT supply, cloud communications and virtual currency. BPO and IT suppliers sit upstream of many tenants, so one compromised help desk can expose more downstream organizations than any single named company in the case. The temporal point matters more than the sentence length: reporting on the plea places the MGM Resorts, Caesars, Transport for London and 2025 UK retail attacks after Buchanan's involvement ended, so this outcome does not mark the retirement of the method.

Treat SMS as a weak second factor on any account that can move money or reset credentials: apply port-out locks and out-of-band verification to number changes and help-desk requests, and move high-value logins to app-based or hardware factors. Hunt for VPN-expiry lures and lookalike domains, for the aliases tylerb, Evefan and Dread Pirate Roberts in help-desk, SMS and messaging logs, and for SIM-swap or port-out events on numbers tied to privileged or wallet-bearing users. The April 2023 search of his Scottish residence found a text file holding wallet seed phrases and one victim's login details, so any recovered credential or seed-phrase list should be handled as a targeting ledger and checked against wallet activity.

Watch the December 11 restitution hearing for the final loss figure and victim list, since $11.6 million already exceeds the $8 million in the plea agreement. Also watch the unresolved cases against Ahmed Hossam Eldin Elbadawy, Evans Onyeaka Osiebo and Joel Martin Evans; if later intrusions are tied to the same conspiracy in those proceedings, the assessment changes from closed case to active campaign.
