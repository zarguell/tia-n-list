Tyler Robert Buchanan, a 24-year-old from Dundee, was sentenced to six years in a US federal prison for his role in a Scattered Spider phishing conspiracy. The charges covered SMS phishing between September 2021 and April 2023 against at least a dozen companies in telecommunications, technology, business process outsourcing, entertainment, cloud communications and cryptocurrency. Hundreds of texts impersonated the victim company or a contracted IT or BPO supplier and directed employees to lookalike pages where they entered credentials and personal information. Buchanan pleaded guilty in April 2026 to conspiracy to commit wire fraud and aggravated identity theft.

The captured logins were collected in a Telegram channel run by Buchanan and an accomplice, then used to reach employee accounts and company systems. Stolen information let the group identify individual cryptocurrency holders; SIM swaps defeated two-factor authentication and enabled wallet access. The theft total reached at least 11.6 million dollars from US victims, with a restitution hearing set for 11 December. DataBreaches notes that the 11 million figure in the DOJ press release covers US victims only, not every victim. Buchanan was arrested in Spain in 2024 and held by US authorities since April 2025. Co-defendant Noah Michael Urban is serving 10 years and agreed to more than 13.2 million dollars in restitution to 59 victims.

Buchanan is the last of the five defendants to be sentenced in the California case. Earlier reporting tied the group to targets including Coinbase, Twilio, Mailchimp and LastPass, and coverage links the wider Scattered Spider activity to the MGM Resorts ransomware attack estimated at 100 million dollars. For defenders, the durable detail is the chain from SMS lure to credential harvest to SIM swap, which moved from corporate intrusion to direct theft from individuals. Watch the December restitution hearing for a final loss figure and any attribution of additional intrusions to the same defendants.
