A malware analysis from malware.news traces a multi-stage intrusion linked to APT36 that begins with a cracked KMS Auto activator. The tool serves as the initial access vector, leading to cryptocurrency mining, remote access deployment, and a final scareware payload that poses as ransomware. Telemetry confirms KMS Auto presence across compromised endpoints but does not establish it as the direct entry point in every instance. The study highlights how unauthorized software activation utilities remain a persistent infection path for threat actors.
