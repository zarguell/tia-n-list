The open-source AI harness Cairn is being actively abused as an autonomous penetration-testing engine, with NorForge identifying three malicious clusters operating through more than 20 exposed control panels. The largest cluster, running in Chinese, spans over 1,100 attack projects across 14 dashboards and targets e-commerce and payment infrastructure in the US, Japan, and Australia. Threat actors rank targets by traffic, validate them with Microsoft Playwright, and feed them to Cairn agents that post intents and findings to a shared blackboard; the observed end goal is persistent access through webshells rather than immediate exfiltration. The events carry no CVE identifiers and no named actor attribution beyond language clustering, which makes this a supply-chain and exposure problem rather than a single-patch vulnerability.

A defender reading only the timeline misses the economic framing that explains why this story matters today: Gambit Security's earlier research (September 2026) reconstructed an operator staging server and showed autonomous attacks running at an average cost of roughly $25 per target, with at least 27 retailers compromised, over 600,000 unexpired card records stolen from two victims, and automated SQL wipe commands embedded in agent skill files. That cost structure means the barrier to entry for autonomous intrusion is now lower than manual red-teaming; the 1,100-project cluster is not an experiment but an industrial pipeline. Because the actor's monetization path is deferred webshell access, victims may not see immediate fraud signals, so exposure should be treated as latent infrastructure compromise rather than a completed breach.

Defenders should hunt for exposed Cairn panels (public-facing dashboards with open project boards), review application and WAF logs for Playwright-based reconnaissance patterns preceding injection attempts, and scan e-commerce and payment endpoints for webshell artifacts. The events mention automated validation and traffic-based target ranking; that implies telemetry fields such as rapid sequential requests from headless browsers against product and checkout paths, followed by anomalous POST patterns to upload handlers. Patch and harden any exposed AI harness instances, restrict dashboard access to internal ranges, and treat any identified webshell as evidence of a broader agent-driven campaign rather than an isolated intrusion.

Watch for NorForge naming additional clusters or mapping the 14 dashboards to specific infrastructure providers, and for Gambit or other researchers publishing updated staging-server artifacts that could yield IoCs. A shift from deferred webshell access to active card-skimmer deployment, or the appearance of a CVE tied to Cairn's agent execution model, would change the severity assessment sharply.
