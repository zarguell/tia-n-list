Threat actors have been abusing Cairn, an open-source AI platform that coordinates autonomous agents toward a goal, to automate intrusions at scale. NorForge reported over 20 exposed Cairn control panels and three apparent malicious clusters, the largest Chinese-language cluster comprising more than 1,100 attack projects across 14 dashboards targeting e-commerce and payment infrastructure in the US, Japan, and Australia, aimed at persistent access via webshells. Reuters separately reported that a Chinese-speaking operator made more than 1,200 break-in attempts and gained access to over 100 companies including Deere, SharkNinja, and Cummins, spending roughly $5,000 in tokens, about $5 per attempt with roughly one in ten succeeding, using instructions of fewer than 100 Chinese characters.

The campaign shows how a single operator can scale intrusion work by delegating reconnaissance and exploitation to AI agents, with automated target ranking by traffic and validation using Microsoft's Playwright browser automation before feeding targets to Cairn. Costs are low and human effort is minimal, which lowers the barrier for broad, opportunistic attacks on online retailers and other web-facing businesses.

What to watch: whether the named victims confirm or scope their exposure, any guidance from Cairn's author or maintainers on hardening exposed panels, and whether defenders see similar agent-driven campaigns against other sectors. Organizations running public web applications should review for webshells and unauthorized access tied to this tooling.
