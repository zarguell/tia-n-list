Threat actors abused the open-source AI platform Cairn to automate intrusions against more than 100 companies, with over 1,200 break-in attempts and roughly $50 in token costs per successful compromise, according to NorForge research published October 19 and Reuters reporting. The largest cluster used Chinese-language instructions and comprised over 1,100 attack projects across 14 dashboards, targeting e-commerce and payment infrastructure in the US, Japan, and Australia. Agents worked step by step toward persistent access via webshells, validating targets with Microsoft's Playwright before feeding them to Cairn.

The campaign shows how autonomous AI agents can scale intrusion work at low cost, with a single operator reportedly spending about $5,000 in tokens total. Named victims include Deere, SharkNinja, and Cummins. Defenders should monitor for exposed Cairn control panels and AI-agent-driven reconnaissance patterns.
