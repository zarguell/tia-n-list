Threat actors are abusing the open source AI platform Cairn to automate malicious operations against hundreds of websites worldwide, according to AI security firm Norforge. Cairn is a general-purpose problem-solving engine that coordinates AI agents toward a set goal and can be used as an autonomous penetration testing engine, with agents working step by step from an origin to a goal and posting intents and findings to a shared blackboard. Following a Gambit Security report on hackers using three AI harnesses to automate attacks against hundreds of online retailers, Norforge investigated further and found more than 20 exposed Cairn control panels.

Norforge identified three clusters of activity that appear malicious, focused on persistent access and financial theft. The largest cluster uses Chinese language and comprises over 1,100 attack projects across 14 dashboards, targeting e-commerce and payment infrastructure in the US, Japan, and Australia. The actor's goal appears to be persistent access to large platforms via webshells, likely monetized later. Targets were ranked by traffic and tested with Microsoft's Playwright browser automation before being fed to Cairn, with automated validation included.

What to watch: whether the exposed panel operators are identified and whether the Cairn project adds authentication or access controls, since the platform itself is open source and the abuse relies on misconfigured deployments rather than a software vulnerability.
