Threat actors abused the open-source AI platform Cairn to automate intrusions against more than 100 companies, with over 1,200 break-in attempts and roughly $50 in token costs per successful compromise, according to NorForge research published October 19 and Reuters reporting. The largest cluster used Chinese-language instructions and comprised over 1,100 attack projects across 14 dashboards, targeting e-commerce and payment infrastructure in the US, Japan, and Australia. Agents worked step by step toward persistent access via webshells, validating targets with Microsoft's Playwright before feeding them to Cairn.

The operator's own records showed about $5,000 in total token spending and instructions written in fewer than 100 Chinese characters. Reuters named Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins among victims after reviewing the attacker's artifacts. NorForge found more than 20 exposed Cairn control panels, three clusters of malicious activity and a workflow that ranked targets by traffic before autonomous testing began. The tool's author said Cairn was not built for hacking.

A Polish report on 24 November again describes a Chinese-speaking operator using a demonstration build of the open-source platform against more than 100 websites. It adds no new victim or technical artifact beyond the earlier reporting, but it confirms the campaign is still being picked up outside the original NorForge and Reuters coverage. Watch for further exposed Cairn panels, any shift from webshell persistence to financial theft, and whether operators patch the e-commerce stacks that the agents tested. The low cost per success is the measurable change here: one operator with a short prompt and a modest token budget reached more than 100 company sites.
