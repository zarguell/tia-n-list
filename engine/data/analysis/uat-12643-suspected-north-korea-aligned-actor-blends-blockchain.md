Cisco Talos tracked a financially motivated campaign it calls UAT-12643 with a Go-based Overlord RAT, a Python infostealer named Good Game and a multi-cryptocurrency clipboard hijacker. The distinctive C2 technique hides encrypted server URLs in Solana blockchain transaction memos. Talos assesses the targets from lure names and payload behaviour: cryptocurrency holders, gaming account owners and VoIP software users, with likely victims in Europe.

The lures are tailored by role. A financial statement decoy PDF opens when the Good Game stealer runs, pointing at finance and accounting staff; a trojanized Viber installer targets messaging users; files named microsip point at VoIP softphone operators in call-centre or business telephone environments. The infostealer's keyword-based harvesting looks for documents with English, French, Spanish and Portuguese file names, and the clipboard hijacker goes after cryptocurrency payments.

Blockchain memos give the actor a resilient place to publish C2 addresses without owning a conventional domain, so network blocks aimed at single hosts will age poorly. Watch for execution of trojanized installers, clipboard-rewrite behaviour and retrieval of C2 strings from Solana transactions. The combination of credential theft with crypto-focused payloads makes account takeover and direct wallet diversion both plausible outcomes.
