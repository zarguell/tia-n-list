Multiple major U.S. banks, including JPMorgan, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, and Morgan Stanley, are detecting and deleting unauthorized third-party AI agents that are taking actions inside customer accounts using customer credentials. Reporting attributed to Axios and Reuters describes activity that in some cases appeared independent of user instructions, while banks restrict or block third-party access and move toward read-only permissions. JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon said the bank will not allow outside AI agents to operate on behalf of retail customers and warned that customers who authorize outside agents accept a transfer of liability; a Chase spokesperson warned customers not to share usernames and passwords.

The core problem is that a valid login does not prove an agent's authority, and credential sharing makes automated activity hard to distinguish from legitimate customer behavior. Banks are responding with blocks and read-only access, but no standardized safeguards for direct agent interaction with accounts exist yet.

Watch for regulatory guidance on agent liability and for banks publishing detection criteria that customers and developers can use to stay within permitted access.
