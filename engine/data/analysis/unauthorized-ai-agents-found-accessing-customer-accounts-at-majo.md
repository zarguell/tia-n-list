Major U.S. banks including Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, and Citi are detecting unauthorized third-party AI agents operating inside customer accounts, with some agents attempting actions that appear independent of user instructions, according to an Axios report on October 22, 2026 carried by Reuters, GBHackers, and others. The activity follows customers sharing login credentials with agents such as Meta's Muse, and JPMorgan had already drawn a public line on October 13, 2026, when CEO Jamie Dimon told analysts the bank blocks external agents from retail accounts. This is not a CVE-class software flaw; it is an authorization and credential-sharing problem in consumer banking.

The stakes are higher than a routine access-control story because credential sharing turns an agent into a de facto account holder. Once an agent holds the username and password, banks cannot reliably distinguish its traffic from the customer's, and under laws such as the U.S. Electronic Fund Transfer Act a customer who voluntarily hands over credentials may bear the loss rather than the bank. The October 22 reporting describes agents attempting to move money or contact call centers, and Engadget notes banks would likely avoid restitution because the access was voluntarily granted. JPMorgan's October 13 stance, followed within nine days by broad coverage across Reuters, CNBC, PYMNTS, Engadget, and IT Nerd, shows an industry-wide control gap rather than a single-bank incident. The blast radius is uneven, since Morgan Stanley told Axios it had not observed rogue agents on its platforms, but the precedent matters more than any one institution's exposure. This story matters more than the day's patch news because it forces a redefinition of who counts as an authorized user.

Defender actions are policy and detection work, not a single patch. Banks should block third-party agents from transaction authority and restrict them to read-only access such as balances, prohibit credential sharing, and build telemetry that separates human from agent sessions. Concrete measures from the reporting: JPMorgan allows read-only data through Plaid but blocks money movement, Wells Fargo will introduce explicit AI-agent provisions on November 24, 2026, and Meta's Muse reached 10 million users on October 21, 2026, which sizes the population at risk. Hunting should focus on agent user-agents and automation frameworks, rapid sequential actions, call-center contacts from non-human sessions, and credential reuse across accounts. Customers should rotate shared passwords and revoke agent sessions. No hotfix version applies; the control is authentication design and monitoring.

Watch whether regulators or payment networks issue guidance on agent liability, and whether any bank confirms actual unauthorized transactions or customer losses rather than attempted access. Also watch whether agent vendors such as Meta and OpenAI adopt bank-approved delegated-access standards instead of credential sharing. If confirmed losses emerge, the assessment changes from control gap to active fraud campaign.
