Multiple major U.S. banks are detecting and removing third party AI agents that took actions inside customer accounts using customer credentials. Reporting from Axios and Reuters described activity in which some agents appeared to act independently of user instructions, while the banks restrict or block third party access and move toward read only permissions.

JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon said the bank will not allow outside AI agents to operate for retail customers, and a Chase spokesperson warned customers not to share usernames and passwords. The core problem is that a valid login does not prove an agent has authority, and credential sharing makes automated activity hard to distinguish from legitimate customer behavior.

Banks have not published standardized safeguards for direct agent interaction with accounts. Watch for regulatory guidance on agent liability and for banks to publish detection criteria.
