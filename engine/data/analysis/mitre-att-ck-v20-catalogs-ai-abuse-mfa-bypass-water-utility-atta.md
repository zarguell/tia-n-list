MITRE released ATT&CK v20, adding nine new Enterprise techniques and sub-techniques. The update tracks prompt injection (two variants: direct input and hidden ingestion), attackers using an organization's own AI agents for data theft, poisoning web content to sway AI recommendations, disrupting LLM-assisted malware analysis, theft of application-specific passwords (bypassing MFA prompts), CAPTCHA evasion (solving and avoidance sub-techniques), and expanded coverage of compromised software dependencies including IDE and coding agent configuration files. In ICS, a new campaign covers attacks on internet-exposed PLCs at over 30 Minnesota water and wastewater utilities, where operators were locked out.

This matters because adversary behavior is now explicitly cataloged around AI systems, MFA bypass, and operational technology. Security teams using ATT&CK for detection mapping and threat modeling need to review the new technique IDs and sub-techniques against their environments. The water utility campaign also reinforces the exposure risk of internet-connected industrial control systems.

Watch for vendor threat reports that reference these new technique IDs, and for any further detail on the Minnesota PLC incidents (operator impact, restoration timelines, attribution) which could elevate this from framework mapping to active incident tracking.
