ILC Dover and ILC Astrospace agreed to pay $13 million to resolve U.S. government allegations that the defense and aerospace firm failed to meet cybersecurity contract requirements from 2018 through 2024. The settlement covers contracts with the Department of the Army, Missile Defense Agency, and NASA, where ILC allegedly submitted false claims for payment while using a third-party cloud provider without proper safeguards and failing to maintain mandated security plans. The parent company, Ingersoll Rand, stated it inherited the issue upon acquiring ILC Dover in June 2024.

The case is a significant enforcement action under the False Claims Act tied to cybersecurity contract obligations, reinforcing that federal contractors can face substantial penalties for misrepresenting compliance. It highlights the risk for defense and aerospace suppliers that handle sensitive government data without meeting NIST-based contract security requirements.

Watch for whether the Department of Justice expands similar actions against other contractors with shared-cloud gaps or incomplete security plans, and whether ILC’s remediation commitments include third-party audits or CMMC-level assessments.
