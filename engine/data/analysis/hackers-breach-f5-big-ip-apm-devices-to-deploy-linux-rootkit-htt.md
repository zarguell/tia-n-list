Sophos X-Ops dissected on September 7, 2026 a Linux userland rootkit recovered from compromised F5 BIG-IP APM environments, a second-stage payload that ESET had already named PoisonedRefresh in April after finding its components on VirusTotal. An installer prepends a malicious ELF to the Apache httpd binary at /usr/sbin/httpd, disables SELinux, and seeds the BIG-IP install media so the implant survives upgrades. The second stage hides its strings with RC4, hooks __libc_start_main so it runs before the host's main(), waits for Apache to load libphp through the APR loader apr_dso_load, then intercepts mmap() inside libphp to prepend a PHP web shell to the in-memory copy of three webtop scripts, apm_css.php3, full_wt.php3, and webtop_popup_css.php3, while the files on disk stay byte-for-byte clean. The shell evaluates requests carrying the magic prefix BSOHAzPB and answers HTTP 201 with a text/css content type, and the delivery vector is CVE-2025-53521, an unauthenticated CWE-121 stack-based buffer overflow in the apmd process on the data plane.

The history is what makes this more than a malware write-up. F5 shipped the fix on October 15, 2025 while categorizing the bug as denial of service at CVSS 7.5, and re-rated it critical RCE at 9.8 only in March 2026, after Schuberg Philis, Fox-IT, and the Dutch NCSC surfaced real intrusions; CISA added it to the KEV catalog on March 27, 2026 with a March 30 deadline for federal agencies. The Sophos sample explains why that rating history matters operationally: an implant that lives in process memory and in the upgrade image is invisible to the file-integrity and web-root scans most teams ran after the March disclosure, so a device patched since October and declared clean can still be serving a web shell from RAM. Exposure is not hypothetical either, since Shadowserver counted over 240,000 BIG-IP instances exposed online in March and still 795 endpoints vulnerable to CVE-2025-53521 on September 7, and each APM system is the gateway that decides which users reach the VPN and everything behind it. The implant also opens a second channel, a token-protected UNIX socket at /run/bigtlog.pipe that hands its streams to /bin/bash, so any additional foothold on the box converts to an interactive shell without a single listening TCP port.

Apply the branch fixes 17.5.1.3, 17.1.3, 16.1.6.1, and 15.1.10.8, but treat that as containment, because F5's guidance in K000156741 and the IoC article K000160486 instructs anyone who ran a vulnerable APM version at any point to hunt for compromise regardless of current version, and to rebuild configuration from a known-good source instead of restoring UCS archives that may carry the malware. On the host, look for creation of /run/bigtlog.pipe or /run/bigstart.ltm, hash or size mismatches on /usr/sbin/httpd and /usr/bin/umount, setenforce 0 in audit logs, Apache workers reading /proc/self/maps, RWX-then-RX permission flips around libphp, and httpd lineage spawning /bin/bash. On the wire, flag rare POSTs to the .php3 webtop endpoints and PHP responses pairing HTTP 201 with Content-Type text/css, with bodies prefixed by BSOHAzPB. Sophos detects the analyzed sample, SHA-256 26bd5b0722d1dbab5db749a063c49bc8638653ac2addfead7a9cb3d6d57bccc9, as Linux/Agnt-IC, and ESET has published installer hashes under the PoisonedRefresh name.

Two developments would move this assessment. A further revision of K000160486 adding files beyond the currently listed set would widen the compromise perimeter, and the vendor guidance is demonstrably still moving, since K000156741 already carries an April 1, 2026 revision date. Sophos also found no client component that talks to the socket, so any report identifying the component that authenticates with the Kzwd6jM5 token and drives /run/bigtlog.pipe would confirm the backdoor is used end to end, while evidence that the umount installer propagates between BIG-IP systems on its own would turn isolated compromises into fleet-wide ones.
