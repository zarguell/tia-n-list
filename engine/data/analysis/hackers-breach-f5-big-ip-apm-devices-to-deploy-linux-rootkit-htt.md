A Linux userland rootkit named PoisonedRefresh by ESET has been recovered from compromised F5 BIG-IP APM systems, and Sophos X-Ops dissected the second stage on September 7, one day before this story surfaced. Delivery runs through CVE-2025-53521, an unauthenticated remote code execution in the apmd process that F5 remediated as denial of service at CVSS 7.5 on October 15, 2025, then re-rated critical RCE at 9.8 (CWE-121, stack-based buffer overflow) in March 2026 once exploitation surfaced. An installer observed as a malicious umount component prepends a 0x5430-byte payload to /usr/sbin/httpd, modifies SELinux configuration, and persists across BIG-IP upgrade images. The second stage hooks __libc_start_main so it runs before Apache's main(), waits via hooked apr_dso_load until libphp loads, then redirects open, mmap, and related calls inside libphp to prepend a PHP web shell to the in-memory copy of three APM webtop scripts, apm_css.php3, full_wt.php3, and webtop_popup_css.php3. Requests whose body carries the magic prefix BSOHAzPB are decrypted and executed through eval(), replies return HTTP 201 with a text/css content type, and a token-protected AF_UNIX socket at /run/bigtlog.pipe can hand its streams to /bin/bash with no listening TCP port.

The rating history is the operational trap. Teams that patched in the spring were hunting with the March picture: webshell checks on disk, log greps, and the F5 IoC list in K000160486 for activity c05d5254. PoisonedRefresh defeats exactly that, because the on-disk PHP files stay byte-for-byte clean while the shell exists only in libphp's memory view, the implant rides the upgrade images admins were told to apply, and the socket backdoor never touches the network stack. A device patched since October and declared clean by file-integrity tooling can still be serving a web shell from RAM. The scale is not marginal: Shadowserver counted over 240,000 BIG-IP instances exposed online when the reclassification landed, and its CVE-2025-53521 tracker still showed 795 vulnerable exposed endpoints on September 7. Each one is an identity gateway deciding who reaches the VPN and everything behind it, and the roughly six months between the March 27 KEV listing and the first public description of the implant means spring intrusions that skipped a memory-aware sweep may plausibly still be live.

Treat the branch fixes, 17.5.1.3 and 17.1.3 on 17.x, 16.1.6.1 on 16.x, and 15.1.10.8 on 15.x, as containment rather than eviction. F5 directs anyone who ran a vulnerable version, or upgraded from one, through IoC article K000160486 and compromise guidance K11438344, and warns that UCS backups taken after a compromise carry persistent malware, so capture volatile evidence first, then rebuild configuration from a known-good source instead of restoring archives, noting that restarting Apache destroys the in-memory shell and resets nothing else. On the host, hunt for creation of /run/bigtlog.pipe, Apache workers reading /proc/self/maps, RWX-then-RX permission flips around libphp, and httpd lineage spawning /bin/bash. On the wire, flag rare POSTs to the .php3 webtop endpoints and PHP replies pairing HTTP 201 with a text/css content type. Match systems against Sophos sample SHA-256 26bd5b0722d1dbab5db749a063c49bc8638653ac2addfead7a9cb3d6d57bccc9, detected as Linux/Agnt-IC, and ESET's SHA-1s E5066C2490197FFBE0E916BC232114A61CB09A16 and 7A2FC3510B502D8FDC2548F907AF08308840851C.

Two developments would change the read. Attribution first: F5 tracks the activity only as c05d5254 with no actor named, and Sophos found no client component that authenticates to the socket with the Kzwd6jM5 token, so a report identifying the operator-side tooling would confirm the backdoor works end to end and sharpen the victim profile. Tracker movement second: a rise above the 795 baseline in Shadowserver's series, or a revision of K000160486 listing artifacts beyond the umount and httpd components, would indicate a renewed or wider deployment window and force another memory-aware sweep of already-patched estates.
