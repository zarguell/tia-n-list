Check Point Research's year-long investigation ties three seemingly unrelated espionage campaigns to a single supplier of Chinese-nexus tooling it tracks as CopperDragon. The clusters, named SilentDelivery, OceanGate and DarkWire, span Latin America, Asia and Europe and hit telecommunications providers, government bodies, military entities, research institutions and critical infrastructure. They are linked through shared infrastructure features and code similarities within the Sparrow malware framework, which Check Point treats as a common toolkit rather than one actor's private arsenal.

CopperDragon is described as a potential "digital quartermaster" supporting China-nexus threat actors including FamousSparrow, Earth Estries, TropicTrooper and UAT-9244, with each cluster running a different toolset and a different initial access path. Intrusion vectors ranged from compromising internet-facing infrastructure to highly selective watering-hole attacks against government, shipping and IT and research sites. The research surfaced previously undocumented tools plus Windows and Linux variants, passive backdoor capabilities and a connection to the Melofee backdoor.

Defenders should treat the three clusters as one supply-side problem rather than three separate incidents: hardening internet-facing systems, monitoring for Sparrow-family implants and watering-hole indicators, and sharing indicators across sectors. The quartermaster model means tooling can be redeployed under new operators, so detection should focus on framework artifacts and infrastructure overlaps rather than a single intrusion set's habits.
