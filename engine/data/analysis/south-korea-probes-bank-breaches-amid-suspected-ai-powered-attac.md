South Korea's Financial Services Commission held an emergency meeting after a series of cyberattacks on the country's financial institutions, confirming a data breach at Shinhan Bank and additional incidents at other banks including Kookmin Bank. Both are large private commercial banks holding more than 400 billion dollars in assets. Authorities have launched on-site investigations and are sharing findings with KISA, the country's data protection agency, while President Lee ordered a thorough investigation into personal data leaks at financial and public institutions.

The disclosed numbers are substantial. Shinhan Bank leaked details of 25,000 customers. Kookmin Bank leaked credit card information for 119,000 clients. Hana Bank separately suffered a limited-scope breach through its sales-support system. BleepingComputer's report frames the incidents as suspected AI-powered attacks, and Yonhap reported that a server used in the attacks carried an HTML page title containing Chinese-language text, an attribution clue that officials have not confirmed.

The cluster matters because it shows multiple major banks hit in a coordinated window, with data exfiltration at each, rather than a single institution's failure. Regulators are already converting the response into mandatory improvements, and consumer compensation is on the agenda alongside the forensics.

Watch for attribution findings, particularly whether the AI-aided attack claim survives review and what the Chinese-language server evidence resolves into. The scale of leaked credit card data, follow-on fraud against the 119,000 Kookmin clients, and any new FSC requirements for bank security controls will define the aftermath.
