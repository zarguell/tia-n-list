Arion Kurtaj, 21, was sentenced to seven years in prison for hacking Rockstar Games and leaking roughly 90 gameplay videos of the unreleased Grand Theft Auto VI, then released the same day on license because of time already served in a psychiatric hospital. Deemed unfit to stand trial due to severe autism, he had pleaded guilty at Woolwich Crown Court to six offenses, four under the Computer Misuse Act plus fraud and possessing a mobile phone in prison. The hacks, committed while on bail for earlier offenses, also hit Uber and Revolut in September 2022, and he carried them out from a hotel room using an Amazon Fire Stick plugged into the television after police had confiscated his computer. The judge initially set nine years, reducing it by two for psychiatric detention, and warned Kurtaj he could be returned to prison if he breached release conditions.

The case matters less for the sentence than for what it shows about high-impact breaches by a single determined actor using improvised tooling, and about how UK courts handle offenders found unfit to stand trial. The extortion play reportedly threatened to leak GTA VI source code, which is the kind of asset that makes even a small intrusion a major commercial incident.

Watch for whether the supervision terms and community monitoring actually constrain reoffending, and whether Rockstar or other targets change their handling of source-code access after the case.
