Arion Kurtaj, a Lapsus$ member, pleaded guilty to six offences after using social engineering and unauthorized access to internal systems at Rockstar Games, Uber and Revolut in September 2022. At Rockstar he breached internal Slack, stole 90 Grand Theft Auto VI clips totaling nearly 60 minutes, and threatened to release source code; with his computer confiscated, he worked from a hotel room through an Amazon Fire Stick, phone, keyboard and mouse. This was account and SaaS abuse, not a CVE-bearing product flaw, and the sentencing phase ended with a seven-year sentence on 23 November 2026 followed by same-day release on licence after credit for time already detained.

The failed control was containment by device: removing the laptop and restricting internet use did not sever the identity, sessions or cloud access that made the intrusion possible. That makes this a warning about identity and SaaS controls rather than another sentencing oddity. The cost was concrete: Rockstar reported $5 million in recovery plus thousands of staff hours, the CPS put Uber's loss above $2.8 million, and thousands of Revolut customers had information compromised. A successful intrusion therefore bought both extortion leverage over unreleased source code and lasting commercial and customer harm.

No vendor patch applies. Revoke sessions and tokens, rotate exposed Slack, VPN, cloud and help-desk credentials, require phishing-resistant MFA, and restrict privileged SaaS and source repositories to managed devices. On corporate and hospitality networks, block unmanaged streaming devices and personal hotspots from reaching business resources, and revalidate help-desk identity checks rather than relying on caller-supplied employee details. Hunt Slack audit logs for the employee-wide attacker message and unexpected integrations or bots, correlate identity and cloud access from new devices with the September 2022 hotel-room activity, and search leak-monitoring output for `TeaPotUberHacker`, `teapotuberhacker` and copies of the 90-clip set.

Two developments would change the assessment: confirmed publication of Rockstar source code, which would increase the commercial loss, and a technical postmortem identifying the initial access path and token-revocation timeline. If victims show that sessions were promptly killed and replacement-device access was blocked, the case is a closed historical breach; if access persisted after those steps, it is evidence of a control design failure that still applies to other enterprises.
