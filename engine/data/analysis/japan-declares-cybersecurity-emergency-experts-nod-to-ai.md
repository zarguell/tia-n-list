Japan announced an emergency cybersecurity package built around artificial intelligence and dedicated cyber rescue teams, following a wave of data breaches and unauthorized access incidents at major companies and government agencies. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi unveiled the measures at a Friday ministerial meeting, warning that rapid response is essential in an era of AI-generated attacks and that cases of unauthorized access are affecting daily life and corporate activity.

The 10 billion yen package, about 63 million US dollars, aims to make the public and private sectors share information and respond swiftly to incidents. It expands active cyber defense, legalized in October, letting the government access servers used by hackers as a preemptive measure, and includes deployment of response teams in cases that challenge national security.
