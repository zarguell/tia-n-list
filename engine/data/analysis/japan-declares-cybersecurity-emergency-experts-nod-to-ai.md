Japan announced an emergency cybersecurity package on Friday after a wave of unauthorized access and client data leaks hit major companies and government agencies. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi told a ministerial meeting that rapid response is essential in the era of AI generated attacks and pledged all government resources to lift national response capability. The package is budgeted at 10 billion yen, about 63 million dollars, and centers on public private information sharing, swift victim assistance and faster recovery. Japan Times reporting on Friday confirms the rollout framing: Takaichi's government presenting the measures as an emergency response to an ongoing national cybersecurity crisis.

Two elements carry the substance. The government plans to use artificial intelligence in detection and response and to stand up cyber rescue teams that deploy to affected organizations. It also leans on the active cyber defense law legalized in October, which gives the state enhanced capability to access hacker operated servers as a preemptive measure, including in cases touching national security.

What to watch is how the preemptive access power is actually used and whether the rescue teams reach beyond the largest firms. The announcement was short on incident level detail about the breach wave that triggered it, so the next signal will be agency level reporting on which sectors were hit and whether the shared information channel produces concrete indicators. Companies operating in Japan should expect closer coordination requests and prepare to share incident data faster than before.
