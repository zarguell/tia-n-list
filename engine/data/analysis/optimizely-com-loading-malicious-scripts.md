On November 16, 2026, malicious JavaScript was placed in Optimizely Web Experimentation customer projects and served from cdn.optimizely.com. Report URI examined 1,611 Optimizely scripts and found 188 infected snippets belonging to 178 organizations, including Zoom, BBC, CNN, Fox News, Salesforce, GitLab, IKEA, HSBC, Equifax, Ledger and Air France. The loader pulled 1688360.cc/track.js. Analysis by Amirul Cyber and Beazley Security breaks the payload into a checksum-validating crypto clipper covering eight chains, a screenshot and cookie exfiltration module posting to /hit.php, a card skimmer using Luhn checks and sendBeacon to /card.php, and an iOS second stage hosted on two .icu hosts tied to Coruna and DarkSword delivery infrastructure.

Krebs on Security reported that the malicious payload was rebuilt 26 times during the day and describes DarkSword and Coruna as zero-click kits chaining about six holes to take over outdated iPhones and iPads. Optimizely confirmed that an unauthorized party exploited a vulnerability in the Experimentation account signup flow to add JavaScript to customer projects. The company says it blocked the vulnerable path, removed injected code, ended active sessions, revoked tokens, reset affected accounts and blocked code delivery in Cloudflare. Reporting puts the exposure window at nearly 19 hours. Two analyzed sites blocked the external payload through CSP script-src even though the poisoned snippet still loaded. Successful iOS exploitation and the volume of stolen card data have not been confirmed.

Hudson Rock and Kirk of Ohey traced the same campaign across four script-delivery providers, with poisoned responses from Kissmetrics, StorifyMe and minute-ly archived as early as November 13, three days before the Optimizely wave. Access paths for those three are unresolved, as are visitor counts and losses. A Censys ARC post published on November 18 maps the MCH control panel and Caishen stack behind the November 16 poisoning and notes that other deployments share it. Defenders should review Project JavaScript settings and account access, check logs for the named domains and track.js hash, and treat payment fields on affected pages during the November 16 window as possible card data exposure.
