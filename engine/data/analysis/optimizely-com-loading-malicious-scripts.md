Attackers injected a one-line loader into customer-editable Project JavaScript served from cdn.optimizely.com, including cdn.optimizely.com/js/5113954737848320.js on an about.gitlab.com page. The loader fetched 1688360.cc/track.js, a script that checksum-validates and swaps cryptocurrency addresses across eight chains, hooks fetch and XHR to skim payment cards with Luhn and brand checks, and exfiltrates cookies, page URL and screenshots to /hit.php and /card.php. On iPhone and iPad user agents it also loads a second stage from two .icu hosts that researchers tie to the Coruna and DarkSword exploit kits. Successful device exploitation has not been confirmed.

BleepingComputer's November 17 report describes a suspected supply chain attack on Optimizely and cites Scott Helme's analysis of 1,611 platform scripts: 188 infected JavaScript files belonging to 178 organizations. urlscan.io scans independently showed the script loaded or attempted to load on Zoom, KLM, GitLab, BBC, Scheels, CNN, American Girl, ManageBuilding, Smartsheet, Stamps.com, Salesforce, Fox News, Mutual of Omaha, American Eagle, Air France, Change.org, Optimizely, HSBC Insurance, Bitly, IG and UNICEF USA. Report URI counted more than 186 affected high-value sites; other research found 21 web properties with verified poisoned script responses.

The same campaign reached scripts from Kissmetrics, StorifyMe and minute-ly, with earliest observations for those providers on November 13, three days before the Optimizely reports. Two captured sites blocked the payload with CSP script-src and still logged the request, so exposure counts need to separate payload requested from payload executed. A clean file on recheck says nothing about earlier hours. Optimizely had published no statement, root cause or remediation guidance at the time of reporting.

Check every served snippet at cdn.optimizely.com/js/PROJECT_ID.js or cdn.optimizely.com/public/ACCOUNT_ID/s/NAME.js for 1688360 and atob(, and remove the snippet from checkout, login and payment pages until the vendor explains what happened. Block 1688360.cc and the named .icu hosts in CSP, hunt proxy and DNS logs for /track.js, /hit.php, /card.php and boot.js paths, and treat first-party card form fields as possible card data exposure. The assessment changes if Optimizely shows a platform-wide compromise or if forensic evidence confirms the iOS stages ran.
