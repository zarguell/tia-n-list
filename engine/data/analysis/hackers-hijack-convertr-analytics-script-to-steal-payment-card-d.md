A Brazilian skimming group compromised a shared analytics script on the Convertr e-commerce platform to steal payment card data during checkout. Convertr powers online stores and checkout for dozens of major Brazilian fashion brands handling roughly R$2 billion ($370 million) in sales, so the injected skimmer reached the checkout pages of every merchant on the platform. The campaign was identified on October 24 and has been attributed to a gang active since at least July 2024 that targets Magento, OpenCart, and WooCommerce stores.

E-commerce security firm Sansec found the attackers modified the Plausible analytics tracker file hosted on a self-managed server operated by Convertr, injecting malicious code into a legitimate script that loads on merchant checkout pages. Sansec suspects initial access came through CVE-2026-8467, an unauthenticated command-execution flaw in Convertr's outdated Plausible Community Edition installation, but that has not been confirmed. The skimmer evolved during the campaign: earlier versions used generic payment-field patterns, while a more refined variant was pushed by October 26.

Merchants on shared analytics or tag-management infrastructure should treat third-party scripts as part of their payment attack surface, verify integrity of hosted trackers, and confirm whether Convertr has patched its Plausible installation. Cardholders who shopped affected stores should monitor statements for unauthorized charges.
