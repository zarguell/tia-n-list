Corma, a San Francisco and Tel Aviv startup, emerged from stealth with a $60 million seed round led by Sequoia Capital with Khosla Ventures and Coatue participating. The company is building what it calls the first foundation model purpose-built for defensive cybersecurity, aimed at closing the gap between AI-powered offensive capability and human-led defense. Corma says its first model is already deployed at Fortune 100 and Fortune 500 organizations and claims it cuts threat response times by roughly 94%.

The round is notable for its size, among the larger seed raises in security, and for the thesis behind it: general-purpose frontier models are increasingly effective at attack work, and defense is still mostly manual analyst labor. A foundation model trained specifically on defensive tasks changes the economics of detection engineering, triage, and response, which is why the funding is being read as a bet that AI-native defense becomes a distinct product category rather than a feature bolted onto existing platforms.

Watch for independent validation of the response-time claims, which are vendor-reported so far. The real test is whether the model generalizes beyond the early customers and whether enterprises adopt it as a platform or as a point solution layered on top of existing SOC tooling.
