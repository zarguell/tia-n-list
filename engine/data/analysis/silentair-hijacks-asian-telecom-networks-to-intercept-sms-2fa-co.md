SilentAir is a newly identified state-level actor that Enea tracks with high confidence, assessed with low confidence as China-nexus, which runs SS7 and sometimes Diameter signaling attacks from network nodes it has hijacked inside mobile operators in at least six Asian countries. Active since before 2025 and monitored by Enea since early 2025, it routes a target's incoming calls and SMS through the compromised node to harvest subscriber details, with location tracking a minor component. Besides Taiwan as the primary target, roughly a dozen other countries and territories are hit, including East Africa, and Enea observed intercepted 2FA codes arriving moments after interception for some targets.

The exposure is not a software bug with a patch but the signaling layer between operators, so every subscriber of an affected operator is reachable and endpoint controls do not help. Enea reports waves ranging from a handful to several thousand subscribers in a month, with interception making up 18.30 percent of SilentAir activity against 2.07 percent for the surveillance companies behind most signaling attacks, which indicates account-takeover intent rather than passive collection. Silent SMS messages that never appear on the recipient's phone are used, likely to confirm successful interception, and activity follows a UTC+8 working day with no weekend traffic. This story matters more than routine malware news because SMS-based one-time codes can be captured in transit by an actor already inside the carrier, turning phone numbers into a weak root of trust for banking and government accounts.

There is no CVE or end-user patch; operators must treat SS7 and Diameter nodes as high-value assets, deploy signaling firewalls and filtering on UpdateLocation, SRI-SM, SendAuthenticationInfo, AnyTimeInterrogation, and AIR traffic, and audit Global Title and node integrity. Enterprises and individuals should move sensitive accounts off SMS OTP to app-based authenticators or hardware keys and watch for account-recovery anomalies. Hunt for silent SMS, unexpected location updates, duplicate node responses, and signaling from Asian operator nodes outside normal routing; Enea has alerted operators hosting SilentAir sources.

Enea says both SilentAir and Liminal Panda have been set back for now but the underlying conditions remain, so watch for renewed waves and for any confirmed link between SilentAir and Liminal Panda, which Enea could not establish. Also watch whether operators publish remediation or whether the actor shifts from SS7 to Diameter and 5G as defenses tighten.
