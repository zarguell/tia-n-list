SilentAir is a previously unknown state-level actor disclosed by Enea that runs SS7 and Diameter signaling attacks from hijacked network nodes inside mobile operators in at least six Asian countries, with Taiwan as the primary target. Enea assessed with high confidence that SilentAir is a new state-level actor and with low confidence that its activity aligns with China-nexus operations, based on the Taiwan focus, the absence of Chinese targets, and attack timing consistent with a UTC+8 working day. The group has been active since before 2025, with Enea monitoring it since early 2025, and has affected a dozen other countries and territories, occasionally targeting people in East Africa and Central Asia. Attacks route a target's incoming calls and SMS through the compromised node, harvest subscriber details, and support location tracking, with waves ranging from a handful to several thousand subscribers per month. Silent SMS messages that do not appear on the recipient's phone are used to check whether earlier interception attempts succeeded, and 2FA codes were intercepted for some targets.

Enea found attack sources in at least six Asian countries and noted overlap between SilentAir activity and CrowdStrike's LIMINAL PANDA at some of the same operators, though it could not confirm a link. CrowdStrike had publicly disclosed LIMINAL PANDA, a China-nexus actor targeting telecom providers since at least 2020, ahead of Adam Meyers' November 2024 Senate testimony; that group uses compromised telecom servers to reach further providers, emulates GSM protocols for command and control, and retrieves subscriber information, call metadata, and SMS. Infosecurity Magazine's coverage recorded the 2FA interception and silent SMS activity directed largely at Taiwanese users around midnight.

Defenders at mobile operators should treat signaling-layer access from internal nodes as a priority detection gap, since the interception happens inside operator infrastructure rather than at the endpoint. The public reporting does not include a named patch or CVE; the response is operational, focused on node hardening, signaling firewalling, and subscriber anomaly monitoring.
