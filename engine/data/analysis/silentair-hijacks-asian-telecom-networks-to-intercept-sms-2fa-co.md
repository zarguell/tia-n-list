Enea describes SilentAir as a previously unknown state-level actor that runs signaling attacks from network nodes it has hijacked inside mobile operators in at least six Asian countries. The traffic is mostly SS7 with some Diameter. By routing a target's incoming calls and SMS through a compromised node, the actor can harvest subscriber details, intercept messages and, in a smaller share of cases, track location. Enea says some targets had SMS two-factor codes intercepted, and links silent SMS messages that do not appear on the handset to checks on whether earlier interception worked. Taiwan is the primary target, with a dozen other countries and territories affected and occasional activity in East Africa and Europe.

Enea assesses high confidence that this is a new state-level actor and low confidence that it aligns with China-nexus operations, citing the Taiwan focus, the absence of Chinese targets and attack timing consistent with a Chinese workday. The activity has been monitored since early 2025, with signs it existed earlier, and arrives in waves ranging from a handful to several thousand subscribers in a month. CrowdStrike's LIMINAL PANDA overlap at some of the same operators is noted, but Enea could not confirm a link between the two.

The Hacker News reshare reduces the finding to interception of SMS 2FA codes and spying on Taiwanese users, which is consistent with Enea's reporting but drops the attribution caveats. The significance is the vantage point: the attacker operates from inside operator infrastructure rather than from a handset or a public SS7 gateway, which makes ordinary device hardening irrelevant. Watch for operator-level signaling audits, any expansion beyond Asian carriers, and whether Enea or another vendor confirms the LIMINAL PANDA connection. The low-confidence attribution should not be repeated as established fact.
