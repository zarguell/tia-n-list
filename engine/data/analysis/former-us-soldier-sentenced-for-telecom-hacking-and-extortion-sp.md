Cameron John Wagenius, the 22-year-old former Army soldier who ran the persona kiberphant0m, was sentenced September 25 to 70 months in federal prison and ordered to pay $294,978 in restitution. Working with alleged co-conspirators including Connor Moucka, he stole call and text metadata for more than 100 million AT&T customers in 2024 by downloading data from Snowflake customer environments that had exposed credentials and no MFA enforcement, a pattern that also hit other large cloud data holders. He bragged about hacking over a dozen telecom firms including Verizon's Push-to-Talk business and extorted companies publicly for silence.

Court documents describe activity from April 2023 to December 2024, while Wagenius was on active duty. The group used the SSH Brute tool he helped develop to harvest credentials, coordinated on Telegram, and extorted at least 10 organizations for a combined target above $1 million, including repeated attempts to squeeze $500,000 from AT&T. Wagenius leaked the call detail records of a government official and family members of another former official, and per DOJ attempted to sell stolen sensitive data to a foreign intelligence service while researching defection to Russia. He pleaded guilty in February and July 2025 after his December 2024 arrest in Texas.

The sentence closes the operational chapter of the Snowflake spree but confirms its scale: insider-enabled tooling, cloud environments without MFA, and telecom metadata as an extortion product. Watch for cases against the remaining co-conspirators, civil or regulatory fallout for the victims that lagged on MFA, and whether the intelligence-selling allegation gets elaborated in future filings.
