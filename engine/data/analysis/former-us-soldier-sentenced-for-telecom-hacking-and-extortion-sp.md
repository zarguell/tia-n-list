Cameron John Wagenius, the former US Army soldier who operated as Kiberphant0m, was sentenced on September 25, 2026 to 70 months in federal prison and ordered to pay $294,978 in restitution. He pleaded guilty in July 2025 to conspiracy to commit wire fraud, extortion in relation to computer fraud, and aggravated identity theft. Court documents cover activity from April 2023 to December 18, 2024, much of it while he was on active duty, defrauding at least 10 organizations using an SSH brute-forcing utility and moving credentials through Telegram.

Kiberphant0m sold data stolen from Snowflake customers whose credentials were exposed and without MFA, a theft wave that hit more than 160 Snowflake customers. AT&T's SEC filing and July 2025 confirmation put call and text metadata for roughly 110 million customers in the taken set, records covering May 1 to October 31, 2022 and January 2, 2023, pulled from an AT&T workspace on a third-party Snowflake platform protected by username and password only. He publicly extorted AT&T over the records of Donald Trump and Kamala Harris, advertised Verizon Push-to-Talk call logs, and claimed intrusions into at least 15 telecommunications firms. Prosecutors also said he attempted to sell stolen sensitive data to a foreign intelligence service and researched defecting to Russia before his December 20, 2024 arrest near Fort Hood.

The case closes the loop on the Snowflake campaign alongside co-conspirator Connor Riley Moucka's October 2024 arrest, and it hardened industry posture: Snowflake has since mandated MFA on all accounts. Sentencing of an active-duty soldier for extorting his own country's largest carriers, with attempted foreign-intelligence sales, also marks an escalation in consequences for the infostealer-to-extortion pipeline.
