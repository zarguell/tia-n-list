Cameron John Wagenius, the former US Army communications specialist who operated as kiberphant0m, was sentenced to 70 months in federal prison and ordered to pay nearly $300,000 in restitution, per DOJ, KrebsOnSecurity, and CyberScoop reporting on the September 25 sentencing. Court documents put the conduct from April 2023 through December 18, 2024, largely while he was on active duty, first at a base in South Korea and earlier in Texas: he and co-conspirators hit at least 10 victim organizations, harvested credentials with a tool called SSH Brute, moved them over Telegram, and extorted victims privately and on BreachForums and XSS.is, attempting to extort at least $1 million. The stolen data included call and text metadata for roughly 110 million AT&T customers, taken in April 2024 from a Snowflake-hosted AT&T workspace protected by username and password only, plus Verizon push-to-talk records. He pleaded guilty in both federal cases; before his December 2024 arrest he also tried to sell stolen data to a foreign intelligence service and researched defecting to Russia.

The case matters beyond its headline because it closes the loop on the Snowflake campaign wave. AT&T's SEC disclosure showed the exposure pattern applied across more than 160 Snowflake customers, and the DOJ case confirms the monetization path: metadata covering 2022 and 2023 traffic with cell-tower identifiers feeds SIM-swapping, which converts old call records into account takeovers years later. The payoff was small relative to the damage, roughly $1,500 in data sales against one confirmed $370,000 bitcoin payment from AT&T in 2024, and the 70-month term reflects early guilty pleas and extensive cooperation rather than the statutory maximums of 20 years plus a mandatory consecutive two for aggravated identity theft. The insider angle is not incidental: a serving soldier ran the operation from his duty station, and Unit 221B's attribution work unmasked him within weeks.

Enforce MFA on every data warehouse, BI, and SaaS tenant and hunt the exceptions, since Snowflake has since mandated MFA on all accounts but legacy workspaces with password-only authentication are where this class of breach lives. Assume 2022 and 2023 era telecom metadata including approximate tower location is already in circulation: drop SMS one-time codes for high-value accounts, require authenticator apps or hardware keys, and set carrier port-out PINs. Watch BreachForums and XSS.is listings for your own domains, and treat cleared or vetted personnel active on Telegram crime channels as reportable telemetry.

Two developments would change the assessment. Any filing naming the foreign intelligence service Wagenius tried to sell data to, or validating the claimed Trump and Harris call logs and the posted NSA data schema, converts a criminal case into a counterintelligence one. Continued charges against co-conspirators, including Connor Moucka's circle or the buyers of the stolen call records, would widen the case's reach, and cooperation-driven fallout from his sentencing memo, which documented him researching exploit code for CVE-2023-45208 and Windows privilege escalation CVEs from custody, is worth monitoring.
