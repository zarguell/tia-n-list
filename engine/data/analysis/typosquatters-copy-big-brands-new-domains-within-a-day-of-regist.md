The campaign tracked by Security.com shows attackers registering one-letter lookalike domains for major brands within a single working day of each authentic domain appearing. The timing points to monitoring of the specialist brand-protection registrars that large companies use to defensively register their names, letting the actors copy a namespace almost as fast as the brand itself claims it. The reporting notes that every lookalike was registered the next working day after its legitimate counterpart, and describes it as an ongoing campaign.

The lookalikes are typo-squat infrastructure aimed at the traffic and trust that flows to newly launched brand domains. Because each fake is registered the next working day, defenders relying on slow takedown cycles will always trail the campaign. Watch for which registrars leak the signal, whether the lookalikes move on to credential phishing or malware delivery, and whether the brands' registrar accounts show signs of compromise rather than mere observation.
