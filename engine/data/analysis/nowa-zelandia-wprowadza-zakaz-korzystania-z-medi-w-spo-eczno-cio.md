New Zealand has introduced a bill to ban children under 16 from using social media platforms and AI companionship services. The Online Safety Bill targets platform features deemed harmful to minors, including recommendation systems, infinite feeds, interaction mechanisms like likes and comments, and time-limited features. AI companion services are explicitly included in the restrictions.

The bill explicitly excludes messaging apps, professional social networks, gaming and music platforms, and educational and health tools. Notably, the legislation rejects self-declared age or official identity documents as sufficient age verification. Platforms would be required to use alternative methods such as facial scanning or activity pattern analysis to verify user age.

The bill also requires platforms to conduct child safety risk assessments for all users under 18 and protect them from harmful content including harassment, violence, and sexual material. This follows a global trend of governments imposing age-based restrictions on social media and AI services, with Australia and several US states having passed similar legislation. For security teams at affected platforms, the biometric age verification requirement introduces new data handling and privacy compliance challenges.
