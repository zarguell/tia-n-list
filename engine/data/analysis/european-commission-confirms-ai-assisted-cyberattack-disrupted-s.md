The European Commission has confirmed an intrusion detected on 22 October and confirmed on 26 October that disrupted multiple internal services. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier said several applications were taken down as a protective measure and most have since been restored. Politico reported disruption lasting more than 10 days, including the HR platform Sysper, the medical reimbursement system MyPMO and translation systems. The Commission says its investigation has found no evidence that staff data was stolen.

Regnier confirmed that the attackers used an AI tool but did not name it, attribute the intrusion to a group, or confirm a ransom demand. The disclosure came about two weeks after CERT-EU warned that threat actors were using the AI-powered penetration-testing framework Artex, though no direct link between that framework and this incident has been established.

Politico later reported union pressure for answers to staff about what was accessed and when. The open questions are the identity of the actor, the role of the AI tool in reconnaissance or exploitation, and the scope of data access on the affected systems. Watch for a Commission post-incident review or an update from CERT-EU that names the tool and confirms whether the downtime was caused by the intrusion itself or by the containment steps.
