The European Commission confirmed on November 19 that it was hit by an AI-assisted cyberattack detected on October 22 and confirmed on October 26. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier said several applications were taken down as a protective measure and most have been restored, but the disruption to HR platform Sysper, medical reimbursement system MyPMO, translation services and payroll and leave functions lasted more than ten days. At the time of that briefing, investigators had found no evidence of a staff data breach and were aware of no ransom demand, and the Commission did not name the attackers. Politico identified the tool as ARTEX, an open-source AI penetration-testing framework.

CERT-EU had warned on November 5, fourteen days before the public confirmation, that it had seen similar AI-driven activity in its constituency and assessed ARTEX as highly likely. The FBI's FLASH-20261113-001 maps 43 ATT&CK techniques for ARTEX, including checks of Azure SAS tokens, service principals, Kudu credentials and storage account keys, plus more than 1,600 exposed ARTEX consoles. Microsoft attributed wider ARTEX-driven intrusions to Storm-0940, a China-state aligned actor active since 2021, with some attacks reaching privileged cloud access in under three hours. That attribution covers the broader ARTEX campaign, not a Commission statement about this incident.

A later Politico report carried in the events says the Commission confirmed a personal data leak after the attack and warned of further disruption, which goes beyond the earlier no-evidence statement. What to watch is which fields were exposed, whether the HR systems holding names, pay grades, home addresses and contact details were involved, and whether the warning of additional disruption means more services will be taken offline. Notifications to affected individuals and any updated CERT-EU or European Data Protection Supervisor findings should settle the scope.
