The European Commission detected an AI-assisted cyber campaign on 22 October 2026 and confirmed it on 26 October. Applications taken offline as a protective measure included the HR platform Sysper, the medical reimbursement system MyPMO and translation services, with disruption lasting more than ten days. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier said most services were restored by 19 November, while the JSIS health portal was still unavailable that day. Politico, Euronews and The Register identified the tool as ARTEX, an open-source AI penetration-testing framework. CERT-EU had warned on 5 November that activity against its constituency was highly likely to involve ARTEX.

The disclosure changed on 23 November, when the Commission confirmed that the EU Academy online learning platform had also been breached. Names and email addresses of external users were taken, the platform was taken offline from 11 November with a planned closure until 14 January, and the European Data Protection Supervisor was notified. Users were told through a website message to watch for spam, identity theft and targeted phishing rather than being contacted individually. The Commission had earlier said it had no evidence of a breach affecting Commission staff and had received no ransom demand.

The wider ARTEX context is substantive but not an attribution of this incident. CERT-EU said the framework turns natural-language objectives into tasks for agent workers using real tooling, with no hard technical enforcement of scope. The FBI alert FLASH-20261113-001 mapped 43 ATT&CK techniques, and reporting cited more than 1,600 exposed ARTEX consoles and cases where attackers reached privileged access in under three hours. Microsoft separately tied ARTEX-driven intrusions to Storm-0940. The Commission has not named an actor. Watch for formal attribution, a completed account of how the attackers entered, and any expansion of the confirmed EU Academy data loss.
