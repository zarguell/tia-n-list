The European Commission confirmed on November 19 that an attack detected on October 22 and confirmed on October 26 used AI tools and disrupted several internal services for more than 10 days. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier said applications were taken down as a protective measure and most had been restored. Systems named in reporting include the HR platform Sysper, the medical reimbursement system MyPMO, and translation services. Politico reported blocked access to salary and leave information and possible large-scale data compromise. Regnier said investigators found no evidence of a broad staff data breach, no ransom demand was known, and the investigation remains open.

CERT-EU had warned on November 5 about attackers using ARTEX, an open-source AI penetration-testing framework, and the Commission identified ARTEX use in the incident. An FBI FLASH alert mapped 43 ATT&CK techniques and more than 1,600 exposed ARTEX consoles, with privileged access reached in under three hours in some cases. Analysis of that alert describes automated checks of Azure SAS tokens, service principals, Kudu credentials, storage keys, admin passwords in JavaScript bundles, forged tokens using unrotated keys, and DCSync after enumeration of more than 110,000 Active Directory objects. Microsoft attributed separate ARTEX-driven intrusions to Storm-0940, a China-state aligned actor active since 2021.

The Commission said it notified affected individuals and is checking other systems with CERT-EU and the European Data Protection Supervisor. Watch for exposed management panels, secrets in front-end bundles and pipelines, unrotated signing keys, changes to privileged role assignments, and sudden API traffic spikes, all of which the ARTEX advisories call out as entry points or speed multipliers.
