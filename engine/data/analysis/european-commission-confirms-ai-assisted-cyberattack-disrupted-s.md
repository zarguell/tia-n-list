The European Commission detected the intrusion on 22 October 2026 and confirmed it on 26 October. Officials described an AI-assisted campaign that forced several applications offline as a precaution. Staff-facing services disrupted for more than 10 days included the Sysper HR platform, MyPMO medical reimbursement system, translation services and the Joint Sickness Insurance Scheme portal. Sysper returned and then failed again on 18 November; translation services were hit on 17 November. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier said no ransom demand had been received and no group had been named publicly.

Reporting tied the activity to ARTEX, an open-source LLM-driven penetration-testing framework that CERT-EU flagged on 5 November for automating scans and exploitation against EU institutions. The FBI alert FLASH-20261113-001, cited in the coverage, mapped 43 ATT&CK techniques, more than 1,600 internet-exposed ARTEX consoles, sub-second checks of Azure SAS tokens and service principal credentials, and cases where attackers reached privileged access in under three hours. Security Affairs stressed that ARTEX was a suspected tool in the Commission incident rather than a confirmed entry method. Microsoft separately attributed other ARTEX-driven intrusions to Storm-0940, a China-aligned actor active since 2021, without tying that attribution to this breach.

On 23 November the Commission confirmed that personal data had been stolen from EU Academy, an online learning platform for external users. The taken fields were names and email addresses; the exact scale was still under investigation. EU Academy had been offline since 11 November and was expected to stay closed until 14 January. The European Data Protection Supervisor was notified, and users received a general website warning about spam, identity theft and targeted phishing rather than individual notices. Watch for confirmation of the initial access path, the full EU Academy record count, and any attribution that links a named ARTEX operator to this incident.
