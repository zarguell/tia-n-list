The European Commission confirmed on November 19 that an AI-assisted attack detected on October 22 and confirmed on October 26 disrupted internal services for more than 10 days. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier said several applications were taken offline as a protective measure and most had been restored by November 19. Reporting names the affected systems as the HR platform Sysper, the medical reimbursement system MyPMO and translation services, covering payroll, leave requests and reimbursements. Regnier said investigators found no evidence of a broad staff data breach, knew of no ransom demand and did not name the tool or attribute the campaign to an actor. Politico reported that the Commission's Corporate Management Board discussed the crisis, and a staff union has since demanded answers.

The tool question is partly answered outside the Commission. Politico, Euronews and CERT-EU sources point to ARTEX, an open-source AI penetration-testing framework. CERT-EU had warned on November 5 that actors were using ARTEX to automate campaigns, mapping the activity to T1595 active scanning, T1588.002 tool acquisition and T1190 exploitation of public-facing applications, with advice to isolate management panels, sanitise error responses and keep low-severity findings in the remediation cycle. The Register cited an FBI alert mentioning more than 1,600 exposed ARTEX consoles and cases where attackers reached privileged access in under three hours. Microsoft attributed the wider ARTEX campaign, including a ConnectWise supply chain compromise, to the China-aligned actor Storm-0940, but that attribution has not been tied to the Commission incident.

FBI FLASH-20261113-001 gives the concrete hunt. It maps 43 techniques and includes sub-second checks of Azure SAS tokens, service principals, Kudu credentials and storage keys, administrator passwords found in JavaScript bundles, forged administrator tokens using unrotated keys, and DCSync against KRBTGT after enumeration of more than 110,000 Active Directory objects. Those are secrets and identities to rotate or restrict, not a CVE to patch. Euronews noted that HR systems hold names, pay grades, home addresses and contact details while internal documents sit on a separate system, which bounds but does not erase the exposure. Associated Press and Reuters reporting on November 20 corroborates the October intrusion and the AI attribution without adding an actor.

The assessment changes on confirmed data access to Sysper, MyPMO or the translation systems, or on an on-record naming of ARTEX by the Commission. There is also a timeline conflict to settle: SecurityWeek reports detection on October 22 and confirmation on October 26, while Euronews quotes Regnier saying both happened on October 22. Watch for a CERT-EU or Commission post-incident review, and for further reporting on whether any of the more than 1,600 exposed ARTEX consoles were used in this intrusion.
