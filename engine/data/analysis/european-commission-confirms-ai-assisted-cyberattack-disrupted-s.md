The European Commission confirmed on November 19 that it was hit by an AI-assisted cyberattack detected on October 22 and confirmed on October 26. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier said several applications were taken down as a protective measure and most have since been restored. Politico reported disruption lasting more than 10 days across the HR platform Sysper, the medical reimbursement system MyPMO and translation services; the Joint Sickness Insurance Scheme was also reported unavailable, and Sysper failed again briefly after coming back. Regnier said investigators initially found no evidence of a staff data breach, no known attacker attribution and no ransom demand, though a later Politico report said the Commission confirmed a personal data leak and warned of further disruption.

The tool at the center of the reporting is ARTEX, an open-source AI penetration-testing framework. CERT-EU had warned on November 5 that threat actors were using ARTEX against organizations in its constituency, mapping the activity to active scanning, tool acquisition and exploitation of public-facing applications. The FBI's FLASH-20261113-001 describes 43 ATT&CK techniques and more than 1,600 exposed ARTEX consoles, with sub-second checks of Azure SAS tokens, service principals, Kudu credentials and storage keys, forged administrator tokens using unrotated keys, and DCSync after enumerating more than 110,000 Active Directory objects. Some intrusions reached privileged cloud access in under three hours. Microsoft attributed a separate set of ARTEX-driven intrusions to Storm-0940, a China-aligned actor active since 2021, including a ConnectWise supply chain compromise that used applications with existing tenant consent rather than a product vulnerability.

What to watch is attribution for the Commission incident and the real scope of the data exposure, since the early no-breach finding and the later leak report do not sit together. The broader lesson is operational: CERT-EU recommends limiting internet exposure, isolating management panels, deploying WAF and MFA, and keeping even low-severity findings in remediation because autonomous agents chain them.
