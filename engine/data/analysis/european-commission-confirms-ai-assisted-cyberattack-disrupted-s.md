The European Commission confirmed an AI-assisted cyberattack after disruptions that lasted more than 10 days. The activity was detected on 22 October 2026 and confirmed on 26 October. Applications taken down as a protective measure included the HR platform Sysper, the medical reimbursement system MyPMO and translation services. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier said most applications were restored by 19 November, and the Commission initially said it had found no evidence of a staff data breach.

The tool was publicly available AI software. Politico, Euronews and The Register identified it as ARTEX, and CERT-EU had warned on 5 November about AI-driven activity against its constituency that was assessed as highly likely involving ARTEX. Regnier did not name the tool at the press conference, did not attribute the incident to a known group and said he was unaware of a ransom demand. Politico reported that staff were blocked from payroll and leave information, and that Sysper briefly failed again after being restored.

On 23 November the disclosure changed: Politico and GBHackers reported a personal data leak from the EU Academy online learning platform, with names and email addresses of users taken. That is the first confirmed data exposure in this story, distinct from the earlier statement about Commission staff systems. Recorded Future News reported that affected individuals were notified and other systems were being checked with CERT-EU and the European Data Protection Supervisor.

What to watch is the completion of the investigation, whether further EU systems are found to be affected, and whether the ARTEX attribution is formally confirmed. The incident matters because a public AI attack framework was used against a major government institution, and because the data-loss finding came weeks after the initial outage was described as contained.
