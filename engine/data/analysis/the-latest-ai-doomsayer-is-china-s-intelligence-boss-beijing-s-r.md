China's Minister of State Security Chen Yixin published an article in China Cyberspace Magazine arguing that artificial intelligence has become "the main battleground for global technological competition and a new arena for strategic rivalry among major powers." Chen warned that AI could be weaponized to detect and exploit software vulnerabilities, attack critical infrastructure, and steal industrial and state secrets. He also expressed concern about remote control capabilities and data leakage in foreign AI products like OpenClaw.

Chen called for China to "ensure the independent control of key core technologies, firmly grasp technological sovereignty, and achieve a virtuous cycle between innovation empowerment and security governance." He advocated for special laws targeting AI research, development, application, and supervision, covering algorithm security, data protection, ethical norms, and privacy rights. The article appeared alongside version 3.0 of the Cyberspace Administration of China's AI Safety Governance Framework, which calls for regulatory sandboxes and "risk-controllable institutional mechanisms."

The stance contrasts sharply with US President Donald Trump's position, who on the same day called concerns about AI safety a "hoax" and said "The only control or 'guardrails' that AI needs is a STRONG AND SMART PRESIDENT." Chen's emphasis on technological sovereignty and control over key core technologies suggests China will continue restricting access to advanced AI chips from companies like Nvidia and AMD, while tightening regulations on both domestic and foreign AI deployments.
