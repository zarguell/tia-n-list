Unit 42's XG Files research describes Falcon C2, a browser-based command-and-control framework that compromises victims through their browsers rather than through a traditional endpoint implant. Falcon runs malicious JavaScript in the victim's browser, and the actor behind it used the framework to compromise visitors by combining malicious scripts, malicious browser extensions and the Antino implant.

The design choice is the point. A browser-resident C2 inherits the user's authenticated sessions, cookies and internal web access, so it can ride an already-trusted session into corporate applications without needing to escalate on the host. Because the payload is script and an extension, the same kit can be dropped onto government and enterprise visitors through a compromised or malicious page, and it leaves little on disk for endpoint tooling that only watches for binaries.

What to watch: the initial access vector that put Falcon in front of government targets, since that determines whether the answer is browser hardening or web filtering; whether Antino is being reused by other actors or is exclusive to this crew; and whether browsers and extensions vendors ship detection for the script patterns, which would be the fastest way to blunt a framework that never touches the endpoint's process table.
