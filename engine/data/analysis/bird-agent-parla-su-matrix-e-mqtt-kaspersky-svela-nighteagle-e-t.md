Kaspersky has identified two new threat clusters targeting Russian companies, dubbed NightEagle and Toy Ghouls, both using Bird Agent malware that communicates over Matrix messaging protocol and MQTT IoT messaging. The use of Matrix as a C2 channel is notable because it provides encrypted, decentralized communication that blends with legitimate enterprise messaging traffic. MQTT, typically used for IoT device communication, offers another layer of protocol diversity that complicates network detection.

The Bird Agent malware itself is designed for long-term persistence and data collection. Kaspersky's analysis of the two clusters suggests they are distinct operations rather than variations of a single campaign, despite sharing the same malware family. This could indicate a common malware supplier or developer serving multiple threat actors. The targeting of Russian companies aligns with the broader pattern of cyber operations against Russian organizations, though attribution to specific nation-state actors remains preliminary.

For defenders, the use of Matrix and MQTT as C2 channels means that traditional network monitoring focused on HTTP/HTTPS and DNS may miss this traffic. Organizations should audit legitimate Matrix and MQTT usage in their environments, establish baselines for expected traffic patterns, and consider deploying protocol-specific detection rules. The convergence of messaging platforms and IoT protocols as attack infrastructure is a trend worth monitoring across all regions.
