The September 22 coverage of UTA0565 is the third wave of a disclosure that began a week earlier. Volexity reported the chain on September 9 and named two users, UTA0560, which hit US NGOs with donation lures and deployed the GRIMWEDGE JScript backdoor, and JungleBamboo (APT31, Violet Typhoon), which installed SUPERSTOMP and the LONGTALE extension disguised as Google Gemini; Proofpoint branded the shared kit BlueMoon the same day and counted four espionage users before mid-September, TA412 from August 28, UNK_LateNight and UNK_DoubleCheck from September 2, and UNK_QuietRacket from September 3. Mechanically the kit chains CVE-2026-85046, a V8 type confusion in TurboFan and Maglev element-kind handling of Array.prototype.sort giving read-write inside the V8 cage, CVE-2026-87491, a WebAssembly sandbox escape, and CVE-2026-85880, an ALPC and WNF based kernel escalation in RtlpCreateServerAcl used to inject into the Chrome browser process. UTA0565 ran that core on September 3 and 4, 2026 while stable Chrome still shipped it unpatched, swapping the default cmd.exe and curl.exe delivery for a COM launcher that pulls chrome_cleanup.exe and installs CLEANGULP.

The delivery is what separates this wave from routine exploit reporting. The UTA0565 clones loaded their visible content from the genuine sites, so a visitor to americanprgoress[.]top saw a working Center for American Progress page while the exploit ran in a hidden zero-size iframe to /config.html; nothing on screen looks wrong and no link invites suspicion, so user-awareness training contributes nothing and detection falls entirely to network and endpoint telemetry. Exposure is narrower than the zero-day headline too: the kernel stage refuses every Windows build above 22000, so the full renderer-to-kernel chain lands only on Windows 10 1809 through 22H2, Server 2022, and Windows 11 21H2, while current builds cap an attacker at browser-process execution. The larger argument is supply chain. Six distinct clusters now appear across the two firms, with Volexity verifying byte-identical p1 and p2 components among its three, and Volexity assesses the developer likely reverse-engineered public Chromium commits, which makes every future Chromium patch diff a target during the commit-to-release gap.

Google shipped the V8 fix in stable 152.0.7977.82/.83 on September 3, 2026 and Microsoft remediated the CVE-2026-85880 root cause, so current cumulative updates plus current builds of every Chromium-based browser, including Edge, Brave, and Vivaldi, close the chain. Hunt for CLEANGULP at %LOCALAPPDATA%\Microsoft\IME\MicrosoftIME.exe with its scheduled task named MicrosoftIME, for chrome_cleanup.exe with SHA-256 8858ea412dc306b3558885af18006c5ca24689e8875733b5e13b3c2692e603cb, for the UTA0560 task "Windows Scheduled System" re-running every five minutes, and for the default BlueMoon process tree of chrome.exe spawning cmd.exe then curl.exe; the LONGTALE extension carries ID ckiknalbeplpcpofpnabcnhjcegckfei and installs through forged Secure Preferences HMACs. On the network side, block the typosquat set thecovnresation[.]com and thecovnresation[.]net, americanprgoress[.]top, chinadigitaltimes[.]top, borneobulletins[.]top, halal-navi[.]net, halaltak[.]net, outsourcingwise[.]net, and personclouds[.]com, alert on POSTs to /beacon/pre-register over plain HTTP, and deploy Proofpoint's ET rules 2071919 through 2071924 and 2071996 through 2072001 together with the MAL_BlueMoon_ExploitKit YARA rule.

Two developments would change this assessment. BlueMoon spreading past espionage crews, which Proofpoint assesses as likely once patched browsers close the zero-day window, would turn a targeted-espionage story into commodity risk. A repeat of the patch-gap pattern matters even more: the CVE-2026-85046 fix sat in public Chromium source from August 7 until the September 3 stable release, four weeks during which the diff was readable and the patch absent, and Google's own advisory confirmed exploitation in the wild. Watch for Google narrowing that commit-to-release interval, and for any new Chromium fix showing the same window with in-the-wild use.
