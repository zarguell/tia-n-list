The Qilin ransomware group claimed a breach of Touring Club Suisse, Switzerland's largest automobile club with over 1.5 million members. The claim appeared on Qilin's leak site on September 20, 2026, marked as unverified with the data volume listed as "Undisclosed." As of this writing, the claim has not been independently verified and Touring Club Suisse has not issued a public statement.

Separately, Qilin was also linked to a breach claim against Thorndale Foundation, an Australian disability support organization. The dual claims in the same timeframe suggest Qilin is maintaining its usual tempo of victim postings. Qilin is a ransomware-as-a-service operation that has been active since 2019 and is responsible for attacks on multiple sectors including healthcare, education, and critical infrastructure.

The Touring Club Suisse claim is notable because of the organization's size and membership base in a country with high per-capita digital penetration. If confirmed, the breach could expose personal data of over a million Swiss motorists including addresses, vehicle registrations, and financial information. The lack of technical details or sample data publication suggests the claim is either very early stage or being held as leverage for extortion.
