A 3AM ransomware affiliate hit a UK school estate, encrypting roughly 37 of nearly 50 machines reached from an unmanaged host. Bitdefender researchers believe the operator may have driven parts of the intrusion with AI-generated commands and an automated agentic loop, based on repeated commands slightly modified each attempt and issued at roughly 40 to 100 second intervals. The activity ran from September 10 to 19, though the initial access method was not established.

The intrusion followed a standard ransomware playbook: a modified public PowerShell tool extracted and decrypted credentials from three Veeam backup servers, a Chrome credential database was copied, and Mimikatz plus LSASS memory dumping were used. The actor mapped domain relationships, searched logon-script folders for references to other schools, and created two backdoor domain administrator accounts named "library" and "svc_bkpmon" before stealing financial data. Bitdefender stresses it could not identify the model or confirm whether a human relayed prompts, so the AI involvement is behavioral inference rather than confirmed automation.
