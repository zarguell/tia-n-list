A 3AM ransomware affiliate hit a school estate in the UK and encrypted roughly 37 of nearly 50 machines reached from an unmanaged host, in an intrusion Bitdefender researchers believe may have been partly driven by an AI agentic loop. The hands-on phase ran September 10-19, 2026, and the actor used a modified public PowerShell tool to extract and decrypt credentials from three Veeam backup servers, copied a Chrome credential database, and deployed Mimikatz and LSASS memory-dumping tools. They mapped domain relationships, searched logon-script folders for references to other schools, and created two backdoor domain administrator accounts named library and svc_bkpmon before encryption.

The AI claim is the interesting part and it is deliberately hedged. Bitdefender's evidence is a sequence of repeated commands, slightly modified at each attempt and issued at roughly regular intervals of 40 to 100 seconds, which looks like an agentic loop but could also be a human copying and pasting between a terminal and a model. The researchers did not have access to the attacker's working machine, so they cannot say which model, whether it ran locally or remotely, or whether a person was in the loop. That uncertainty is the honest finding, and it is arguably more useful than a headline, because it describes a cheap hybrid attack that follows the standard ransomware playbook and is easily reproducible with a local model.

Defenders in education and other flat-network estates should treat unmanaged hosts as the likely entry point, since that is where this intrusion started, and monitor for PowerShell credential-extraction against Veeam servers, LSASS dumping, and new domain admin accounts with service-like names. Watch for Bitdefender or others publishing the deobfuscated command sequence, which would let teams build detections for the behavioral signature regardless of whether a human or a model generated it, and for any disclosure of the initial access method, which remains unknown.
