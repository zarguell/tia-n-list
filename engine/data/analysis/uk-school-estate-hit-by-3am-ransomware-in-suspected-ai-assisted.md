A 3AM ransomware affiliate intruded a UK school estate and encrypted roughly 37 of nearly 50 machines reached from an unmanaged host, with malicious activity spanning September 10 to 19, 2026. Bitdefender researchers believe the hands-on phase may have been AI-assisted: they observed a sequence of slightly modified commands issued at roughly regular 40 to 100 second intervals, consistent with an agentic loop. Bitdefender could not establish the initial access method or identify the language model, and could not rule out a human copying commands between an implant and a model.

The attacker used a modified public PowerShell tool to extract and decrypt credentials from three Veeam backup servers, copied a Chrome database containing credentials, and deployed Mimikatz and LSASS memory-dumping tools. They mapped domain relationships, searched logon-script folders for references to other schools, and created two backdoor domain administrator accounts named 'library' and 'svc_bkpmon' before encrypting systems and stealing financial data.

The case is notable as a likely hybrid human-plus-AI ransomware operation that did not require expensive infrastructure, and Bitdefender expects more such attacks in 2027. Defenders should monitor for repeated, slightly varied command sequences at regular intervals, protect backup servers, and hunt for unauthorized domain admin accounts.
