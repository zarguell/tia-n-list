A 3AM ransomware affiliate encrypted roughly 37 of nearly 50 machines reached from an unmanaged host in a UK school estate between September 10 and 19. Bitdefender researchers believe the operator may have used AI-generated commands in an automated agentic loop, based on repeated commands that were slightly modified at each attempt and issued at roughly 40 to 100 second intervals, though they could not confirm which model or whether a human was relaying prompts.

The intruder extracted and decrypted credentials from three Veeam backup servers using a modified public PowerShell tool, copied a Chrome credential database, deployed Mimikatz and LSASS memory-dumping tools, mapped domain relationships, searched logon-script folders for references to other schools, and created two backdoor domain administrator accounts named "library" and "svc_bkpmon" before stealing financial data. Bitdefender could not establish the initial access method.

Schools and trusts running similar estates should hunt for the named backdoor accounts, unmanaged hosts, and credential-dumping artifacts, and review Veeam and Chrome credential stores. Watch for any attribution of the initial access vector and for further 3AM activity against education sector targets.
