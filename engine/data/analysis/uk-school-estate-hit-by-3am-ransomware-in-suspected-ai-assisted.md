A 3AM (ThreeAM) ransomware affiliate breached a UK school estate and encrypted roughly 37 of nearly 50 reachable systems between September 10 and 19, operating from an unmanaged host that Bitdefender could not directly inspect. The attacker used a modified public PowerShell tool to extract and decrypt credentials from three Veeam backup servers, copied a Chrome credential database, and deployed Mimikatz along with LSASS memory-dumping tools. Bitdefender observed slightly modified commands retried at regular 40-to-100-second intervals and described the behavior as resembling an agentic loop, though they explicitly could not confirm which model ran, whether it was local or remote, or whether a human simply copied output back and forth.

What makes this more than another ransomware incident is the hybrid execution model it documents: a human likely set the strategic goals, while repetitive, low-level tasks were handled by something that behaved like an automated agent. The victim profile is a multi-site school estate with 47 systems (about 21 servers, eight domain controllers, ten Windows 10 workstations), meaning a single unmanaged entry point gave the attacker domain-wide reach across educational institutions. Because initial access remains unknown and the attacker mapped logon-script folders specifically to find references to other schools, defenders should treat this as a targeted, cross-site intrusion rather than opportunistic encryption.

Concrete actions: audit for unmanaged hosts that can reach domain controllers; hunt for the two backdoor domain administrator accounts named `library` and `svc_bkpmon`, and for use of a modified PowerShell credential extractor against Veeam servers. Inspect Chrome database access and LSASS dumps in memory, check Veeam backup server authentication logs for anomalous decryption attempts, and restrict logon-script folder access to prevent cross-school lateral mapping. Monitor for retries of slightly modified commands at 40-to-100-second intervals, which Bitdefender identified as the key behavioral signal distinguishing this agentic loop from purely manual keyboard activity.

The assessment would shift significantly if Bitdefender releases the actual language model name or confirms remote infrastructure, or if similar 40-to-100-second retry patterns appear on other education or public-sector estates using Veeam backups. Watch also for replication of the `library` and `svc_bkpmon` account naming convention elsewhere, since that naming appears specific to this intrusion and could become a reliable indicator of follow-on 3AM affiliate operations.
