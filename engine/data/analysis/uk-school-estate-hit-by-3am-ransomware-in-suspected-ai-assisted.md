Bitdefender researched a 3AM ransomware affiliate intrusion against a UK school estate where about 37 of nearly 50 machines reached from an unmanaged host were encrypted. The attacker used a modified public PowerShell tool to extract and decrypt credentials from three Veeam backup servers, copied a Chrome database with credentials, and deployed Mimikatz and LSASS memory-dumping tools. The threat actor mapped domain relationships and searched logon-script folders for references to other schools before creating two backdoor domain administrator accounts named library and svc_bkpmon. The activity spanned September 10 to 19, and the initial access method could not be established.

Researchers noted a sequence of slightly modified commands issued at regular intervals of roughly 40 to 100 seconds, which they interpreted as possible AI-assisted agentic behavior. Bitdefender explicitly cautions that they could not document which language model was used, whether it ran locally or remotely, or even whether a person was copying instructions between an implant and a model. The behavior looked similar to an agentic loop but was slow rather than machine-speed, which fits a hybrid human-agent model more than a fully autonomous operation. The conclusion is behavioral inference, not confirmed AI attribution.

The incident is significant for its use of standard ransomware sequencing combined with potential automation of command retries. Whether the loop was AI-driven or manually assisted, the outcome is the same: rapid domain compromise, backup server access, and widespread encryption in an educational environment. Schools and similar estates should audit unmanaged hosts, restrict PowerShell execution, segment Veeam backups, and monitor for unexpected domain administrator accounts such as library or svc_bkpmon.
