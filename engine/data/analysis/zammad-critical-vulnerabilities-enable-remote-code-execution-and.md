Zammad, the widely used open-source helpdesk and ticketing platform, shipped fixes for two critical flaws that chain into remote code execution and root-level control of the server: CVE-2026-102489, a session fixation vulnerability, and CVE-2026-102490. The Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure (DIVD) and Merlon Security found them under the worst possible circumstances, while investigating a breach of DIVD's own Zammad instance.

The intrusion stands out for who ran it: Sysdig's writeup describes an agentic AI threat actor exploiting the Zammad zero-days to breach DIVD, a documented case of AI-driven offensive operations rather than human-only tradecraft. CISA added the Zammad flaws to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 2 (advisory CISA-2026:1002), which puts known exploitation on the official record, and secondary trackers describe CVE-2026-102489 as exploited in the wild.

Helpdesk platforms are high-trust pivot points: they hold session state, mail integrations, and often SSO relationships with every organization that files tickets through them, so root on Zammad means credential theft and lateral movement across all of those tenants at once. Organizations running Zammad should patch immediately, invalidate all existing sessions, and review the instance for fixation artifacts. Watch for Sysdig's detection guidance, DIVD's disclosure of what the intruders accessed, and whether further victims surface beyond DIVD now that the flaw pair is KEV-listed.
