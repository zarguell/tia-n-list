Manifold Security discovered 77 counterfeit extensions on the Open VSX marketplace between July 26 and August 1, impersonating namespaces for AMD, Azure, Salesforce, Hyperledger, LEGO Education, IOTA, and a US government agency. All 77 shared C2 infrastructure at mangorbit[.]com (registered July 15), were published from unrelated accounts at version 0.0.1, and replaced the legitimate extension.js with a data-collection stub masquerading as an "active" status indicator. 58 sent minimal system info (hostname); 19 ran deeper reconnaissance 4 to 5 seconds after activation, harvesting OS username, machine ID, workspace paths, .git metadata (remote hosts, developer email domain, branch, HEAD commit), up to 60 installed extensions, and CI/cloud identifiers from GitHub, GitLab, Azure DevOps, Buildkite, CircleCI, Codespaces, and Gitpod.

Packages were removed from Open VSX by August 3 but must be manually uninstalled from developer machines. The update on August 24 marks partial resolution: Open VSX has unblocked three extension IDs after the legitimate projects they impersonated reclaimed their names. This restores publishing access for the affected maintainers but exposes a supply-chain tracking gap where a single extension ID can represent both a removed malicious artifact and a later legitimate release. Between August 16 and the unblocking, legitimate developers were locked out of their own namespaces.

The campaign was not about code theft or credential harvesting in the traditional sense. The 19 deeply-profiled extensions gathered enough organizational intelligence, including private repo URLs, team size indicators, and CI pipeline details, to enable targeted follow-on attacks against specific companies. Developers should audit extension inventories against Manifold's published ID list, treat extension marketplaces as a supply-chain trust boundary, and block mangorbit[.]com at the network level.
