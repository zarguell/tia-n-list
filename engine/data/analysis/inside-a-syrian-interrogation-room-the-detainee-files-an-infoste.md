In May 2023, an infostealer infected a computer at the Military Police Investigation Section in Suluk, northern Syria, a unit of the Turkish-backed Syrian National Army. The malware exfiltrated saved passwords, cookies, a live Telegram session, and the unit's active detainee files from the officer's Desktop. Seven interrogation records were exposed, containing detainee photographs, family maps, fingerprinted confessions, and full identification details. Among the cases: an alleged espionage network, a smuggling ring run by serving soldiers, and one statement that exists in two different versions.

The files surfaced on Hudson Rock's Cavalier platform, an infostealer credential marketplace. The original documents are unredacted, carrying the Military Police crest, unit letterhead, and detainee ID photographs. The incident demonstrates how commodity infostealers can produce intelligence-grade exposure when they land on military or government endpoints, particularly in conflict zones where operational security is already fragile.

This is not a targeted espionage operation but a case of opportunistic malware collecting whatever the filesystem offers. The geopolitical consequence is significant: sensitive law enforcement and military intelligence from an active conflict zone is now in the hands of anyone who accesses the credential dump.
