South Korea may have logged the first documented case of an AI agent orchestrating intrusions against a financial sector. Police and the Financial Security Institute link breaches at seven institutions, including Shinhan Bank, KB Kookmin and Hana Bank, to ARTEX, an open source AI-agent penetration testing tool developed in China. CrowdStrike covered the campaign as an unknown actor using AI-driven tooling against South Korean finance; the Wall Street Journal flagged it as evidence of a new risk class.

The scale and the forensic detail make this concrete rather than theoretical. Korean press tallies put affected people between 65,000 and 68,000: Shinhan alone reported 25,727 customers, Yegaram Savings Bank around 40,000, KB Kookmin 119, Hana 89, plus outsourced developer data at BNK Busan and loan-agent records at Hyundai Capital. Stolen fields include names, contacts, Korean resident ID numbers, loan applications, credit limits and income figures. The tell was an artifact left on compromised web servers, the string ARTEX-自主渗透試控制台, roughly "ARTEX autonomous penetration test control console." FSI forensics at Shinhan traced over twenty proxy IPs across about a dozen countries, including the US, Japan and Germany. Detection times varied wildly by institution, from about 15.5 hours at Shinhan to over 67 at KB Kookmin.

Reporting describes ARTEX as a pipeline that orchestrates multiple LLMs, including Claude, GPT and DeepSeek, for autonomous vulnerability assessment and potentially exploitation, attributed to a Chinese developer listed as Li Fuhua. Attribution of the operator remains open. Watch the FSI's emergency review of some 500 institutions, whether the same artifact appears in intrusions outside Korea, and any move to restrict or monitor the tool's development. The detection-time spread is itself the lesson: banks need sub-hour alerting on web server tampering, not day-scale.
