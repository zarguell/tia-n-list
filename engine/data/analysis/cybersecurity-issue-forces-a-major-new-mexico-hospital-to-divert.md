The University of New Mexico Health System suffered a cybersecurity incident that forced ambulance diversions and clinic closures, affecting the state's only Level 1 trauma center. The network conducts about 900,000 outpatient visits and 100,000 emergency room visits annually, making disruption significant. Multiple employees reported computer messages claiming theft of student, employee, and patient data, though a spokesperson declined to confirm and cited an active law enforcement investigation with FBI assistance. Operations began resuming in phases later the same day, with Presbyterian Healthcare Services and Lovelace Health System coordinating patient intake.

This event is a confirmed operational disruption rather than a reported breach with verified data exfiltration. The claim of stolen data remains unverified by the health system or federal authorities. Security leaders in healthcare should treat diversion-level incidents as indicators of possible ransomware or destructive access, review backup and isolation procedures, and expect further updates once the FBI completes initial assessment.
