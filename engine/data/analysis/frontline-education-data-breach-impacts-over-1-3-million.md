Frontline Education is notifying more than 1.3 million people that their personal data was compromised in an August 14 breach. Attackers exploited a vulnerability in a third-party software product to access Frontline's environment, stealing data from the Frontline Central and School Health Management products before the incident was contained on August 15. The exposed records may include names, Social Security numbers, dates of birth, driver's license and passport numbers, Medicaid IDs, and student IDs for both school district staff and students.

The breach hits a vendor whose software is purpose-built for K-12 districts, so the blast radius extends across multiple school systems rather than a single institution. Affected districts should expect direct notification obligations and follow-up phishing risk against families and staff whose government identifiers were exposed. Frontline says it patched the underlying defect and reinforced security, but the third-party origin of the flaw means other customers of that component may face similar exposure.
