Software engineer and blogger Graybeard published an essay arguing that the conditions surrounding software development systematically distort people's sense of proportion. The piece observes that software combines speed, money, complexity, abstraction, and nearly unlimited freedom to change course, creating an environment where otherwise rational adults lose perspective. The author notes that unlike construction, where changing a wall's position has visible physical cost, software changes accumulate invisibly inside systems and people's heads.

The core argument is that the absence of natural friction between idea and implementation makes every proposal seem equally feasible, leading to chronic overcommitment. The essay also examines how software complexity becomes self-reinforcing: complicated systems provide psychological rewards (things to design, debate, own) that simple systems do not, creating organizations that exist partly to support the complexity itself. Money amplifies the problem by raising the emotional stakes of mundane work.

This is an opinion piece, not a security event. It touches on organizational dysfunction in software teams rather than any vulnerability or threat. The essay was circulated widely on Hacker News and Mastodon.
