Cloud hosting provider Railway disclosed that attackers exploited two vulnerabilities in its build infrastructure between 20 and 22 October 2026 to steal customer authentication secrets and run roughly $740,000 in fraudulent AI usage. The first flaw allowed a filesystem isolation escape that yielded an internal credential, which the attackers used to retrieve Railway's internal source code; a second flaw involving an improperly configured trust relationship then granted access to credentials used to decrypt customer environment variables. Railway says at most 1.85 percent of workspaces, about 100,000, had decrypted variables exposed, and that this figure is the maximum potentially impacted rather than a confirmed count of stolen secrets.

The intrusion lasted roughly 26 hours, and Railway urged all customers to rotate stored secrets while notifying those at increased risk by 23 October. The incident shows how a partial sandbox escape can be chained into a platform-wide credential compromise, with AI API keys as a likely target given the fraudulent usage. Customers who stored secrets on Railway before the cutoff should treat those credentials as compromised and rotate them.
