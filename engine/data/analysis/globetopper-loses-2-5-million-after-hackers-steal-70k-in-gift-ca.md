iQSTEL told the SEC that a cyberattack at its GlobeTopper unit cost an estimated $2.5 million in lost revenue and let the attacker redeem about $70,000 in digital gift cards. GlobeTopper is a business-to-business top-up and gift card distributor whose API is used by more than 4,000 brands in 65 countries, including IKEA, Adidas, Spotify, Target and Roblox. The intrusion was detected on October 22. After more unauthorized activity appeared the next day, the company took the platform and affected servers offline in the early hours of October 23.

The filing says the attacker obtained API credentials and integration keys belonging to certain GlobeTopper business customers. Those credentials were revoked and replaced, and the company reported no unauthorized use of them after that action. A rebuilt platform was deployed on AWS on October 31, with commercial operations restoring in stages from November 1. The outage and the slower recovery explain most of the revenue impact; the direct gift card loss was much smaller.

This incident is a reminder that API credentials held by a distributor can be a wider supply chain problem than the distributor's own systems. Customers whose integration keys were taken should confirm replacement and check redemption and account activity for abuse. Watch for any follow-up from iQSTEL on how the attacker entered the platform, whether customer data was accessed, and whether other businesses in the top-up ecosystem saw the same credential misuse.
