Ars Technica reported on a case of erythema ab igne, commonly called "toasted skin syndrome," in a boy who developed the condition from daily laptop use. The condition causes a net-like hyperpigmented rash from prolonged exposure to low-level heat, typically below the threshold for thermal burns. Laptops can generate surface temperatures in the 40-50 degree Celsius range on the skin-facing side, sufficient to cause the condition with chronic exposure. This is a consumer health story, not a cybersecurity event. The relevance to a security audience is limited, though it occasionally intersects with hardware security discussions around thermal management and device safety.
