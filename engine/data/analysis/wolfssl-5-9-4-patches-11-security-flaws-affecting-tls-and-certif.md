wolfSSL released version 5.9.4 on September 25, 2026, fixing 11 vulnerabilities: three high, five medium, and three low severity. The bugs span TLS and DTLS handshakes, X.509 certificate validation, revocation checking, OCSP stapling, session resumption, and memory safety. Several could let attackers bypass peer authentication, forge certificates that vulnerable clients accept, or make revoked certificates trusted under specific configurations. The deployments most exposed use DTLS, Raw Public Keys, OCSP stapling, custom certificate-verification callbacks, or the legacy TLS session-cache APIs.

The highest-impact flaw is CVE-2026-93302 in the MatchTrustedPeer verification path: wolfSSL ignored the public key associated with a trusted peer certificate, so a forged certificate clone of a CA could pass validation. Exploitation requires the WOLFSSL_TRUST_PEER_CERT feature with certificates loaded through wolfSSL_CTX_trust_peer_cert() or wolfSSL_trust_peer_cert(), but when OpenSSL-compatible defaults are enabled the exposure expands to all CA-loading operations. A malicious DTLS server that knows which CAs a client accepts could use this to bypass peer authentication. Affected releases run from wolfSSL 5.3.0 through 5.9.2. A second tracked flaw, CVE-2026-89102, affects OCSP handling, and the advisory's remaining issues cover the revocation and resumption code paths.

wolfSSL ships inside appliances, SDKs, and embedded firmware where updates routinely lag years behind, so the practical risk is the installed base rather than the library at HEAD. Organizations should inventory where wolfSSL is embedded, prioritize devices with trust-peer certificates or OpenSSL-compatible settings enabled, and apply 5.9.4. Watch for vendor advisories from appliance makers that bundle wolfSSL, and for exploitation reports against DTLS services since that is where authentication bypass is directly reachable.
