Google issued a warning about malware campaigns targeting Chrome users through deceptive click-based attack vectors. The Golem.de report, titled "One wrong click is enough," describes social engineering attacks that trick users into executing malicious code with a single interaction. While the specific malware family and campaign details are behind Golem's paywall, the warning aligns with ongoing trends in browser-targeted credential theft and session hijacking campaigns that have accelerated throughout 2026.

This advisory comes during a week of heightened browser and endpoint security activity. The BlueMoon exploit kit was observed chaining Chrome V8 and Windows kernel zero-days for espionage operations, and session hijacking attacks are increasingly bypassing MFA through stolen browser tokens. Chrome users should ensure auto-updates are enabled, verify they are running the latest stable release, and exercise caution with unexpected prompts or download requests that appear to originate from legitimate websites. Enterprise administrators should verify Chrome deployment versions across their fleet and consider enforcing Safe Browsing Enhanced Protection mode.
