Attackers published three malicious Firefox extensions impersonating the Phantom, Jupiter, and Bitget cryptocurrency wallets to steal recovery phrases and private keys. The campaign combined deceptive branding, bundled credential stealers, and remotely delivered JavaScript, with stolen data routed through infrastructure under bonto.io. All three listings were removed before Socket published its findings on October 27, 2026, but the extensions had already been available for download.

The risk here is credential theft at the wallet layer rather than a browser or OS vulnerability: a user who installed one of the impersonators and entered a seed phrase or private key would have handed direct control of the associated funds to the attacker. Remote JavaScript delivery means the payload could be changed after installation, and the bonto.io routing gives the operators a collection point that may outlive the listings themselves.

Watch for reappearance of the same or renamed extensions in other browsers or stores, for follow-on draining of wallets whose seeds were captured, and for whether Socket or Mozilla publishes indicators of compromise that defenders can use to hunt. Users and exchanges should treat any wallet that touched these extensions as compromised and move funds, and enterprise extension policies should restrict installs to allowlisted publishers.
