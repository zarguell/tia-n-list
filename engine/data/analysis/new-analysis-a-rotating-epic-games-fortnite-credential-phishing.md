An analysis from carlesi.vg maps a rotating Epic Games and Fortnite credential-phishing network built on 9 pivot IPs spread across 4 bulletproof-hosting /24 blocks, using lookalike domains swapped to stay ahead of takedowns. The kit poses as a Fortnite locker checker, a lure aimed at players who want rare in-game cosmetics, and harvests Epic account logins. A separate teardown by nsshq.com dissected the anti-bot gate in front of the kit and documented the panel read-only, describing how the operator filters out researchers and automated scanners before a victim reaches the credential page.

The rotation is the point. By cycling domains and fronting them with a bot-detection layer, the operator keeps the phishing pages alive long enough to matter and forces defenders into a slow round of chasing fresh lookalikes. The victim pool skews young, and Epic accounts often carry saved payment methods and linked consoles. Watch for new apex domains reusing the same /24 hosting blocks, for the specific anti-bot fingerprint nsshq documented as a detection signal, and for the credential pages shifting from simple login capture to session-token theft that outlives a password reset.
