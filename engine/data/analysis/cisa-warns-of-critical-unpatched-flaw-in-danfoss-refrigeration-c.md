CISA warned about CVE-2026-129713, a stack-based buffer overflow in the server of Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA controllers with a CVSS score of 9.8. An unauthenticated attacker can trigger it to restart the server and may achieve remote code execution with the web server's privileges. The controllers manage refrigeration, HVAC and lighting in supermarkets and convenience stores, including the 820A, 850A and 880A models. CISA says they are deployed worldwide but does not give a count.

The warning sits on top of a disclosure made public on November 11. Midnight Blue researcher Carlo Meijer found six pre-authentication buffer overflows in the AK-SM800A firmware in about an hour of manual review; all versions through 5.3.2 were affected and, at disclosure, there was no CVE and no fixed firmware. BSI rated the issue criticality 3, Orange, its immediate-action level, the Belgian CCB confirmed the findings on November 12, and Ireland's NCSC joined the call to take management interfaces off the internet, with a VPN as the minimum for remote access.

Attacks against internet-exposed Danfoss and Copeland controllers in Europe began on October 11, with reports of cooling systems shut down or temperatures raised. Danfoss said on November 20 that CVE-2026-129713 would be addressed in an upcoming 5.5.x release, so the immediate fix remains exposure reduction rather than patching. Watch for the 5.5.x release, confirmation of which CVEs map to Meijer's six bugs, and any victim details from the supermarket incidents.
