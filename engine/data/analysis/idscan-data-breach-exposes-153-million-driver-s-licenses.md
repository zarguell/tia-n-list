IDScan, a provider of identity document scanning services, confirmed a breach of its IDScan.net cloud environment that exposed approximately 153 million U.S. and Canadian driver's license scans. The company acknowledged the incident after BleepingComputer and other outlets reported the breach, stating that hackers accessed its cloud infrastructure and that customer data may have been compromised. The compromised database contained scanned images of driver's licenses collected through IDScan's verification platform, which is used by businesses for age and identity verification.

This breach is significant because driver's license images contain both biometric data (photos) and personally identifiable information (names, addresses, dates of birth, license numbers) that cannot be easily changed like a password. The volume of 153 million records suggests the breach affected nearly every adult driver in both countries, making it one of the largest identity document exposures on record. Stolen license scans are high-value targets for identity theft and synthetic identity fraud.

Watch for follow-on fraud: threat actors typically monetize driver's license data through account opening, loan applications, and deepfake-enhanced identity verification bypasses. Organizations that rely on IDScan or similar services should audit their identity verification pipelines and consider compensating controls. The full scope of exfiltration and whether the data has appeared on dark web marketplaces remains to be determined.
