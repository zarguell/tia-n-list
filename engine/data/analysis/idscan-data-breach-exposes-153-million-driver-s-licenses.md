IDScan, a major identity verification provider, has confirmed that attackers accessed customer data in its IDScan.net cloud platform. The company said it learned on or around September 1 that data may have been accessed without authorization and published a breach notice on September 4, a page that was configured to keep search engines from indexing it. Exposed information includes customer full names and driver's license or other government ID numbers, and reporting indicates scans of driver's licenses were also taken.

The scale claimed for the incident is large. A database of 153 million US and Canadian driver's license scans was offered for sale on the dark web, a listing Brian Krebs detailed, and multiple lawsuits were filed against IDScan before the company publicly confirmed anything. Follow-up reporting established that hackers stole at least 13 to 15 million driver's licenses in the attack, while the 153 million figure covers the database tied to the incident. Coverage today reiterates the company's confirmation that more than 150 million driver's licenses are connected to the breach.

The practical risk is durable identity fraud: a license number plus full name plus the document image is enough to pass many identity verification checks and to seed convincing phishing. Watch the scope of notifications, whether the remaining inventory of scans is resold or leaked, further litigation, and fraud reports from organizations that rely on IDScan for onboarding checks.
