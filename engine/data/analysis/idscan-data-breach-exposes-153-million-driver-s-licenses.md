IDScan, a Louisiana-based identity verification service used by venues including cannabis dispensaries, confirmed on September 10 that hackers stole driver's license data from its cloud infrastructure. The breach affects over 150 million records spanning U.S. and Canadian residents. Stolen data includes full names, driver's license numbers, and identity numbers from other government-issued documents such as passports.

The breach was first reported by journalist Brian Krebs on September 1, when he identified a dark web website allowing anyone to search the driver's license information of over 150 million people, including accessing their photos. Krebs verified the data by examining his own record. The database contained high-profile individuals including U.S. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth. IDScan received notice of the hack claim on or around September 1 but did not confirm the intrusion until September 10.

The FBI is investigating the incident, and the Pentagon confirmed awareness of the suspected breach. IDScan noted that "full access to the information required payment," indicating the hackers demanded money to access the full dataset. The company has not disclosed how many individuals are affected, though its website states it holds over 150 million driver's license records. The breach underscores the risk of centralized identity verification services accumulating massive datasets of government-issued documents.
