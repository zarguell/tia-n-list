IDScan, an identity verification company whose platform is used by car rental firms, retailers, financial institutions, cannabis dispensaries, gun shops, and hospitality businesses, confirmed on September 4 that an unauthorized third party accessed or copied customer data from its cloud platform. The company discovered the breach on or around September 1. The exposed data includes full names, driver's license or government ID numbers, and reportedly scans of driver's licenses. The scale is massive: over 153 million U.S. and Canadian driver's license scans, along with 10 million ID cards, 3 million travel documents, and 579,000 medical cards.

The breach first came to public attention on September 1 when Brian Krebs reported that a dark web platform called "Nexus" was advertising access to the database. Krebs verified samples by searching for records of consenting individuals and traced the data back to IDScan. After news spread, Nexus went offline, but multiple threat actors subsequently claimed to be selling the full database. IDScan's breach notification was published on September 4 but configured with a noindex directive, keeping it out of search engines until TechCrunch spotted it on September 10.

The FBI has confirmed it is investigating, and multiple lawsuits have already been filed against IDScan. The company is offering free credit monitoring and identity protection services. The breadth of exposed documents (driver's licenses, travel documents, medical cards) makes this a high-value dataset for identity theft, synthetic identity fraud, and document forgery at scale.
