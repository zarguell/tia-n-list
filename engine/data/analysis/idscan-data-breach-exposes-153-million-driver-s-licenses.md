IDScan, a provider of identity document scanning services, confirmed a breach of its IDScan.net cloud environment that exposed approximately 153 million U.S. and Canadian driver's license scans. The company acknowledged the incident after BleepingComputer and other outlets reported the breach, stating that hackers accessed its cloud infrastructure and that customer data may have been compromised. A September 19 follow-up from This Week in Security clarified that IDScan confirmed hackers stole at least 13 to 15 million driver's licenses during the attack, though the full exposed database contained 153 million records.

This breach is significant because driver's license images contain both biometric data (photos) and personally identifiable information (names, addresses, dates of birth, license numbers) that cannot be easily changed like a password. The gap between 153 million exposed records and 13-15 million confirmed stolen suggests the attacker may have exfiltrated a subset, but the full scope of data access remains unclear. Stolen license scans are high-value targets for identity theft and synthetic identity fraud.

Watch for follow-on fraud: threat actors typically monetize driver's license data through account opening, loan applications, and deepfake-enhanced identity verification bypasses. Organizations that rely on IDScan or similar services should audit their identity verification pipelines and consider compensating controls. The confirmed theft of millions of licenses makes this one of the largest identity document breaches on record.
