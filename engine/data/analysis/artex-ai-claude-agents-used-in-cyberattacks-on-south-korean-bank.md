CrowdStrike identified a Chinese-speaking actor who targeted South Korean banks using the open-source ARTEX agentic penetration-testing suite with Claude agents. The campaign ran from late September into early October 2026, hitting Shinhan Bank, KB Kookmin Bank, and Hana Bank. The attacker used DeepSeek v4.1-flash as the primary LLM backend, supplemented by GLM-5.3 and Grok 4.6, and exposed Claude session histories, ARTEX configs, and memory files in open directories at a Hong Kong IP. Overlapping targets and attacker infrastructure allowed high-confidence linking to previous reports.

The exposure of AI tool session histories and configuration files is unusual: it gave researchers direct visibility into the attacker’s methods, including a resume created with the same AI pipeline that revealed a Telegram account (@YY520CN) and other identifiers. The South Korean government convened an emergency meeting and called for stronger protections for critical IT systems after data exfiltration and service outages were confirmed.

What to watch: whether CrowdStrike’s disclosure prompts wider scanning for exposed Claude/ARTEX directories, and whether South Korean authorities release a formal attribution linking the actor to a specific state-aligned group. The use of LLM-reseller proxies like xcai[.]pro also points to a growing supply chain of AI-enabled attack infrastructure.
