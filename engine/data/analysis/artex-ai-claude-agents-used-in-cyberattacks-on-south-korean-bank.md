CrowdStrike identified a Chinese-speaking actor using the open-source ARTEX AI agentic penetration-testing suite paired with Claude agents to target South Korean financial institutions from late September into early October 2026. The attacker targeted Shinhan Bank, KB Kookmin Bank, and Hana Bank, exposing client personal data and credit card information and causing outages. Open directories at a Hong Kong IP held Claude Code session histories, Claude memory files, and ARTEX configs. The LLM stack included DeepSeek v4.1-flash as primary backend, supplemented by GLM-5.3 and Grok 4.6; researchers noted probable use of an LLM proxy/reseller xcai[.]pro. The same AI setup produced a resume that exposed the actor's identification, Telegram account (@YY520CN), and contact details. The suspected actor denied responsibility. South Korea convened an emergency meeting and pushed for stronger critical-IT protections.

The campaign links AI-tool misuse directly to a real-world financial-sector breach with named victims and exfiltrated data. It matters because an open-source pentest framework combined with commercially available LLMs enabled autonomous targeting, and the exposed session artifacts allowed attribution. Watch for additional victims in the overlapping target set, confirmation of the xcai[.]pro proxy role, and whether South Korean regulators impose new IT-security mandates.
