European law enforcement, supported by Europol and Eurojust, arrested seven suspects and seized more than 100 servers on November 3, 2026 in a coordinated action against an unnamed company suspected of running a crime-as-a-service model that supplied fraudulent trading websites to criminal gangs. The operation involved authorities from Albania, Cyprus, Finland, Germany, Kosovo, and North Macedonia and followed four years of investigation plus earlier interventions in 2022 and 2025. The probe began in 2022 after a German couple could not withdraw money invested through an online trading platform, leading investigators to link multiple call-center operators behind investment scams to potential victims in at least 50 countries.

The scheme used deceptive advertisements promising substantial, quick returns from foreign currency, stock, or cryptocurrency trading to steer victims to fraudulent investment platforms and harvest their personal data. That stolen data was then sold to criminal groups running call centers, whose operators contacted victims with supposed investment opportunities. Seizing the infrastructure disrupts the platform supply chain, but the unnamed provider's clients and the already-sold victim data remain unaccounted for.

Watch for follow-on arrests of call-center operators, victim notification waves, and any publication of client lists recovered from the seized servers. There is no CVE or technical vulnerability involved; this is a law-enforcement disruption, and the main open question is how much of the fraud network's customer base investigators can identify from the seized systems.
