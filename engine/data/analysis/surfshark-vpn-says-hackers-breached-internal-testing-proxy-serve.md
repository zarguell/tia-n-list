Surfshark disclosed that hackers breached an internal test server after a misconfiguration made it reachable from the internet. The exposed environment contained service configurations, build-related credentials, portions of system binaries, and code history. A separate proxy server used for content-accessibility optimization was also accessed, though Surfshark says it held no sensitive data such as user identities, IP addresses, encryption keys, or browsing traffic. The company detected suspicious activity on August 31, contained the incident on September 2, and completed remediation by September 5. Surfshark states production VPN infrastructure and customer data were not impacted, and that VPN traffic is not logged or retained. The company did not specify which exact credentials or files were exposed. For a VPN provider, any breach of build infrastructure raises supply chain concerns, even when customer data is not directly accessed.
