Cryptocurrency exchange Binance is investigating a potential security breach involving up to $5.8 billion in digital assets, which if confirmed stolen would far exceed the $1.5 billion taken from Bybit by North Korean hackers in February 2025, the largest confirmed crypto heist on record. The suspected breach has already prompted stablecoin issuers Tether and Circle to freeze more than $2.3 billion in assets associated with the incident, including more than $1.567 billion in USDT frozen by Tether. Binance confirmed to BleepingComputer that its teams are aware of reports of a potential security issue and are actively investigating, while reassuring customers that user funds are completely safe. Founder Changpeng Zhao said on X that the incident appeared to be a hack, noting he no longer manages the exchange and expects all user funds to be fully covered.

The key uncertainty is whether the funds are actually stolen or merely moved or frozen as a precaution, since Binance has not confirmed a loss and is still investigating. The freeze of over $2.3 billion by Tether and Circle suggests the issuers treated the associated addresses as high risk, but it does not by itself establish theft. Customers should watch official Binance channels for verified updates and treat any unofficial links, messages, or requests for account information as scams, as Binance warned. If confirmed, this would be the largest cryptocurrency theft on record by a wide margin.
