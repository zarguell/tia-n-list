A search-hijacking campaign spanning 319 Chrome extensions with 13.4 million reported installations abuses Chrome's chrome_settings_overrides capability to replace the default search provider and route every query through an operator-controlled proxy before landing on Bing or Yahoo under a syndication partner ID. Socket researcher John Tuckner's October 29, 2026 report expands his April 2025 discovery of 57 extensions with 6 million users by adding 264 newly identified add-ons accounting for another 8.7 million users. The extensions masquerade as PDF converters, manual finders, safe search tools, and AI search tools, yet largely just redirect queries through a chain of sites. They are unlisted on the Chrome Web Store and are promoted through shady advertisements on websites rather than store search, which is why they accumulated installs without organic discovery.

The exposure is not a traditional vulnerability: chrome_settings_overrides is a documented Chrome feature, so there is no CVE to patch and no single update that neutralizes the threat. What makes this story stand out among the day's extension abuse reports is the combination of scale, with 13.4 million reported installs accumulated over roughly a decade, and the operational template reused by a handful of organized operators who need almost no code to hijack search. Every query a victim types, including sensitive terms, passes through the operator's proxy, enabling both query capture and referral revenue, while the extension remains invisible to store search. Because distribution relies on deceptive ads rather than the store, takedown of the listings does not remove already-installed copies, so the long tail of affected browsers persists until each user uninstalls.

Defenders should inventory installed extensions against the full ID list published in Socket's report and remove any match, then reset the default search provider in Chrome settings. Hunt in enterprise fleets using the Chrome Enterprise API or chrome://extensions reviews for unlisted add-ons, and inspect network logs for redirect chains through suspicious proxy hosts terminating at Bing or Yahoo with partner referral parameters. Network restrictions on the proxy domains and strict extension allowlisting prevent recurrence, since blocking unlisted and sideloaded extensions closes the promotion path. Users should also clear cached search settings and rotate credentials if queries included account or financial terms.

The assessment changes if Google removes the remaining listings and tightens Chrome Web Store policy on unlisted extensions, or if operators reappear under new extension IDs and publisher accounts. Continued proxy traffic after removals would indicate the campaign is still active, and expansion to Edge or Firefox would widen the affected population beyond Chrome's 13.4 million reported installs.
