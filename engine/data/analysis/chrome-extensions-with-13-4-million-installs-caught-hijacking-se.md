A Socket report by researcher John Tuckner documents a search-hijacking campaign affecting 319 Chrome extensions with a reported 13.4 million combined installations. The campaign redirects user queries through tracking servers to generate advertising revenue from Bing and Yahoo. Tuckner added 264 newly identified add-ons with 8.7 million users to a prior April 2025 discovery of 57 extensions with 6 million users. The extensions pose as PDF converters, safe search tools, and AI search aids but rely on Chrome's built-in chrome_settings_overrides setting to replace the default search provider.

These extensions are unlisted on the Chrome Web Store and promoted through shady advertisements rather than official listings. The event text names no specific CVEs or exploitation timelines, only the scale of installations and the affiliate model. Security teams should audit installed extensions and block the identified proxy redirection paths rather than treat this as a targeted intrusion campaign.
