A search-hijacking campaign involving 319 Chrome extensions with a combined 13.4 million reported installations redirected users' searches through tracking servers to generate advertising revenue from Bing and Yahoo. Researcher John Tuckner of Socket expanded on an April 2025 discovery of 57 extensions with 6 million users by identifying 264 additional add-ons with another 8.7 million users. The extensions masquerade as PDF converters, manual finders, safe search tools, and AI search tools, yet largely just route queries through a series of sites before landing on Bing or Yahoo.

Although hosted on the Chrome Web Store, the extensions are unlisted and do not appear in store searches; they are promoted through shady advertisements on websites. Tuckner notes that many need almost no code, since Chrome's built-in chrome_settings_overrides setting lets them replace the default search provider. The campaign is attributed to a handful of organized operators working from the same template. Users who installed any of these extensions should remove them and reset their browser search settings.
