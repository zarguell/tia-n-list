Socket researcher John Tuckner identified 319 Chrome extensions with roughly 13.4 million reported installs that hijacked users' default search settings over the course of a decade. The extensions posed as PDF converters, manual finders, safe search and AI search tools, but in practice routed every query through operator-controlled proxy servers before landing on Bing or Yahoo, earning referral payments under a syndication partner ID. Most were unlisted in the Chrome Web Store and were promoted through advertisements, including Google Ads, rather than organic store searches. The April 2025 precursor to this report found 57 such extensions reaching about 6 million users.

The primary risk is privacy and search integrity rather than traditional malware: queries can reveal health, financial or workplace interests, and Socket found they were often tied to a unique identifier that could link a user's searches over time. The proxy could also steer where searches were sent. The extensions needed little code because Chrome's built-in chrome_settings_overrides mechanism lets an extension replace the default search provider. Users should review installed extensions, remove unfamiliar or ad-promoted add-ons, and avoid installing browser tools from advertisements, even when the listing sits in Google's official store.
