Socket researcher John Tuckner identified 319 Chrome extensions with roughly 13.4 million reported installs that quietly took over users' default search engine. The set expands his April 2025 finding of 57 extensions with about 6 million users by 264 new add-ons and another 8.7 million installs. The extensions posed as PDF converters, manual finders, safe search and AI search tools. Most were unlisted in the Chrome Web Store and pushed through advertisements, including Google Ads, so they appeared in search results only through a direct link.

The mechanism needed little code. Chrome's built-in chrome_settings_overrides setting let an extension replace the default search provider, after which queries passed through operator-controlled proxy servers before landing on Bing or Yahoo. The operators earned referral payments under a syndication partner ID and could read the queries in transit. Socket says the queries were often tied to a unique identifier, which allowed searches about health, finances or workplace matters to be linked to the same user over time. The proxy could also choose where a search was sent.

The November 23 social post is a repost of Socket's October 29 research, not a new campaign wave. It adds no new victim counts, tooling or infrastructure beyond the original report, but it keeps the 319-extension figure in circulation. What is worth watching is whether removed listings reappear under new names, whether Google changes the unlisted-extension path used by these ads, and whether any follow-up work ties the search hijackers to the earlier 57-extension set that also requested broad cookie and token permissions.
