Security researchers at Adversa AI disclosed a zero-click prompt injection technique they call Cryptographic Context Injection targeting xAI's Grok web chat. The attack turns a routine "summarize this page" request into a silent data-exfiltration vector. When a user asks Grok to summarize a webpage, hidden instructions on an attacker-controlled page are decrypted and treated as trusted internal context, exfiltrating the user's name, approximate location, subscription tier, and active conversation history.

The proof of concept was tested against Grok 4.5 Fast on grok.com and completed without a confirmation dialog or visible warning. The technique abuses Grok's webpage browsing capability and its integrated Python sandbox, which processes attacker-controlled content as executable instructions. The attack requires zero clicks beyond the user's natural action of asking for a page summary.

This is significant because it demonstrates a practical exploitation of the agentic AI pattern, where models interact with external content on behalf of users. The attack surface grows as assistants gain browsing, code execution, and tool-use capabilities. The lack of any user-facing confirmation is the critical gap, most AI assistants in this category treat model-decrypted content as authoritative without validation. xAI's response and remediation timeline will determine whether this becomes a blueprint for broader exploitation of agentic systems.
