OpenAI confirmed that GPT-6 Astra is the first broadly deployed model to reach the "Critical level" under its Preparedness Framework for cybersecurity. The Critical threshold applies when a model can identify and develop functional zero-day exploits of all severity levels in hardened real-world systems without human guidance, or devise new end-to-end attack strategies against hardened targets. In evaluations, Astra discovered and used previously unknown zero-day vulnerabilities as part of exploit chains, and OpenAI is in the process of disclosing two such flaws to maintainers.

The company said Astra represents a significant step up in cyber capabilities: with the right tools and access, it can find previously unknown security flaws and develop exploitation approaches across many well-protected systems without step-by-step human direction. OpenAI has strengthened Astra's jailbreak resistance, isolation, checkpoint encryption, and monitoring as mitigations, but acknowledged the model is harder to monitor than its predecessors.

For defenders, this marks a concrete milestone in AI-enabled offensive capability. The gap between model capability and defensive readiness is narrowing faster than expected, and the disclosure pipeline for AI-discovered vulnerabilities is still nascent. Security teams should expect more sophisticated automated exploit chains appearing in the wild, and the two undisclosed vulnerabilities being patched via responsible disclosure represent a brief window before similar techniques are replicated by less scrupulous actors.
