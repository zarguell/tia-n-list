A BlackNevas ransomware engagement investigated by a global emergency response team destroyed an organization's virtualization estate over ten days without exploiting any virtualization-layer vulnerabilities: no guest-to-host escapes or hypervisor memory corruption. The attacker used legitimate VMware management interfaces to hit four ESXi hosts, every VM on them, both vCenter Server Appliances, and all connected backup infrastructure. The group was first observed in November 2024 and is one of at least ten ransomware families that leverage the Babuk ESXi locker leaked in September 2021, which made ESXi ransomware accessible to operators lacking hypervisor exploitation expertise. The Babuk ESXi code has been combined with leaked LockBit Windows builds by multiple operators.

The key lesson is that hypervisor ransomware does not require a hypervisor bug: stolen or misused management credentials and interfaces can wipe an entire virtualized estate, including backups reached through the same management plane. The 10-day dwell also shows the value of detecting lateral movement toward vCenter and backup infrastructure before encryption begins.

Defenders should segment and monitor vCenter and ESXi management access, protect backup infrastructure from the virtualization management plane, and treat hypervisor administration as a tier-zero identity. Because the attack used legitimate interfaces, patching alone will not stop it; access control and detection are the operative controls.
