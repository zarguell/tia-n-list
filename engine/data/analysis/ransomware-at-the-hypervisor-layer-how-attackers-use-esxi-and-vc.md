A BlackNevas ransomware engagement investigated by Kaspersky's Global Emergency Response Team destroyed an organization's virtualization estate over ten days without exploiting any virtualization-layer vulnerability: no guest-to-host escapes and no hypervisor memory corruption. The attacker used legitimate VMware management interfaces to hit four ESXi hosts, every virtual machine on them, both vCenter Server Appliances, and all connected backup infrastructure. The ransom notes identified BlackNevas, first observed in November 2024, and the same incident was corroborated by gbhackers coverage citing the Kaspersky GERT findings.

The attack worked because ESXi ransomware is now cheap to obtain. The Babuk ransomware source code leaked online in September 2021, including its ESXi version, and at least ten subsequent ransomware families have leveraged it, including operators who otherwise lacked the expertise to build ESXi-compatible lockers from scratch. Multiple operators combined Babuk-derived ESXi lockers with leaked LockBit Windows builds.

The lesson is that hypervisor ransomware does not require a hypervisor bug. Stolen or misused management credentials and interfaces can wipe an entire virtualized estate, including backups reached through the same management plane. Defenders should segment and monitor vCenter and ESXi management access, protect backup infrastructure from the virtualization management plane, and treat hypervisor administration as a tier-zero identity. Because the attack used legitimate interfaces, patching alone will not stop it; access control and detection are the operative controls.
