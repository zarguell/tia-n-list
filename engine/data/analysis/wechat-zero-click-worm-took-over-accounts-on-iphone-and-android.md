Security researchers disclosed a zero-click worm vulnerability in WeChat that allowed attackers to take over accounts on both iPhone and Android devices through incoming calls. The attack required no user interaction beyond receiving the call, making it a particularly dangerous exploitation vector for the messaging platform's billions of users. The worm capability means successful exploitation could spread automatically from compromised accounts to the victim's contacts.

This vulnerability is significant because WeChat is the dominant messaging platform in China and widely used internationally. Zero-click exploits targeting mobile messaging apps represent the highest tier of surveillance and attack capability, typically reserved for nation-state operations. The worm component elevates this from individual account compromise to potential mass exploitation. Organizations with operations or employees in China should consider this in their threat models, particularly for executives and personnel with access to sensitive communications.
