Security firm Calif built a zero-click worm that takes over WeChat accounts through incoming calls, with no tap, answer, or user action required. The exploit was demonstrated jumping across three test phones: an Android device called an iPhone and seized its WeChat account, then the compromised iPhone called a second Android device and repeated the takeover. Each compromised account inherits the victim's contact list, which becomes the propagation engine for the next hop. The account at stake carries payments, official accounts, and mini programs across Tencent's 1.439 billion combined monthly active users for WeChat and Weixin.

Calif reported the flaw in July 2026. Tencent shipped WeChat 8.0.77 for Android and 8.0.76 for iOS on August 21, and on August 28 Calif confirmed the exploit was blocked on Tencent's servers. There is no CVE, no Tencent security advisory, and no published indicators of compromise. Calif's writeup describes the attacker needing to be in the target's contact list, which sounds like a mitigation but functions as the worm's propagation mechanism. The only user action that stops an attempt is declining the call, but the attacker can simply retry later.

A skeptical analysis from The Perimeter Site argues WeWorm will remain a boutique tool because developing stable zero-click exploits for modern iOS and Android is extremely expensive and state actors would not burn such an asset on mass infections. The real concern is for high-value targets in markets where WeChat is mandatory for business, and for users in restrictive regimes who rely on WeChat for encrypted communications. Calif also claims it used AI-assisted techniques to discover the bug in roughly two days of engineering, signaling that the cost of finding zero-click bugs in large messaging clients is declining. Desktop and HarmonyOS clients have not been tested and should be treated as unknown, not clean.
