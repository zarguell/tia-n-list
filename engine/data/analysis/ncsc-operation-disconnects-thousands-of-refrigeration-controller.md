The UK story is a prevention operation, not a breach disclosure. Researchers and the NCSC quietly moved thousands of refrigeration controllers at supermarkets and food suppliers off the public internet. Pen Test Partners described how Neil McCourt of Omega Solutions found a significant flaw in Resource Data Management controllers and how its hardware team validated it and found additional issues. RDM's customer advisory RDM-SA-2026-001 was dated 2 November and revised 3 November, and Omega alerted the NCSC before a wider industry notification effort.

CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA and DOT published a joint fact sheet warning that malicious actors target unpatched or internet-accessible electronic temperature controllers and commercial refrigeration controllers in grocery and cold storage. The agencies said successful exploitation could change temperatures and cause fire hazards, inventory loss and changes to system service life, with healthcare, chemical, food and agriculture, and transportation among affected sectors. Garry Turner-Smith of Omega said an attacker with full control of vulnerable RDM data managers could interfere with refrigeration across multiple sites.

Pen Test Partners used Shodan to find exposed controllers and contacted large operators, but said smaller cold-chain operators may still be exposed. Ken Munro said the discovery method was not published and advised taking controllers off the public internet or applying updated firmware. Cooling Post said RDM released new firmware and that its units are more common in the UK than Danfoss units. The researchers withheld the exploit method. Watch for confirmation that the firmware closed the issues Pen Test Partners found, and for exposure checks at small operators that were never contacted.
