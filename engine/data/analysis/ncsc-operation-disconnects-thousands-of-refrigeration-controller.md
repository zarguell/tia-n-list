A Whitehall and NCSC operation, reported by the BBC, moved thousands of refrigeration controllers at major UK supermarkets and food suppliers off the public internet. The BBC describes tens of thousands of fridges and freezers covered by the work. The weaknesses were found by Neil McCourt of Omega Solutions and validated by Pen Test Partners, which found additional issues. Pen Test Partners criticized the initial RDM advisory, RDM-SA-2026-001, dated November 2 and revised November 3, for lacking impact detail and independent validation.

Pen Test Partners used Shodan to locate exposed Resource Data Management controllers and contacted large operators during the quiet notification effort. Ken Munro said many thousands of controllers at small stores were still exposed and that the discovery method was withheld. Garry Turner-Smith of Omega said an attacker with full control of the RDM data managers could interfere with refrigeration across multiple sites. Cooling Post reports that Glasgow-based RDM released new firmware and that RDM units are more common in UK supermarkets than Danfoss units.

CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA and DOT separately warned that internet-accessible temperature and commercial refrigeration controllers in grocery and cold storage can be changed to alter temperatures, with fire, inventory-loss and service-life consequences. The joint fact sheet names healthcare, chemical, food and agriculture, and transportation as affected sectors. Guidance is to remove controllers from the public internet, apply updated firmware and use a VPN or private APN where cellular modems are involved. Watch for remaining exposed RDM units at smaller cold-chain operators and any field report of temperature tampering, which the event material does not claim so far.
