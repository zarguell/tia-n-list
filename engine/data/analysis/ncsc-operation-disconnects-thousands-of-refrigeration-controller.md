A Whitehall and NCSC operation, reported by the BBC, moved thousands of refrigeration controllers at major UK supermarkets and food suppliers off the public internet. The BBC describes tens of thousands of fridges and freezers covered by the work. The weaknesses were found by Neil McCourt of Omega Solutions and validated by Pen Test Partners, which found additional issues. Pen Test Partners criticized the initial RDM advisory, RDM-SA-2026-001, dated November 2 and revised November 3, for lacking impact detail and independent validation.

CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA and DOT separately warned that internet-accessible temperature and commercial refrigeration controllers in grocery and cold storage can be manipulated to change temperatures, with fire, inventory-loss and service-life consequences. Cooling Post says thousands of Resource Data Management controllers were exposed and that smaller cold-chain operators may still be reachable. Guidance from the researchers and trade press is to remove controllers from the public internet, apply updated firmware and use a VPN or private APN where cellular modems are involved. Watch for remaining exposed devices and any evidence of actual tampering.
