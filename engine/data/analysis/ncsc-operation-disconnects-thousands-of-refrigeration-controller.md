The UK story is a prevention operation, not a breach disclosure. In late October, Omega Solutions engineer Neil McCourt found a weak-password flaw in Resource Data Management (RDM) temperature controllers, the dominant UK supermarket refrigeration platform, and Pen Test Partners validated it and found additional issues; RDM's customer advisory RDM-SA-2026-001 went out on 2 November and was revised on 3 November. Unlike the Danfoss controllers hit in France on 11 and 12 October, which relied on a backdoor account with a daily password seeded from the date, the RDM problem was related but not identical, and RDM says it saw no malicious activity on its controllers. Thousands of controllers were pulled from the public internet, remotely or by unplugging cables, and one large chain removed hundreds a day for a week while first patch releases were deployed.

This outranks the day's other industrial-control notes because of the gap between reach and evidence of use. A single RDM controller can manage dozens of fridges and freezers, and Omega's Garry Turner-Smith said full control of a data manager would let an attacker interfere with refrigeration across multiple sites; warehouses hold millions of pounds of food, and the 1 and 2 November wave already showed the physical payoff, hitting dozens of supermarkets in seven countries and a small number of French blood banks. The UK avoided that outcome because its stores mostly run RDM rather than Danfoss, not because the attack path was absent. Pen Test Partners found thousands of exposed controllers through Shodan and could identify only the large operators, leaving small cold-chain sites as the unowned tail of the exposure.

Defenders with RDM estates should first verify that no controller management interface is reachable from the public internet, including cellular modems, and move remote access to a VPN or private APN; the researchers say keeping the discovery method quiet buys time, not protection. Apply the latest RDM firmware and first patch releases, track RDM-SA-2026-001 and later revisions, and treat private-network deployments as lower exposure rather than exempt. Hunt with Shodan for the organisation's own controller interfaces and any location metadata they expose, inventory each data manager and the sites it serves, and put temperature telemetry and manual temperature checks under alerting for unexpected temperature or defrost changes, the physical effect CISA and the FBI flagged on 5 November.

The assessment changes in two ways. Confirmed malicious activity on RDM controllers would turn this into an active incident rather than a preemptive hardening effort, and public disclosure of the exploit or discovery method would compress the timeline, since Pen Test Partners says recreation is easy once the method is known and nation-state actors are active around refrigeration hacks. Watch also for independent validation of the firmware fix and for a workable notification path for the small operators still reachable, because the claim of broad mitigation rests on owners identifying and removing their own controllers.
