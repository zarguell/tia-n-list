The UK's NCSC, FBI, and Dutch AIVD issued a joint advisory on September 15 detailing "CHOSEN BRICK," an Iranian state-linked spyware family used against dissidents, journalists, and activists. The malware spreads through spear-phishing on WhatsApp and Telegram, where attackers impersonate trusted contacts and sometimes send fabricated documents (including fake MRI results) to trick victims into installing it. Once on a device, CHOSEN BRICK collects emails, messages, contact lists, social media data, screen captures, and microphone audio. Some victims' personal details later appeared on pro-Iranian leak sites.

This is the third joint advisory from these agencies in recent months targeting Iranian cyber operations, and it underscores a pattern of Tehran using digital surveillance tools to suppress perceived threats abroad. The advisory does not name a specific spyware vendor, which may indicate the tooling is custom-built or acquired through opaque channels. Organizations supporting at-risk populations in the Iranian diaspora should treat this as a current, active threat vector and brief users on messaging app hygiene.
