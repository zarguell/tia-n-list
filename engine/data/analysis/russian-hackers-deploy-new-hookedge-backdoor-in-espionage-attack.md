Russian state-sponsored group APT28 (BlueDelta/Fancy Bear/Forest Blizzard) has deployed a new lightweight Windows backdoor called HOOKEDGE in espionage campaigns against European government, diplomatic, and defense-manufacturing organizations. PolySwarm documented the activity targeting entities in Romania, Spain, and Turkey between late September 2025 and early April 2026, with newer variants appearing in June and July 2026.

The infection chain begins with spearphishing attachments containing macro-enabled Word documents. Early lures used diplomatic themes, including one impersonating material from Spain's Ministry of the Presidency. Later campaigns shifted to generic documents with "Enable Content" prompts. When the victim enables macros, a fake Word error message appears while AutoOpen() runs a multi-stage infection in the background, writing batch, command, VBScript, HTML, and XHTML files into the user profile directory. Persistence is established via a Windows Scheduled Task that launches HOOKEDGE at configured intervals. Installation scripts then remove themselves and associated artifacts to hinder forensic recovery.

HOOKEDGE operates as a persistent backdoor providing sustained access for long-term intelligence collection. The campaign's focus on government and defense-sector targets in multiple European countries, combined with the group's known ties to Russian military intelligence (GRU), makes this a significant state-sponsored espionage operation. The shift from diplomatic lures to more generic documents suggests the group is optimizing for volume while maintaining effectiveness. Organizations in the targeted sectors should review detections for the documented TTPs, particularly scheduled task creation patterns and the specific file artifacts dropped during installation.
