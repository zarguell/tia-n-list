A China-nexus threat actor is targeting Myanmar government and diplomatic personnel with a custom Golang backdoor called QUICAgent, delivered via VHD files disguised as benign images. Seqrite APT Team researchers named the campaign Operation QUICSILVER. The infection chain uses Burmese-language social engineering: a fake graduation ceremony invitation impersonating Myanmar's Information Technology and Cyber Security Department under the Ministry of Transport and Communications, complete with an official-looking ministry seal.

The technical execution is multi-stage. The initial VHD container, named to look like a JPEG (e.g., TrainingAnnouncement.jpg), mounts to expose an LNK file. That LNK triggers a split-payload reconstruction script that assembles and executes the QUICAgent implant. Researchers recovered deleted files suggesting the attackers have been iterating on their lures and infrastructure. The campaign specifically targets government and IT-sector personnel in Myanmar, consistent with Chinese APT interest in Southeast Asian diplomatic networks.

No CVEs are associated with this campaign. The threat is entirely custom malware and social engineering, not a vulnerability exploitation chain. This matters because it demonstrates continued Chinese-nexus APT investment in Myanmar-targeted operations using low-volume, highly tailored lures that would bypass signature-based detection. Watch for additional Seqrite IOCs and any expansion of the target set beyond Myanmar government entities.
