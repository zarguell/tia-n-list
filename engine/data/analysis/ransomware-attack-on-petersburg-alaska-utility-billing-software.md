The Petersburg Borough government in Alaska disclosed in an October community notice that a ransomware attack on July 11, 2026 affected its utility billing software, which is managed by the borough's technology provider, Professional Computer Solutions. Officials limited public and digital communication during the response after being advised the attackers might still be monitoring borough systems, and employees were informed through hand-delivered notices and individual conversations. The potentially exposed data is limited to customer full names and utility account numbers. The borough confirmed that credit card and banking information was not stored on the compromised network and was not accessed, and it has not confirmed whether customer records were viewed or copied. The number of affected individuals has not been disclosed.

The response has been conservative. PCS took steps to stop the attack, secure the network and investigate, and the borough notified the Federal Bureau of Investigation and the Alaska Department of Public Safety. A separate server issue delayed September utility statements, but the borough has not linked that disruption to the July intrusion. The delay between the July 11 intrusion and the October disclosure reflects a three-month investigation and the borough's assessment that the attackers could still be watching its systems.

What to watch is whether the actor claims a larger haul than names and account numbers, whether PCS discloses the ransomware family or an initial access vector, and whether other municipalities that use the same billing software are affected. Customers should be alert for phishing messages referencing their utility accounts, since the exposed names and account numbers are enough to make convincing lures, and should watch for unexpected changes on accounts.
