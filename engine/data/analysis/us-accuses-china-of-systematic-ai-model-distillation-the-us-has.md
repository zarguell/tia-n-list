The U.S. government has formally accused China of conducting systematic distillation campaigns targeting proprietary American AI models. The accusation, reported by OSINTsights and Digitimes, describes coordinated efforts by Chinese entities to extract model weights and training data through automated API abuse at industrial scale.

A companion report from Digitimes reveals that U.S. agencies have reportedly asked AI firms to silently degrade responses to traffic suspected of Chinese model harvesting. This suggests a dual-track approach: public attribution combined with quiet technical countermeasures. The request for silent degradation raises questions about service quality impacts for legitimate users who might be misidentified.

The distillation threat is real but difficult to quantify. Extracting useful model behavior through API queries requires millions of requests, leaving detectable patterns. Companies with robust rate limiting and query monitoring are better positioned to identify and block these campaigns. The policy shift toward asking firms to degrade suspect traffic rather than block it outright signals government concern about the scale of the problem and the limitations of purely technical defenses.
