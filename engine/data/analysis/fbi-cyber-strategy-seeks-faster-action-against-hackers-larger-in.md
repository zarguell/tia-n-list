The FBI released its first comprehensive public cyber strategy, reported by CyberScoop and Malware.News from the Billington Cybersecurity Summit. FBI cyber chief Brett Leatherman said the bureau wants to move faster against hackers, including enabling partners to act when the FBI is not positioned to do so. He pointed to Cyber Command and authorities in Britain and Japan as potential collaborators for quicker disruption.

Leatherman also addressed a persistent problem: private sector hesitancy to engage the FBI after breaches. Organizations operate under the false assumption that the FBI shares breach information with regulators for enforcement purposes, he said. The bureau has held outside counsel summits to clarify what it offers victims and adjusted its standards for information sharing. Leatherman specifically cited PRC-linked intrusions as cases where FBI involvement should be the default, not the exception.

The strategy signals a shift from reactive investigation toward proactive disruption and faster timelines. The private sector engagement push is notable: the FBI is explicitly trying to reduce the friction that keeps breached organizations from calling law enforcement. Whether this translates to measurably faster response times or remains aspirational depends on resourcing and execution.
