Huntress reported that an intruder used access to multiple cloud-hosted ScreenConnect panels on Wednesday to push commands to managed endpoints, installing attacker-controlled copies of the remote-management software on at least 288 computers across 48 organizations, with roughly a third of machines reached in the first 10 minutes and all of them within about 90 minutes. ConnectWise, which makes ScreenConnect, said it was investigating unauthorized access to certain partner environments and disabled five independently developed extensions across approximately 400 partner environments. The company said it found no evidence of a compromise of ConnectWise infrastructure or a vulnerability in ScreenConnect itself, attributing the activity to abuse of third-party extensions installed in partner environments.

Huntress traced the activity to the RMM+ extension, which lets other tools manage ScreenConnect sessions without using the console and bypasses user accounts, roles, and MFA by authenticating with a shared access key. In versions 1.0.31 and 1.0.32 that key defaulted to blank, effectively disabling the authentication check. Organizations using ScreenConnect should review partner-hosted panels, installed extensions, and endpoint telemetry for unauthorized ScreenConnect installs, and treat third-party extensions that bypass MFA as high-risk.
