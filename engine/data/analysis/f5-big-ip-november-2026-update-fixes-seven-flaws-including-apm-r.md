F5 issued an out-of-band BIG-IP update on November 18, 2026 with seven fixes, six of them Critical at CVSS 9.8. The issues are in the data plane, not the management interface. Cyber Centre advisory AV26-1178, dated November 19, lists BIG-IP 17.1.0 before 17.1.3.7.0.21.4-ENG, 17.5.0 before 17.5.1.11.0.61.12-ENG and 21.1.0 before 21.1.0.4.0.19.3-ENG, plus CNF 2.2.0 to 2.4.0 before their EHF1 hotfixes and BIG-IP Next for Kubernetes on the same branches. BIG-IQ Centralized Management before 8.4.2.3 is also listed.

CVE-2026-123637 is a stack-based buffer overflow in BIG-IP APM when a per-request policy uses the Client Information and Endpoint Security agents on a virtual server. Undisclosed requests can terminate the Traffic Management Microkernel and may allow unauthenticated remote code execution; F5 tracks it in K000163937. CVE-2026-121805 is the matching iRule overflow, and BleepingComputer's November 20 report lists CVE-2026-116953, CVE-2026-120285 and CVE-2026-120292 as the other critical stack overflows across APM and iRules. A high-severity APM SAML flaw, CVE-2026-126822, is an integer underflow that remote unauthenticated attackers can use for denial of service. F5 says it has no mitigation and recommends the engineering hotfixes.

Exposure depends on configuration rather than build alone. Censys counted more than 2 million web properties in its rapid response note, and Shadowserver tracks over 177,000 BIG-IP instances plus more than 14,600 IP addresses with APM fingerprints, though neither count separates patched devices or honeypots. A device is only affected by the critical triggers if it has the relevant APM per-request policy or an iRule using the affected command. Inventory those configurations first. No event in this window reports exploitation or CISA KEV listing; watch for both and for further hotfixes on BIG-IP CNF and Next for Kubernetes.
