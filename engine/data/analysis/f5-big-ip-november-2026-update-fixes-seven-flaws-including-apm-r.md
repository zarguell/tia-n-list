F5's 18 November 2026 BIG-IP update covers seven flaws. CVE-2026-123637 is an unauthenticated stack overflow in APM: when a per-request policy with the Client Information and Endpoint Security agents is applied to a virtual server, undisclosed requests terminate the Traffic Management Microkernel and may allow RCE, scored 9.3 in CVSS 4.0 and 9.8 in CVSS 3.1. Four iRule flaws share that data-plane failure, CVE-2026-120722 on DNS::scrape, CVE-2026-116953 on GENERICMESSAGE::route, CVE-2026-120292 on MR::message and CVE-2026-120285 on PSC::ip_address. CVE-2026-120718 is the control-plane exception, letting an attacker who holds an iControl REST token extend its life until the owner deletes it. StackFlag and Rapid7 mark the APM issue as not known exploited, outside CISA KEV and without an EPSS score.

The value here is the shared failure mode, not the headline count. An internet-facing virtual server that only has to parse attacker-controlled traffic can be forced into a TMM restart, giving an unauthenticated actor a denial-of-service lever and a possible code-execution foothold on a device that terminates TLS and enforces access policy. APM code execution is an identity-boundary compromise, and the token flaw means a credential stolen once can remain usable after a password reset, two outcomes a severity number tends to flatten together. The record carries a 6.8 score, one source and no victim count, so the case for priority rests on how ordinary the trigger configurations are rather than on observed attacks.

Move BIG-IP to BIGIP-21.1.0.4.0.19.3-ENG and BIG-IP Next for Kubernetes to BNK-2.4.2-EHF1, the fixed builds named in the CVE entries, and check F5 articles K000163937, K000163939, K000163940, K000163941, K000163945 and K000163929 for branch-specific hotfixes across the listed BIG-IP 21.1.0, 17.5.x and 17.1.x branches. Until patching, disable iRules calling DNS::scrape, GENERICMESSAGE::route, MR::message or PSC::ip_address and remove the Client Information and Endpoint Security agents from per-request policies on exposed APM virtual servers. Restrict iControl REST to management networks and enumerate live tokens, since the token-lifetime bug has no data-plane workaround. Hunt for TMM terminations and restarts on virtual servers running those profiles, and treat repeat crashes as suspicious until the build is confirmed.

Two developments would move this from patch triage to incident response: a public exploit, reproduction or CISA KEV listing for CVE-2026-123637 or one of the named iRule CVEs, and TMM restart telemetry or scanner fingerprints tied to the crafted requests. Current sources list no known exploitation and no KEV entry, so neither condition is met today.
