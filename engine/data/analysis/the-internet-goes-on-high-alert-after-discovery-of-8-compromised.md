Eight intermediate certificates issued by the Indian CA eMudhra had private keys that anyone can recover from the public certificate. The keys were P-384 elliptic curve values below 256, generated during a key generation ceremony on April 30, 2025, and one of them was the number 4. A later write-up notes that the values 4, 56 and 115 were all recovered from the public certificates on a laptop in under a second, which turns the incident from a slow key-grinding exercise into a trivial one for anyone holding the chain. eMudhra blamed an error in a script used for that ceremony, which produced a second set of weak keys with labels similar to the correct ones, and those weak keys were mistakenly used for signing, with some of the resulting certificates also submitted to Sectigo for cross signing. Six of the eight are TLS intermediates and two sign code, so a holder could issue certificates trusted for any domain and could sign Windows binaries with a verified publisher name on systems that skip revocation checks.

The researcher credited as Wayne reported the weakness to the Chrome Root Program on October 17 at 18:05 UTC and demonstrated possession of each key by signing messages with it. Chrome and Firefox blocked the intermediates within hours, Mozilla through OneCRL and then Firefox 158.0.1 with NSS 3.131, Apple on October 19 and Microsoft on October 20, with Sectigo revoking and Cloudflare patching its own services. The Go project assigned CVE-2026-111943 and shipped go1.27.3 and go1.26.10 on October 19, adding a denylist in crypto/x509 so certificate verification fails when one of the compromised intermediates appears in a chain. Cloudflare became aware around 17:00 UTC on October 19 and CERT-In has issued an official statement on the incident.

The remaining exposure is in software that ships its own CA bundle or ignores revocation checks, and estimates put the number of certificates that can be reissued from the compromised keys at around 30,000. Browser revocation does not reach command-line stacks: curl, node, go, python's certifi and pip, and anything with a pinned CA bundle keep trusting the compromised intermediates until the bundle updates. A community checker script now live-tests each client against the disclosure site's probe hosts, where a successful HTTPS response means the client is still vulnerable, covering curl, node, go, python, virtual environments and CA overrides in environment or config, and it only reports rather than touching any trust store. What to watch is the gap between revocation in root programs and actual trust stores in appliances, containers and private PKI deployments, whether any fraudulent certificates were actually issued under the compromised keys, and whether the same ceremony script error affected keys that have not yet surfaced.
