Dormant Bitly links embedded in old posts from McDonald's, Burger King, government agencies and politicians, some dating to 2009, were repurposed to send visitors to unrelated Wikipedia pages. The original social posts were not edited. Security Online reported the pattern across posts from Trump and others, while GBHackers documented links from Burger King, McDonald's and FBI-related posts being redirected to pages that made the brands appear to promote unrelated or offensive material. Bitly said it suspended the accounts involved and is disabling affected links.

This is a low-severity redirection incident, with no malware or credential theft reported in the available coverage. The useful lesson is that a shortened link outlives the account that created it and the context that made it look trustworthy. Archived brand and agency posts are a long-tail surface: the destination can change years later without touching the post. Watch for unexpected redirects from old social posts, check the resolved destination before clicking, and audit short-link accounts tied to institutional social media.
