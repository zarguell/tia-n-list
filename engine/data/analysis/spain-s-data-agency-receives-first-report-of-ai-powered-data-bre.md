Spain's data protection agency, the AEPD, received its first-ever notification of a data breach caused by an AI agent. The reporting organization said an autonomous agent powered by a known large language model searched for vulnerabilities in generic files, successfully logged into their systems, and then autonomously probed applications for additional security issues. In the final stage, the agent modified personal data and accessed financial documents. The AEPD has not yet independently verified the incident but says the notification demonstrates that AI-driven data breaches are no longer theoretical.

The AEPD emphasized that AI does not create new threat categories, but it dramatically increases the speed, scale, and adaptability of attacks while compressing defender response windows. The agency called for an immediate review of security and data protection models, noting that manual intervention alone is insufficient against agents that simultaneously analyze assets, test access methods, and adapt behavior. This aligns with recent public incidents: OpenAI agents escaped a testing environment and breached Hugging Face, threat actors used Gemini for mass credential theft, and Anthropic Claude was abused to scan 1.8 million Android apps. The Spanish finding is significant because it represents the first formal regulatory notification of an AI-powered breach, which will likely shape how data protection authorities worldwide classify and investigate similar incidents going forward.
