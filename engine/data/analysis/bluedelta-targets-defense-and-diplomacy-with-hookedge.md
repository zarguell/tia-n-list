Insikt Group documented BlueDelta (APT28/Fancy Bear/Forest Blizzard) running initial access campaigns from late September 2025 through early April 2026 against government and diplomatic targets in Romania, Spain, and Turkiye. The group delivered HOOKEDGE, a lightweight Windows batch-script backdoor, through macro-enabled Word documents with diplomatic lures, including material impersonating Spain's Ministry of the Presidency created shortly after a September 2025 meeting with Moldovan officials. The campaigns reflect Russian intelligence collection priorities around European governance and NATO-adjacent affairs.

HOOKEDGE operates as a polling backdoor: a scheduled task fires every 30 minutes, retrieves .cmd payloads from a webhook.site staging endpoint via headless Microsoft Edge, executes them, and exfiltrates output to a separate webhook. BlueDelta deployed a second-stage HOOKEDGE variant to high-value targets with a 5-minute beaconing interval for more responsive tasking. The two-tier architecture manages webhook.site's 100-request-per-endpoint free-tier limit, separating initial access infrastructure from active collection. The malware is a direct evolutionary successor to HEADLACE, sharing identical JavaScript payload structures, variable names, and GUID-based file naming.

The group refined HOOKEDGE across seven observed variants between September 2025 and July 2026, transitioning from diplomatic lures to generic "Enable Content" prompts, shifting Edge execution from headless to hidden window, and adding email-open canary tracking. BlueDelta administered all webhook.site endpoints from NordVPN IPs, blending C2 traffic with legitimate browsing. Detection priorities should focus on scheduled task creation from user directories, headless Edge execution with local HTML files, and outbound connections to webhook services.
