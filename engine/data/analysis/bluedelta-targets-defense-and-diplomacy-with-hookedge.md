BlueDelta, the Russian GRU-linked threat group overlapping with APT28/Fancy Bear, ran a sustained espionage campaign from September 2025 through early April 2026 against government, diplomatic, and defense manufacturing targets in Romania, Spain, and Turkey. The operation used macro-enabled Word documents with diplomatic lures (impersonating Spain's Ministry of the Presidency) to deploy HOOKEDGE, a lightweight Windows batch-script backdoor.

HOOKEDGE is an evolution of the HEADLACE backdoor previously tied to BlueDelta. Instead of traditional C2 infrastructure, it abuses legitimate services: Microsoft Edge DevTools Protocol for command execution and webhook.site for data exfiltration. This blends malicious traffic with normal network activity and reduces infrastructure overhead. The implant retrieves commands, executes them, and sends results back through these channels.

The installer chain creates a scheduled task for persistence, then deletes itself and related files to minimize forensic artifacts. Code and tradecraft overlap between HOOKEDGE and HEADLACE, consistent infrastructure patterns, and targeting aligned with known Russian intelligence priorities support the moderate-confidence attribution to BlueDelta. Organizations in the targeted sectors should review network logs for Edge DevTools Protocol and webhook.site activity, and hunt for scheduled tasks created by unknown installers.
