Houthi fighters seized Perim Island in the Bab al-Mandab Strait on September 11, according to Yemeni government officials and AFP eyewitness reports. The island sits in the middle of one of the world's most critical shipping chokepoints, connecting the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden and linking Europe to Asia via the Suez Canal. The Houthis also captured the port city of Mokha and now claim control of Yemen's entire Red Sea coast, a "lightning advance" that began on September 3.

The strategic picture is straightforward. Saudi Arabia has been routing oil exports through the Red Sea after the US-Israel war against Iran effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz earlier in 2026. The Houthis say international shipping remains safe except for Saudi-flagged vessels, which are "subject to a blockade." Satellite imagery confirms Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline connecting the Gulf to the Red Sea coast, suggesting coordinated pressure on Saudi energy infrastructure from multiple directions.

The geopolitical fallout is already visible. Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman asked Trump for direct US military action against the Houthis; Trump declined, offering intelligence and targeting support instead. The administration wants to avoid opening another front while focused on Iran. Meanwhile, 46,000 people have been displaced in the last week alone, according to the International Organization for Migration. The risk is that the Houthis, backed by Iranian weapons and technology, now hold leverage over two of the world's most important maritime chokepoints simultaneously, Hormuz from Iran's side and Bab al-Mandab from their own.
