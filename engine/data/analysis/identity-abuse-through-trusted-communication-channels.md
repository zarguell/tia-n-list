Unit 42 published research on how attackers abuse enterprise collaboration platforms for identity phishing and credential theft. The research focuses on the attack surface created when trusted internal communication channels become the delivery vector for credential harvesting.

The core finding is that collaboration tools such as Slack, Microsoft Teams, and similar platforms are increasingly used as phishing channels because messages from internal tools carry implicit trust. Attackers compromise or impersonate accounts within these environments to deliver credential-harvesting links to employees who would otherwise be suspicious of external email. The research outlines defense strategies but the event summary does not detail specific campaigns or victim counts.

This matters because enterprise collaboration tools are now mission-critical infrastructure for most organizations, yet many security stacks still treat them as lower priority than email for phishing defense. When attackers pivot to these channels, traditional email filtering and URL sandboxing provide no coverage. Organizations should audit their collaboration tool configurations for message filtering, external sender warnings, and conditional access policies that restrict cross-tenant communication.
