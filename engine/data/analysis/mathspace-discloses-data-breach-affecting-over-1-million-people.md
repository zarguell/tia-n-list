Mathspace, a Sydney-based online maths learning platform used by thousands of schools across Australia, New Zealand, the US, and the UK, has disclosed a breach affecting 1,079,819 people. Attackers exploited a vulnerability in Mathspace's self-hosted Metabase reporting system to obtain administrator access without credentials. The company confirmed unauthorized access on September 3, 2026, but the intrusion began on August 10 and data was exfiltrated from the Australian reporting database on August 27.

The stolen records include names and personal information of students, their parents or guardians, teachers and school staff, and Mathspace employees. Only Australian and New Zealand users were affected in the data theft. Mathspace reported 3,432 Australian schools and 3,557 international schools using the platform as of 2023, so the education sector footprint is substantial.

The Metabase vulnerability is the focal point here. Metabase is an open-source business intelligence tool widely deployed for internal analytics. Organizations running self-hosted instances should verify they are on a patched version, since the credential-bypass flaw gave the attackers full admin access without needing to authenticate. This breach fits the pattern of attackers targeting internal reporting and analytics tools that often sit outside the primary security perimeter but hold sensitive data.
