Mathspace, a Sydney-founded online maths learning platform used by thousands of schools in Australia, New Zealand, the US, and the UK, disclosed that attackers stole data on more than 1 million students, staff, and parents. The breach targeted a self-hosted Metabase internal reporting system. CTO Alvin Savoy said attackers exploited a security vulnerability in Metabase to obtain administrator access without a legitimate login.

The intrusion began August 10, 2026. Attackers downloaded the data from Mathspace's Australian reporting database on August 27, and the company confirmed the breach on September 3. Only Australian and New Zealand student and school staff records were stolen in the incident, along with parent and guardian information and Mathspace staff records. The company said US and UK data was not affected.

The five-source coverage across BleepingComputer, Malware.news, GBHackers, CyberPress, and SecurityWeek reflects the breadth of impact across the education sector. Schools using Mathspace should notify affected families and review whether the Metabase instance or any connected systems need additional hardening. The incident is a reminder that internal reporting tools, often deployed with elevated database access, are high-value targets when left internet-facing or unpatched.
