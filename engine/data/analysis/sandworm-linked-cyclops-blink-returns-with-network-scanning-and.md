Sophos CTU researchers identified a new 64-bit Linux variant of the Cyclops Blink malware deployed against Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center (FMC) appliances. The implant adds active internal-network scanning and programmable packet-capture capabilities on top of the modular architecture seen in earlier versions. Cyclops Blink has been linked to the Sandworm group (also tracked as IRON VIKING and Seashell Blizzard) with high confidence, and the campaign targets network infrastructure that sits upstream of segmentation boundaries.

The new variant matters because it signals a shift from opportunistic device infection to purpose-built network reconnaissance. FMC appliances manage firewall policies across an entire organization; an attacker who controls one can alter rules, extract configs, and pivot into protected segments without triggering host-based alerts. The packet-sniffing feature lets the operator harvest credentials and session tokens directly from management traffic. Cisco FMC is widely deployed in enterprise and government environments, so the blast radius of a successful compromise is large. Operators should verify FMC integrity, check for unauthorized Groovy or Lua plugins, and review firewall rule-change logs for unexplained modifications since mid-August 2026.
