Google pushed Chrome 152.0.7977.64/.65 to stable channels across Windows, macOS, and Linux, packing 327 security fixes. Ten of those are rated Critical, with the rest spanning high and medium severity. The bulk of the fixes address memory-safety bugs across V8, Blink, and other core components. CVE identifiers in this batch include CVE-2026-19556 through CVE-2026-19560 and CVE-2026-76017 through CVE-2026-76023, plus CVE-2026-79282. Multiple sources flagged at least two of these as particularly dangerous: both can be triggered by simply visiting a malicious page, no user interaction beyond navigation required.

The scale of this release is notable. 327 fixes in a single stable update is above Chrome's typical cadence, suggesting either a backlog of responsibly disclosed bugs reaching disclosure or a particularly productive fuzzing cycle. The Critical-rated vulns likely include type confusions and heap buffer overflows in V8 and rendering, the classes that routinely land in the "exploit chain" category for sandbox escapes. Chrome's site isolation architecture limits blast radius, but a Critical RCE in the renderer is still a high-priority patch.

This update follows Chrome 151 (released August 11) which fixed five high-severity use-after-free bugs. The rapid succession of two major patches in two weeks reinforces the pattern: Chromium's attack surface keeps expanding, and the pace of vulnerability discovery is not slowing. defenders should treat this as a forced-update situation, especially for environments where browsers are internet-facing or handle sensitive sessions.
