Google released Chrome version 152 for Windows, macOS, and Linux, patching 327 security vulnerabilities including 10 rated Critical. The stable-channel update ships as version 152.0.7977.64 for Linux and 152.0.7977.64/.65 for Windows and macOS. The sheer volume of fixes is driven primarily by memory-safety issues across Chrome's rendering engine and media components.

The 10 Critical-severity flaws affect core browser functionality. While Google did not disclose detailed exploitability information for every CVE, Critical ratings in Chromium typically mean the vulnerabilities are exploitable through crafted web content without additional user interaction beyond visiting a page. The update also addresses a large batch of High-severity use-after-free and heap-buffer-overflow bugs in components like V8, WebRTC, and the shader compiler.

This is a routine but large update. Browser auto-update should push it to most users quickly, but enterprise environments with managed Chrome deployments should prioritize testing and rollout. The 327-vulnerability count reflects the scale of Chromium's attack surface and the ongoing investment in fuzzing and code auditing. No active exploitation has been reported for the Critical bugs, but the volume makes it likely that exploit chains will be developed once the details are public.
