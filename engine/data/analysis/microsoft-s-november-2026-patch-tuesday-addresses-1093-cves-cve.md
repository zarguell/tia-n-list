Microsoft's November 2026 Patch Tuesday addresses 1,093 CVEs across 287 products and components, with 63 rated critical, 1,029 important, and one moderate. Elevation-of-privilege flaws account for 48.9% of the patched vulnerabilities and remote code execution for 27.1%. The single actively exploited zero-day is CVE-2026-124194, a moderate-severity Outlook spoofing flaw rated CVSS 6.5 that Microsoft added to CISA's KEV catalog. A specially crafted message file can make Outlook connect to an attacker-controlled location without the usual external-content warning, disclosing the user's NTLMv2 hash for impersonation. It does not trigger through the Preview Pane and requires the victim to open the file.

Sources disagree on the exact total, with some counting 1,094 or 1,235 CVEs depending on whether Edge and Chromium issues are included, but all agree only one flaw is under active attack and none were publicly known before release. Dustin Childs of Trend Micro's Zero Day Initiative attributes the drop in zero-days to AI being better at reversing patches than at finding bugs in closed-source software, not to less exploitation overall.

Prioritize the Outlook zero-day and any KEV-listed entries, then address the elevation-of-privilege and remote-code-execution volume across Windows and Office. Watch for follow-on exploitation of the NTLMv2 disclosure technique and for whether AI-driven patch reversal shortens the window between release and weaponization.
