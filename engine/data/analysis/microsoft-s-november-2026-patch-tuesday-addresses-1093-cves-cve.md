Microsoft's November 2026 Patch Tuesday, released November 10, addressed roughly 1,094 to 1,260 vulnerabilities depending on counting methodology, with 63 to 68 rated critical and about 1,029 important. The release covers 287 products and components, with Microsoft Office Word at 65 CVEs and Excel at 64 among the most affected; elevation-of-privilege flaws account for 48.9% and remote code execution for 27.1%. Only one vulnerability is under active attack: CVE-2026-124194, a moderate spoofing flaw in Outlook rated CVSS 6.5. A specially crafted message file can make Outlook connect to an attacker-controlled network location without the usual external-content warning, disclosing the user's NTLMv2 hash for impersonation in follow-on attacks. The Preview Pane is not an attack vector, and the flaw was privately reported by Benoit Sevens and a researcher using the moniker nono. Microsoft patched it in builds 16.0.14334.21048 for Microsoft 365 Apps for Enterprise and 16.0.17932.21140 for Office LTSC 2021 and 2024, and CISA added CVE-2026-124194 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog shortly after release. Windows 10 users on the extended security update program receive the fixes through KB5131096, which brings Windows 10 to build 19045.7846.

The volume continues Microsoft's AI-driven patch surge, following 1,411 flaws in October, with Dustin Childs of TrendAI's Zero Day Initiative noting that the shift toward fewer exploited zero-days reflects AI being better at reversing patches than finding and exploiting bugs in closed-source software. The most severe other fix is CVE-2026-116928, a Windows Search vulnerability reachable through Exchange Server. None of the patched flaws were publicly known before release. Windows accounts for 726 of the CVEs and Office for 174, with 142 Edge (Chromium-based) fixes. Qualys InstaScan surfaced customer findings 40 minutes before Microsoft's advisory page updated.

What to watch: organizations must prioritize CVE-2026-124194 because of its KEV listing and NTLMv2 disclosure potential, especially for Office LTSC and Microsoft 365 Apps deployments. Windows 10 Enterprise LTSC and ESU-enrolled systems need KB5131096. The counting discrepancy across sources, 1,094 Microsoft-listed versus 1,235 or 1,260 including earlier-month fixes, means patch totals should be compared carefully. Whether the AI-assisted discovery trend continues and whether attackers adapt to the shrinking zero-day window are the strategic questions for the coming months.
