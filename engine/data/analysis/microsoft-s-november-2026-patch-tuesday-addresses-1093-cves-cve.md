Microsoft's November 10, 2026 security release was another four-digit Patch Tuesday. Tenable counted 1,093 CVEs, 63 critical, 1,029 important and one moderate across 287 products and components; other roundups reported 1,094 Microsoft CVEs or totals above 1,200 when Chromium and other non-Microsoft identifiers were included. Elevation of privilege made up about 49 percent of the release and remote code execution about 27 percent. The counts differ by methodology, but the direction is consistent: a second consecutive month above 1,000 fixes, with Windows accounting for 726 and Office for 174 in one breakdown.

The one flaw Microsoft marked as exploited is CVE-2026-124194, a moderate Outlook spoofing bug with a CVSS score of 6.5. A crafted message file makes Outlook connect to an attacker-controlled location without the usual external-content warning and disclose the user's NTLMv2 hash. The Preview Pane is not an attack vector. Microsoft 365 Apps for Enterprise and Office LTSC 2021 and 2024 are affected, with fixes in builds 16.0.14334.21048 and 16.0.17932.21140; the fix comes through Office channels rather than the Windows cumulative update. CISA added the bug to KEV on November 10 with a November 24 federal deadline.

Other high-priority fixes include CVE-2026-116928, a CVSS 9.8 Windows Search issue reachable through Exchange Server, CVE-2026-113862, an 8.1 Windows NAT use-after-free RCE, and 8.8 RCEs in Active Directory and Exchange Server, CVE-2026-118146 and CVE-2026-118207. Three Office flaws omitted from the original drop, CVE-2026-124222, CVE-2026-124223 and CVE-2026-126526, got fixes later, and November 24 brought a new CVSS 9.8 Office advisory, CVE-2026-112732. Qualys telemetry rated about 35 percent of the November patches as low reliability, and The Register reported installation trouble with KB5132061. Watch for revised counts, out-of-band fixes and whether the Outlook hash theft is seen in incident reports.
