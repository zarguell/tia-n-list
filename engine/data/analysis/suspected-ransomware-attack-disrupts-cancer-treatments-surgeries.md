France's ANSSI is investigating a suspected ransomware attack that impaired systems at the University Hospital of Guadeloupe after the incident began on Saturday night. Staff lost access to applications and databases and the hospital activated its emergency plan. Surgeries, consultations and imaging tests were canceled through at least Friday, radiation therapy was suspended over safety concerns and Monday's chemotherapy sessions were canceled. Emergency care, dialysis and urgent cardiac procedures continued, and the hospital asked residents not to come to emergency departments unless immediate care was needed.

Investigators are examining whether the attackers entered through an unnamed IT supplier reportedly compromised several months earlier. French prosecutors have opened a criminal case. Hospital management told local media no ransom demand had arrived and said it would not pay one. No actor has claimed the attack. The hospital serves an island of about 380,000 people and reverted to pen and paper while systems were down. A French security outlet's brief report on November 25 repeats the treatment disruptions without adding a claim of responsibility, so the ANSSI and hospital statements remain the basis for the incident description. Watch for confirmation of the supplier link, restoration of radiotherapy and chemotherapy schedules, and any attribution from the criminal investigation.
