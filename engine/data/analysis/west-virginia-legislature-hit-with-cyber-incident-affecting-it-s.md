The West Virginia Legislature is responding to a cyberattack that began on Thursday and continued into Friday, taking its website offline and affecting internal IT systems. Ann Ali, deputy chief of staff for the House of Delegates, told Recorded Future News that teams contained the incident and started an investigation with outside cybersecurity specialists. Access to certain Legislature systems was temporarily disabled, and Ali said the incident appeared limited to legislative systems with no other state agencies or offices affected.

Ali did not answer whether the incident was ransomware, and no hacking group had claimed credit as of Friday. A later report described the event as a targeted cyberattack that left systems offline. The state has seen municipal ransomware cases in Beckley and Westmoreland, and Comparitech counted 132 ransomware attacks on US government bodies in the third quarter, a 91 percent increase over the same quarter a year earlier. Watch for a ransomware claim, a data exposure notice, or a timeline for restoring the legislature's website.
