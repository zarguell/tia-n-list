Two posts cite a 2026 review of the 150 most-used Chrome extensions. The finding is that 94 of them can read and change content on every page the browser opens. The earlier post adds that 12 of those extensions had not been updated for more than a year, while the newer post says 48 come from developers who are not registered.

The numbers describe permission reach, not confirmed malicious behavior. An extension with all-site access can see page content and form input, so its update cadence and publisher identity matter even when the extension itself is legitimate. The reports do not name the extensions or the registry used to judge developer registration, so the claims should be treated as a prompt to review deployed extensions rather than as an enumeration of bad actors. Watch for the underlying report to publish its methodology and the list behind those counts.
