ExfilSquad, a data-extortion group that first appeared on July 26, 2026, has leaked 382.64GB of data from 13 organizations, containing approximately 27 million records. The data includes personally identifiable information, customer support records, internal business records, and account-related details. The victims span multiple industries and were all running Microsoft Dynamics 365 CRM or ERP environments.

Researchers at Fortra assessed that the breach does not stem from a Dynamics 365 software vulnerability. Instead, the evidence points to misconfigured Microsoft Power Pages portals that allowed anonymous users to read Dataverse tables directly. There was no observed lateral movement, ransomware encryption, or traditional network intrusion. The access was to SaaS-hosted data through exposed web portals rather than compromise of each victim's internal infrastructure.

This is a configuration and permissions issue, not a platform vulnerability. Power Pages portals that expose Dataverse data without proper row-level security or authentication create an attack surface that bypasses all the network-level defenses organizations have invested in. The 13 victims likely did not realize their CRM data was publicly accessible through the portal layer. Organizations using Dynamics 365 with Power Pages should audit their portal permissions, verify that Dataverse tables are not exposed to anonymous access, and review Power Pages web configuration for any publicly readable data endpoints.
