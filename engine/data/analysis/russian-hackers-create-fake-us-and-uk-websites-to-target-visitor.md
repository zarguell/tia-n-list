A watering-hole operation attributed with moderate-to-high confidence to APT29 (Midnight Blizzard, Cozy Bear, The Dukes; linked to Russia's SVR) ran from August 27 to September 3, 2026 and reached iOS and Android visitors through three delivery paths: JavaScript injected into legitimate sites (a Ukrainian gas producer, a US chemical-industry trade association, a Bulgarian military-related site, and a far-right Czech party site), adversary-in-the-middle reverse proxies cloning america250.org at america250[.]holiday and Raffles London at The OWO at theowo[.]club, and a supply-chain compromise of the Ukrainian Plerdy SEO Tracker (a.plerdy[.]com). All three load "UltimateFingerprinter," padded with roughly 2.3 MB of dead AngularJS, which collects 394 fields across 61 collectors and gates exploit delivery on a plausible phone screen size and non-zero touch count. Chain A targets iOS 16.7 with CVE-2025-43529 and CVE-2024-27834 to steal cookies; Chain B is the full DarkSword kit targeting iOS 18.5 using CVE-2026-20700, CVE-2025-14174, CVE-2025-43510 and CVE-2025-43520 to harvest messages, WhatsApp and Telegram databases, Keychain secrets, Wi-Fi passwords, location caches, photos and iCloud Drive; Chain C hits Android Chrome 139-145 (excluding 143) via CVE-2026-3910 and CVE-2025-27038, delivering AndroSnitch to exfiltrate Chrome cookies, saved logins, history and Wi-Fi configuration. All chains exfiltrate to ad.starx-net[.]com:8881/telemetry/v12 and establish no persistence.

The scope is the point: unlike APT29's 2024 Mongolian watering hole, which only stole cookies from a hard-coded list of webmail and login sites, this campaign collects any cookie and harvests the entire device, and it reached sites serving military and defence-equipment pages through a tracker used by thousands of sites. The DarkSword chain is the same family Google TAG documented in March 2026 as used by UNC6353 and commercial surveillance vendors, so a state-aligned operator is reusing a commoditized kit rather than burning private zero-days; every vulnerability in all three chains was already patched before the campaign, meaning fully updated devices are not exposed and the at-risk population is unpatched phones. Stolen session cookies let operators reuse authenticated sessions into webmail without passwords, which is the realistic payoff for an SVR-linked actor focused on Ukraine and the US, with a far-right Czech party site among the compromises matching APT29's history of targeting European political parties. The operation is intelligence collection, not disruption: no persistence, a single telemetry endpoint, and a device-model kill switch (SM-S938B) in the Android chain all point to selective, quiet harvesting.

Defenders should treat this as a patch-and-hunt problem: update iOS to at least 26.3 (Apple patched all DarkSword flaws by 26.3) and Chrome past 145, and block or monitor theowo[.]club, america250[.]holiday, static.flux-telemetry[.]com, edge-ord.clouds-stacks[.]com, adsprism-stat[.]com and ad.starx-net[.]com, plus the VDGH_TOKEN_REF cookie marker. Network teams should review any use of the Plerdy tracker (a.plerdy[.]com) on externally facing sites, since the August 27 to September 3 compromise turned a common analytics include into an exploit loader. On endpoints, hunt for UltimateFingerprinter or the AngularJS-padded script, the ad.starx-net[.]com:8881/telemetry/v12 POST, and on Android the AndroSnitch payload pulling Chrome cookies, saved logins, history and Wi-Fi configuration. Google recommends Lockdown Mode where updates are impossible; Sekoia is working with affected countries' CERTs on victim identification.

Watch for follow-up reporting naming the specific victim organizations identified through the CERT coordination, any expansion of the Plerdy compromise window beyond August 27 to September 3, and whether the same infrastructure (starx-net, flux-telemetry, clouds-stacks) reappears in a new campaign now that the domains are burned. A shift from n-day to zero-day delivery, or a move to persistence on the device, would materially raise the assessment beyond espionage collection.
