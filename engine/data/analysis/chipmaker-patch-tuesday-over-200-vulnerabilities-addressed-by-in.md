Intel and AMD disclosed more than 200 vulnerabilities across over 50 advisories on November 11, 2026. Intel's 24 advisories cover just over 100 flaws, three rated critical and twenty high severity. The largest is a thirty-hole advisory for the TDX Migration Trust Domain (MigTD) reference software, including three critical authentication issues exploitable for privilege escalation; CVE-2026-61887 (CVSS 9.4) could also allow remote code execution through a missing authentication step in the MigTD SPDM rebind implementation. Intel also patched high-severity privilege escalation in Slim Bootloader, the UEFI firmware of the D50DNP and M50FCP server boards, and VTune Profiler, plus denial-of-service flaws in PROSet/Wireless WiFi and Bluetooth products and the Performance Counter Monitor software. AMD's 29 bulletins and two security briefs cover roughly 100 unique CVEs, none rated critical, led by a forty-four-issue bulletin affecting Radeon and Instinct graphics products (ten of them high severity), plus high-severity UEFI, Secure Debug Unlock, IOMMU, and System Management Mode flaws.

The argument is exposure, not novelty. These are firmware and confidential-computing flaws in the substrate every workload trusts: a MigTD authentication bypass affects the migration path for trusted domains, and AMD's graphics and SMM issues sit below the operating system where endpoint tooling is blind. The blast radius is defined by the hardware footprint of TDX-enabled Xeon platforms and Radeon and Instinct fleets, and the patch gap is the real risk because AMD states some mitigations will not ship until 2027 while Intel has already discontinued BigDL with no fix. That makes this a supply-chain and lifecycle story rather than a routine Patch Tuesday: defenders inherit unpatched silicon for months, and the critical MigTD issue is reachable by a network-adjacent unauthenticated attacker with no user interaction.

Apply the Intel mitigations now: update MigTD to the v0.7.2 reference release published November 10, patch Slim Bootloader, the D50DNP and M50FCP UEFI firmware, and VTune Profiler, and update PROSet/Wireless WiFi, Bluetooth, and Performance Counter Monitor components. Remove BigDL entirely, since Intel will not patch its three medium-severity privilege escalation bugs. On the AMD side, apply bulletin AMD-SB-6028 for Radeon and Instinct graphics, the UEFI and SMM firmware updates, the AMD Management Console fix for the missing-authentication flaw that exposes stored credentials, and the SEV and EDK II updates carrying the TSA mitigations. Because neither vendor names log paths, telemetry fields, or indicators of compromise, detection has to lean on TDX migration and attestation audit records, firmware version inventories, and unexpected SMM or management-console access rather than signature-based hunting.

Watch whether CVE-2026-61887 or the other two critical MigTD authentication issues appear in CISA KEV or in GreyNoise and Shodan telemetry, since a network-adjacent unauthenticated path makes them attractive once proof-of-concept code circulates. Also watch AMD's delivery dates for the updates deferred into 2027 and whether downstream OEM firmware actually incorporates the EDK II TSA mitigations, because the patch gap, not the disclosure, is what turns these into incidents.
