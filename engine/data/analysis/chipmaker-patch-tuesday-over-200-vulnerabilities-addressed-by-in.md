Intel and AMD together published more than 50 new security advisories covering over 200 vulnerabilities, with fixes available for most but not all of them. Intel's 24 advisories describe just over 100 flaws: three rated critical and 20 rated high severity. The largest covers 30 holes in the TDX Migration Trust Domain (MigTD) reference software, including three critical authentication issues that can be exploited for privilege escalation, one of which, CVE-2026-61887, could also allow remote code execution. High-severity privilege escalation flaws were fixed in Slim Bootloader, the UEFI firmware of the D50DNP and M50FCP server boards, and VTune Profiler, while denial-of-service issues were fixed in PROSet/Wireless WiFi and Bluetooth products and Performance Counter Monitor. Three medium-severity privilege escalation bugs in the discontinued BigDL product will not be patched, and Intel advises uninstalling or discontinuing use.

On the AMD side, some mitigations were released months or even years ago and others are still in progress, and Intel has discontinued one impacted product. Administrators should inventory affected Intel and AMD platforms, apply the available firmware and software updates, and plan retirement for discontinued components such as BigDL where no fix will ship.
