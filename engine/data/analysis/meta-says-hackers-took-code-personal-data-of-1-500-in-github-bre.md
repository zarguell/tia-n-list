Meta is notifying about 1,500 people, mostly US employees, after attackers used a stolen employee credential to get into company GitHub repositories. The intrusion was detected weeks ago, notifications started October 13, and Meta brought in GitHub and law enforcement while cutting off the attackers' access. Meta says 99 percent of what was taken was business data in the form of product source code, and the remaining 1 percent was personal data that should never have been in the repositories under company policy.

Meta's framing is notably calming: much of the apparent personal data was synthetic or anonymized test data, and investigators do not believe Meta was specifically targeted. Still, source code theft from a company of this size invites scrutiny over what exactly was taken. Watch for regulatory filings that may specify the data types, any leak or sale of the code, and whether the stolen credential traces back to infostealers or phishing.
