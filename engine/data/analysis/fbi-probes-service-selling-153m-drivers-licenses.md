A dark web service called "Nexus" surfaced on the Russian cybercrime forum Exploit on August 31, offering digital scans of over 153 million US and Canadian drivers licenses, plus more than 10 million ID cards, three million travel documents, and 579,000 medical cards. The dataset appears to originate from a Louisiana-based identity verification company, based on interviews with affected individuals. KrebsOnSecurity first identified the service after its operator offered the reporter's own Virginia drivers license as a free sample.

The FBI's New Orleans field office opened a formal investigation into the source of the images. Among the documents available for sale are those belonging to high-ranking US government officials, including Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. The breadth of the dataset, spanning multiple document types and tens of millions of identities, suggests a systemic compromise of an identity verification pipeline rather than a scattered collection of breaches.

This is a major identity theft infrastructure event. The sale of high-resolution government-issued ID scans at this scale enables downstream fraud including synthetic identity creation, account takeover, and deepfake-assisted social engineering. The Louisiana connection points to a specific vendor in the identity verification supply chain. Watch for the FBI to name the compromised company, and for downstream fraud campaigns exploiting this dataset.
