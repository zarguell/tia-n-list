US authorities seized the domain of Virtualine, a Russian-speaking bulletproof hosting provider used for ransomware, malware hosting, data theft, and a large-scale sextortion scheme. The Justice Department said Virtualine facilitated prolific criminal activity, including the distribution of child sexual abuse material. Blockchain analysis by TRM Labs showed Virtualine received the highest illicit-attributed value among tracked bulletproof hosts in 2026, with a Russian drug marketplace accounting for nearly 44 percent of direct payments and sextortion schemes nearly 17 percent.

The seizure is part of a broader effort to disrupt the infrastructure that supports organized cybercrime rather than only prosecuting individual actors. Because bulletproof hosts provide resilient hosting for malware and command-and-control, removing them can temporarily disrupt multiple criminal operations. However, the distributed nature of such services means replacement providers often emerge quickly, so the tactical benefit is primarily time-limited disruption combined with intelligence collection.
