On October 27, 2026, the FBI seized the domain of Virtualine, a Russian-speaking bulletproof hosting provider advertised on cybercrime forums, following an investigation involving the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of Virginia, Germany's Polizei Hessen, the Netherlands' Politie Midden-Nederland, the U.K.'s National Crime Agency, and private partners OverRIPE and Lumen. Virtualine facilitated ransomware, data theft, malware hosting, money laundering, phishing, spamming, carding, and port scanning, and the Justice Department said it knowingly hosted a forum distributing child sexual abuse material, ignoring repeated notices from NCMEC, law enforcement, and a victim's attorney. After legal pressure against that forum increased, Virtualine allegedly helped its administrators stand up a new site that remains hosted and has already received at least one abuse complaint. The service also operated under the aliases Secury, Massgod, Spamhouse, Lanedo, and Omegatech, and promised to shield customers from law enforcement and abuse complaints.

TRM Labs reported that Virtualine received the most illicit-attributed value in 2026 to date among tracked bulletproof hosts, with a Russian drug marketplace accounting for roughly 44 percent of direct payments, a sextortion scheme for nearly 17 percent, a cryptocurrency mixer for almost 13 percent, ransomware for nearly 5 percent, and malware for 2 percent. Chainalysis noted the service accepted direct bitcoin payments through unhosted wallet addresses. Although not among the largest by total inflows, its concentration of illicit-attributed value made it a priority target.

What to watch: whether the seizure disrupts the downstream criminal services that depended on Virtualine, and whether the operator or customers reconstitute on replacement infrastructure. Defenders and threat researchers should track any successor hosting provider and update blocklists and intelligence feeds accordingly.
