Researchers from Blackpoint Cyber and GreyNoise have documented a suspected Russian-speaking threat actor who used AI agents at scale to exploit PaperCut NG/MF vulnerabilities CVE-2026-81578 (authentication bypass) and CVE-2026-82078 (remote code execution). The campaign, originating from IP 45.142.193[.]132, compromised at least 440 PaperCut instances across 395 identified victim organizations in 48 countries, primarily targeting the education sector in the US, UK, France, Spain, Canada, and others.

What distinguishes this campaign is the attacker's use of AI as the core operational architecture. The actor built a lab environment with vulnerable PaperCut and Active Directory, then used OpenAI Codex and a DeepSeek model to automate the full attack lifecycle: vulnerability research, exploit development, target identification via Netlas.io scanning, filtering by country (with a 28-country exclusion list that notably includes Russia and China), and post-exploitation tasks. Open-source tools Mimikatz, SharpHound, Certipy, Rubeus, and Impacket were integrated into the AI-driven pipeline.

The speed is alarming. GreyNoise reports the attacker went from an empty workspace to first achieving RCE against a real victim in under four hours. Once the campaign began, 11 organizations were compromised in 26 seconds. In one case targeting a US high school, the time from initial access to full domain administrator access was seven minutes. However, domain admin was achieved against only 12 of the 440 victims.

Blackpoint's analysis of recovered source code shows the AI workflow handled everything from comparing patched and unpatched PaperCut builds to managing retry waves for failed targets. The tools Hindsight (persistent memory for AI agents) and AionUi (graphical workspace for concurrent AI agents) supported the operation. The attacker's end goals remain unclear, with GreyNoise noting uncertainty about whether this is access brokering for later handoff or direct monetization through data theft or ransomware.
