Rakuten Securities, one of Japan's largest online brokers, said login IDs and branch codes for up to 11.25 million accounts may have been exposed in an incident this month. The initial report said data held in a test environment may have been taken out. Japan Times reporting on 18 November put the affected population at 11.25 million accounts and described the event as unauthorized access.

The exposed fields are account identifiers and branch codes rather than a confirmed password dump, but they are enough to make targeted phishing against brokerage customers credible. A caller or email that can name a customer's login ID and branch can impersonate the firm convincingly, especially when the recipient has no warning that the test environment was touched. The coverage does not attribute the access to an actor and does not say that passwords or full personal records were in the removed data, so the practical risk sits in follow-on lures and account takeover attempts that reuse these identifiers.

Watch for the company's follow-up on exactly what the test environment contained, whether every one of the 11.25 million accounts is in scope, and whether customer notifications have gone out. Defenders with Japanese retail trading customers should look for Rakuten-themed login pages and unexpected login or password-reset activity tied to those accounts, and treat any message that quotes a branch code as suspicious until verified through the broker's own channel.
