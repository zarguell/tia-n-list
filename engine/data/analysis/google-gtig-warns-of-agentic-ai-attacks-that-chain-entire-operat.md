Google Threat Intelligence Group published an analysis on September 8 describing the shift from isolated prompt-based AI attacks to agentic systems that autonomously chain together multiple stages of an operation with minimal human input. One documented pipeline was assembled and launched in under six hours. After compromising a cloud resource, the attacker deployed a multi-agent system that autonomously scanned for vulnerable services, harvested credentials, corrected its own errors, and rotated IP addresses. The reconnaissance panel processed over 23,800 stolen secrets, including API keys for cloud and AI services.

GTIG also identified UNC6780 concealing malicious instructions within project configurations and JavaScript comments, using prompt injection to coerce AI coding assistants into executing arbitrary commands or to evade LLM-based code scanners. This mirrors a separate ReversingLabs finding where Claude Opus was manipulated into co-authoring a commit that introduced a malicious dependency into a cryptocurrency project. The group further documented AI systems becoming targets themselves, with Mandiant investigating thefts of models, source code, prompts, and research across multiple industries, and Google observing distillation campaigns exceeding 100 million requests through thousands of compromised accounts.

LLMjacking, the abuse of cloud-hosted AI services for costly computation, is also growing. In one case, an attacker used an exposed GitHub token to enable Gemini Enterprise, create a service account with Editor privileges, and request additional GPU quotas on NVIDIA RTX 6000. Underground marketplace prices for AI-service accounts more than doubled in 2026. GTIG notes that fully autonomous attacks against real targets have not yet been observed, but the speed at which attackers convert published CVEs into working exploits and bind models to offensive tooling continues to accelerate.
