A massive breach of Denmark's Central Person Register (CPR) exposed approximately 8.8 million records, linked to weak credentials at the IT firm Pays ApS. At least three accounts, including an administrator account, used the password "123456". The breach demonstrates that basic credential hygiene failures at a single third-party vendor can expose national-scale identity data.

CPR numbers are persistent national identifiers; exposure creates long-term identity fraud risk rather than temporary account compromise. The incident highlights supply-chain risk in government identity systems where access is delegated to small IT contractors. Denmark's data protection authority and affected agencies should expect sustained follow-on fraud attempts.

No CVE applies; the root cause is poor credential management, not a software vulnerability. Monitor for victim notifications, official breach scope updates, and potential regulatory action against the contractor. Organizations managing identity data through vendors should enforce multi-factor authentication and periodic credential audits.
