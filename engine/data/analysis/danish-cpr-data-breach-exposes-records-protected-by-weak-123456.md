Denmark’s Central Person Register (CPR) breach exposed names, addresses, and CPR numbers for approximately 8.8 million registered citizens, roughly 80 percent of the register’s 11 million records. The incident was not a technical compromise of the registry itself but an abuse of a private firm’s legitimate search access. At least three user accounts at the Odense-based IT company Pays used the weak password “123456”, including an administrator account.

Authorities revoked the company’s access, reported the incident to the Danish Data Protection Agency, and opened a police investigation. The exposure raises risks of targeted phishing, social engineering, impersonation, and identity fraud, and can enrich data from other breaches.

What to watch: whether Danish authorities publish a forensic timeline identifying exactly when the unauthorized access began and ended, and whether Pays faces regulatory penalties or criminal charges related to the weak credential practices. The scale of the exposure exceeds Denmark’s current population, which highlights how centralized government registers amplify breach impact.
