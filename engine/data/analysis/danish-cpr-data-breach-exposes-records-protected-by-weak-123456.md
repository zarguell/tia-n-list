Denmark's Central Register of Persons, the CPR, suffered a major data breach that exposed roughly 8.8 million personal records. A Politiken investigation traced the compromise to Pays ApS, a small IT company in Odense, where at least three accounts, including an administrator account, used the password 123456.

The person claiming responsibility for the breach says that trivially weak password was all it took to get in, and coverage from the Copenhagen Post, GBHackers, and Tom's Hardware repeats the 123456 detail. Danish authorities' own register data was reachable through a small vendor with default-grade credential hygiene, which is the whole failure in one sentence.

Breach scale makes this national news, but the durable lesson is vendor access control: a government-grade dataset was one guessable password away from public exposure through a contractor. Watch for Danish regulator findings on Pays ApS, any confirmation of how the data is being redistributed, and fallout for the CPR's data-sharing arrangements with small IT suppliers.
