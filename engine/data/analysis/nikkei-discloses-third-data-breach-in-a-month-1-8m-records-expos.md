Nikkei Inc. disclosed its third data breach in roughly a month, this time exposing 1.83 million personal information records. Attackers used stolen credentials to access a development system inside Nikkei's network during the early morning hours of October 7, which exposed personal data the company stored in the cloud. Nikkei revoked the abused credentials and notified Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission on October 11, and as of October 16 had found no evidence that the accessed data was misused or leaked online and no signs of broader system compromise.

The bulk of the exposure is low-sensitivity: 1.68 million records contain no identifiers, and no credit card information was exposed. The sharper concern is the 150,000 records that include at least one identifier such as full names, email addresses, physical addresses, telephone numbers, or dates of birth, with email addresses present in 120,000 of those records. Subsets of the data contained personally identifiable information and other sensitive details.

Nikkei is a major Japanese business news and financial information group that owns the Financial Times, with over 10,100 employees and roughly $2.5 billion in annual revenue. Three breaches in a month suggests a recurring access problem rather than a one-off incident, likely tied to credential hygiene and development-system exposure. Watch for whether the company confirms scope, whether the stolen credentials trace to a shared or reused secret, and whether any of the 150,000 identifiable records surface in criminal markets.
