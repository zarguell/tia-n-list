Nikkei Inc. disclosed a third data breach within about a month, exposing 1.83 million personal information records. The incident, discovered in early October, involved stolen credentials used to access a development system during early morning hours on October 7. According to the company's notice, 150,000 records contained identifying data such as full names, email addresses, physical addresses, telephone numbers, and dates of birth; email addresses appeared in 120,000 records. The remaining 1.68 million records contained no identifiers, and no credit card information was exposed. Nikkei revoked the abused credentials, notified Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission on October 11, and reported no evidence of misuse or online leakage as of October 16.

This is the third breach Nikkei has reported recently, raising questions about recurring access control failures in its development environment. The company employs over 10,100 people and holds major media assets including the Financial Times. No CVE or specific vulnerability was named, and the attack vector was credential theft rather than an unpatched software flaw. Organizations with similar cloud-hosted development systems should review credential rotation and multi-factor access controls for development environments that hold production data.
