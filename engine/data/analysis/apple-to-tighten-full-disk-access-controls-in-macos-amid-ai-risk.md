Apple is changing a consent model, not patching a vulnerability, and no CVE is attached to this story. On October 2, 2026 the company said Full Disk Access largely sidesteps macOS privacy controls so backup apps can work, and that some developers use it to expose files, mail, messages and browsing history. The concrete design landed in macOS 27.3 beta 2 on November 16: Device Control and Data Access splits into System & App Protection Override and Accessibility, with app-by-app selection, folder-scoped Full Disk Access guidance, an ask every time option, and a transparency indicator when one app controls another.

The risk is standing consent, not a remote exploit. The Muse messages dispute of September 30 and the October 2 WIRED report on Patrick Wardle's patched ChatGPT Mac app flaw both turned on broad local access, and OpenAI acknowledged that flaw and its fix on September 25. Apple says all app permissions previously granted remain in effect, and known apps keep their existing experience without an entitlement. Beta 2 does not retroactively shrink access on a Mac that already granted Full Disk Access, so machines can look current on macOS 27.3 while retaining grants that reach messages and browsing history. The privacyguides post added on November 20 restates that risk without new release detail.

Treat this as a permission audit with a beta test attached. Check Full Disk Access under Apple menu, System Settings, Privacy & Security, remove agents that do not need whole-disk reads, and narrow remaining grants to specific folders or apps under System & App Protection Override. Enable ask every time for high-risk agents and watch the controlling-app indicator in beta pilots. The assessment changes if general availability notes narrow the known-app exemption, make pre-existing grants expire, or show a documented data read through an unchanged grant.
