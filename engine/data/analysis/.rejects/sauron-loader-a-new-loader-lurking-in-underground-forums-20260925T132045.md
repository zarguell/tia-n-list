DCSO describes Sauron Loader, a previously undocumented malware family found as the final stage in real incidents against multiple organizations in Germany. It is sold as malware-as-a-service in the Russian-speaking underground by an actor using the alias S4ur0n, and the seller restricts sales exclusively to Russian-speaking cybercriminals.

Delivery relies on social engineering in two forms: ClickFix-style lures and voice phishing where attackers spam employees, then call while posing as IT support. In one incident the caller walked the victim into a Microsoft Quick Assist session. The observed chain used a malicious MSI installer dropping files into C:\ProgramData\keyroll: a legitimate rnpkeys.exe that side-loads a malicious rnp.dll, which in turn loads tdwp.dll to decrypt the loader in memory, with deliberate execution delays to hinder automated analysis. Persistence comes from a scheduled task named keyroll, and the loader registers infected systems with its embedded C2 addresses.

Watch for Sauron Loader spreading beyond its current customer base, since exclusive Russian-language distribution typically erodes once the tool is public. The combination of vishing plus Quick Assist is the actionable part for defenders: lock down or monitor remote-assistance tools, warn staff about unsolicited IT support calls, and add detections for the keyroll artifacts and side-loading chain that DCSO published.
