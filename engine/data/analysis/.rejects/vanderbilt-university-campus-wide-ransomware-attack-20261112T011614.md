Vanderbilt University in Nashville is responding to a campus-wide ransomware incident claimed by the BYOD ransomware gang, which added the university to its leak site early Monday. BYOD claims it encrypted the university's databases and holds more than one million Social Security numbers along with full names, email addresses, phone numbers, student and employee records, and domain controller access. The university warned that devices, systems, and cloud-based services may be impacted, and told students, faculty, and staff to shut down university devices and disconnect from campus Wi-Fi. VUIT staff reportedly went into classrooms Monday morning to warn faculty that school-issued computers were not safe to use, and the AlertVU campus warning went out at 11:45 a.m.

Vanderbilt serves roughly 14,000 students and more than 6,000 staff, although the university has not confirmed whether any data was stolen. Vanderbilt University and Vanderbilt Health are legally and financially separate, so the hospital was not affected. By Tuesday morning select systems including campus Wi-Fi, Okta, Outlook, Brightspace, Zoom, and YES were restored, while others such as CampusPrint remained restricted. BYOD emerged in September and its first victim appeared on September 29; it has since claimed attacks on academic and health care institutions including the University of New Mexico Health Sciences Center. Watch for confirmation of data exfiltration, any ransom demand, and whether the group publishes stolen records from the claimed 1 million SSNs.
