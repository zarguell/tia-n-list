Canadian cybersecurity executive Edward Dubrovsky, 54, was arrested in Pennsylvania on conspiracy and Hobbs Act extortion charges tied to the ShinyHunters group that recently breached FBI systems. Court records show he appeared in the Eastern District of Pennsylvania before being transferred to Texas, where charges were filed, and he remains in custody. The complaint is sealed; the docket lists conspiracy to threaten information confidentiality with intent to extort and interference with commerce by threats.

Dubrovsky co-founded CYPFER, a ransomware-negotiation firm, and is linked to CyberSteward. Multiple sources — KrebsOnSecurity, The New York Times, Politico — place him at the Cyber Risk Summit in Philadelphia (Oct. 5-7) when the arrest occurred. FBI Director Kash Patel announced the arrest as "another suspected co-conspirator of the ShinyHunters group" without naming the suspect. The case aligns with the ongoing FBI crackdown on ShinyHunters after the FBI Jobs hack exposed personal data on thousands of agents.

What to watch: whether the unsealed complaint confirms Dubrovsky's role as a primary co-conspirator in the FBI breach, and any additional arrests from the centralized Texas-based investigation.
