DragonForce ransomware deployed Backdoor.Turn, a custom Go-based RAT that tunnels command-and-control through Microsoft Teams TURN relay infrastructure, making C2 traffic appear as legitimate Teams traffic. Symantec documented the attack against a major U.S. services company; the malware uses an anonymous Teams visitor token, connects through a legitimate Microsoft TURN relay during setup, then reaches attacker-controlled C2. The intrusion also used BYOVD with drivers from Huawei, Topaz Antifraud, and K7 Security to gain kernel privileges and disable security tools. Capabilities include command execution, LDAP and Active Directory search, TLS certificate capture, and browser credential theft.

This is the first reported in-the-wild malware to abuse Teams TURN relays for stealth C2. Defenders relying on network telemetry tied to Microsoft infrastructure alone will miss it. Watch for any Symantec follow-up with broader victim scope and Microsoft guidance on detecting malicious Teams TURN use.
