Sony Network Communications suffered unauthorized access to its So-net and plala (S Course) member database on October 19, 2026, with detection on October 23 and a public disclosure on October 27. The breach exposed 2,585,481 customer records — 2,141,986 from So-net and 443,495 from Plala — plus passwords for 54,401 email accounts across four mail services (Mail Service G, A-Box, So-net VAIO Mail, and PostPet). No banking or payment card data was taken, but names, dates of birth, gender, phone numbers, user IDs, and connection IDs for roughly 293,000 former subscribers (those who left in March 2026 or later) were included, which makes post-breach identity fraud and targeted phishing against retained billing records a real exposure rather than a historical nuisance.

This matters because the 54,401 email passwords are active credentials, not just contact details, and the retention of 293,000 former-customer records shows the database scope exceeds live subscribers. The attacker had four days of undetected access before server shutdown and traffic blocking on October 23, which suggests the intrusion was not an instant smash-and-grab but a sustained database read — a distinction that should push defenders to assume broader data exfiltration than the disclosed counts. Among the many October breach disclosures, this one stands out for the combination of scale (2.58 million records at a single Japanese ISP subsidiary) and credential exposure (54,401 passwords), not just PII leakage.

Defenders should enforce the forced password resets Sony began on October 27 for all Mail Service G, A-Box, So-net VAIO Mail, and PostPet accounts, and treat the 54,401 email-password pairs as confirmed IoCs requiring rotation on any reused services. Network monitoring should focus on the October 19 to October 23 window for persistent access indicators, since the company shut down targeted servers and blocked external attacker communication only after confirmation; log paths and telemetry fields mentioned in the disclosure center on the So-net member database server and blocked external traffic channels. Customers should be alerted to phishing campaigns impersonating NTT Docomo d POINT Club and Universal Studios Japan campaigns, which the company specifically warned about in its notification.

Watch for confirmation of whether any of the 54,401 email passwords have appeared in active credential-stuffing campaigns or grey-market listings, which would upgrade the incident from a disclosed database breach to an actively exploited account-takeover event. Also monitor whether NTT Docomo, which contracts Sony Network Communications for Docomo Hikari services through the affected Plala infrastructure, reports secondary impact beyond the 443,495 Plala records already confirmed.
