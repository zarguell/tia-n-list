Attackers compromised third-party operators of the country-code top-level domains for Ghana (.GH), American Samoa (.AS), and Sierra Leone (.SL) and modified authoritative DNS records. With control of DNS they satisfied certificate authority validation and obtained unauthorized HTTPS certificates for several Google domains, and affected other organizations' names in those zones. Google states its own systems were never compromised and that it has no reason to believe the issuing CAs acted improperly.

The DNS position is what makes this serious. Domain validation typically requires creating a TXT record containing a CA-provided value, so whoever edits authoritative DNS can validate any name in the zone. Valid certificates let the attacker impersonate legitimate brands and serve arbitrary content over HTTPS with no browser warnings. Google blocked the unauthorized certificates in Chrome through CRLSets and worked with the issuing authorities to revoke them, which extends protection to other clients; Certificate Transparency log review surfaced the scope.

Watch for disclosure from the three registry operators on how access was obtained, evidence of whether any counterfeit certificate was actually used against end users, and copycat activity against other ccTLDs run on the same kind of third-party infrastructure. Registry compromise turns an entire zone into a cert-minting machine, which makes CT log monitoring the practical early warning for every domain owner.
