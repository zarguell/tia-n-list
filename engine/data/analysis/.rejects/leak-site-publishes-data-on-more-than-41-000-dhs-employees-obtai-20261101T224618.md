A hacktivist group calling itself the Department of Peace published a dataset through Distributed Denial of Secrets that exposes internal records on more than 41,000 Department of Homeland Security employees and contractors. The data was obtained via a breach of an identity service used by the agency, according to the group, and covers personnel tied to covert operations, leaker investigations, deportation programs, and the failed BlueLeaks inquiry. CyberScoop verified numerous current CISA employees and former leadership in the database, which spans years and includes references to COVID-19 planning and 2023 internal summits.

The same group previously released a DHS Contracts dataset listing over 6,000 entities, including Anduril, L3Harris, Raytheon, Palantir, Microsoft, and Oracle, and claimed responsibility for the later Icepick employee-data leak in October 2026. The breach has drawn attention because it identifies officials responsible for sensitive DHS operations and was framed by the group as retaliation for stepped-up Immigration and Customs Enforcement operations. Watch for confirmation of the Okta single sign-on entry point mentioned by the group, any CISA or DHS response, and whether the data triggers further leaks or operational adjustments inside the agency.
