A Chinese-speaking actor targeted South Korean banks — Shinhan, KB Kookmin, and Hana — using the open-source ARTEX AI penetration-testing suite and Claude agents. CrowdStrike linked the campaign (late September to early October 2026) to open directories at a Hong Kong IP containing Claude session histories, ARTEX configs, and Claude memory files. The attacker used DeepSeek v4.1-flash as the primary LLM backend, supplemented with GLM-5.3 and Grok 4.6. Exfiltrated data included clients' personal data and credit card information; some systems suffered outages. The actor's infrastructure also exposed a resume and Telegram details.

Why it matters: agentic AI tools (ARTEX + Claude) are being weaponized for real financial-sector breaches at scale, not just proof-of-concept. The overlap of AI tooling, LLM APIs, and traditional banking targets blurs the line between automated pentesting and criminal operations.

What to watch: whether CrowdStrike's attribution leads to confirmed arrests or government response in South Korea; whether other ARTEX users appear in similar campaigns; and whether DeepSeek and proxy reseller xcai.pro become recurring infrastructure in such operations.
