Defense contractor ILC — combining ILC Dover and ILC Astrospace — agreed to a $13 million settlement to resolve False Claims Act allegations from the Department of the Army, Missile Defense Agency, and NASA. The government charged that between 2018 and 2024 the firm submitted false claims for contracts because it failed to meet cybersecurity obligations, including using a third-party cloud provider without proper safeguards and neglecting required security plans for covered systems.

The case underscores how contract-level cybersecurity compliance is now a direct enforcement target, not paperwork. The U.S. Attorney for Delaware emphasized that these obligations exist specifically to prevent adversaries from exploiting technical innovation, and parent company Ingersoll Rand noted it inherited the issue upon acquiring ILC Dover in June 2024.

No CVE or exploitation claim is attached, but the settlement amount ($13M) and the multi-agency scope (Army, MDA, NASA) make it a concrete procurement-security story rather than a generic compliance note. Watch for whether other defense suppliers face similar False Claims Act actions as agencies audit contract cybersecurity postures.
