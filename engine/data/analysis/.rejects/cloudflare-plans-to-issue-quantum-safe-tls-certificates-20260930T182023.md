Cloudflare said on September 30, 2026 that it plans to issue quantum-safe TLS certificates, positioning it among the first certificate authorities to commit publicly to post-quantum website authentication. The plan uses an open source platform that issues both classic TLS certificates and post-quantum Merkle Tree Certificates, free for paying and non-paying users. To bootstrap trust across the TLS ecosystem, Cloudflare is acquiring an already trusted certificate root from GlobalSign. Cloudflare engineers state that issuance has not started; the company expects to begin in the first quarter of 2027.

The hard problem is size. Quantum-resistant signatures in today's X.509 format would add roughly 40 times the data to each TLS handshake, which would break the web PKI as it works now. Merkle Tree Certificates, a design Google announced in February 2026 and has piloted with Cloudflare, compresses the proof to about 40 kilobytes, close to current handshake sizes. A certificate authority signs a single tree head that can stand for millions of certificates, and browsers mostly handle a lightweight landmark proof. The design also couples issuance with transparency logging, so transparency becomes a requirement for operation rather than a separate add-on, a lesson traceable to the 2011 DigiNotar compromise where 500 counterfeit certificates were minted and used for interception in Iran. Shor's algorithm on a mature quantum computer could forge the classical signatures in certificate chains and in certificate transparency logs, including signed certificate timestamps.

What to watch: root programs accepting Merkle Tree Certificates, browser and operating system support for landmark proofs, progress on the GlobalSign root acquisition, and Cloudflare's promised public milestones before Q1 2027 issuance. The design includes an ACME-based issuance and renewal flow and an out-of-band signature delivery path, such as browser updates, for when a server fails to serve a landmark. No operational impact exists today, but CA and browser policy decisions will land well before the first certificate is issued.
