A new variant of PamStealer, a macOS information stealer, has been documented by The Hacker News. The updated family decrypts its payload live from the command-and-control server instead of shipping it fully formed inside the dropper, and it adds multi-layer persistence to survive reboots and cleanup attempts. The JXA-based dropper has also been refreshed with a new lure and an updated delivery chain.

The live C2-side payload decryption is the notable change. Malware assembled on the attacker's server at execution time defeats static scanning of the dropper and makes network-based extraction harder, since defenders who sinkhole or block the C2 endpoint see nothing but an empty shell. Multi-layer persistence on macOS also signals a shift toward longer dwell times rather than quick credential grab-and-run operations.

The updated JXA dropper carries a new lure and delivery chain, per the reporting. Watch which lure theme the new delivery chain uses, whether the multi-layer persistence survives standard macOS cleanup guidance, and whether the live C2-side payload decryption pattern shows up in other macOS stealer families, since it materially weakens dropper-level static detection.
