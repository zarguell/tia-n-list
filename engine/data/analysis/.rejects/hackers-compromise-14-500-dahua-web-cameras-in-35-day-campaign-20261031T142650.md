A 35-day campaign dubbed CameraSwarm, running from June 17 to July 22, 2026, compromised more than 14,500 Dahua IP cameras, concentrated in Ukraine and Russia with additional victims in Mexico and Vietnam. Hunt.io reconstructed the operation after finding an unprotected HTTP directory on an attacker server holding 2,616 files across 234 folders, including scanning tools, exploitation utilities, stolen credentials, and camera snapshots. Three parallel methods drove the compromises: brute-forcing the Easy4IP service on TCP port 37777 (12,324 IP addresses), exploiting CVE-2021-33044 and CVE-2021-33045 to install a persistent backdoor account (p2pwn / p2password) on 1,923 cameras, and abusing cloud relay with serial numbers to reach 283 cameras behind NAT.

The backdoor account survives password changes and, on most firmware versions, factory resets, so affected devices remain reachable even after remediation attempts. CVE-2021-33044 and CVE-2021-33045 are authentication bypasses in multiple Dahua product families covered by advisory DHCC-SA-202106-001, affecting firmware built before June 2021 or May 2020. Watch for Dahua firmware updates, audit Easy4IP cloud registrations and serial-number exposure, and assume any camera that ran vulnerable firmware during the window is compromised.
