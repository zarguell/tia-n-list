A manually soldered hardware implant inside Ledger signers sold through Southeast Asia reseller CryptoBilis intercepts the 24-word recovery phrase shown on screen during device setup, then transmits it over LTE from a hidden microcontroller. The theft, disclosed October 9 and confirmed by Ledger on October 13, accounts for at least $86 million in cryptocurrency losses, with the company stating that all confirmed fund losses involved signers from that reseller. The implant sits inside the enclosure, reads screen data, and activates specifically when the setup phrase is displayed.

This undermines cold-storage trust at its core: users bought an offline hardware wallet specifically to isolate keys from networked attackers, yet a supply-chain modification turned the device into a remote exfiltration channel. The exposure is bounded but severe — Ledger told customers who purchased from CryptoBilis in the last 90 days to abandon the device, and anyone who completed setup has likely had their seed phrase intercepted. With the 24-word phrase in attacker hands, moving assets is not optional; it is the only remediation.

Affected users must stop using any CryptoBilis-sold signer immediately, create a new Ledger device with a fresh seed, and transfer all assets to it; Ledger explicitly advises not initiating setup on unreplaced units. Defenders should hunt for devices sold by that reseller in their environments and treat any 24-word phrase displayed on a compromised signer as fully exposed. Watch for Ledger identifying additional compromised resellers or confirming whether the implant reached markets beyond Southeast Asia, since a broader distribution chain compromise would expand exposure well past the current $86 million scope.
