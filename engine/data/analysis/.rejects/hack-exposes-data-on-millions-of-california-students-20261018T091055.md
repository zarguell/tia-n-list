A breach at testing provider ETS exposed records of millions of California public school students. The LA Times reports hackers used a compromised login against an ETS secure file transfer site, taking names, addresses, demographic details, and disability status for students across the past three school years. The incident affects current and former students statewide.

What matters is the scale and the nature of the data: disability status and demographic details are high-sensitivity fields, making this more damaging than a routine name-and-email leak. ETS operates assessment infrastructure for districts, so a single compromised credential reached a broad dataset spanning multiple years.

Watch for confirmation from the California Department of Education and any disclosure of the entry vector beyond the initial report. Check whether ETS has rotated credentials and whether any downstream district systems were accessed beyond the file transfer site.
