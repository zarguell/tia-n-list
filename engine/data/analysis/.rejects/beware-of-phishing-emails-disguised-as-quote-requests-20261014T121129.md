AhnLab SECurity intelligence Center (ASEC) documented two related phishing waves using quote and purchase-order lures. The first set disguises emails as project quote requests, attaching compressed files that prompt victims to download malicious attachments for verification. A second wave uses purchase order forms impersonating Korean company employees, delivering malicious XLS and DOCX attachments designed to trigger malware when opened. Both campaigns rely on business-context social engineering rather than generic spam templates.

The attachments include compressed files, XLS spreadsheets, and DOCX documents disguised as project materials. ASEC confirms the emails impersonate specific company employees to increase credibility. Defenders should treat unsolicited quote and purchase-order attachments as high-risk, particularly when paired with external download prompts. No CVEs are mentioned; the threat is delivery-focused malware rather than a specific vulnerability.
