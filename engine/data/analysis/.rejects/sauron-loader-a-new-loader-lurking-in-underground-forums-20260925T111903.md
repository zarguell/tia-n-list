DCSO has documented Sauron Loader, a previously unreported malware family found as the final stage in multiple real incidents against organizations in Germany. It is sold as malware-as-a-service in the Russian-speaking underground by a vendor using the alias S4ur0n, and the forum advertisement matches the samples DCSO analyzed. It is offered exclusively to Russian-speaking cybercriminals, and DCSO has published indicators and tooling on its GitHub.

Delivery relies on social engineering at the front of the chain: ClickFix-style lures and voice phishing in which victims are flooded with spam and then called by attackers posing as IT support, in one case walking the victim into a Microsoft Quick Assist session. In the examined chain, a malicious MSI installer drops a legitimate rnpkeys.exe alongside malicious rnp.dll and tdwp.dll into C:\ProgramData\keyroll. The signed executable side-loads rnp.dll, which loads tdwp.dll, which decrypts the loader in memory after a deliberate delay to frustrate sandbox analysis. Persistence is a scheduled task named keyroll; once running, the loader decrypts an embedded configuration with C2 addresses and registers the infected system with its server.

Watch two things. First, the delivery layer: Quick Assist and IT-support vishing are now standard intrusion openers in German-speaking targets, and Sauron Loader gives those operators a maintained loader backend. Second, the MaaS model means infection attribution follows the customer base rather than one group. The keyroll artifacts, the side-loading triplet, and the C2 config are the practical detection anchors.
