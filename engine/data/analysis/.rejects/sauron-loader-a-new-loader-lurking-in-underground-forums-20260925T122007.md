DCSO has analyzed a previously undocumented malware family called Sauron Loader, encountered as the final stage in attacks against multiple organizations in Germany. The malware is sold as a service in the Russian underground, advertised by a user with the alias S4ur0n, and restricted to Russian-speaking cybercriminals. Delivery chains start with social engineering: ClickFix-style lures and voice phishing in which victims were flooded with spam and then called by attackers posing as IT support, including one incident where the victim was walked into starting a Microsoft Quick Assist session.

Technically, a malicious MSI installer drops three files into C:\ProgramData\keyroll: the legitimate rnpkeys.exe, a malicious rnp.dll, and a second malicious library tdwp.dll. The signed executable side-loads rnp.dll, which loads tdwp.dll, which decrypts the loader code in memory, with deliberate execution delays likely intended to hinder automated analysis. Persistence comes from a scheduled task named keyroll, and once active the loader decrypts an embedded configuration holding command-and-control addresses, then registers the infected system with its C2.

The story matters less for novel techniques than for the marketplace signal: a curated loader offered exclusively to Russian-speaking buyers lowers the entry bar for ransomware-grade intrusions. Watch for Sauron Loader appearing outside Germany, reuse of the keyroll task and side-loading trio as detection anchors, and any infrastructure overlap with established ransomware crews. DCSO published indicators and tooling on its GitHub.
