Times Car, a Japanese car-sharing service operated by Park24 Group, confirmed that about 6.6 million current and former user accounts were breached. The intrusion began at the start of September; unauthorized access was blocked on September 26. The company confirmed on September 29 that exposed data includes full names, physical addresses, dates of birth, telephone numbers, email addresses, driver's license information, identity verification document images, account passwords (stored in an unrecoverable form), linked service IDs, and corporate members' department names. Credit card information was not affected. There is no evidence the stolen data has been distributed online. The breach affects both Times Car members and the Times Business Service corporate account program.

This is a significant data breach by scale — 6.6 million accounts — with a broad mix of PII and identity documents exposed. The fact that passwords were stored in an unrecoverable form is a mitigating control, but the exposure of driver’s license images and identity verification documents creates long-term identity fraud risk. No evidence of data distribution is a thin comfort: absence of proof is not proof of absence, and breach disclosure timelines mean the data could surface later.

Watch for follow-up reports on whether the stolen data appears in dark-web marketplaces or is used in targeted identity fraud. Monitor whether Japanese regulators issue guidance or penalties. The breach is confined to Times Mobility / Park24 Group systems, but linked service IDs may create downstream exposure if partner services reuse credentials.
