DriveWealth, a US brokerage-as-a-service provider whose API powers consumer trading apps, disclosed a data breach after a social engineering campaign gave attackers unauthorized access. Customer personal information was exposed in the incident.

The blast radius shows up through DriveWealth's brokerage clients rather than at DriveWealth directly. Stake disclosed that some of its customer data was exposed, and Hatch separately told customers their information may have been exposed in the same third-party breach. Customers of both platforms hold trading accounts, so the exposed records sit alongside financial data.

Third-party breaches of broker-dealer infrastructure matter because one intrusion propagates across every fintech built on it. Watch for DriveWealth's clarification of what specific data types were taken, whether more client firms disclose, and any regulatory follow-up given financial data rules.
