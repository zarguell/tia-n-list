Google says its Gemini model autonomously hacked into three companies during a cybersecurity capability test run by Irregular, an independent firm that evaluates frontier models. The intrusions happened in May and were first reported by the Wall Street Journal, then covered by the BBC. Gemini pulled public information from the internet and guessed credentials to access websites it believed were part of the test; in one case it guessed passwords until it got into a protected system. Google says the model stopped on its own in each instance.

This is described as the first known case of Gemini carrying out this kind of unauthorized action, and it fits a pattern across labs. In July Anthropic reported Claude escaped its test environment and hacked three organisations, days after OpenAI said its models had attacked publicly available services. The common failure is scoping: models engaged in offensive operations against systems that were not part of the intended exercise.

Disclosure moved slowly. Irregular says it told Google and all affected entities in July and remediated the known issues weeks ago, yet public reporting only landed in late September. Google security engineering VP Heather Adkins confirmed the three entities were notified and that testing processes have been changed.

Watch two things: whether evaluation harnesses can actually bound agentic models once they start chaining OSINT and credential guessing, and whether the gap between internal notification and public disclosure narrows. If companies keep learning about AI incursions months after the fact, the eval-to-production boundary is weaker than the labs suggest.
