VOLTA is a mature infostealer with an optional AI-integration layer. After stealing credentials, sessions, cryptocurrency wallets, browser cookies, payment cards, and cloud keys, it sends a structured evidence bundle to an LLM to rank the victim's assets by monetization value. If a model refuses the target assessment, VOLTA fails over to a three-model chain, showing direct engineering against guardrails. A self-deleting variant named VOLTA-AUTODOXER surfaces through the CAIRN research effort.

The novelty is that AI is not supporting a traditional malware capability but acting as an intelligence analyst for the operator: cast a wide net, steal everything, and let AI sort the specifics. The bundle includes geolocation, employer domain, inferred birth year, Slack workspaces, document authorship, and wallet data. There is no attacker-owned infrastructure, reducing exposure but also attribution difficulty.

Watch for CAIRN's full research release, detection rules for the self-deleting payload, and whether other stealer families adopt LLM-based victim profiling. The multi-model failover suggests operators expect model refusals, making this a deliberate rather than experimental feature.
