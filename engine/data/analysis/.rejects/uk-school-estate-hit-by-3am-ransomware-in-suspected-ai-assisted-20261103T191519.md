Bitdefender investigated a 3AM ransomware affiliate intrusion at a UK school estate where approximately 37 of nearly 50 reachable machines were encrypted from an unmanaged host. The attacker used modified PowerShell tools to extract and decrypt credentials from three Veeam backup servers, deployed Mimikatz and LSASS dumpers, and created backdoor domain administrator accounts named library and svc_bkpmon before encryption. Researchers noted a sequence of slightly modified commands issued at regular intervals of 40 to 100 seconds, which they interpreted as possible AI-assisted agentic loop behavior.

The story matters because it documents a hybrid human-agentic ransomware operation rather than a fully automated or fully manual intrusion. The attacker moved slowly, not at machine speed, using retries and minor adjustments consistent with an agentic workflow. This is a realistic preview of how AI assistance may lower the skill barrier for ransomware affiliates without eliminating human oversight. Organizations should focus detection on credential extraction from backup systems and the creation of unexpected domain-admin accounts, rather than relying solely on speed-based anomaly detection.
