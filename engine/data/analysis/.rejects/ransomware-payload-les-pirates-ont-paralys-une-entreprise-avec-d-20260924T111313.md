A ransomware payload paralyzed a company without encrypting a single file. The attackers used two Group Policy Objects (GPO) in Active Directory to block access rather than deploy traditional encryption malware. The event references an it-connect.fr article describing a ransomware technique that relies solely on GPO restrictions to disable systems. There is no executable malware, no file encryption, and no persistent binary on endpoints. This approach avoids many endpoint detection systems that look for encryption behavior or malicious payloads. The absence of file-level damage does not reduce business impact; the company remains paralyzed until policies are reversed. This represents a shift from data-destruction ransomware toward pure operational disruption using native Windows administration tools.

What to watch: whether this technique spreads to other ransomware groups and whether security vendors update detection rules for malicious GPO modifications in Active Directory.
