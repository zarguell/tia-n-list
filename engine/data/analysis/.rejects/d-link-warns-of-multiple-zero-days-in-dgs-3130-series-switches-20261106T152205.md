D-Link disclosed multiple unpatched zero-day flaws in its DGS-3130 series managed Gigabit stackable switches and has not published CVE IDs for them. The bugs were found by Giresh Kumar Enugu, a network engineer at Spectrum Community Solutions, in the D-Link Discovery Protocol (DDP), covering authentication, access control and information handling. The affected models are aimed at small and medium businesses and metro Ethernet deployments.

According to D-Link, successful exploitation could expose sensitive device data, permit unauthorized credential or configuration changes, leak SNMP communities, or cause service disruptions and device resets. The zero-days affect multiple DGS-3130-30TS firmware versions, and D-Link is still investigating the impact on other switching platforms and firmware security mechanisms, including SSH service configuration, operating-system credential safeguards, and firmware encryption and embedded cryptographic keys.
