DCSO has documented a previously unknown malware family called Sauron Loader, encountered as the final stage in attacks against multiple organizations in Germany. The loader is sold as malware-as-a-service in the Russian underground under the alias S4ur0n and is offered exclusively to Russian-speaking cybercriminals. DCSO matched the functionality and settings of the analyzed samples to the forum advertisement and has published indicators and detection tooling on its GitHub.

The delivery chains all begin with social engineering. Attackers use ClickFix-style lures and voice phishing in which victims are flooded with spam emails and then called by someone posing as IT support; in one incident the caller talked a victim into starting a Microsoft Quick Assist session. The payload arrives as a malicious MSI installer that drops three files into C:\ProgramData\keyroll: the legitimate rnpkeys.exe, a malicious rnp.dll that it side-loads, and a second library, tdwp.dll, which decrypts the loader in memory. Execution is deliberately delayed, likely to defeat automated analysis, and persistence comes from a scheduled task named keyroll that reruns the loader.

A new MaaS loader restricted to Russian-speaking buyers lowers the barrier for crews that previously lacked their own final-stage implant. The combination of ClickFix, vishing with Quick Assist, and DLL side-loading mirrors techniques that have worked against well-defended German enterprises all year. Watch for additional victims named by DCSO, new samples sharing the keyroll staging directory or C2 infrastructure, and any broadening of sales beyond the original forum post.
