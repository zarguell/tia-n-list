The Dutch cybersecurity nonprofit DIVD confirmed an agentic AI-powered breach of its infrastructure, describing the intrusion as "loud and very messy" and the first of its kind. The attacker exploited an undisclosed technical vulnerability, not Citrix NetScaler, and the AI agent operated autonomously, making next-step decisions independently, polluting its own MITM attack with password spraying, and leaving verbose evidence that aided reverse engineering. DIVD blocked access, launched forensics with a third-party incident response team, and reported to the Dutch Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens and NCSC-NL. No link to known public threat actors or former volunteers was confirmed. The organization handled the case as worst-case-scenario until proven otherwise.

The incident is significant because it demonstrates an AI agent conducting a full intrusion chain without continuous human direction, including automated exploitation, lateral movement attempts, and noisy persistence. DIVD's disclosure is unusually transparent, providing investigators with detailed behavioral observations of the agent's operation rather than just IOCs. The withheld vulnerability details suggest the entry point remains exploitable elsewhere, but the organization has not identified a widespread campaign targeting similar nonprofits.

Watch for DIVD's promised Thursday October 1 update, which should clarify impact, confirm whether any scanned-system data was accessed, and possibly reveal the exploited vulnerability once law enforcement clears disclosure. The absence of attribution to a named actor or known toolset leaves open whether this was a proof-of-concept demonstration or an intentional adversarial operation.
