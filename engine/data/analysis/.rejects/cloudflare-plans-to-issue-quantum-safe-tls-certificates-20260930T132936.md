Cloudflare has publicly committed to becoming one of the first certificate authorities to issue quantum-safe TLS certificates, announcing the plan on September 30, 2026. The company will use an open source platform that issues both classic certificates and post-quantum Merkle Tree Certificates, free to paying and non-paying users, and is acquiring an established trusted root from GlobalSign to shortcut years of browser and OS trust-store validation. Actual issuance is not expected before the first quarter of 2027.

The hard problem is size. Quantum-resistant signatures in today's X.509 format would add roughly 40 times the data to every TLS handshake, which is why Google and Cloudflare have been piloting Merkle Tree Certificates since Google announced the design in February. A CA signs a single tree head covering millions of certificates, browsers carry a lightweight proof called a landmark, and handshake data stays around 40 kilobytes, close to today's norm. Logging becomes part of issuance itself, which hardens the transparency guarantees that were built after the 2011 DigiNotar breach, and Shor's algorithm on a future quantum computer would otherwise forge both certificate signatures and log timestamps.

This matters operationally because WebPKI migration is the longest-lead item in post-quantum readiness. Cloudflare claims millions of websites could switch to post-quantum certificates with no performance overhead once the system is live, which would move PQC for web traffic from pilot to default.

Watch the milestones Cloudflare said it will publish, root-program acceptance for the GlobalSign root and Merkle Tree Certificate entries in browser and OS trust stores. The Q1 2027 issuance target is the first concrete date to hold the ecosystem to.
