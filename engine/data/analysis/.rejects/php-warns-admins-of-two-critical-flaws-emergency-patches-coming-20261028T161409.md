PHP2Shell is a pair of unauthenticated remote-code-execution flaws in the PHP runtime itself, CVE-2026-109618 (OOM corruption path) and CVE-2026-109619 (stale global state path), both rated CVSS v4.0 9.2. PHP shipped emergency fixes on October 21, 2026 (releases 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36, 8.2.35) after partial disclosure leaked. The event content confirms Censys tracked roughly 32 million vulnerable web properties at disclosure time, Amazon Threat Intelligence recorded exploitation attempts on October 17 (four days before patches), and researchers including watchTowr and Censys observed post-disclosure scanning and exploit attempts. Perplexity researcher Adel Karimi and Expel's Marcus Hutchins later confirmed in-the-wild RCE against honeypots running PHP 8.4.26, with memory disclosure followed by shell execution. CISA added both CVEs to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 26 and set a federal remediation deadline of October 29. A separate unpatched path, CVE-2026-115560, requires a non-default session configuration (session.upload_progress.enabled combined with an unusual serialize-handler switch) and does not yet have an emergency patch; the same setting (disable upload progress) mitigates it. The story also notes AI-assisted discovery: researchers at Calif and Wiz identified the flaws in August 2026 with assistance from OpenAI and Google models.

Why it matters is scale and timing. Because the bugs live in the PHP core rather than a single application, every PHP-backed service — WordPress, Drupal, Joomla, custom applications, and embedded appliance management interfaces — is potentially exposed. Network appliances embedding PHP (Palo Alto PAN-OS, QNAP, F5, Cisco, Schneider Electric OT products) cannot always apply the upstream PHP patch directly and must wait for vendor firmware updates. The InfraTrust event (rss:4624ea46db2211e2) specifically warns that PHP-backed firewall, VPN, and ADC management interfaces remain exposed and recommends removing them from the internet until vendor patches arrive. The blast-radius commentary (rss:3de185b82e20d956) emphasizes that post-exploitation severity depends heavily on service-account and identity access, not just the initial RCE.

What to watch: vendor firmware timelines for embedded PHP (PAN-OS hotfixes 10.2.18-h13 / 11.1.16-h4 / 11.2.10-h16 were released, with others pending), CISA KEV enforcement progress, and whether CVE-2026-115560 sees confirmed exploitation beyond its unlikely configuration prerequisite. The confirmed RCE evidence is still mostly honeypot-based; production compromises beyond the European government claim (later retracted by Palo Alto Unit 42) remain unverified, so attribution to specific actors should be treated cautiously. Patch adoption rates from Qualys telemetry (only 199,000 of 156 million tracked installations updated in the first 12 hours) suggest a long tail of exposure.
