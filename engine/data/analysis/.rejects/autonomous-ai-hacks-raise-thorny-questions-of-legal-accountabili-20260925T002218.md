Autonomous AI agents are beginning to run offensive security operations, and the legal framework around them is thin. The SecurityWeek discussion frames the core liability question as authorization rather than authorship: when an AI agent runs an exploit, the issue is not just who wrote the code but who authorized the operation. There are no CVEs attached to this story; it is a policy and legal risk discussion. The event is brief but points to a growing gap between autonomous agent capabilities and existing accountability structures. What to watch is whether regulators or vendors begin defining liability models for agent-driven security actions, and whether incident reports start attributing breaches to agent actions rather than human operators.
