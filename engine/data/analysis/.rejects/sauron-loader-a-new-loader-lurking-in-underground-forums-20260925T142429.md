DCSO has documented Sauron Loader, a previously undocumented malware family found as the final stage in real-world incidents against multiple organizations in Germany. It is offered as malware-as-a-service in the Russian-language underground by an actor using the alias S4ur0n, and is sold exclusively to Russian-speaking cybercriminals. DCSO matched the forum advertisement's advertised functionality and settings to the samples it analyzed, confirming the product and the incidents share one codebase.

The attack chains start with social engineering, either ClickFix-style lures or voice phishing in which employees flooded with spam are called by fake IT support; in one incident the caller walked the victim into a Microsoft Quick Assist session. In the examined chain, a malicious MSI installer dropped three files into C:\ProgramData\keyroll: the legitimate rnpkeys.exe, a malicious rnp.dll that the executable side-loads, and a second malicious library, tdwp.dll, which decrypts the loader in memory. Execution delays in this stage likely hinder automated analysis. Persistence comes from a scheduled task named keyroll. Once active, the loader decrypts an embedded configuration with C2 addresses and registers the infected system with its C2 server.

A closed distribution model limited to Russian-speaking buyers keeps this family out of most telemetry, which is what makes the published indicators and detection tooling from DCSO useful. Worth watching: further German incident reporting, reuse of the keyroll side-load pattern, and continued abuse of Quick Assist and ClickFix as first-contact techniques.
