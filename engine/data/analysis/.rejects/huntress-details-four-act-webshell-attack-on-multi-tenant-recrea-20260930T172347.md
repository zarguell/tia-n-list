Huntress published an intrusion report on a compromise of a multi-tenant parks-and-recreation platform, structured as a four-act webshell attack with evolving tradecraft. The attacker made multiple failed attempts before gaining access, then adapted tooling as the intrusion progressed through its stages.

The report is worth attention because multi-tenant SaaS platforms are single points of failure: one webshell on the provider's infrastructure exposes every customer organization using the platform, including municipalities that would never expect their recreation software to be an entry point. Huntress's staging of the attack also documents how a persistent actor iterates, which is useful defensive reading for any hosting provider.

Watch how the initial access was achieved and whether the platform vendor has notified tenants. If the tradecraft overlaps with known commodity webshell kits, indicators from this case may apply broadly to other IIS or ASP-hosted environments.
