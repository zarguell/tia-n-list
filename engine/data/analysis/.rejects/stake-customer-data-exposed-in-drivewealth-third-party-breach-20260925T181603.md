DriveWealth, the brokerage infrastructure provider whose API back-ends consumer trading apps, disclosed a data breach that began with a social engineering campaign against the company. The downstream effect is now surfacing at its clients: Stake disclosed that some customer data was exposed, and Hatch separately disclosed that some customer information may have been exposed in the same third-party incident.

The pattern is the important part. One compromise at a shared B2B provider propagates to every platform that rides on it, so the blast radius is measured in client firms rather than in DriveWealth's own customer list. Stake's disclosure indicates attackers gained unauthorized access to data tied to its users, which for a brokerage context typically means identity and account information rather than funds, though the reporting captured here does not enumerate the data types.

Watch for DriveWealth's formal notification with per-person data categories, additional client platforms making parallel disclosures, and any regulatory action, since brokerage-adjacent breaches draw SEC and state attorney general scrutiny. Users of Stake and Hatch should treat unsolicited contact referencing their account details as hostile until the exposed fields are confirmed.
