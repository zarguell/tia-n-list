Sony Network Communications confirmed a breach of its So-net and Plala (S Course) subscriber database in Japan, disclosing on October 27. The intrusion began October 19; the company detected it October 23 and took the server offline. The attackers accessed about 2.58 million customer records — 2.14 million So-net subscribers and 443,000 Plala subscribers. Roughly 293,000 of those belong to people who left the services after March 2026, kept for billing and support. The stolen data includes names, birth dates, gender, phone numbers, email addresses, user IDs, and internet service IDs. Additionally, about 54,401 email credentials from four auxiliary mail services (Mail Service G, A-Box, So-net VAIO Mail, PostPet) were taken. No membership passwords or credit card data was exposed. So-net began forced password resets on October 27 for the affected email accounts.

This is a substantial subscriber data exposure with a clear timeline: initial access October 19, detection October 23, confirmation and disclosure October 27. The breach spans both current and former subscribers, and includes a secondary credential leak from auxiliary email services. The company responded by isolating the server and resetting passwords, but has not identified the attacker or provided indicators of compromise. The 54,000 email passwords represent a concrete authentication risk separate from the larger identity dataset.

Watch for attribution updates and whether the stolen subscriber data appears on leak markets. The dual exposure — identity records plus auxiliary email passwords — increases the risk of account takeover and targeted phishing against So-net and Plala users. Monitor for follow-up disclosure from Sony's investigation team on the intrusion vector.
