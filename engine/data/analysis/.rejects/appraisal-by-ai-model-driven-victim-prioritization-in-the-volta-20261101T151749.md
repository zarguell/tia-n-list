VOLTA (also called VOLTA-AUTODOXER) is a self-deleting Windows infostealer documented by Cisco Talos through the CAIRN research framework. After harvesting the standard stealer haul — browser passwords, cookies, payment cards, cryptocurrency wallet seed phrases, Discord, Telegram, Steam, and Roblox sessions, Microsoft OAuth tokens, SSH and cloud-provider keys, Wi-Fi profiles, screenshots, and document metadata — it builds a structured evidence bundle about the victim. That bundle includes geolocation, employer domain, inferred birth year, card last-4 digits, Slack workspaces, and authorship metadata. The malware then sends this bundle to an LLM to rank the victim's assets by monetization value rather than relying on fixed targeting rules.

The AI integration is designed for resilience against guardrails. If the first model refuses the assessment, VOLTA falls back to a three-model chain, showing direct engineering effort to work around refusal. The malware is self-deleting and has no attacker-owned persistent infrastructure, which means the AI layer acts as an intelligence analyst on the operator's behalf rather than a support tool for traditional malware capabilities. This represents a shift in cybercrime economics: the operator casts a wide net, steals broadly, and lets AI sort out which victims are most profitable.

What to watch is whether this model-driven profiling is adopted by other stealer families. If the LLM ranking proves faster and more accurate than manual triage, the approach could spread quickly. The three-model fallback also suggests attackers expect model vendors to tighten guardrails, so future variants may add more fallback layers or switch to locally hosted models. Monitor CAIRN and Talos for updated indicators and any observed in-the-wild campaigns using VOLTA.
