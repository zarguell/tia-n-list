U.S. law enforcement seized the primary domain of Virtualine, a bulletproof hosting service marketed on cybercrime forums under aliases including Secury, Massgod, Spamhouse, Lanedo, and Omegatech. The service promised to shield customers from legal complaints and law-enforcement requests, and according to the DOJ it marketed hosting for "illegal/black hat activities" ranging from phishing and spamming to carding and port scanning. The FBI seizure involved Germany's Polizei Hessen, the Netherlands' Politie Midden-Nederland, the U.K. National Crime Agency, and private partners OverRIPE and Lumen.

Authorities also allege that Virtualine's operator knowingly hosted an online forum promoting child sexual abuse material, ignored repeated notices from law enforcement, NCMEC, and a victim's attorney, and later helped that forum's administrators spin up a replacement site that remains active and has already drawn at least one abuse complaint. The operator has rejected the allegations, so the criminal case is still developing. The seizure removes a long-running enabler of phishing, spam, and fraud infrastructure, but defenders should expect displaced customers to migrate to other bulletproof providers.
