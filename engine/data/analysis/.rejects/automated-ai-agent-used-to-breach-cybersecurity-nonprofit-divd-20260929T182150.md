The Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure (DIVD), a volunteer nonprofit that scans the internet for known vulnerabilities and notifies system owners, disclosed it was breached by an attack it assesses was carried out autonomously by an AI agent. DIVD described the incident as "loud and very, very messy" and said the modus operandi indicates an agentic AI-powered attack, the first of its kind the organization says it has seen. Evidence gathered so far indicates the attacker exploited a vulnerability, but the purpose and full impact remain unclear.

DIVD said the intrusion ended seven years of uneventful operations and that the agent's automated behavior left substantial evidence, letting investigators reconstruct what happened. The organization informed Dutch police, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens data protection authority, and the National Cyber Security Center (NCSC). In its Monday update it withheld specific details, citing the ongoing investigation and the risk of creating more victims.

The significance is less the outcome at DIVD than the method: an AI agent executing an intrusion chain with minimal human handling against a vulnerability-focused nonprofit that itself holds data about vulnerable systems worldwide. DIVD's database of scanned hosts and contact details would be a meaningful target, which is presumably why the Dutch data protection authority is involved.

Watch DIVD's follow-up disclosures for the exploited vulnerability, whether the victim data included researcher or system-owner records, and whether technical indicators of the agent's tooling are published. Attribution and any reuse of the same agent against other organizations are open questions.
