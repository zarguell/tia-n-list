DragonForce ransomware deployed Backdoor.Turn, a Go-based remote access trojan that hides command-and-control traffic inside Microsoft Teams TURN relay infrastructure. Symantec documented the attack against a major U.S. services company. The malware obtains an anonymous Teams visitor token, uses a legitimate Microsoft TURN relay during connection setup, then connects to the attacker's C2. Defenders only see traffic associated with Microsoft Teams infrastructure. The attack also employed BYOVD techniques with drivers from Huawei, Topaz Antifraud, and K7 Security to obtain kernel privileges and terminate security tools. Capabilities include command execution, LDAP and AD search, TLS certificate capture, and browser credential theft. This is the first known in-the-wild malware to abuse Teams TURN relays for stealth C2.

The story matters because it demonstrates a new evasion technique: routing malicious C2 through legitimate Microsoft Teams relay infrastructure, which makes network-level detection much harder. Watch for broader adoption of TURN-relay abuse by other ransomware groups, updates to EDR rules that can distinguish malicious token usage from normal Teams traffic, and follow-up reports from Symantec or Microsoft on the full scope of the victim company compromise.
