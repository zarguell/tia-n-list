DriveWealth disclosed a data breach after a social engineering campaign led to unauthorized access to its systems, exposing customer personal information. DriveWealth is brokerage infrastructure: it powers trading and investing apps, so the breach is surfacing downstream. Stake disclosed that some of its customer data was exposed in the incident, and Hatch disclosed the same for its users.

The pattern is voice-based social engineering against a central financial infrastructure provider, which turns one successful call or help-desk manipulation into exposure across every fintech that rides on the platform. Affected data reportedly includes personal information, and the downstream disclosures suggest customer-level records, not just internal documents. For affected users the practical risk is targeted phishing and account-takeover attempts that reference real holdings.

Watch for the full list of DriveWealth partners confirming exposure, the specifics of what data types were taken, and whether the same social engineering crew shows up in other fintech backends. Fraud monitoring on affected customer accounts is the metric that will show whether this stays a disclosure event or becomes measurable fraud.
