Hackers breached Harvard University's Alumni Affairs and Development systems in November 2025 via a phone-based phishing (vishing) attack discovered November 18, 2025. The compromised records cover more than 851,000 unique email addresses and include email addresses, telephone numbers, home and business addresses, event attendance, donation details, and biographical information tied to fundraising and alumni engagement. Harvard's CIO Klara Jelinkova said the breached systems do not generally contain Social Security numbers, passwords, payment card information, or financial account numbers; affected groups include alumni, spouses and partners, donors, parents of students, and some current students, faculty, and staff.

ShinyHunters, operating within the Scattered LAPSUS$ Hunters collective, claimed responsibility and said it stole over one million records, leaking a 1.1GB archive on its dark web leak site in early February 2026 after Harvard refused to pay ransom. TechCrunch verified a portion of the published dataset against alumni and public records. The attackers told TechCrunch the intrusions were part of a phishing campaign targeting Okta and single sign-on providers. The incident sits alongside similar donor and alumni record breaches at Princeton University and the University of Pennsylvania.
