Hudson Rock found a cybercrime forum service that adds AI-generated descriptions to stolen infostealer logs. Each compromised machine is presented with its apparent owner, workplace, installed applications and potentially valuable data. Buyers can search those descriptions in natural language and filter by country, operating system and price. The listings frame machines as a clinic's reception desk, a defense contractor's household computer or a government employee's family workstation. GBHackers reported the same service on 16 November, describing searches by job title and company.

Hudson Rock matched several of the advertised logs to records in its own database, which supports the claim that the samples are real infections. The change is in discovery: buyers no longer need to sift raw credential dumps to find a victim with access worth buying. Watch for reporting on how the descriptions are produced, whether the operators expose specific sessions, and any action against the marketplace.
