The Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure, the volunteer nonprofit that scans the internet for vulnerable systems and notifies owners, disclosed late last week that it had been hacked after seven years of uneventful operations. DIVD says the intrusion was carried out autonomously by an AI agent and describes the attack as loud and very messy, an operation it had not seen before because the modus operandi points to agentic AI rather than a human operator.

The attacker exploited a technical vulnerability in an undisclosed system, which DIVD explicitly says was not Citrix NetScaler, then used the agent for post-exploitation. The agent decided each next step itself and left a noisy trail, including over-explained comments in its actions and mistakes such as interfering with its own adversary-in-the-middle attack through password spraying. DIVD believes the agent was poorly trained and configured for intrusion work, which left enough evidence to reconstruct the incident.

DIVD notified police, the Dutch data protection authority, and the National Cyber Security Center, and promised a detailed update on October 1. It also plans to notify other possible victims of the same vulnerability. Watch that update for the exploited flaw's identity and patch state, and for confirmation of what data was touched, since the organization's scanning role means its systems hold records on vulnerable systems worldwide.
