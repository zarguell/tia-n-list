Attackers obtained fraudulent TLS certificates for Google properties and other large services by compromising the operators of three country-code top-level domains rather than by attacking anyone's servers. According to Google's incident writeup, the threat actor gained access to third parties running the .GH (Ghana), .SL (Sierra Leone), and .AS (American Samoa) registries and modified authoritative DNS records. With control of the zones, the attacker could answer certificate authority validation checks, which normally rely on a requester publishing a CA-provided TXT record, and walk away with valid HTTPS certificates for domain names they do not own. Google states several of its domains received unauthorized certificates and that domains belonging to other organizations in those ccTLDs were affected too.

The impact is impersonation at the brand level. A valid cert for a real domain lets the holder serve arbitrary content from that domain and pass browser padlock checks, which is precisely what makes this class of DNS compromise dangerous for phishing and traffic interception. Google says there is no indication its own systems were compromised and no reason to believe the issuing certificate authorities acted improperly; the failure was in the registries. Google blocked the unauthorized certificates for its properties in Chrome through CRLSets and worked with the issuing CAs to revoke them, which extends protection to other clients that honor the revocations.

What to watch: Google was still examining Certificate Transparency logs when reporting, so the full set of fraudulently issued certificates, including ones for non-Google brands in .GH, .SL, and .AS, may be larger than what is currently known. Registry operators in small ccTLDs are a soft target for exactly this validation bypass, and further disclosures of certs issued through other country-code registries would suggest the operation was broader than three zones. Defenders should treat CT log monitoring for their own domains as a control, not an afterthought, since revocation only helps clients that check it.
