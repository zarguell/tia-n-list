Helpfeel confirmed a breach of its Gyazo image-sharing service affecting 23.62 million user records. Exposed data includes email addresses and password hashes. Approximately 490 million image metadata records were also compromised, predominantly images from January 2019 or earlier, including internal IDs. The breach was disclosed in a notice published Wednesday by the Kyoto-based company.

The attack exploited a vulnerability in Gyazo's upload server. Helpfeel has not detailed the specific flaw or provided a timeline for when the intrusion began versus when it was detected. The company is the developer behind the Gyazo screenshot and image-sharing tool, which has a substantial user base among developers, designers, and content creators.

Password hashes alone are a significant risk if users reused credentials across services. The volume of metadata exposed (490 million records) suggests the attackers had deep access to the database layer, not just the user table. Organisations using Gyazo should rotate credentials and review account activity. The breach adds to a pattern of Japanese tech companies being targeted for large-scale data theft.
