Helpfeel confirmed on September 11 that attackers exploited a remote code execution vulnerability in Gyazo's image upload server, gaining full access to their user database of approximately 23.62 million records and roughly 490 million image metadata records, mostly from January 2019 or earlier. The exposed data includes email addresses, password hashes, and image metadata containing unique identifiers. Private image links were also accessible through the compromised database.

The attack surface was a single upload endpoint. Threadlinqs threat intelligence documented 9 detections and 9 IOCs tied to the exploitation, confirming the breach left observable artifacts across monitoring feeds. The combination of credentials and image content is especially damaging for a screenshot service: users routinely capture sensitive information in screenshots, from work documents to credentials and personal data, all of which was potentially accessible to the attacker.

Gyazo is widely used for quick screenshot capture and sharing. The breach underscores how a single misconfigured service can cascade into mass data exposure affecting millions. Helpfeel has not disclosed whether the upload vulnerability has been patched or the full scope of affected data fields beyond what was listed in their notice. Users should rotate passwords immediately and audit what content they stored in Gyazo.
