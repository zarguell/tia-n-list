Helpfeel has confirmed a data breach affecting its Gyazo screenshot service, with approximately 23 million user records exposed. Attackers exploited an upload-server flaw to gain remote code execution, giving them full access to the user database and stored images. The attack surface was a single upload endpoint, underscoring how a single misconfigured service can cascade into a mass data exposure.

Threadlinqs threat intelligence provided additional context with 9 detections and 9 IOCs related to the breach, indicating the exploitation left observable artifacts. The breach exposed both account information and the image database, which for a screenshot service means potentially sensitive content captured by users for work, documentation, or personal purposes.

Gyazo is used by millions for quick screenshot capture and sharing. The combination of credentials and image content makes this breach particularly concerning for individuals who captured sensitive information in their screenshots. Helpfeel has not yet disclosed the full scope of affected data fields or whether the exploited upload flaw has been patched. Users should rotate passwords and review what content they have stored in Gyazo.
