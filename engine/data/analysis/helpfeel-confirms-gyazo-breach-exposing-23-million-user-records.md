Helpfeel, the Kyoto-based company behind the Gyazo screenshot-sharing platform, confirmed on September 17 that attackers exploited a server vulnerability on September 11 to access its database and steal 23.62 million user records. The breach exposed email addresses and password hashes for the full user base, plus roughly 490 million image metadata records, most from January 2019 or earlier, including the short URLs that point to uploaded images. The company detected the suspicious activity on September 12, patched the vulnerability, and took Gyazo offline for maintenance, but the data was already exfiltrated.

The compromised records include the shareable image links themselves, which could allow attackers to access private screenshots without authentication. Gyazo claims 23 million users worldwide and 3.1 billion media items on the platform, and is widely used in gaming and streaming communities. BleepingComputer, The Hacker News, SecurityWeek, and multiple other outlets covered the breach, bringing it to wide attention.

The breach is notable for its scale and the nature of the exposed data. Password hashes were exposed, and users who reused credentials across services face follow-on risk. The exposed image links represent a separate privacy concern: anyone with the short URL can view the original screenshot. Users should change Gyazo passwords, rotate any reused credentials, and review the sensitivity of images uploaded to the platform.
