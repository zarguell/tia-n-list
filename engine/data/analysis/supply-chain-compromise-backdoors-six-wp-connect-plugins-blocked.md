On November 22, 2026, a threat actor pushed backdoored releases of six WordPress plugins developed by WP connect, including Air WP Sync, WP Sync for Notion and Add-on Brevo for Gravity Forms, with a combined estimate of more than 3,600 active installations. Each release silently loaded the same remote-control module, which beacons to an attacker-controlled server, executes PHP sent to it and exposes shell command execution, password hash theft and webshell planting. SecurityOnline tracks the backdoor as CVE-2026-135202.

The releases never reached sites. WordPress.org's Protect The Shire safeguards held the backdoored versions, and all six plugins were closed the same evening. Wordfence says its PRISM monitoring independently found the threat and notified the plugins team, which validated it; the Air WP Sync backdoor was also reported independently by Computeronfire. That sequence is why this is a blocked supply-chain attempt rather than a live mass-compromise count, and no victim count or actor attribution appears in the material.

Watch for confirmation that no backdoored build was served from any mirror, for plugin restoration or permanent closure decisions, and for any overlap with other WP connect releases. The operator-side information to hunt on is the beaconing module and its command set, not a CVE patch: there was no distributed release to patch. The November 24 SecurityOnline post adds the CVE label to an incident whose technical detail comes from the original Wordfence disclosure.
