Dream Research reports a suspected China-nexus actor compromised 25 production firewall servers, including three in a U.S. government-affiliated environment, by chaining the PHP2Shell vulnerability with a Linux kernel privilege-escalation flaw. Palo Alto Networks separately confirmed active PHP2Shell exploitation against Internet-exposed management interfaces. Dream's finding rests on recovered attacker artifacts, so it is evidence of real compromise rather than honeypot telemetry, but the two companies have not publicly established that their findings concern the same campaign.

The significance is the move from proof-of-concept to production impact. Earlier PHP2Shell reports covered exploitation of deliberately vulnerable honeypots; this is the first published evidence of real server compromise at scale, including government-adjacent targets. The kernel privilege-escalation chain also raises the severity beyond a web-management-interface bug, since a successful attacker can move from the firewall's management plane to full control of the underlying host.

What to watch: whether Palo Alto or Dream publish indicators of compromise and whether the kernel escalation component is identified and patched independently. Administrators of Internet-exposed management interfaces should assume scanning is ongoing and restrict management access to trusted networks or VPNs until patches are applied.
