Kaspersky's threat intelligence team has identified three distinct APT clusters conducting coordinated attacks against Russian enterprises. NightEagle (also tracked as APT-Q-95) leverages stolen VPN credentials to establish footholds, then deploys custom backdoors for persistent access. Hacking Cat has shifted tactics to target Russian organizations specifically, moving beyond its previous geographic focus. Toy Ghouls rounds out the trio, deploying ransomware and wiper malware in destructive campaigns.

What makes this cluster report notable is the convergence of three independent threat actors on the same target set within the same timeframe. The combination of backdoors for access, ransomware for extortion, and wipers for destruction suggests either coordinated operations or opportunistic actors exploiting the same geopolitical moment. The use of stolen VPN credentials by NightEagle indicates credential harvesting campaigns that may have broader victims than currently identified.

Defenders running Russian-facing infrastructure should prioritize VPN access controls and monitor for the specific backdoor families Kaspersky has documented. The wiper component is particularly concerning, as it suggests some operations aim for disruption rather than financial gain alone.
