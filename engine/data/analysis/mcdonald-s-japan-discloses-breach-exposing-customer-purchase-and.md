McDonald's Japan disclosed on November 20, 2026 that an unauthorized third party accessed a database behind its official mobile app. The exposed records include purchase histories, IP addresses, internal customer management IDs and location data from mobile devices used with the service. The company said names, email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses and credit card information were not part of the breach, and that the internal IDs alone cannot identify a customer.

The company is invalidating the authentication credentials used in the intrusion but has not said how the attacker obtained them. It also has not given a victim count, an intrusion date or an attribution, and says it has not detected unauthorized use of the app or related services. It told customers they do not need to change passwords, while reporting the incident to Japan's data protection authorities.

What to watch is whether McDonald's Japan publishes a scope figure or a timeline for the intrusion, and whether the credential invalidation is followed by any account-level misuse. Location data tied to purchase histories is sensitive even without names or card numbers, so any later confirmation that the records were copied would change the assessment. Reports so far do not say whether data was exfiltrated.
