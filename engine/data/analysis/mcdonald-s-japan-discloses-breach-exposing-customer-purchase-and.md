McDonald's Japan disclosed on November 20, 2026 that an unauthorized third party accessed a database supporting its official mobile app. The exposed fields were purchase histories, IP addresses, internal customer management IDs and location data from mobile devices when customers used the service. The company said the internal IDs could not identify a customer on their own, and that names, email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses and card data were not affected.

McDonald's Japan did not say how many customers were affected, when the intrusion occurred or who was behind it. It was invalidating authentication credentials used in the attack but did not explain how the attacker obtained them, and it has not confirmed that any customer information was stolen. No unauthorized use of the app or related services has been detected, and the company reported the incident to Japan's data protection authority.

What to watch: a later count of affected customers, a confirmed data-extraction finding, and details on the compromised credentials. Location histories tied to purchase records are sensitive even without names or payment data, especially when combined with other retail breach data reported in Japan in the same period.
