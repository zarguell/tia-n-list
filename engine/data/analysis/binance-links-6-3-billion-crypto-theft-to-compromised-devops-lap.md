Binance suffered the largest cryptocurrency theft on record on October 29, 2026, when about $6.3 billion in assets were drained from hot wallets across roughly ten blockchains, mostly within about 20 minutes. Binance's initial investigation points to a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop that gave the attacker access to a service container and allowed submission of unauthorized withdrawal transactions through Binance's own signing path. The exchange found no evidence of a leaked wallet private key, reported additional points of breach, and suspended deposits and withdrawals while conducting security checks. TRM Labs calculated about $6.36 billion siphoned and attributed the operation with high confidence to North Korea's TraderTraitor group, noting the attackers spent at least a day and a half preparing, funded the operation wallet through Tornado Cash, and leveraged EIP-7702 on Ethereum to delegate wallet behavior.

The FBI subsequently confirmed North Korean state-sponsored hackers known as TraderTraitor, also tracked as Lazarus Group, Jade Sleet, and APT38, were behind the theft, and said the group was converting stolen assets primarily into Ethereum for laundering through DeFi platforms and instant swap services. Tether and Circle froze more than $2.3 billion in linked assets, and Binance reported roughly $3 billion blocked through on-chain freezes. Binance committed to covering all losses from the SAFU fund and company funds, promised an updated proof of reserves after remediation, and revoked authorizations for the affected address while working with blockchain intelligence partners and law enforcement.

The incident's broader lesson is that developer laptops are high-value targets: they hold API keys, cloud tokens, and SSH keys that enable lateral movement into production signing infrastructure. Organizations should inventory developer credentials, verify their permissions and validity, and remove plaintext copies before a compromise forces the question. Crypto operators should review service-container access paths and signing-path authorization controls, since here the attacker made Binance's own infrastructure sign and send funds as if legitimate.
