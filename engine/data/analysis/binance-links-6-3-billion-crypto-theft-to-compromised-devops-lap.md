The Binance breach is the largest cryptocurrency heist on record: about $6.36 billion stolen across ten blockchains, with most moved in roughly 20 minutes. Binance linked the intrusion to a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop that gave attackers access to a service container, letting them submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions. The company said it has not found evidence of a leaked private key and described the attack as more complex than a single action; Binance also uncovered additional breach points and suspended deposits and withdrawals while working to trace and recover funds.

The FBI confirmed attribution to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-sponsored group also tracked as Lazarus Group, Jade Sleet, and APT38. The agency said the theft of more than $6 billion occurred around October 29, 2026, and that TraderTraitor converts stolen assets primarily into Ethereum, expected to be laundered through DeFi platforms and instant swap services. Blockchain firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis had earlier linked the theft to TraderTraitor, and Binance stated it will cover losses through its SAFU fund and company reserves, with user funds protected and an updated Proof of Reserves planned after remediation.

Watch for FBI, Secret Service, and IRS coordination outcomes; whether Binance's additional breach points expand the scope; and any recovery of the $6.36B across the ten affected blockchains. Monitoring should focus on TraderTraitor's ETH laundering path through DeFi and swap services, possible freezes by stablecoin issuers (previous reports note Tether and Circle froze about $2.4B in linked assets), and whether the compromised DevOps laptop reveals a broader supply-chain or container-access weakness in Binance's infrastructure.
