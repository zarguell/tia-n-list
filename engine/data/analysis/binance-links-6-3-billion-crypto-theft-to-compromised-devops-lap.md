Binance linked a roughly $6.3 billion cryptocurrency theft to a compromised DevOps engineer laptop, confirming the breach in a statement shared with BleepingComputer. The attacker used the access to reach a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions; Binance stated it has not found evidence of a leaked wallet private key. Co-CEO Richard Teng emphasized the operation was complex and not a single-click action, and said the company uncovered additional points of breach it is still investigating.

The exchange suspended deposits and withdrawals, revoked affected authorizations, and is working with blockchain intelligence partners and law enforcement to trace stolen funds. Binance pledged to cover losses from its SAFU fund and company reserves, reassured users that funds are safe, and promised an updated Proof of Reserves after remediation. Blockchain analysis firm TRM has been cited in reporting linking the incident to broader theft campaigns.

This event shows how a single compromised development endpoint can cascade into massive financial loss in a crypto environment. The incident also highlights the importance of container-level access controls and rapid revocation pipelines when insider or contractor endpoints are breached.
