On October 29, 2026, attackers drained roughly $6.36 billion from Binance hot wallets across ten blockchains in about 20 minutes, the largest cryptocurrency theft on record. Binance's initial investigation points to a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop that gave the attacker access to a service container used to submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions; the company says it found no evidence of a leaked wallet private key but has since identified additional points of breach. About $3 billion was frozen on-chain through Tether, Circle, and other partners, including a single rapid transaction of about $2.8 billion via the EIP-7702 account-abstraction mechanism, and Binance pledged to cover all losses from the SAFU fund and company reserves while suspending deposits and withdrawals during remediation.

TRM Labs attributes the attack with high confidence to North Korea's TraderTraitor group, matching signatures from earlier state-linked heists, and Chainalysis dates the outflow to about 4:20 p.m. ET. TRM notes the attackers spent at least a day and a half preparing: funding the operation wallet through small Tornado Cash withdrawals and trialing malicious code on other wallets before going live, and using EIP-7702 on Ethereum to delegate wallet behavior. The haul dwarfs the $1.5 billion Bybit theft of February 2025. The FBI is engaged with public and private partners but has not itself attributed the intrusion to North Korea.

The concrete artifact to track is Binance's still-pending technical post-mortem. Watch for confirmation of how credentials on the laptop were stolen, how the attacker progressed through Binance's infrastructure, and whether the EIP-7702 and Tornado Cash trail yields further recoveries beyond the roughly $3 billion already frozen.
