Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, was hit on October 29, 2026 by the largest crypto heist on record, with about $6.3 billion siphoned from hot wallets across roughly ten blockchains, mostly within about twenty minutes. Binance's early investigation points to a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop giving attackers access to a service container used to submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions through Binance's own signing path, making the withdrawals look legitimate. The exchange says it found no evidence of a leaked wallet private key, and Co-CEO Richard Teng stressed the attack was not a single one-click action but a complex process involving additional points of breach still being investigated.

TRM Labs attributed the operation to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-sponsored actor, and calculated about $6.36 billion moved. The attackers prepared for at least a day and a half: the operation wallet was funded with small Tornado Cash withdrawals, and malicious code was trialed on other wallets before going live. On Ethereum the attackers used EIP-7702 to make a standard wallet delegate its behavior. Roughly $2.8 to $3 billion has been frozen on-chain by Tether, Circle, and blockchain partners, and Binance has committed to reimbursing all losses from company funds and its Secure Asset Fund for Users, with an updated Proof of Reserves to follow. Deposits and withdrawals were suspended for security checks, authorizations for the affected address revoked, and law enforcement and blockchain intelligence partners engaged.

Earlier reporting put the figure at up to $5.8 billion before Binance confirmed $6.3 billion. The incident dwarfs the previous record, the $1.5 billion Bybit theft, and highlights the risk of a single compromised DevOps endpoint bridging into production signing infrastructure. Defenders should treat developer and DevOps workstations as part of the production trust boundary and monitor for container access and withdrawal-signing anomalies.
