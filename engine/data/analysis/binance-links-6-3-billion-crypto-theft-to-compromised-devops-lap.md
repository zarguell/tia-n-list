Binance suffered the largest cryptocurrency theft on record on October 29, 2026, with approximately $6.3 billion (TRM Labs estimates $6.36 billion) drained from hot wallets across roughly ten blockchains in about twenty minutes. The entry point appears to be a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop, which gave the attacker access to a service container used to submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions, including via the EIP-7702 account-abstraction mechanism. Binance says it found no evidence of a leaked wallet private key but has identified additional breach points. Blockchain intelligence firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis, along with the FBI, attribute the theft with high confidence to North Korea's TraderTraitor group (also tracked as Lazarus Group, Jade Sleet, and APT38), and the FBI has linked over 600 crypto wallet addresses to the operation. Roughly $3 billion in assets were frozen by Tether and Circle, and Binance has committed to covering remaining losses from its Secure Asset Fund for Users and company funds. North Korea has publicly denied the attribution.

The incident is significant on two levels. First, the scale dwarfs the previous record, the $1.5 billion Bybit heist, by roughly four times, and it shows state-sponsored actors can move billions across ten chains in minutes once they reach an exchange's signing path. Second, the root cause is an endpoint compromise rather than a smart-contract or key leak, which is a reminder that developer laptops holding API keys, cloud tokens, and SSH credentials are a primary target for lateral movement into financial infrastructure. The FBI expects the stolen Ethereum to be laundered through decentralized finance platforms and instant swap services.

Watch for Binance's full technical post-mortem, the recovery rate on frozen assets, and whether the stolen funds are successfully laundered or intercepted. The case will likely drive regulatory scrutiny of exchange key management and endpoint security, and of how quickly stablecoin issuers can freeze assets at this scale. Users should ignore fake refund and support messages claiming withdrawals are open.
