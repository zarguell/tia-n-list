Binance confirmed a hot wallet compromise on October 29, 2026 affecting about $6.3 billion moved from wallets across roughly ten blockchains in about 20 minutes, with the exchange pointing to a compromised DevOps engineer laptop that gave attackers access to a service container used to submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions through Binance's own signing path. TRM Labs and Chainalysis attributed the operation to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-sponsored group, noting the attacker wallet was funded with small Tornado Cash withdrawals and malicious code trialed on other wallets before execution. The attackers leveraged EIP-7702 on Ethereum to delegate a standard wallet's behavior, and Binance stated there was no evidence of a leaked private key while also acknowledging additional points of breach still under investigation.

The $6.3 billion estimate exceeds the previous record $1.5 billion Bybit theft from February 2025 by more than four times, making this the largest confirmed crypto heist on record; with roughly $2.8 to $3 billion frozen through on-chain actions by Tether, Circle, and blockchain partners and Binance committing to cover remaining losses from company funds and the SAFU reserve, user solvency is protected but the breach exposes a critical gap between developer workstation hygiene and production signing infrastructure. This is not a routine exchange incident among thirty others this week because the attacker did not exploit a smart contract bug or steal a private key directly but instead used a legitimate withdrawal path through a compromised internal endpoint, which means any organization with container-based signing pipelines and DevOps laptops in scope needs to reassess its trust boundary immediately.

Defenders should treat DevOps workstations as part of the production trust boundary, audit container access logs for the affected service container, and hunt for EIP-7702 delegation anomalies on Ethereum tied to operation wallets; Binance has revoked authorizations for the affected address, suspended withdrawals for security checks, and engaged blockchain intelligence partners and law enforcement, with an updated Proof of Reserves promised after remediation completes. Monitor for further TRM Labs updates on TraderTraitor infrastructure and any Binance disclosure of the additional breach points, because the investigation is ongoing and the full attack chain has not been mapped.

A change in assessment would come if Binance confirms private key leakage (contradicting its current statement) or if law enforcement links this operation to additional victims beyond Binance; also watch whether the unfrozen portion grows beyond the current $3 to $3.5 billion gap and whether the additional points of breach reveal broader supply-chain exposure.
