On October 29, 2026, attackers drained approximately $6.3 to $6.36 billion in cryptocurrency from Binance hot wallets across roughly 10 blockchains in about 20 minutes, the largest crypto heist on record, roughly four times the $1.5 billion Bybit theft of February 2025. TRM Labs and Chainalysis attribute the attack with high confidence to North Korea's TraderTraitor group, also tracked as Lazarus Group, Jade Sleet and APT38, and the FBI officially attributed the theft to TraderTraitor on October 31, linking 628 wallet addresses and noting the group is converting stolen assets primarily into Ethereum for laundering through decentralized finance platforms and instant swap services. Binance's initial investigation points to a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop that gave attackers access to a service container used to submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions, with no evidence of a leaked wallet private key; the attackers spent at least a day and a half preparing, funded operations with small Tornado Cash withdrawals, trialed malicious code on other wallets, and on Ethereum leveraged EIP-7702 to delegate wallet behavior. About $3 billion, including $1.567 billion in USDT, was frozen through Tether and Circle, and Binance suspended deposits and withdrawals, revoked authorizations for the affected address, and committed to covering losses from the Secure Asset Fund for Users and company funds while publishing an updated Proof of Reserves after remediation. The FBI, US Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are involved; Binance has identified additional breach points beyond the laptop and a full technical post-mortem is pending.

The case is significant not only for its scale but for its method: a state-sponsored group used a single developer endpoint to reach production signing infrastructure and push unauthorized withdrawals through Binance's own signing path, bypassing controls meant to stop them. It underscores that developer laptops hold API keys, cloud tokens and SSH keys enabling lateral movement, and that endpoint and credential hygiene are part of exchange security, not just wallet key management. The fallout included a roughly 3 percent crypto market dip, billions in user outflows, and a wave of fake refund and support scams targeting users.

What to watch next is Binance's pending post-mortem and remediation, further attribution detail as investigators trace cross-chain outflows, and recovery efforts. Users should rely only on official Binance channels and ignore unsolicited refund or withdrawal messages. Organizations holding developer credentials should inventory API keys, cloud tokens and SSH keys, check their validity and permissions, and remove plaintext copies from endpoints before a compromise forces the question.
