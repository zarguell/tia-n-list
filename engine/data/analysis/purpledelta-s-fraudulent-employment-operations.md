Recorded Future's Insikt Group published a detailed report on PurpleDelta, their designation for North Korean IT workers operating from China. Between late 2024 and early 2025, one cluster applied to jobs at over 1,100 companies, concentrating on software/technology, staffing/consulting, and healthcare/biotech sectors. The operators maintained at least 22 fabricated personas across multiple clusters, some supported by AI-generated profile photos, custom-configured ChatGPT assistants, and identity documents sourced from an illicit ID-generation service.

The operational tempo is notable: operators applied to at least 60 positions per day across multiple job platforms, used multi-account management browsers and separate Chrome profiles to manage distinct personas, and maintained detailed tracking spreadsheets to coordinate applications across identities. During interviews, they used screen recording software alongside AI transcription and chatbot tools to generate real-time answers, often repeating ChatGPT responses verbatim. Once placed, operators recorded internal meetings at victim organizations and used Google Translate to draft pre-written excuses to justify using personal devices and bank accounts for work purposes.

The report assesses these operators were highly likely actively employed by at least ten organizations at the time of the investigation. This represents the most detailed public accounting yet of the scale and tradecraft of DPRK IT worker infiltration schemes, which have been a growing concern for corporate HR and security teams. The combination of AI-assisted identity fabrication and high-volume automated job application tooling marks an evolution in the technique beyond the earlier manual approaches documented by the FBI and CISA.
