Gen Threat Labs researchers found that GPT4Free's hosted chat service routes user prompts through a complex network of third-party endpoints that may not match the model selected in the interface. Tests showed requests labeled as Gemini models passing through OpenAI-compatible endpoints at g4f.space before reaching Google infrastructure, with returned model identifiers sometimes differing from user selections.

The investigation identified several privacy concerns: prompts may traverse logging systems and external Ollama endpoints not disclosed to users, the defaultModel logic does not reliably predict which system processes a request, and model substitution occurs without user awareness. The discrepancies mean a menu selection cannot prove which model actually processed prompt data or where that data was sent afterward.

What to watch: GPT4Free operates as an open-source project providing free access to various AI models, but the routing opacity creates accountability gaps. Users assuming their prompts go directly to the selected provider may be unknowingly sharing data with intermediary services. The findings raise questions about data retention policies across the routing chain.
