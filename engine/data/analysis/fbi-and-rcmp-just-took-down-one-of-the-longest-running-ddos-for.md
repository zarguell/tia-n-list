FBI and RCMP seized the primary domain and associated websites of NightmareStresser, one of the longest-running DDoS-for-hire services operating since at least 2022. The operation, part of the ongoing globally coordinated "Operation PowerOFF," targeted a service that facilitated hundreds of thousands of DDoS attacks or attempted attacks. The court-ordered seizure now displays a law enforcement notice on the primary domain.

NightmareStresser operated openly on the public web and claimed to operate under Russian laws, according to Infoblox staff threat researcher Zach Edwards. The service was built on hijacked home routers, as documented by Threadlinqs research citing 9 detections and 16 IOCs. Authorities have seized more than 100 domains associated with DDoS-for-hire services since 2018, but Edwards noted it was "somewhat shocking that it's taken law enforcement this long to take action" given the service's prominence.

This takedown illustrates the persistent gap between law enforcement action and DDoS-for-hire availability. Despite hundreds of seizures over eight years, these services remain proliferate because the barrier to resurfacing under a new domain is low. The reliance on hijacked home routers for infrastructure also means the underlying botnet survives the domain seizure. Organizations should treat DDoS resilience as an ongoing operational requirement, not something addressed by takedown events alone.
