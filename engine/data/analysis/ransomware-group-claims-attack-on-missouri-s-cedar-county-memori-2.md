Cedar County Memorial Hospital in El Dorado Springs, Missouri, shut down its IT networks on August 14, 2026 after a disruption made its electronic health record, patient portal, and internet access unavailable. Diagnostic imaging was also disrupted, preventing the emergency department from transmitting images to radiologists, which forced a partial diversion of trauma and critical care patients. The Wallstreet ransomware group later claimed responsibility for the attack.

The hospital confirmed the cyberattack in a disclosure, and the Wallstreet group posted evidence of the breach on its leak site. This is the latest in a string of ransomware attacks targeting rural and community hospitals in the United States, where limited IT staffing and aging infrastructure create favorable conditions for attackers. The partial ER diversion highlights the direct patient safety impact of healthcare ransomware beyond data theft.

Small and rural hospitals remain high-value targets for ransomware groups precisely because they often lack the resources for robust backup systems and incident response. The Cedar County attack underscores the need for healthcare organizations of all sizes to maintain offline backups and tested incident response plans that account for the possibility of extended system outages.
