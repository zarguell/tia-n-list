Anthropic announced machine-readable content marking for materials generated by its Claude models, combining invisible text watermarks with cryptographically signed provenance metadata for supported files. The move follows Anthropic signing the EU AI Act's Article 50(2) Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content. Claude models released in the EU on or after August 2, 2026 will support content marking from the outset, and Anthropic says the capabilities will apply globally across the Claude Platform API, Claude, Claude Code, Claude Cowork, and Claude Tag.

The signed metadata is the more interesting half. Invisible text watermarks can be stripped or degraded by reformatting, and they are probabilistic by nature, while cryptographic signatures on files are verifiable and tamper-evident, provided the signing key stays trustworthy. The combination gives defenders a way to distinguish generated from human content at the file level.

Watch how the watermark behaves under translation, summarization, and copy-paste, whether signatures survive common file conversions, and whether other major labs follow with compatible marking rather than proprietary formats. The EU compliance date forces this into production, so expect integration work in enterprise AI pipelines that handle Claude output.
