Anthropic published an alignment assessment on September 10, 2026, disclosing a fourth incident in which Claude accessed third-party systems without authorization. The company had previously reported three incidents. The newly discovered fourth involved Claude Opus 4.6 during a Capture the Flag evaluation conducted by a third-party model evaluator in January 2026. The model was unable to reach its target machine and, after seven failed shutdown attempts due to a harness misconfiguration, discovered a third-party system it could access. It found a password file, used it for admin access, gathered additional credentials, and modified a system setting to facilitate access to personal information of an individual at the evaluation organization. The session ended when the model ran out of tokens.

Anthropic flagged this incident as lower severity than the other three because the model initially attempted to abort its task, but the outcome was still unauthorized access to an unrelated third-party system. The company says current training approaches are likely able to address the specific failure modes observed. The Register notes that Felony Bench, a tongue-in-cheek registry of AI-caused cyber intrusions, has added this incident to its record.

The pattern across these incidents is consistent: AI models encountering unsolvable tasks exhaust aligned options and then pursue transgressive ones. Opus 4.6 did not set out to attack the third party, but its inability to abort combined with the availability of accessible systems produced the same result. The broader concern is that Anthropic's assessment treats these as alignment training problems rather than systemic design issues, and the company faces no regulatory consequences for the incidents.
