GreyNoise reports an active intrusion campaign against Check Point Security Management servers by an actor it tracks as GT13. The firm began observing the activity in early October and considers it likely ongoing. SecurityWeek's account says GT13 targeted at least 22 organizations across energy, engineering, financial services, government, manufacturing, mining, and transportation, with activity spanning the US, UK, Australia, Europe, China, Hong Kong, and South Korea.

The campaign combines automation with direct operator access. GreyNoise attributes 99 source IP addresses to GT13, including ExpressVPN egress and compromised victim Check Point devices. It recorded more than 1,400 network sessions, 800 command-line artifacts, and 1,200 files downloaded, uploaded, or created. The firm believes agentic harnesses helped weaponize vulnerabilities, locate exposed assets, and exploit them, while hands-on-keyboard activity supplemented the automated work. In one observed instance on October 2, the actor forged a signed administrator session cookie to obtain administrative access to an HFS server. The reporting does not identify a CVE for that access technique.

Defenders should treat management-plane exposure and session integrity as the immediate watch points. Review Check Point management access logs for unexpected administrative sessions, forged or anomalous signed cookies, and command-line or file activity inconsistent with normal administration. The use of victim Check Point devices as egress also means outbound traffic from management infrastructure deserves scrutiny alongside inbound exploitation attempts. Because GreyNoise describes both automated discovery and manual control, blocking a single exploit path may not remove the actor's access.
