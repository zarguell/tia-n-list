GreyNoise tracked a campaign it calls GreyNoise Threat 13 against Check Point Security Management servers from early October, and assessed it as likely still active. The actor used 99 IP addresses, mostly ExpressVPN egresses or compromised Check Point devices, across more than 1,400 network sessions. GreyNoise counted over 800 command line artifacts and more than 1,200 files downloaded, uploaded or created. At least 22 organizations in the energy, engineering, financial services, government, manufacturing, mining and transportation sectors were targeted across 17 countries.

The initial path exploited CVE-2026-16232, a SmartConsole authentication bypass that yields an application login token and administrator access when the Management server IP is internet reachable and Trusted Clients are unrestricted. Check Point patched it on July 22 in R82.10 Take 36, R82 Take 118 and R81.20 Take 158, and CISA added it to KEV with a July 25 federal deadline. Rapid7 later published root-cause detail and proof of concept code: the login service trusted an attacker-supplied SIC distinguished name instead of binding identity to the peer certificate.

Post-compromise activity included stolen certificate authority files, newly generated certificates, unloaded firewall policies, forged signed admin session cookies and a forged admin session on an HFS server observed on October 2. BleepingComputer counted 24 compromised management servers at 22 organizations between October 17 and November 2. Hunt for the published IP addresses and for SmartConsole audit-log entries reading Authentication method: application token.
