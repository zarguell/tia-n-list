GreyNoise tracks an intrusion cluster it calls GT13, active since early October and likely still running, against Check Point Security Management servers. The actor used 99 IP addresses, either ExpressVPN egresses or compromised Check Point devices, across more than 1,400 network sessions. GreyNoise counted over 800 command-line artifacts and 1,200 files downloaded, uploaded or created, and attributes the activity to agentic tooling used for scanning, weaponizing and exploiting flaws alongside hands-on keyboard work. BleepingComputer dates the confirmed run of 24 compromised servers at 22 organizations in 17 countries to October 17 through November 2, spanning energy, engineering, finance, government, manufacturing, mining and transportation victims.

In one case GT13 forged a signed administrator session cookie to reach an HFS server on October 2. Another intrusion saw the actor patch the compromised server after entry, apparently to keep other intruders out. The July cluster of Check Point management bugs includes CVE-2026-16232, an authentication bypass in SmartConsole login that Check Point confirmed as exploited in the wild before the July 22 hotfix. Fixed builds are R82.10 Take 36, R82 Take 118 and R81.20 Take 158; CISA gave federal agencies until July 25 to remediate. Beazley Security listed six attacker IPs in that wave, including 194.213.18.137.

What to watch: management servers exposed without Trusted Client restrictions, application-token logins, forged admin cookies and unexpected firewall policy changes. Censys counted 3,836 hosts presenting the default cp_mgmt SIC identity while tracking the separate pre-auth management overflow CVE-2026-91843, a reminder that exposure remains broad even where specific exploit confirmation is absent.
