GreyNoise reported an intrusion campaign against Check Point Security Management servers that ran from early October into early November 2026 and was still likely active. The firm calls the actor GT13 and says it compromised 24 servers at 22 organizations in 17 countries across energy, engineering, financial services, government, manufacturing, mining and transportation. GT13 used 99 IP addresses that were either ExpressVPN egresses or compromised victim Check Point devices, with more than 1,400 network sessions, 800 command line artifacts and 1,200 files downloaded, uploaded or created.

The initial access path aligns with CVE-2026-16232, a CVSS 9.1 authentication bypass in SmartConsole that Check Point confirmed as a zero-day against a small number of customers with internet-exposed Management servers and no Trusted Client restrictions. Rapid7 showed the flaw accepted an attacker-supplied SIC distinguished name and issued an application token plus SmartConsole SSO ticket; fixes shipped in R82.10 Take 36, R82 Take 118 and R81.20 Take 158. CISA added the CVE to KEV on 22 July with a 25 July federal deadline. In one 2 October case GreyNoise saw a forged signed admin session cookie used against an HFS server, and the actor later patched the compromised server to keep other intruders out, stole CA files, generated certificates and unloaded firewall policies.

A later Check Point flaw, CVE-2026-91843, is a CVSS 9.8 stack overflow in pre-authentication login; Censys counted 3,836 hosts presenting the default cp_mgmt SIC identity, though that is not a confirmed vulnerable count and Check Point reported no exploitation at the time. Watch SmartConsole audit logs for "Authentication method: application token", new administrator accounts or certificates, and any management server still reachable from untrusted networks without Trusted Clients enabled.
