GreyNoise tracked an intrusion campaign against Check Point Security Management servers beginning in early October and likely still active. The actor, named GT13, used 99 IP addresses that were either ExpressVPN egresses or compromised victim Check Point devices. GreyNoise recorded more than 1,400 network sessions, 800 command-line artifacts and 1,200 files downloaded, uploaded or created, with at least 22 organizations in 17 countries hit across energy, engineering, financial services, government, manufacturing, mining and transportation. In one case the actor forged a signed admin session cookie to reach administrative access on an HFS server.

The campaign combined automated exploitation with hands-on work. GreyNoise assesses that GT13 used agentic tooling to weaponize vulnerabilities, find exposed assets and exploit them, then continued interactively after access. One intruder patched a compromised server to keep other intruders out. The story also carries the earlier Check Point management-server flaws that made such access possible: CVE-2026-16232, an authentication bypass in SmartConsole login that Check Point confirmed was exploited in the wild in July, fixed in R82.10 Take 36, R82 Take 118 and R81.20 Take 158. CVE-2026-91843, a pre-authentication stack overflow with 3,836 hosts showing the default cp_mgmt identity in Censys scans, is another management-plane risk, though Censys stressed that is not a vulnerable-host count.

Watch for new Check Point management servers appearing in victim infrastructure, forged admin session artifacts, and any expansion of the 22-organization count. The use of compromised Check Point devices as egress nodes makes internal management traffic an unreliable trust signal, so device integrity and jumbo-hotfix levels need checking alongside perimeter logs. The exact entry vulnerability for each of the roughly 24 breached servers has not been published.
