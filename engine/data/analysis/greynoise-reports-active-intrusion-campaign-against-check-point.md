GreyNoise tracked an intrusion campaign against Check Point Security Management servers from early October 2026 and assessed it as likely still active. The actor, labelled GT13, operated from 99 IP addresses that were either ExpressVPN egresses or compromised victim Check Point devices. GreyNoise recorded more than 1,400 network sessions, 800 command line artifacts, and 1,200 files downloaded, uploaded, or created. At least 24 management servers at 22 organizations in 17 countries were compromised, across energy, engineering, financial services, government, manufacturing, mining, and transportation. The activity combined automated vulnerability exploitation with hands-on-keyboard work.

The campaign sits on top of a busy year for Check Point management flaws. CVE-2026-16232, a SmartConsole authentication bypass that yields an application token and administrator access, was exploited as a zero-day in July against internet-exposed Management servers without Trusted Client restrictions. Fixes shipped in R82.10 Take 36, R82 Take 118, and R81.20 Take 158, and CISA added the flaw to KEV on July 22 with a July 25 federal deadline. Rapid7 published exploit code on July 28. Censys later counted 3,836 hosts presenting the default cp_mgmt SIC identity, while noting that this is not a confirmed vulnerable count.

GreyNoise describes post-compromise behavior that goes beyond scanning: a forged signed admin session cookie was used on October 2 against an HFS server, operators stole CA files and generated certificates, unloaded firewall policies, and patched one compromised server to keep other intruders out. Watch for new victim counts, overlap with the July CVE-2026-16232 exploitation, and threat hunts around SmartConsole audit entries for application-token authentication, restricted Trusted Clients, and management servers reachable from the internet.
