GreyNoise has tracked an intrusion campaign it calls GT13 against Check Point Security Management servers since early October, and assesses it is likely still active. The actor used 99 IP addresses, either ExpressVPN egresses or compromised victim Check Point devices, across more than 1,400 network sessions. GreyNoise counted 800-plus command-line artifacts and 1,200-plus files downloaded, uploaded or created. Reporting on the campaign puts the compromise at 24 management servers in 22 organizations across 17 countries between October 17 and November 2, spanning energy, engineering, financial services, government, manufacturing, mining and transportation.

GreyNoise believes GT13 used agentic tooling to weaponize vulnerabilities, find exposed assets and exploit them automatically, with hands-on-keyboard work alongside the automation. In one case traced to October 2, the actor forged a signed admin session cookie to gain administrative access to an HFS server. The latest reporting describes theft of CA files, generation of new certificates and unloading of firewall policies on compromised servers. One intruder patched a server it had entered, apparently to keep other actors out.

The campaign sits on top of earlier Check Point management flaws. CVE-2026-16232, a SmartConsole authentication bypass that yields admin tokens, was exploited as a zero-day against a small number of internet-exposed customers before the July 22 hotfixes in R82.10 Take 36, R82 Take 118 and R81.20 Take 158. It is in CISA KEV with a July 25 federal remediation deadline, and Rapid7 published root-cause analysis and PoC on July 28. Watch for application-token authentication entries in SmartConsole audit logs, management servers exposed without Trusted Client restrictions, and build levels below those hotfix takes.
