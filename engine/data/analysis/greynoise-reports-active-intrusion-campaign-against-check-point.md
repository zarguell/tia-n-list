GT13 is a hands-on intrusion operator, not a scanning blip. GreyNoise watched scanning, exploitation and post-compromise control from 99 IP addresses, each either an ExpressVPN egress or a compromised victim Check Point device, generating more than 1,400 network sessions, 800 command-line artifacts and 1,200 files downloaded, uploaded or created. Confirmed activity ran from October 17 to November 2, 2026, hitting roughly 24 Security Management servers at 22 organizations in 17 countries. GreyNoise assesses that the actor used agentic harnesses to weaponize bugs, find reachable assets and automate exploitation before switching to hands-on-keyboard work. The entry set is described only as multiple vulnerabilities; the named management bypass defenders can act on is CVE-2026-16232, disclosed by Check Point on July 22, 2026 as exploited in the wild when Management was exposed directly to the internet without Trusted Clients restrictions. Rapid7 traced that bug to a caller-supplied SIC distinguished name accepted in place of the authenticated peer certificate, which yields an application token, then a SmartConsole admin session, across FWM/CPMI on TCP 18190 and CPM SOAP on TCP 19009.

Owning the management server means owning the policy plane for every gateway it controls: firewall rules, administrator permissions, VPN configuration and logging can all be changed with one stolen admin session. That makes roughly two dozen confirmed servers a larger blast radius than the host count suggests, since each one governs fleets of gateways. Censys counted 3,836 hosts presenting the default management and log-server role identity in September, a role count rather than a vulnerable count, and The Hacker News tallied five critical unauthenticated management flaws disclosed since July 22, so clearing CVE-2026-16232 alone does not close the category. This story outranks today's other vulnerability chatter because a July zero-day with public exploit code from July 28 was still producing fresh victims in November across energy, government, finance and transport. The event worth dwelling on is the intruder who patched a server after breaking in to keep rivals out: an unexplained clean hotfix state can be evidence of occupation, not proof of recovery.

Patch on-premises Security Management and Multi-Domain Management with the July 22 jumbo hotfix: R82.10 Take 36 or later, R82 Take 118 or later, R81.20 Take 158 or later. Check Point lists no fixed Take for R81.10 and older branches, which need a support case, while Smart-1 Cloud is already protected. Where patching waits, restrict Trusted Clients to named addresses, block management access from untrusted IPs and keep control-connection implied rules enabled; Rapid7 found the permissive Trusted Clients default exploitable in testing. Hunt `Authentication method: application token` under Logs and Monitor, Logs and Events, Audit Logs View, pivot on `151.241.99.207`, `151.241.99.233`, `158.62.198.182`, `192.142.10.99`, `139.28.37.250` and `194.213.18.137`, and review administrator, API and application-token activity plus `DLESESSIONID` and `CLIENTSESSIONID` sessions on TCP 19009 at `/cpmws/` even after patching.

The assessment changes if GreyNoise extends confirmed activity beyond November 2 or ties GT13 to specific exploited CVEs instead of an unspecified set, because that would turn a broad hardening exercise into a single patch-and-hunt priority. It also changes if CVE-2026-91843 or another pre-authentication management flaw enters CISA KEV or gains in-the-wild telemetry; Censys recorded no such evidence on September 16, and that absence is the current ceiling on this story.
