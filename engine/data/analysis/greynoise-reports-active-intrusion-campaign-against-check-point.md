GreyNoise tracks an ongoing intrusion campaign it calls GT13 against Check Point Security Management servers. The activity includes scanning, exploitation and post-compromise control from 99 IP addresses, many of them ExpressVPN egresses or compromised victim Check Point devices. GreyNoise counted more than 1,400 network sessions, 800 command line artifacts and 1,200 files downloaded, uploaded or created. The firm began tracking the campaign in early October and links it to at least 22 organizations in energy, engineering, financial services, government, manufacturing, mining and transportation across 17 countries, including the US, UK, Australia, Germany, France and Hong Kong. BleepingComputer puts the observed window at October 17 to November 2 and roughly two dozen compromised management servers.

GreyNoise assesses that the actor used agentic harnesses to weaponize vulnerabilities, find reachable assets and automate exploitation, then switched to hands-on-keyboard work. In one incident the intruder forged a signed admin session cookie to gain administrative access to an HFS server. In another, the intruder patched a server after breaking in, apparently to keep rival actors out. The campaign is therefore not mass scanning alone: it combines automated discovery with manual access, cookie forgery and changes to the compromised host itself.

The infrastructure is both source and victim, since GreyNoise says many of the 99 addresses were compromised Check Point devices. That makes device inventory and egress logs as important as perimeter blocks. Watch for additional management servers with unexplained administrative sessions or unexpected patch state changes, for further reporting on which vulnerabilities were exploited, and for any overlap with the victim sectors GreyNoise named.
