Meta suspended sharing in its Muse AI assistant after researchers found that conversation titles were embedded directly in subdomains of shareable links. Passive DNS monitoring recorded those hostnames, letting outsiders enumerate roughly 6,800 supposedly private conversations with a single query. Exposed material included corporate revenue reviews, loan applications, internal schedules, and personal notes. The issue, dubbed GhostShares, affected public sharing links whose identifiers appeared in searchable hostnames.

The flaw is a structural URL construction error rather than a single buggy endpoint, so the lesson extends beyond Muse: any AI assistant that places user content in hostnames can leak it through passive DNS. Meta said on October 20 that it was suspending the feature while investigating fixes. Watch for whether Meta re-enables sharing with path-based or opaque identifiers, and whether other agents with similar link schemes face the same exposure.
