Push Security researchers tracked a malvertising campaign they call Adception that abuses Bing's click-tracking redirects inside Google search ads. A sponsored result for searches like "claude mac" displayed the legitimate bing.com domain instead of an attacker-controlled one, then passed through Google's ad redirect to Bing's bing.com/ck/a tracking endpoint, which forwarded the victim to a compromised WordPress site belonging to a South American retailer. That site redirected to claude-desk-code[.]com, a fake Claude download page built to trick macOS users into running malicious commands under the ClickFix scheme.

The nesting is the point. Routing the ad through Bing's trusted tracking domain is designed to defeat advertising security checks, and the campaign layers cloaking so scanners and direct visitors never see the payload. Fake AI installers pushed through sponsored search results have become one of the most reliable infection chains this year, and this variant shows the ad ecosystems' own infrastructure being weaponized as cover.

Watch for copycats reusing the Bing redirect trick against other brand queries, and for Google and Bing tightening validation on click-through URLs in response. Users should avoid installing software from sponsored search results entirely and navigate to vendor domains directly. Defender teams can flag flows that chain search ad redirects through unrelated compromised sites before landing on a download page.
