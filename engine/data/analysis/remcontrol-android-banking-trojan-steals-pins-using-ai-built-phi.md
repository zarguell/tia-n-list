RemControl is a previously undocumented Android banking trojan that Group-IB disclosed on September 23, 2026, run as a malware-as-a-service platform whose operator the researchers track as UNKK. The first samples reached VirusTotal on July 19, 2026, but the C2 domain bnbnhura[.]top was registered on May 12, 2026, so the infrastructure was staged about two months before the first build went live. Delivery is malvertising, not an exploit: fake Google Play pages impersonating the TVTap IPTV app, with an Italian campaign serving the APK only to mobile User-Agents on Italian IP addresses. On install the dropper takes BIND_VPN_SERVICE and routes com.android.vending into a null tunnel so Play Protect cannot scan in real time, then signs the payload with a fresh Keystore certificate per installation, subject CN=Android Debug, O=Android, C=US. The payload rides Android's Accessibility Service to inject full-screen overlays fetched from C2 at /overlay/{result_id} and POSTs the captured PINs, mobile banking codes, and card expiry dates back to that same endpoint.

The important read here is not another overlay trojan, it is the production quality behind it. Group-IB found the C2 proxy's FastAPI documentation exposed, and it describes the credential endpoints as handling quiz answers and parental-monitoring, a cover story alongside an AI assistant response left verbatim in a live phishing overlay. That combination shows AI-assisted tooling is already good enough to scaffold bank-grade fraud infrastructure cheaply, which changes how fast new operations of this type can appear. The event count of 30-plus targeted institutions across Italy, France, Spain, Poland, Portugal, Canada, and GCC states also understates the exposure, because the target list is provisioned per device at registration, meaning any infected phone can be retargeted at banks not yet in the list without reinfection. With the dropper shipping more than 30 languages and affiliate tracking built into the panel, expansion into new countries and new operators is a configuration change rather than a rebuild, and ToxicPanda, which already uses the same VPN-based Play Protect suppression, shows the ceiling at 349 apps across 16 countries.

There is no patch because nothing is broken; this is a fraud operation, so the controls are app-origin and permission based. Enforce Play Protect, block installs from unknown sources through MDM, and treat any Accessibility Service grant to an app without an accessibility purpose as a compromise indicator, which matches Group-IB's own guidance. The specific hunt: devices holding an active VPN service whose blocked-package list contains com.android.vending, recurring signing certificates under subject CN=Android Debug, O=Android, C=US paired with ever-changing hashes, and accessibility events that fire back-navigation the moment a user opens app management, accessibility settings, or factory reset screens. On the network side, block and alert on tvtap-hd[.]app, tvtap-liveapp[.]com, and bnbnhura[.]top, and keep the dead-drop Telegram channels telegram[.]me/ftestera and telegram[.]me/+Psyt04xu-cRjMTg0 plus Meta Pixel IDs 997470916598588 and 1909605966397328 as pivots for campaign tracking. The threadlinqs entry TL-2026-2625 carries 25 IoCs and 9 detections worth pulling, but per-install re-signing makes hash lists rot within days, so behavioral and infrastructure indicators carry the detection weight here.

Two developments would move this assessment. Confirmation that the affiliate panel is renting beyond UNKK would turn a contained operation into a distributed one, and the exposed documentation suggests the platform is built for exactly that. New campaigns outside the currently observed Italy, France, Spain, Poland, Portugal, Canada, and GCC footprint would confirm the 30-plus language dropper is being exercised, and the continued spread of VPN-permission Play Protect suppression across malware families, already visible in ToxicPanda, would signal a broader drop in the cost of defeating Play Store scanning.
