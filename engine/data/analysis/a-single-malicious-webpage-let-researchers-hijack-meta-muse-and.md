Meta's Muse runs each user's agent in a dedicated cloud VM with a systemd-nspawn runtime container, a host-side Sentinel egress gate and credentials held outside the runtime cell. NebuSec showed that asking Muse to export an attacker-controlled page through its bundled Chromium gave code execution inside the container. The PDF-export process ran without an IPC sandbox. The chain used V8 issue 554034656, then generated kernel exploits against a 7.0.0-39-generic Hatch build to escape the container and reach root in the user VM.

Sentinel is described as a DNS and proxy service at 198.19.0.1 on ports 53 and 3128, with eBPF redirection, content screening and 60-minute egress budgets of 2 MiB per originator and 5 MiB per domain. NebuSec found its outbound classification let GET-only traffic carrying conversation history and stored memories leave without an approval prompt, and that the researchers could also alter those records. After VM root, processes outside the container could bypass Sentinel and reach the unlocked 100 GiB LUKS2/Btrfs volume, secure credential storage and Sentinel itself. Chromium versions observed were 152.0.7977.82 in early October and 155.0.8059.80 later.

The work was a laboratory demonstration inside one user's VM, not an attack on real users. NebuSec says there was no hypervisor or cross-tenant escape and that Meta fixed the issues before publication. Meta's launch post had opened the Muse bug bounty with awards up to $300,000, including up to $130,000 for prompt-injection issues affecting one user.

The chain is a concrete example of an AI agent's browsing and export features becoming the entry point for host-level privilege escalation, even when the agent harness itself holds no real credentials. Watch for Meta's fix notes, any revision to Sentinel's classification of outbound GET requests, and whether follow-up research tests the same PDF-export path after patching. Claims of real-world Muse compromise are not supported by the reporting.
