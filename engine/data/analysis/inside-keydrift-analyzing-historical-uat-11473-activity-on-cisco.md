Cisco Talos identified UAT-11473, a highly sophisticated APT actor that exploited CVE-2026-20127, a maximum-severity authentication bypass in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Controllers and Managers, to deploy a custom malware framework called KeyDrift. The flaw carries a CVSS score of 10 and was patched by Cisco in February 2026; it allows remote, unauthenticated attackers to bypass peering authentication and gain admin rights, which can be used to alter SD-WAN configuration. Observed targeting spans space and satellite services, defense and aerospace technology, and policy research organizations. KeyDrift operates inside SD-WAN control-plane processes, intercepting decrypted control traffic and exfiltrating keying material; its primary component, PolyFlame, is a memory-resident module deployed through modified runit service paths.

Talos assesses with high confidence that UAT-11473's infrastructure overlaps considerably with Volt Typhoon's "Silent Train" covert access network, though it drew no definitive link. Lumen's Black Lotus Labs tracked Silent Train for three years and found it is not a standalone network but rotating chains of compromised SOHO routers, IoT devices, and enterprise infrastructure assembled and dismantled daily, with chains rebuildable in under two minutes. Volt Typhoon used Silent Train to exploit at least four zero-day vulnerabilities against edge and management infrastructure across telecom, government, defense, utilities, satellite, IT, MSP, transportation, aviation, and emergency services, with heavy focus on California, Texas, and Hawaii. Hundreds of victim notifications were issued, and the FBI previously disrupted the group's KV-Botnet staging ground in December 2023. Organizations running Cisco SD-WAN should verify they are patched against CVE-2026-20127 and audit for KeyDrift artifacts such as modified runit paths.
