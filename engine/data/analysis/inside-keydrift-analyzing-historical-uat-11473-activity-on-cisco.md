Cisco Talos attributed a historical campaign to UAT-11473, a highly sophisticated APT, that exploited CVE-2026-20127, a critical authentication bypass in Cisco SD-WAN Catalyst Controller instances (CVSS 10.0) disclosed in February 2026. The flaw sits in the vdaemon service and lets an unauthenticated remote attacker bypass peering authentication and gain administrative privileges, with in-the-wild exploitation confirmed. GreyNoise observed eight distinct surges of Cisco-targeting attacker activity before the February disclosure, the earliest beginning 39 days beforehand.

UAT-11473 installed KeyDrift, a custom malware framework designed for long-term persistent access and automated exfiltration of sensitive keying material inside Cisco SD-WAN control-plane processes. It intercepts decrypted control traffic, extracts control-session metadata and data-plane keying material, and stages or transmits the collected material in encrypted records. Its primary component, PolyFlame, is a memory-resident module deployed through modified runit service paths and injected into legitimate running vdaemon processes, acting as a backdoor and relaying commands to other nodes. Observed targeting spans space and satellite services, defense and aerospace technology, and policy research.

Talos assesses with high confidence that UAT-11473's operating infrastructure overlaps considerably with Volt Typhoon's Silent Train covert access network. Lumen's Black Lotus Labs, which has tracked Silent Train for three years, describes it as rapidly rotating chains of compromised SOHO routers, IoT devices, and enterprise infrastructure that Volt Typhoon assembles and dismantles daily, rebuilding a new chain in under two minutes. Black Lotus Labs notified hundreds of victims and confirmed at least four zero-day vulnerabilities exploited against telecom providers, government agencies, defense contractors, universities, utilities, satellite providers, IT firms, MSPs, transportation, aviation, and emergency services, with the heaviest concentration in California, Texas, and Hawaii. Volt Typhoon has previously targeted US critical infrastructure across communications, energy, transportation, and water systems, seeking persistent access for potential disruption during a future crisis. Defenders running Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN should patch CVE-2026-20127, hunt for modified runit service paths and injected vdaemon processes, and review historical controller access for the pre-disclosure exploitation window.
