Cisco Talos identified UAT-11473, a highly sophisticated APT actor that exploited CVE-2026-20127, a maximum-severity (CVSS 10.0) authentication bypass in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Controllers and Managers, to deploy a custom malware framework called KeyDrift. The flaw, in the vdaemon peering-authentication path, allows remote, unauthenticated attackers to bypass authentication and gain admin rights, which can be used to alter SD-WAN configuration. Cisco disclosed it in February 2026 and CISA added it to the KEV catalog following active exploitation reports. Talos assesses with high confidence that UAT-11473's infrastructure overlaps considerably with Volt Typhoon's "Silent Train" covert access network, though it stopped short of a definitive attribution. Observed targeting spans space and satellite services, defense and aerospace technology, and policy research organizations.

KeyDrift operates inside SD-WAN control-plane processes, intercepting decrypted control traffic and exfiltrating keying material and control-session metadata. Its primary component, PolyFlame, is a memory-resident module deployed through modified runit service paths and can relay commands to other compromised controllers. GreyNoise observed eight distinct surges of Cisco-targeting attacker activity beginning 39 days before the February disclosure, compressing to two days before publication, indicating sustained pre-disclosure exploitation. A new BleepingComputer report ties the same CVE-2026-20127 flaw to active exploitation deploying KeyDrift malware on SD-WAN controllers.

Lumen's Black Lotus Labs tracked Silent Train for three years and found it is not a standalone network but rotating chains of compromised SOHO routers, IoT devices, and enterprise infrastructure assembled and dismantled daily, rebuildable in under two minutes. Volt Typhoon used Silent Train to exploit at least four zero-day vulnerabilities against edge and management infrastructure across telecom, government, defense, utilities, satellite, IT, MSP, transportation, aviation, and emergency services, with heavy focus on California, Texas, and Hawaii. Hundreds of victim notifications were issued, and the FBI disrupted the group's KV-Botnet staging ground in December 2023. Organizations running Cisco SD-WAN should verify they are patched against CVE-2026-20127 and audit controllers for KeyDrift artifacts such as modified runit service paths and anomalous control-plane processes.
