Cisco Talos attributed a sophisticated campaign to UAT-11473, an advanced persistent threat actor that exploited CVE-2026-20127, a critical authentication bypass (CVSS 10.0) in the vdaemon service of Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Controller and Manager instances, to gain administrative access and deploy a custom malware framework called KeyDrift. CVE-2026-20127 was disclosed on February 25, 2026 and added to CISA KEV; Cisco confirmed in-the-wild exploitation. UAT-11473's targeting spans space and satellite services, defense and aerospace technology, and policy research organizations.

KeyDrift is designed for long-term persistent access and automated exfiltration of sensitive keying material. Its primary component, PolyFlame, is a memory-resident module deployed through modified runit service paths and injected into legitimate vdaemon processes, where it intercepts decrypted control traffic and extracts control-session metadata and data-plane keying material. Talos assesses with high confidence that UAT-11473's operating infrastructure overlaps considerably with Volt Typhoon's "Silent Train" covert access network, though it made no definitive link. GreyNoise telemetry showed eight distinct surges of Cisco-targeting activity beginning 39 days before the February advisory, compressing to 2 days before publication, indicating sustained pre-disclosure exploitation.

Defenders should verify SD-WAN controllers and managers are patched for CVE-2026-20127, hunt for PolyFlame and modified runit service paths, and review control-plane keying material for compromise. Even patched systems may have been compromised during the pre-disclosure window, so credential and key rotation and historical log review are warranted.
