Check Point disclosed two critical vulnerabilities in its Quantum VPN certificate handling on September 9, 2026. CVE-2026-85102 is an improper certificate trust validation flaw during VPN negotiation. CVE-2026-85103 is a heap buffer overflow in ASN.1 certificate decoding. Both carry a CVSS of 9.8 and allow unauthenticated remote code execution on Security Gateways and Quantum Security Management servers. Affected branches are R81.20 (Take 165 and below), R82 (Take 125 and below), and R82.10 (Take 43 and below). Check Point began rolling out Live Patch on September 9, but has not yet published the fixed Jumbo Hotfix Take numbers for each branch. No exploitation has been observed and no IOCs have been released.

The two new CVEs land in a rough stretch for Check Point's certificate handling. CVE-2026-50751 (CVSS 9.3), a certificate validation bypass in deprecated IKEv1 workflows, was added to CISA KEV on June 8 with a federal deadline of June 11. CVE-2026-16232 (CVSS 9.8), an authentication bypass in SmartConsole that grants full admin access, hit KEV on July 22. Both were used in real attacks. The new pair is not yet in KEV and Check Point says there is no known exploitation, but the pattern of high-severity VPN and management-server flaws within a single quarter raises the stakes for organizations running Check Point infrastructure.

Defenders running R81.20, R82, or R82.10 should verify that Live Patch deployment is complete on every gateway and management server. Organizations on R81.10 or older branches have less clear remediation paths; the advisory suggests disabling implied VPN rules as a mitigation, but customer reports indicate this guidance is incomplete. No permanent patch builds have been published yet, and several customers reported broken download links on announcement day.
