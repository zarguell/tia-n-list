Chinese-speaking threat operators ran a second intrusion campaign using commercial AI models as operational components. The targets included Taiwan's Kuomintang Party History Archives, Indonesia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, government systems in mainland China, and industrial organizations in Vietnam. Researchers linked five attacker workspaces through a shared SOCKS proxy endpoint at 152.42.200[.]25. The infrastructure ran SecFlow, an AI orchestration framework that routed tasks to specialized AI workers for reconnaissance, exploitation, data collection, and reporting.

SecFlow allowed operators to swap between Claude, Qwen, and DeepSeek models without changing the task workflow. The AI models did not independently compromise systems. Instead, they organized and automated conventional offensive activity: vulnerability scanning, credential testing, exploitation attempts, webshell deployment, evidence collection, and report generation. Researchers found eight CVEs in active attack workflows, including exploits for Log4Shell, Spring4Shell, Ghostcat, Apache Shiro deserialization, Grafana path traversal, Nexus path traversal, and Nacos authentication bypass.

This is a distinct campaign from an earlier July operation that used Claude Code and DeepSeek against government and financial targets. The pattern of multiple campaigns using the same general approach suggests this is becoming a standard operational playbook rather than a one-off experiment. The AI models serve as force multipliers that lower the skill floor for conducting multi-stage intrusions. Organizations in the Asia-Pacific region, particularly government and education sectors, should treat this as an active and evolving threat.
