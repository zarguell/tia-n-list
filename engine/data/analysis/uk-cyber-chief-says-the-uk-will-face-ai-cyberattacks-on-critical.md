Richard Horne, head of the UK's National Cyber Security Centre, warned that the UK is likely to face AI-driven cyberattacks on critical infrastructure by 2028, as criminals and states including China and Russia increasingly use AI to scale their operations. The warning accompanied the NCSC's annual cyber report, which identifies Chinese cyber activity as the most sophisticated and sustained challenge facing the UK and notes Russia's use of campaigns below the threshold of armed conflict. Horne cautioned that AI will let long-tolerated vulnerabilities be discovered and exploited at a scale previously unimaginable, including AI-powered malware for ransomware.

The report frames AI less as a new attack class than as an accelerant: states and criminals will use it to increase the scale and efficiency of existing hostile operations. Horne placed responsibility on business and organizational leaders who have "got away with tech on the cheap," warning they will have to answer for shortages, outages, and business failures if they do not invest in cybersecurity and retire outdated technology. Iran's targeting of individuals seen as threats to the regime is also noted.

What to watch is whether UK operators of critical national infrastructure treat this as a procurement and patching deadline rather than a forecast. The 2028 horizon is close enough that asset inventory, vulnerability management, and AI-assisted defense adoption need to start now, particularly in sectors with legacy industrial control systems.
