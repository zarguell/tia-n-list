The Department of Homeland Security is banning DeepSeek and its affiliates from US civilian federal agencies. A Federal Register notice cites the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Act and a Federal Acquisition Security Council review to bar purchases from parent company Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence. The notice includes a June 26 letter from DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin ordering exclusion, showing the ban has been in effect for months. Mullin cited "multiple threat vectors" including data storage in China, where national security laws allow government access, collection of personally identifiable information, tracing of digital fingerprints, and potential data transfer through the prompt-and-response process.

The order applies not only to DeepSeek's models but also to third-party products built with them. The policy aligns with a growing US government pattern of restricting Chinese AI providers over supply chain and data-sovereignty risks. Agencies using embedded AI components in procurement or internal tools should audit vendors for DeepSeek dependencies, since the ban covers any technology built with DeepSeek assistance.
