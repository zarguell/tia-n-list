The Department of Homeland Security is excluding DeepSeek (Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence) and its affiliates from federal civilian agency IT systems under the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Act, following a Federal Acquisition Security Council review. Secretary Markwayne Mullin signed exclusion and removal order 2026-01 on 2026-07-24, with a letter dated 2026-06-26 indicating the ban had been in effect for months before its public Federal Register publication in October 2026. The order covers not only DeepSeek's models and chatbot/API services but also third-party products and services built using DeepSeek technology, and it applies only to federal civilian agencies, not private or consumer use.

DHS cited multiple threat vectors: DeepSeek models store data on servers in China where national security laws give the government wide latitude to request data from domestic companies; models hosted directly by DeepSeek collect personally identifiable information and trace users' digital fingerprints; and the prompt-and-response process may transfer sensitive data to the Chinese government. The notice also raised potential malware concerns, citing a February 2025 NowSecure report that identified security vulnerabilities in the DeepSeek iOS app, including unencrypted data transmissions and weak encryption.

What to watch: whether other federal agencies or allied governments adopt similar exclusions for DeepSeek-derived products, and whether the NowSecure findings lead to further mobile-security actions.
