The Department of Homeland Security issued Exclusion and Removal Order 2026-01, signed by Secretary Markwayne Mullin on July 24, 2026, barring all civilian federal agencies from acquiring or using DeepSeek products, including third-party tools built on DeepSeek models. The public Federal Register notice, set to publish October 20, 2026, cites the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Act of 2018 and a Federal Acquisition Security Council review, and includes Mullin's June 26 letter showing the exclusion has been in force for months. The order targets parent company Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence and covers data storage in China, collection of personally identifiable information, tracing of digital fingerprints, and potential data transfer through the prompt-and-response process.

This is a supply-chain and data-sovereignty directive rather than a vulnerability patch, which makes it distinct from typical security advisories. Every civilian federal agency is exposed, not just IT procurement shops, because the ban explicitly reaches embedded AI components in third-party products, and Mullin's letter identifies multiple threat vectors unique to DeepSeek's country of origin. The July 24 signing date means agencies have been operating under a hidden exclusion for nearly three months, so any DeepSeek integration discovered today is already a compliance violation, not just a future risk.

Defenders should audit vendor contracts for Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence and any embedded DeepSeek models, check procurement records back to June 26, and remove or isolate affected systems immediately. Network restrictions should block DeepSeek-hosted endpoints used for model inference, and log hunts should look for references to DeepSeek API endpoints, embedded model weights, or vendor documentation naming DeepSeek assistance.
