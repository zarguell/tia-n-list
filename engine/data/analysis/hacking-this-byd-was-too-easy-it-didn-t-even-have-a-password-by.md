Australia's ABC Four Corners program demonstrated that a BYD Shark 6 hybrid ute could be trivially hacked by cybersecurity researcher Dan Hreszczuk of Fortify Labs. The vehicle's connected systems had no password protection, allowing the researcher to gain remote access to vehicle functions including headlights, wipers, speakers, door locks, and the infotainment screen within two weeks of analysis.

The attack chain began with an unauthenticated access point that Hreszczuk described as "the front door being left open." From there, he accessed the vehicle's digital systems and demonstrated remote control of safety-relevant functions: killing headlights on a dark road, activating wipers at full speed, and spraying windshield washer fluid while driving. He also demonstrated surveillance capabilities, including real-time vehicle tracking via GPS and remote activation of the cabin microphone to record conversations.

The surveillance scenario was demonstrated by recording a phone call where the driver discussed internet banking credentials, then using edited audio played through the car's speakers to trigger Siri and extract personal information. Hreszczuk noted he could not access brakes or cameras due to better protections on those systems, but the demonstrated capabilities represent serious safety and privacy risks.

Australia currently has no minimum cybersecurity standards for connected vehicles, meaning BYD is not required to maintain software updates or risk management systems. BYD stated data is stored in Australia and would not be handed to Chinese authorities. Former Australian national cyber security adviser Alastair MacGibbon said a cabinet minister should not be able to own a Chinese EV given the demonstrated capability. The UK military has already banned Chinese EVs from within 3 kilometers of sensitive sites.
