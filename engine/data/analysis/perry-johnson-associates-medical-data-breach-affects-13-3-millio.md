Perry Johnson & Associates (PJ&A) and four related companies reached a settlement with the New York Attorney General and three other state attorneys general over a medical data breach that exposed personal and medical information belonging to more than 13.3 million people, including roughly 4.2 million New York residents. The companies agreed to pay $300,000 and implement extensive security reforms, with an additional $9.7 million in penalties if they fail to meet the settlement terms. New York AG Letitia James announced the agreement on November 6 alongside the attorneys general of Connecticut, Michigan, and New Jersey.

The investigation describes basic security failures: remote access without multifactor authentication, nearly identical passwords circulated by email, sensitive files stored without file-level encryption, and security software that detected malicious activity but did not alert staff. The companies only learned of the attack when the attackers contacted them on May 2, 2023 and demanded payment. The affected count rose from earlier reporting of about 8.95 million patients to more than 13.3 million in the settlement documents, and the total could be higher.

This is a settlement-level case rather than a new intrusion, so the immediate watch item is compliance: whether PJ&A completes the mandated reforms and avoids the $9.7 million in suspended penalties. The settlement also sets a measurable baseline of what regulators expect for healthcare data security, particularly around MFA and file-level encryption.
