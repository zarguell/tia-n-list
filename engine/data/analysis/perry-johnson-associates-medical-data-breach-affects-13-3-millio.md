Perry Johnson & Associates (PJ&A) and four related companies settled with the New York Attorney General and the attorneys general of Connecticut, Michigan, and New Jersey over a 2023 medical data breach that exposed personal and medical information belonging to more than 13.3 million people, including roughly 4.2 million New York residents. The settlement, announced November 6, 2026, requires the companies to pay $300,000 and implement extensive security reforms, with up to $9.7 million more in penalties if they fail to meet its terms. The AG press release describes the deal as a $10 million settlement, reflecting the maximum exposure.

The investigation found basic security failures: remote access without multifactor authentication, nearly identical passwords circulated by email, sensitive files stored without file-level encryption, and security software that detected malicious activity but did not alert staff. The companies only learned of the attack when the attackers contacted them on May 2, 2023 and demanded payment. The affected count rose from earlier reporting of about 8.95 million patients to more than 13.3 million in the settlement documents, and the total could be higher.

This is a settlement-level case rather than a new intrusion, so the watch item is compliance: whether PJ&A completes the mandated reforms and avoids the suspended $9.7 million. The case also sets a measurable baseline of what regulators expect for healthcare data security, particularly around MFA and file-level encryption, and the 13.3 million figure makes it one of the larger US health data breach enforcement actions this year.
