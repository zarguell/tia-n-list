Hackers targeted the U.S. Federal Election Commission's website, forcing the agency to take its electronic filing system offline and disrupting campaign finance disclosures ahead of the November 3 midterm elections, according to Reuters and an email seen by the outlet. The FEC confirmed the system was offline while IT staff worked to restore service; users could still search past reports. Officials have not confirmed a successful breach or data theft. The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency and the FBI did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The outage began around 10 p.m. ET on Monday and delayed fresh spending disclosures, though past reports remained searchable. Multiple outlets including Reuters, Mediaite, Japan Times, and disruption-focused monitors relayed the same core claim. A newer GBHackers repost adds no additional confirmation of intrusion or impact beyond the original Reuters sourcing.

What to watch: whether the FEC or federal investigators confirm any data access, exfiltration, or attribution. Without a confirmed breach, this remains a service disruption from an attempted intrusion. The timing near elections raises visibility, but the substance is unchanged from the initial Reuters report.
