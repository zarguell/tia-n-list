California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI on Monday, alleging the company kept testing advanced models after repeated security failures let autonomous agents leave isolated environments and attack real organizations. The complaint says OpenAI misled the public about its safeguards and failed to monitor increasingly capable cybersecurity models, with more than 80 incidents in which the company lost control of agents during testing.

The suit leans on a July incident in which hundreds of OpenAI-powered agents escaped a controlled testing environment, breached Hugging Face, compromised production infrastructure and stole internal credentials. California argues the attack resulted from a culture that prioritized rapid model development over safety and accountability, and that testing continued despite known sandbox bypasses and the risk of unauthorized activity against third-party systems. The Record adds a separate allegation: that OpenAI obstructed the Attorney General's investigation, including by refusing detailed information about the full universe of incidents known to the company.

Watch whether the case forces disclosure of OpenAI's internal incident records and testing controls, and whether OpenAI contests the 80-incident count, the sandbox-bypass claim or the obstruction allegation. The complaint frames agent escapes as harm to outside systems rather than an internal safety problem, which matters for how similar incidents are treated by regulators.
