Chrome 157 reached the stable channel on November 3, 2026, patching 213 vulnerabilities, seven of them critical. The two named criticals with bounties are CVE-2026-121156, a buffer overflow in Dawn (WebGPU) reported by Zhenpeng (Leo) Lin at depthfirst on September 30 and paid $10,000, and CVE-2026-121229, memory corruption in ANGLE reported by @mfx on September 17 and paid $1,000. The remaining five criticals carry no bounty: CVE-2026-121246 (use-after-free in WebGL, Google, September 8), CVE-2026-121035 (use-after-free in Prerender, Xinyang Ge of Anthropic assisted by Claude, September 28), CVE-2026-121247 (buffer overflow in Dawn, Google, October 3), CVE-2026-121203 (use-after-free in Geometry, Xinyang Ge, October 7), and CVE-2026-121126 (use-after-free in Core, Wenbin Liu, October 8). The release also fixes 77 high-severity issues including CVE-2026-121107 ($10,000, incorrect authorization in BackgroundFetch) and CVE-2026-121197 ($2,000, out-of-bounds read in ANGLE), with $31,000 total paid and bounties undecided for 39 flaws. Google's advisory states no in-the-wild exploitation of any of these.

The ANGLE critical (CVE-2026-121229) can let a remote attacker run code outside the sandbox through a specially crafted HTML page, and it affects Chrome for Android as well as desktop, so any unpatched browser that renders attacker-controlled content is in scope. The weakness mix is dominated by authorization errors (51) and use-after-free (45), the classes that drive renderer-to-sandbox-escape chains, which is why a routine browser update here is a patching priority rather than background noise. Exposure is broad by construction: most users ride the stable channel, and Google is staging the rollout over days to weeks, so a large population remains on 156.x until the update lands. The AI-assisted discovery footprint is notable too, with Anthropic's Xinyang Ge credited on multiple critical and high bugs and OpenAI Codex Security on several more, signaling that the 213-fix volume reflects automated fuzzing scale rather than a one-off crisis.

Defenders should force the update rather than wait for the staged rollout: Chrome can lag if the browser is never fully closed or an extension blocks the update, so a manual check via About Google Chrome (Help menu) or Settings is warranted, relaunching to apply. Target versions are 157.0.8103.38 on Linux and 157.0.8103.38 or .39 on Windows and macOS, and 157.0.8103.38 on Android via Google Play. Hunt for browsers still on 156.x or early 157 builds (157.0.8103.15 or .16 from the October 22 early stable release) in asset inventories and browser management consoles, and treat any process rendering untrusted HTML on an unpatched build as exposure. No CVE-specific network signatures or IoCs are published in the available coverage, so version-based inventory and prompt patching are the controls that matter.

Watch for Google to lift the bug-detail restrictions once a majority of users are updated, which will expose the full exploitability of the seven criticals, and for any later reports of in-the-wild use, which would convert this from a patch priority to an incident response. Also watch for CVE assignments and NVD scoring to settle on the TBD bounties and for Chrome 158, due roughly two weeks out, to see whether the AI-assisted discovery pace continues.
