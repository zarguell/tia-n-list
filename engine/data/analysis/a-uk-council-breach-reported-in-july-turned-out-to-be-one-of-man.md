When King's Lynn and West Norfolk Borough Council disclosed a cyberattack in July 2026, it appeared to be an isolated incident. Hunt.io research now ties it, at moderate confidence, to a large-scale campaign exploiting CVE-2026-15409, a SonicWall SMA1000 vulnerability disclosed just two days before the exploitation began. The operator left their full toolkit exposed in an open directory, revealing the scope: roughly 250 targets, with credentials stolen from at least nine Active Directory domains. Confirmed victims span France, India, Italy, and the United States, though most were never publicly disclosed.

The campaign is notable for what it says about VPN appliance monitoring. The credential theft ran directly from SonicWall appliances, a device category many organizations treat as set-and-forget. Attackers scraped credentials at the network edge before defenders even had visibility into the compromise. The two-day gap between disclosure and mass exploitation underscores how quickly weaponization happens for edge devices.

Watch for follow-on activity from the stolen credentials. Organizations running SonicWall SMA1000 should verify patch status and audit authentication logs for anomalous sessions. The exposed toolkit also suggests additional targeting data may surface as researchers parse it further.
