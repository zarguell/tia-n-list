Researcher Gal Weizman disclosed the BragJack attack, which uses a single browser extension to hijack AI assistants across Chrome, Comet, Edge, Opera Neon, and Claude-in-Chrome. The attack earned $20,000 in bounties from Anthropic, Perplexity, Google, Microsoft, and Opera. The Hacker News provided independent coverage of the same research.

The attack exploits the way browser extensions interact with AI assistant sidebars and embedded agents. A malicious extension can intercept and manipulate the communication channel between the user and the AI assistant, effectively taking over the assistant's actions within the browser context. This is a novel attack class that targets the emerging category of browser-integrated AI, where assistants have access to page content, user sessions, and can perform actions on behalf of the user.

The broad cross-browser impact is the key takeaway. This is not a single vendor's bug but a design pattern issue in how browsers expose AI assistant APIs to extensions. Watch for browser vendors to restrict extension permissions around AI assistant interfaces, and for security teams to add browser extension auditing to their AI adoption risk assessments.
