CrowdStrike has signed agreements to acquire Fiddler AI and Dreadnode in deals reportedly worth a combined $495 million, per The Wall Street Journal: roughly $400 million for Fiddler and $95 million for Dreadnode. CrowdStrike has not disclosed financial terms itself; payment is mostly cash with a stock portion subject to vesting, and closing is expected in the fourth quarter of its fiscal year 2027, ending January 31, 2027. Fiddler, founded in 2018 in Palo Alto with $100 million in total funding, becomes Falcon Observe and monitors AI agents for behavioral drift, hallucinations, and audit trails. Dreadnode, founded in 2023 with $14 million in Series A funding, provides automated security testing of AI models and agents before deployment.

The acquisitions target a gap that widens as enterprises adopt AI agents: these systems can hold valid credentials and approved tools yet still make bad decisions, ignore instructions, or fabricate completed work, while traditional security testing built for deterministic code does not reach the model or reasoning layer. Fiddler works post-deployment, evaluating agents before production, tracing decisions across agent systems, flagging drift and made-up answers, and producing audit records, with support for frameworks such as LangGraph, Amazon Bedrock, and Google ADK. Dreadnode works pre-deployment, probing models and agents for weaknesses.

For defenders, the deal signals that AI agent observability and pre-deployment testing are consolidating into the endpoint and cloud security stack rather than remaining point solutions. Watch whether CrowdStrike delivers Falcon Observe on schedule and whether the integration covers agent frameworks beyond the named three, since coverage gaps would leave blind spots in multi-agent pipelines. The January 31, 2027 close date and the mostly-cash structure also mean the transaction is unlikely to be disrupted by financing conditions, but integration risk remains the variable to track.
