The Iran-aligned Handala Hack operation has deployed two previously undocumented malware families: CRUDEEXCLUDE, which abuses Microsoft Defender's exclusion mechanism to disable protections, and HEAVYGRAM, a surveillance tool that uses Telegram channels as command-and-control infrastructure. The campaign targets Iranian dissidents, journalists, and perceived opponents of the Iranian government through targeted social engineering followed by multi-stage loader deployment.

Group-ib's analysis of HEAVYGRAM's Telegram-based C2 reveals the malware exfiltrates data and receives commands through Telegram bots and channels, a technique that blends into normal traffic and avoids traditional C2 domain takedown. CRUDEEXCLUDE adds Defender exclusions to prevent detection before HEAVYGRAM is installed. The combination gives the operator persistent surveillance capability with minimal detection surface. U.S. government disclosures have previously linked Handala Hack to Iranian state interests.

This campaign is part of a broader trend of state-aligned actors using commercially available platforms (Telegram, in this case) for C2 to complicate takedown efforts. The Defender exclusion abuse via CRUDEEXCLUDE is notable because it exploits a legitimate administrative feature rather than a vulnerability, making it harder to patch away. Organizations with Iranian diaspora users or dissident communities should be aware of this targeting profile.
