Apify, a Czech web scraping and browser automation platform with roughly 80,000 customers including Microsoft, Samsung, T-Mobile, Accenture, Siemens, the European Commission, and Roche, disclosed a breach in which an unauthorized third party used a compromised credential on November 9 and 10, 2026 to obtain account credentials and access related accounts. The access path was closed and no further attacker activity was detected after November 11. Apify revoked some OAuth connections to external integrations (including GitHub) as a precaution, rotated critical internal secrets and API tokens, reset active sessions, and prompted 2FA resets; impacted customers' Apify API tokens and proxy passwords were rotated. The company said it found no indication that account data stored on the platform was affected, while a sample notification to affected customers referenced access to user PII, authentication data (hashed passwords and 2FA), and payout details. Apify is notifying affected users individually and continuing its investigation, with a post-mortem to follow.

The incident is a credential-compromise breach rather than an unpatched vulnerability, and the response suggests the attacker reached internal systems but not necessarily customer-stored scraped data. The rotation of OAuth connections and internal secrets limits downstream abuse of the compromised access.

Watch for the post-mortem to clarify scope, which accounts and data types were actually exposed, and whether the revoked OAuth connections were a precaution or a confirmed exposure. Customers should treat rotated tokens as the baseline and re-verify connected integrations.
