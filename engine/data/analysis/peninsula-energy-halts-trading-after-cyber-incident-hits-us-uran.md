Peninsula Energy asked the Australian Securities Exchange for a trading halt after a cyber incident hit its Lance Project in Wyoming. The company told the ASX the incident happened on Friday night and breached its operating system, while administrative systems in the United States and Australia were not affected. The filing says a preliminary investigation is under way and that the incident will temporarily affect operations. It does not say which technical systems were compromised or whether uranium production stopped.

The halt was requested until the earlier of normal trading on 25 November 2026 or an announcement about the incident. A later databreaches.net item repeats the same ASX disclosure, so the known facts still come from the company filing. There is no named actor, no CVE, no ransom claim and no leak notice in the available material.

The Lance Project sits in Crook County and uses in situ recovery to produce yellowcake for nuclear fuel. The United States added uranium to its critical minerals list in 2025, and the project is expected to become a key supplier. What to watch is the company's announcement: whether production control was interrupted, which operating systems were reached, and whether this was a targeted intrusion or opportunistic disruption. Until that detail exists this is an operational incident with confirmed trading impact, not a confirmed data breach.
