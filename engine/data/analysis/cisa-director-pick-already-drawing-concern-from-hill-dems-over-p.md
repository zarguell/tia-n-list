President Trump nominated Michael LoSapio, Palantir's chief information security officer since September 2024, to direct CISA on Monday, November 9, 2026, sending the nomination to the Senate the same day. CISA has had no Senate-confirmed director since Jen Easterly departed in January 2025; Sean Plankey, the first nominee, withdrew in April 2026 after more than a year without a vote. LoSapio would inherit an agency that has lost more than 1,000 of roughly 3,000 staff, about a third of its workforce, plus billions in budget since 2025, with Nick Andersen serving as acting director since February 2026 after Madhu Gottumukkala's reassignment. The action is a personnel nomination, but it lands on the agency that runs the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and sets federal civilian cybersecurity baselines, so the leadership question is operational for defenders, not just political.

The exposure is structural rather than personal. CISA is already a Palantir customer across DHS, so the office that judges the security of federal civilian systems would be led by the security chief of a vendor whose platforms run across the department, with ethics rules this administration has weakened and a one-year recusal as the main safeguard. That matters because CISA's leverage depends on perceived independence, and critical-infrastructure partners have already stopped trusting the agency after two years of layoffs, program freezes and political attacks. The Senate math cuts both ways: Republicans hold the chamber until January 2027, so confirmation is possible in the lame-duck session, but if it slips, an incoming Democratic majority chaired by Maggie Hassan in 2027 would re-scrutinize Palantir's DHS and ICE work and LoSapio would need renomination. Either outcome keeps CISA in leadership limbo through at least the first quarter of 2027, which is the real risk for defenders who depend on the agency's advisories and KEV cadence.

Because no patch applies, the work is governance and verification. Track the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee calendar for LoSapio's hearing and any holds, using Ron Wyden's telecom-cybersecurity-report hold on Plankey as the precedent for how one senator can stall a CISA nominee. Keep KEV-driven patching SLAs independent of agency staffing headlines, and if you consume CISA's free external vulnerability scanning or the Incident Coordination Group's threat sharing, confirm service continuity with your regional CISA contact rather than assuming it. For DHS and Palantir environments, document which security attestations rest on Palantir tooling so a leadership change cannot silently invalidate compliance evidence.

Two developments would change the assessment: a Senate confirmation vote in the lame-duck session, which would give CISA its first Senate-confirmed director since January 2025 and likely accelerate rebuilding, or a collapse of the nomination followed by Andersen hitting the statutory acting-director limit again, which would force another nominee or a legal workaround and extend the vacancy. Also watch Wyden's telecom report and Gary Peters' retirement: the former is the proven lever for holds, the latter removes the retiring Democrat who has been the most vocal on CISA independence.
