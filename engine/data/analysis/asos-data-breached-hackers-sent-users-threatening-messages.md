Attackers pushed a ransom note straight into ASOS customers' hands: on October 6, 2026 the ASOS mobile app delivered a push notification titled "ASOS HACKED", addressed to the company's DPO and IT, reading "we have fully compromised the Snowflake instance. Engage with us, or we will leak it." Thousands of users received it and ASOS shares fell almost 12 percent on the London Stock Exchange. The company confirmed unauthorized access to third-party customer communication platforms, and a group tracked as Xuanye Group claimed responsibility. ASOS later said attackers tricked an employee into handing over login credentials; Snowflake stated its platform was not the vulnerability.

Why it matters: the extortion channel was the retailer's own trusted app, which lends credibility to a claim ASOS has not corroborated. ASOS first described exposure as basic personal information, names and contact details, but a data sample the BBC received from the attackers included home addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, customer numbers, dates of birth, on-site search terms, and customer tenure. Payment-card data and account passwords are believed unaffected. The marketing stack is the suspected vector: ASOS used Simon AI, which runs on Snowflake, alongside Braze to trigger push notifications.

Watch whether ASOS confirms or denies the Snowflake compromise with specifics, the final affected-customer count, and regulator or GDPR follow-up in the UK and EU. The BBC sample showing richer data than first disclosed raises the risk that early reassurances get revised again. Any repeat delivery through the notification pipeline would indicate the intrusion path into the third-party platforms was not fully closed.
