TSMC disclosed that personal data on a subset of current and former employees was posted on an external website without authorization. In an SEC Form 6-K signed by CFO Wendell Huang and dated November 12, the company said the exposed records matched data stored in an independent cloud service provider's systems, that its own systems showed no evidence of compromise, and that operations and customer data were not involved. TSMC said it was notifying affected personnel, providing support, and had engaged the cloud vendor, investigating authorities, and a third-party cybersecurity firm for a joint investigation.

Subsequent reporting sharpened the scale and the extortion angle. MLQ.ai, citing Wccftech and Taiwanese press, put the leak at roughly 46,000 employee records with a ransom demand of 3 bitcoin, and a later report described a 6.9GB dataset that included national IDs, home addresses, and bank details offered for auction. TSMC itself has not confirmed a record count, and the figure traces to press reporting rather than the SEC filing.

The distinction TSMC draws, that the exposure originated in a cloud vendor's environment rather than its own network, shifts the control failure to third-party data handling while still leaving sensitive employee PII exposed and monetized. Watch whether TSMC confirms the 46,000-record figure and 6.9GB size, whether the cloud provider is named, and whether the auction data is verified as authentic, since the count and ransom details currently rest on secondary reporting.
