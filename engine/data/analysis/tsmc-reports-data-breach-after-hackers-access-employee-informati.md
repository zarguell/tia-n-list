TSMC confirmed that personal data on current and former employees was posted to an external website without authorization. In an SEC Form 6-K dated November 12, the company said the exposed records matched data stored in a cloud service provider's systems, that it found no evidence its own systems were compromised, and that operations and customer data were not involved. BleepingComputer, citing the company and the listing, reports a threat actor put 6.9GB of information up for auction, roughly 46,000 records that include national IDs, addresses, and bank details.

The extortion angle is specific. A seller using the alias SmithBianl leaked a sample text file on a hacker forum on November 8 and was asking 3 BTC for the full set, according to SecurityOnline. TSMC is notifying affected staff and has engaged a third-party cybersecurity firm. The company is the world's largest contract chipmaker, shipping about 15 million 12-inch-equivalent wafers and generating $122.4 billion in revenue last year for customers such as Apple and Nvidia.

Because the data is employee PII rather than manufacturing or customer data, the immediate risk falls on the affected staff through phishing and identity fraud. Watch whether the auction data is released or sold, whether the cloud provider is named, and whether TSMC confirms the 46,000-record and 6.9GB figures, which so far rest on press reporting rather than the SEC filing.
