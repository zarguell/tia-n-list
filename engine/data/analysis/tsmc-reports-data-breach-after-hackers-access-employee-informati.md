TSMC disclosed that personal data of roughly 46,000 current and former employees appeared on an external website without authorization. The company stated that its own systems were not compromised and that the exposed records matched information stored in a cloud service provider's systems, unrelated to company operations or customer data. TSMC filed an SEC Form 6-K signed by CFO Wendell Huang, engaged the cloud vendor, relevant authorities, and a third-party cybersecurity firm, and began notifying affected personnel. Press reports, not TSMC, put the record count at about 46,000 and said the seller demanded 3 bitcoin.

The incident is a third-party cloud exposure rather than a direct compromise of TSMC, which limits the operational impact but still exposes employee personal information held by a vendor. TSMC has not named the cloud provider or confirmed the record count, and the leak's scope beyond employee PII has not been detailed.

Watch for the cloud provider's identification, confirmation of how many records were exposed, whether the data is further distributed or sold, and whether the investigation attributes the leak to a specific threat actor. Any expansion beyond employee records would change the severity.
