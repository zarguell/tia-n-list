TSMC disclosed that personal information belonging to roughly 46,000 current and former employees was posted on an external website without authorization. The company says the exposed records match data held in a cloud service provider's systems and that there is no evidence its own systems were compromised; the breach is unrelated to TSMC operations or customer data. A seller is reportedly seeking 3 BTC for the records.

The incident is a third-party cloud exposure rather than a direct intrusion into TSMC, which limits the immediate operational impact but still puts employee PII in criminal hands. The distinction matters for response: TSMC's own environment appears intact, so containment centers on the cloud provider's access controls, logging, and credential hygiene, plus notifying affected employees. The seller's asking price and the record count are unverified claims from the criminal side.

What to watch is whether the cloud provider is identified and whether additional employee data surfaces. TSMC's statement that customer data and operations were unaffected should hold unless the cloud tenancy shared infrastructure with production systems. Employees should be alerted to phishing and identity-theft risk, and the cloud vendor's root cause and remediation timeline will determine whether this is an isolated leak or a broader access problem.
