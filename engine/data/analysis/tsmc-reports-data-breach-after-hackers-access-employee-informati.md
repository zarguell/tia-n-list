TSMC confirmed that personal information belonging to current and former employees was found on an external website without authorization. The company says the exposed records match data held in a cloud service provider's systems and that it found no evidence of compromise in its own systems. That framing puts the incident on the vendor side of the boundary, which matters for how the breach is assessed: TSMC's production environment and its IP are not implicated on the current evidence, but employee records were already outside the company's control.

A seller is claiming 46,000 records and asking 3 BTC for them, which is a low price for a dataset of this size and suggests either a small extract or a sample used to advertise a larger cache. TSMC has not confirmed the record count, and the number should be treated as a claim until the company or the marketplace listing is independently verified.

For defenders the useful signal is the concentration risk in cloud providers that hold HR and identity data for semiconductor staff. Worth watching: whether the provider is named, whether the exposure came from a misconfigured bucket or a compromised provider account, and whether the same provider serves other large employers, since a single vendor incident can cascade into credential-stuffing and targeted phishing against a workforce that has physical access to fabrication tooling.
