Unit 42 documented a scareware campaign built on cloud-hosted browser lockers: fake popups styled as social media and search engine alerts trap the victim's browser full screen using HTML5 Keyboard Lock, with alarming but hollow "infected" messages. The pages are hosted in the cloud rather than on compromised sites, which makes takedowns and blocklisting harder. Escaping is possible without paying anything, since nothing is actually installed; the lock is page content, not malware.

Ars Technica separately reported that Google ads are a delivery channel: sponsored results push fake tech support scams that lock up Mac and Windows browsers with the same alarming messages. Ads give the campaign legitimate-looking distribution at scale, aimed at users searching for support or security help.

The risk is mostly fraud and wasted support calls rather than infection, but the pattern matters: scareware pages that survive on cloud hosting and buy reach through search ads will keep resurfacing under new domains. Watch for the campaign expanding to other ad platforms, for cloning kits lowering the entry cost, and for any shift toward payloads with real consequences, such as remote access tooling pushed through fake support sessions.
