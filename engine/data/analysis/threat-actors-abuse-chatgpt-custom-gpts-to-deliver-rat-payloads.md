Huntress is tracking a ClickFix campaign that abuses ChatGPT Custom GPTs as the lure. Attackers programmed two Custom GPTs to answer user prompts with a Google Sites link leading to a ClickFix page that instructs visitors to run PowerShell commands, which pull a malicious MSI file in a multi-stage infection chain delivering RAT payloads. The GPTs were titled to impersonate legitimate products, one named "Plus 5.6" and posing as a community builder, and victims reached them through sponsored search results at the top of Google queries for ChatGPT, lending the legitimate chatgpt.com domain as apparent cover.

Huntress counts at least 40 infections, at least two tied directly to a Custom GPT instance. OpenAI took down the first GPT on September 25, and a second was discovered September 27, which the firm reads as threat actors finding the technique successful enough to keep using. The abuse needs no ChatGPT vulnerability; it weaponizes trust in the platform and in sponsored search placement.

This extends the ClickFix pattern from fake CAPTCHAs and update prompts into AI platform surface areas. Watch for additional malicious GPTs and takedown latency, whether OpenAI adds review or provenance signals for Custom GPTs, and copycat campaigns on other agent marketplaces. Users should reach ChatGPT by bookmark rather than search ads, and defenders can alert on PowerShell invocations following browser sessions on chatgpt.com.
