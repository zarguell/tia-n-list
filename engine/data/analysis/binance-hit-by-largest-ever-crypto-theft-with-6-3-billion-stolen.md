Attackers stole approximately $6.3 billion from Binance wallets across ten blockchains on October 29, 2026, in what blockchain intelligence firm TRM Labs calls the largest cryptocurrency theft on record. The amount exceeds the $1.5 billion Bybit heist from February 2025 by roughly four times. TRM Labs attributes the attack with high confidence to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-linked group. Binance says it has frozen around $3 billion with blockchain partners, will cover all losses via its emergency fund and company reserves, and points to a compromised DevOps engineer laptop as a possible entry point.

The FBI confirmed it is engaged with public and private partners on the incident but has not formally attributed the intrusion to North Korea. AP reported blockchain intelligence firms linking North Korea to the $6.3 billion hack. Additional outlets including malware.news and GBHackers published corroborating coverage. The scale and rapid transfer of roughly $6.36 billion in about 20 minutes make this a landmark event in exchange security.

Watch for confirmed attribution from US government sources, the outcome of Binance's internal investigation into the DevOps entry vector, and whether frozen assets are recovered. Monitor for follow-up campaigns using fake support or recovery offers targeting Binance users. Any formal attribution would also set a precedent for linking state actors to exchange-level theft at this scale.
