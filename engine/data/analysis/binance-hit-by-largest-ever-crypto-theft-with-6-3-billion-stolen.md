On October 29, 2026, attackers moved approximately $6.36 billion out of Binance wallets across ten blockchains, the largest cryptocurrency theft on record. TRM Labs attributes the attack with high confidence to North Korea's TraderTraitor group. Most funds moved in about 20 minutes. The amount exceeds the February 2025 Bybit heist of $1.5 billion by roughly four times. Binance halted withdrawals, froze roughly $3 billion with blockchain partner help, and promised to cover losses from its emergency fund and company reserves.

Initial investigation points to a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop as the possible entry point. The scale and speed of the multi-chain exfiltration suggest a well-prepared operation rather than opportunistic theft. The company described the event as a security incident and warned users to watch for fake support or recovery offers.

What to watch: whether blockchain tracing identifies additional downstream movement beyond the frozen $3 billion, and if the TraderTraitor attribution holds up under independent analysis. The use of a single engineer laptop as entry also raises questions about internal access controls and endpoint security at major exchanges.
