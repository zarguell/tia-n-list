CISA's "A Tale of Two SOCs" advisory, published August 25, 2026, contrasts red team assessments of two critical-infrastructure organizations: a Government Services and Facilities Sector entity and a Water and Wastewater Systems Sector facility. In both cases, the red team began with phishing and then exploited common Active Directory weaknesses, including the default Machine Account Quota setting and misconfigured AD Certificate Services templates. These conditions enabled privilege escalation, credential access, and lateral movement.

At Organization A (government services), the attack progressed without meaningful resistance. The red team obtained elevated domain privileges, reached sensitive business systems, accessed cloud resources, and eventually read SOC personnel email and deployed keyloggers on defenders' workstations. No effective response interrupted the operation. At Organization B (water sector), defenders contained initial access quickly, but the assessment still exposed significant identity and cloud security gaps.

The core lesson is that large security budgets and sophisticated tools cannot compensate for a SOC that fails to recognize, escalate, and contain malicious activity. AD Machine Account Quota abuse and weak ADCS template configurations remain reliable escalation paths. Organizations should audit these settings and ensure SOC workflows can detect and respond to identity-based attacks before they reach domain-wide scope.
