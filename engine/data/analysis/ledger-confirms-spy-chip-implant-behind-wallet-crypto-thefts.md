Ledger confirmed that at least one hardware wallet sold through CryptoBilis, a Malaysia-based reseller operating across Southeast Asia, contained an unauthorized hardware implant. The devices drained users' cryptocurrency wallets. Reports of fund losses tied to the reseller go back to September 16, when researchers showed a tampered Ledger X being sold for nearly half the original price.

Ledger opened an investigation on October 9 and asked CryptoBilis to stop selling and shipping its devices. The next day it confirmed hardware tampering on at least one impacted user's device. It is still unclear how or when the tampering happened, and Ledger is working with authorities to identify the perpetrator. The company is contacting impacted users and recommends anyone who bought a Ledger from this reseller not initialize a device that has not been set up yet.

The significance is the supply chain angle: the compromise sits below the software update channel users are told to trust, in the physical hardware itself. A genuine device with an implant defeats the core guarantee of a hardware wallet. Watch for Ledger's findings on how many devices were affected and how the implant was installed, plus any expansion of the case to other resellers or regions.
