Boston Scientific detected a cyberattack on August 25 that disrupted IT systems and caused a network outage affecting its ability to process and ship customer orders globally. The medical device manufacturer, which has 59,000 employees and 13 manufacturing facilities, activated incident response procedures and engaged external cybersecurity experts. As of August 31, the company says it does not know when all affected systems will be restored.

The attack impacted access to operating systems and business applications supporting operations, including order processing and shipment functions. Boston Scientific manufactures stents, catheters, pacemakers, defibrillators, endoscopes, and other devices used in minimally invasive procedures. Disruption to order processing in the medical device supply chain can have downstream effects on hospitals and surgical centers awaiting critical equipment.

This is a significant operational breach at a major medical device manufacturer. The extended timeline for restoration suggests substantial compromise of core business systems. Organizations in the healthcare and medical device supply chain should monitor for potential downstream impacts on product availability and assess whether their procurement channels depend on Boston Scientific systems.
