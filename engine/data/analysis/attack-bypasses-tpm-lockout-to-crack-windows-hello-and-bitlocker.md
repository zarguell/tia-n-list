Researchers at Technische Universität Berlin report that the TPM lockout protecting Windows Hello and BitLocker PINs can be reset on AMD-based PCs and some Intel PCs by someone who opens the computer case. That removes the anti-guessing limit that makes short PINs practical, leaving them open to brute force in days rather than years. In lab tests the team recovered a four-digit Hello PIN on an AMD laptop and a six-digit BitLocker PIN on an Intel laptop.

The attack requires physical access, so it matters most for machines that can be opened unattended: lost laptops, devices in shared offices, and systems in transit. The events give no CVE, affected TPM firmware list, or vendor fix, and the reported success depends on short PINs and on the specific AMD or Intel platform. Watch for vendor responses on lockout design and for guidance to move away from short PINs on hardware where case access defeats the TPM lockout.
