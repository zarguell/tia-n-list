Huntress incident responders traced a September 2026 intrusion to CVE-2025-4632, the path traversal in Samsung MagicINFO 9 Server before 21.1052 that lets an attacker write arbitrary files with system authority, a 9.8 CRITICAL bug per NVD. Every post-exploitation step ran as a child of tomcat9.exe, the Tomcat instance MagicINFO is deployed on: three tries to pull AnyDesk from 194.87.89[.]30:8899 (certutil, then PowerShell Invoke-WebRequest, both killed by Defender, the third landed), a password set on AnyDesk, a local administrator account named oldadministrator sharing the credential F@x2020!@#, and Defender silenced through SystemSettingsAdminFlows.exe. The payload stage is the novelty. Rather than dropping a binary, the actor ran Silent XMR Miner Builder.exe from the new user's Documents folder, driving csc.exe, cvtres.exe, donut.exe, tcc.exe, and MinGW64's cc1.exe and gcc.exe to compile the miner on the host, then launched it under explorer.exe with RandomX flags mining Monero to auto.c3pool.org:19999.

The striking part is the clock, not the technique. Samsung shipped the fix in SVP-MAY-2025, NVD published the entry on 13 May 2025, and CISA added the CVE to KEV on 22 May 2025 with a 12 June deadline, yet a victim was still exploitable in September 2026, and the same endpoint was re-reported eight days after the first remediation notice, meaning cleanup without patching simply invited the actor back. That gap defines the exposed population: signage servers are Windows boxes running a Java web app on an embedded Tomcat, forgotten in patch cycles and rarely watched, and they hand out SYSTEM on a platter with spare CPU to monetize. History compounds the risk, because the same servlet family was already "fixed" once as CVE-2024-7399 in August 2024 and the 2025 flaw only went public after SSD Secure Disclosure exhausted its 90-day window when Samsung called the report a duplicate. Anyone who patched in 2024 and moved on got false closure, and a foothold this quiet, complete with AnyDesk and a rogue admin, is a resale asset whether the buyer wants to mine or encrypt.

Remediation is version-bound: get MagicINFO 9 Server to 21.1052 or later and off the internet, then verify the account and access cleanup, since the May 2025 wave showed actors working within days of a public PoC. Hunt the process tree instead of the miner: children of tomcat9.exe spawning certutil.exe, powershell.exe with Invoke-WebRequest, or anydesk.exe; compiler tooling (csc.exe, cvtres.exe, tcc.exe, cc1.exe, gcc.exe, donut.exe) under an unsigned builder in a user profile Documents folder; local account oldadministrator; AnyDesk under C:\ProgramData; and Event ID 7045 service creations referencing C:\MagicInfo Premium\tomcat\bin, the persistence pattern from May 2025 (service PHP5.3.8, binaries php-cli.exe and php-fpm.exe fetched from 185.225.226[.]53). Block 194.87.89[.]30 and 185.225.226[.]53 at the perimeter. Any explorer.exe carrying mining arguments such as --algo="rx/0" or --url=auto.c3pool.org:19999 is malicious on its face, and the compiled sample in this incident hashes to 0d202e16408770e8b6cceb14e1e3e72946b154bf881d27fe33d0060315b30dd1.

Two developments would change the read. If endpoint-side compilation migrates from miners to ransomware or loaders under a Tomcat or other appliance parent, hash-based detection loses far more than miner coverage and MagicINFO-class exploits become general-purpose initial access inventory. And any recurrence of 194.87.89[.]30 or new MagicINFO CVEs in Samsung's SVP feed (the cadence continued with SVP-NOV-2025 covering CVE-2026-25201, unauthenticated file upload to RCE, and SVP-DEC-2025 covering CVE-2026-25203, local privilege escalation) would confirm a standing exploitation campaign against signage fleets rather than a one-off intrusion.
