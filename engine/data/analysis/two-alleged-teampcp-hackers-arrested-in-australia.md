The Australian Federal Police, working with the FBI and Western Australia Police, arrested two men on August 27 for their roles in TeamPCP, the group behind the longest-running software supply chain attack campaign on record. The suspects were identified by Australian media as Ruben Ian Thomson, 21, and Louis Michael Gaebler, 23, both from Western Australia. Thomson faces eight charges including four counts of unauthorized data modification and dealing in criminal proceeds exceeding $100,000. Gaebler faces six related counts. Both were arrested after property searches where electronic devices were seized for forensic analysis.

TeamPCP operated by injecting malicious code into open-source packages on GitHub and NPM, then using a self-propagating worm called Shai-Hulud to spread through developer environments. The group's compromises potentially affected over 1,000 organizations worldwide, with the AFP reporting 500,000 credentials stolen and at least 300GB of data exfiltrated. High-profile victims included Trivy, LiteLLM, Telnyx, SAP, TanStack packages, the European Commission, Mistral AI, OpenAI, and GitHub. Rather than a structured organization, investigators described TeamPCP as a loose-knit collective of threat actors frequenting the same hacking forums, Discord servers, and Telegram channels.

KrebOnSecurity reported that it had identified the 21-year-old suspect's identity back in June and had been communicating with him since then. The FBI's Brett Leatherman, assistant director of the Cyber Division, said further arrests have not been ruled out. The case represents a significant law enforcement disruption of a supply chain attack infrastructure that had been active since late 2025, though the loose collective structure means other participants may continue operating independently.
