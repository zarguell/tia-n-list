NIST published IR 8547, Transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography Standards, on 27 October 2026. The report maps algorithms known to be vulnerable to quantum attack to the post-quantum standards NIST finalized in August 2024, and sets out a migration framework with federal deadlines and implementation priorities. NIST mathematician Dustin Moody said the report describes different use cases and why one migration may take longer than another, rejecting a single strategy for all environments. Reporting on 17 November adds a GAO assessment that federal agencies are behind in building defenses against future quantum decryption.

This is guidance rather than an incident, and the source items carry no CVEs or confirmed exposure. What to watch is how agencies translate the migration framework into inventories of quantum-vulnerable cryptography, procurement requirements and dated transition plans, and whether the GAO findings produce oversight or funding changes. For private operators, the practical signal is the priority ordering in IR 8547 for long-lived sensitive data, where harvest-now, decrypt-later risk makes migration timelines consequential before fault-tolerant quantum computers exist.
