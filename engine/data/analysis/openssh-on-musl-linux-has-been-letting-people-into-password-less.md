A decade-old flaw in OpenSSH on musl-based Linux, nicknamed *sshole and tracked as CVE-2026-123158 with a CVSS score of 8.1, lets attackers log in to accounts that should never accept a password by entering any password between 257 and 1,024 characters. The issue affects OpenSSH servers running on musl libc with PAM disabled, the default openssh-server configuration on Alpine Linux, OpenWrt, Buildroot, and Yocto. Root and similar accounts store an asterisk in /etc/shadow to block password logins, but a 2016 patch for CVE-2016-6210 changed how OpenSSH handled accounts without usable password hashes, causing musl's crypt() to return an asterisk for overlong passwords and match the stored value. Researcher Ozgun Kultekin discovered and disclosed the flaw, which has existed since 2016. OpenSSH shipped version 10.6p2 on November 5, 2026 as an emergency fix, and Alpine published patched packages for all branches from 3.16 to edge the same day. No exploitation in the wild was reported at release, though SANS ISC later observed honeypot scans targeting the flaw.

Developer Damien Miller attributed the bug to interaction between sshd and musl rather than sshd alone, and noted the rapid release was motivated by recent cases of bug rediscovery by AI agents within hours. Affected operators were urged to disable password authentication immediately via PasswordAuthentication=no or stop SSH entirely until patched.

What to watch: administrators of musl-based systems, especially Alpine containers, routers, and IoT devices, should apply 10.6p2 or distribution updates and restart sshd. Accounts configured with an asterisk in /etc/shadow are the exposed targets, and long-password probes are an indicator of scanning activity.
