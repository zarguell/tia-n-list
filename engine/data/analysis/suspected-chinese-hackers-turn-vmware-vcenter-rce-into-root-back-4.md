A suspected Chinese-speaking threat actor is exploiting CVE-2026-59310, a critical directory-traversal flaw in VMware vCenter's Syslog Server (CVSS 9.8, disclosed July 29, 2026), to gain root-level access to vCenter appliances. The campaign turns a single compromised management server into a launch point for broad virtual-infrastructure compromise: attackers establish multiple backdoors, steal Active Directory credentials, and deploy Babuk-derived ransomware on ESXi hypervisors. Security firm QUIRSO identified 361 unique victim IP addresses across 47 countries. Germany, the United States, Turkey, Iran, and France are the most affected locations.

First observed August 3, five days after disclosure, the campaign is moving fast. VMware vCenter is the central management plane for ESXi environments, so a compromised vCenter gives attackers administrative control over every VM and host in the cluster. The Babuk ransomware payload targets ESXi directly, encrypting virtual machines at the hypervisor level. Organizations running on-premises vCenter should treat this as an immediate priority: patch CVE-2026-59310, audit for indicator of compromise, and check whether any vCenter appliance has contacted untrusted infrastructure since early August. The speed from disclosure to 361 victims underscores how quickly exploit-driven campaigns now move after vendor advisories.
