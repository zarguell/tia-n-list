ID verification service IDScan has confirmed a data breach in which hackers stole driver's licenses from its cloud infrastructure. The stolen data includes full names, driver's license numbers, and identity numbers from other government-issued documents such as passports. IDScan holds over 150 million driver's license records and serves corporate customers ranging from entertainment venues to cannabis dispensaries.

The breach came to light on September 1 when cybersecurity journalist Brian Krebs reported a dark web site allowing anyone to search over 150 million US and Canadian driver's license records, including photos. Krebs verified the data's authenticity with his own record. High-profile individuals in the database include US Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth. The Pentagon confirmed awareness of the breach, and the FBI is investigating.

IDScan acknowledged the breach on its website but provided limited details, noting only that "full access to the information required payment," suggesting the hackers demanded ransom. The company's investigation remains ongoing. The incident follows a pattern of identity verification services becoming high-value targets due to the concentrated volume of personal data they hold.
