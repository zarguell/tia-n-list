Thirty-five industry groups, including the U.S. Chamber of Commerce, Business Software Alliance, American Bankers Association, Airlines for America, the American Public Power Association and the National Rural Water Association, sent a Nov. 1 letter to the House and Senate Armed Services committees asking negotiators to keep a provision in the House defense bill that would extend the Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015 through 2035. The law gives companies legal protections when they share cyber threat information with the federal government.

The statute lapsed during last year's record government shutdown and has since been kept alive through a series of spending bills. Its current extension expires Dec. 11, so the NDAA conference is the nearest vehicle for a longer renewal. The groups argue that the speed and breadth of information sharing determines whether defenders keep pace with adversaries.

Watch whether the final NDAA retains the extension and whether a standalone renewal moves if it does not. A lapse would remove the liability and antitrust safe harbors that underpin most private-to-government threat sharing, pushing companies back toward bilateral or informal channels.
