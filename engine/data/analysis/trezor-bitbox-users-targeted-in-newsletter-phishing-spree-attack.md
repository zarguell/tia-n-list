A phishing campaign reported in September 2026 targeted users of Trezor and BitBox hardware wallets through legitimate newsletter and mailing channels, with lures demanding wallet backups. A later report from November describes a hardware wallet campaign with a different end goal: installing ConnectWise ScreenConnect, a legitimate remote-support tool, so the attacker can take control of the device, run commands and steal data.

The November report lists lure domains and artifacts. Coldcard-themed lures used coldcard[.]pro with v1.5.3Q-q1-coldcard.bat and a matching .zip, with SHA-256 hashes 13a340968f98b27eb4e39dd61f0b75822d68c8fbc35fa7f0ef791b65f87a8997 and 190702a08dbb3b2c2b07b75876b6b88eec04f827070514b9ef387990fcce8b2b. Ledger-themed lures used ledger-wallet[.]net and ledgeradvisorinfo[.]app with Ledger-wallet-Transition-Transcript.bat and a matching .zip, hashes a6e49e8686488acbe8938da3726f8510ec58e638ee804b55fda43600c4f741a2 and 52d7d663bb916e4cbf09fe0f3317d7ec379bf64cfa326b64e52cf75c0c71dd6e.

The brand set in the later report is broader than the original Trezor and BitBox framing, which suggests wallet owners are targeted across vendors rather than at two brands only. Watch for further lure domains reusing the same filenames and hashes, and for reports of ScreenConnect sessions established from wallet-themed downloads.
