Trezor disclosed a third-party data breach affecting approximately 13,689 customers after unauthorized actors accessed systems operated by its shipping and fulfillment provider ShipMonk. ShipMonk notified Trezor of unauthorized access to systems holding customer information on August 10, 2026. The exposed data is order-related personal information, and Trezor emphasized the breach happened inside ShipMonk's environment, not Trezor's own infrastructure: devices, products, services, and custom hardware were not affected, and private keys, wallet backups, and cryptocurrency holdings were never at risk.

The real exposure is downstream fraud, not direct theft of funds. The leaked order data enables targeted phishing, social engineering, and physical-world fraud against cryptocurrency holders, a population that is already a favorite target for impersonation scams. With shipment details and contact information, attackers can run convincing fake-support or fake-delivery lures that reference real orders, which materially raises the success rate of credential and seed-phrase theft.

Watch for the shape of follow-on phishing: Trezor-branded emails referencing real order numbers, fake firmware update prompts, and calls impersonating shipping or support staff. Affected customers should treat unsolicited contact referencing their Trezor order as hostile by default, verify any firmware update only through official channels, and never enter a seed phrase into any website or app. The incident is another reminder that third-party logistics data is a legitimate high-value target in crypto-adjacent fraud.
