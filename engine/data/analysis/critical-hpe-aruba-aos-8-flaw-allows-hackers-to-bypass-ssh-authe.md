CVE-2026-79855 is an SSH daemon authentication bypass in HPE Aruba AOS-8 with a CVSS v3.1 score of 9.8, allowing remote, unauthenticated attackers to gain administrative privileges and fully compromise the host. The broader HPE advisory (HPESBNW05026) fixes 72 vulnerabilities across AOS-8 and AOS-10, including eight maximum-severity flaws affecting mobility conductors, controllers, gateways, and access points. HPE credited its internal networking security researchers for identifying the issue and stated it has not observed public proof-of-concept exploits, though the attack requires no privileges and no user interaction.

This is not just another wireless firmware patch: the SSH bypass grants root-level control over infrastructure that often bridges guest, corporate, and management traffic. The events note 72 patched issues but only eight reach maximum severity, which concentrates defender attention on the authentication bypass family, not the full list. Because AOS powers Aruba's mobility controllers and gateways, a successful attacker can pivot from wireless management into layer-2/3 network segments that are rarely segmented by default, making this story more urgent than routine endpoint CVEs today.

Patch immediately using HPE's AOS-8 updates; restrict CLI and web-based management interfaces to a dedicated VLAN segment with layer-3 firewall policies and accounting controls until updates are deployed. Hunt for unauthorized SSH sessions and unexpected administrative logins in AOS management logs, and watch for crafted packets against UDP port 8211 (CVE-2026-79860 in the same advisory). Monitor HPE's security bulletin for any future proof-of-concept release, which would shift the risk from theoretical to actively exploited.

Watch for public exploit code and the first confirmed in-the-wild reports against AOS-8 SSH daemons, since HPE has not yet observed either but the low-complexity, unauthenticated attack surface is broad. A second development that would change the assessment is confirmation of exploitation against mobility conductors or gateways in production, which would elevate this from advisory-level urgency to incident-response priority.
