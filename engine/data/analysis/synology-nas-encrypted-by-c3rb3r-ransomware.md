C3RB3R is a Cerber-family ransomware encrypting Synology NAS data with the `.L0CK3D` extension and leaving bitcoin ransom notes in affected folders. The events reference a user-level recovery attempt on three measured files: a video partially restored for 9 seconds without a decryption key, photos unrecoverable, and everything else lost. The backfill shows the campaign was already documented by October 29 through a 360 investigation linking the malware to Synology-SA-24:20, the December 2024 advisory that patches the exploited vulnerability (CVE-2024-10441, a 9.8-rated unauthenticated remote code execution bug). No CVE is listed in the story record, but the external research names that advisory explicitly.

The exposure is concrete and small-scale in this incident (three measured files), yet it reflects a broader NAS-targeting wave since early October. Successful encryption on a NAS means irreversible data loss for owners without offline backups, which explains why the user notes partial video restoration rather than full recovery. The score of 5.9 and four source outlets (julienweb.fr, fr.cnet.com, shop.zimaspace.com, raymondtec.com) make this notable not for scale but for demonstrating that the campaign hits consumer-grade storage where patch adoption lags. The absence of a vendor advisory dated at or after the first_seen (2026-10-31) is the gap that lowers confidence: without Synology confirming in-the-wild exploitation of any new flaw, defenders must treat recovery claims as user observations rather than authoritative breach statistics.

Defenders should enforce the Synology-SA-24:20 patch immediately on any exposed DSM instance, disable external access to NAS management interfaces, and verify backup isolation. Hunt for `.L0CK3D` file extensions, ransom notes referencing bitcoin demands, and abnormal SMB or file-share encryption activity in NAS logs. The events mention no specific IoC filenames beyond the extension and no network ports; the backfill research points to outdated DSM releases as the entry vector, so focus telemetry on unpatched systems running DSM versions earlier than the December 2024 fix.

Watch for a new Synology security advisory that either confirms in-the-wild exploitation of a fresh vulnerability or expands SA-24:20 with updated IOCs. A second development to track is whether 360 or another vendor publishes a detailed technical analysis with file hashes or C2 indicators; the current reporting is descriptive, not forensic, and that limits active hunting.
