Cameron Nicholas Curry, a 27-year-old North Carolina man also known as Loot, was sentenced to two years in prison for orchestrating an insider extortion scheme against his employer Brightly Software. Working as a data analyst contractor for the Siemens-owned company, Curry launched an elaborate insider attack in late 2023, stealing a trove of corporate data including sensitive employee and compensation information, then threatening employees and executives over a six-week period in late 2023 and early 2024. He ultimately extorted the company for $7,540.92 in late January 2024 and was found guilty of six counts of extortion in March. The Justice Department announced the sentencing Thursday.

The case is a compact demonstration of insider threat economics. Brightly, a SaaS company formerly known as SchoolDude that was acquired by Siemens in August 2022, employs over 700 people and serves more than 12,000 clients worldwide; the stolen payroll and corporate data let Curry hold the company for a $2.5 million threat, of which he collected a small fraction before the scheme collapsed. That an analyst-grade contractor could walk out with payroll and executive correspondence, and then weaponize it, is the recurring failure mode: trusted data access without adequate monitoring.

Watch for whether the case drives changes in Brightly's insider monitoring or triggers audits of how contractor access is scoped. The operational lesson for similar SaaS employers is to treat contractor credentials as higher risk, monitor for bulk access to payroll and HR data, and segment compensation records from general corporate access. Sentencing of $2.5 million extortion attempts to two years also signals how these cases are treated, which may or may not deter the next insider.
