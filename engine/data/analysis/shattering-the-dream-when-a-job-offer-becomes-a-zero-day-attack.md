Check Point Research is tracking a new wave of Operation Dream Job, the long-running DPRK-affiliated Lazarus campaign. The latest variant focuses on defense-sector targets in Europe and India, distributing SecurityPDF, a modified PDF viewer that opens attacker-crafted documents and drops a backdoor named Troy.

The intrusion chain exploits CVE-2026-68820, a zero-day in Microsoft's AFD.sys driver, to install a new version of the FudModule kernel rootkit. That is full kernel compromise behind a lure that looks like a routine job offer. Lazarus still invests in zero-days for initial access into high-value targets, and the earlier campaign history here also includes the Windows LDAP RCE patched as CVE-2025-49113.

Watch for detection content around SecurityPDF and Troy, and for the patch status of the AFD.sys flaw. Organizations in defense, aerospace, and adjacent supply chains should treat unsolicited recruiting messages as a phish vector and verify the sender before opening attachments.
