ConnectWise published its first advisory on 16 October 2026 at 17:47 UTC on unauthorized access to application identities in ConnectWise Cloud Backup, formerly SkyKick. Huntress confirmed that a vendor-controlled Microsoft 365 application credential, a client assertion rather than a customer password, was used to obtain Graph tokens and sign into customer tenants. Suspicious authentication from 144.172.64[.]128, instead of the service's usual Azure private infrastructure, was seen on 15 October. Huntress counted 162 affected tenants at 19:00 UTC on 16 October and 173 by 17 October; in one case the actor granted itself Global Administrator and added credentials to two other applications.

ConnectWise disabled seven application identities at 07:00 UTC on 17 October, revoked certificates for affected connections and suspended Cloud Backup, SaaS Security and Cloud Management. The company said backups remained encrypted and it had no evidence of access to them, while the shutdown interrupted scheduled backups, restores and SaaS Security alerting. Sign-ins used the trusted application identity, so customer user MFA did not apply. One report notes that successful authentication alone does not establish data theft.

Microsoft attributed the supply-chain compromise to Storm-0940, a China state-aligned actor active since 2021, and tied it to the open-source ARTEX agentic AI framework. In the four days ending 14 October the actor targeted 327 Microsoft 365 and Exchange Online accounts across eight organizations with more than 151,000 failed logon attempts, peaking at 2,900 per minute, then attempted sign-ins to more than 2,250 tenants. Recorded Future tracked related infrastructure as TAG-217 from August; CrowdStrike and Zscaler linked ARTEX use to South Korean financial targets. Watch for tenant OAuth reviews, disabled ConnectWise application connections, anomalous Entra ID sign-ins and any confirmed data-access findings.
