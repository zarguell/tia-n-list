Air Force Secretary Troy Meink made the first public confirmation Monday that the United States has placed "space control weapons" in orbit, speaking at the Air and Space Forces Association's annual Air, Space & Cyber Conference near Washington, DC. "The United States now has on-orbit space control weapons capable of defending the joint force against hostile adversary action," Meink said in prepared remarks. He declined to reveal details about the weapons, their deployment timeline, or whether they have been tested.

The disclosure represents a reversal from the Pentagon's previous stance of avoiding public discussion of orbital warfare. Meink cited deterrence as the sole reason for the announcement, noting that China and Russia have been developing space weapons for over a decade. China operates highly maneuverable satellites that routinely approach US military assets, and Russia tested an apparent anti-satellite weapon in orbit starting in 2020. The Biden administration accused Russia in 2024 of deploying operational anti-satellite weapons.

Analysts at the Center for Strategic and International Studies described the lack of detail as limiting the deterrent value of the announcement. "Deterrence is not only about a weapon's fact of existence but also about having an awareness of how the weapon will be used," said Clayton Swope, CSIS deputy director of the Aerospace Security Project. Victoria Samson of the Secure World Foundation speculated the weapons could be orbital jammers, noting that China and Russia are both thought to have space-based jammers. The announcement opens the door for China and Russia to publicly acknowledge their own orbital weapons deployments.
