CrowdStrike reports that VAPOR PANDA, a China state-backed threat group, ran a global campaign using ARTEX, an agentic AI penetration-testing framework, paired with the Chinese-developed DeepSeek large language model. Since October 17 the group targeted more than 200 organizations in at least 14 countries and compromised roughly 140 sensitive targets in 11 days, with about 70 percent of targets confirmed compromised or showing moderate-confidence evidence of intrusion. Confirmed victims include foreign ministries, a European country's presidency and prime minister's office, defense and intelligence agencies, 19 U.S.-based organizations across pharmaceutical, semiconductor, defense, nuclear, and financial sectors, and telecom providers in the United States, Belgium, and the Philippines. The framework let the group issue requests as often as every 10 seconds and pursue multiple attack paths concurrently, which CrowdStrike's Adam Meyers called the first nation-state agentic adversary at this scale.

ARTEX is a multi-agent LLM system written in Go with a Next.js frontend that can use any AI model, and the same framework shows up in adjacent activity. Zscaler ThreatLabz analyzed a campaign abusing ARTEX against South Korean financial institutions in late September 2026 that exfiltrated personally identifiable information for roughly 67,000 individuals. Microsoft attributed to Storm-0940 a supply-chain compromise of ConnectWise Cloud Backup that used ARTEX to attempt sign-ins against more than 2,250 tenants after stealing application identities. Elastic Security Labs had earlier documented the BLOODALCHEMY backdoor in the REF5961 intrusion set, later deployed by VAPOR PANDA at a Middle East interior ministry and a Latin American foreign ministry. An independent Shodan and Censys point-in-time analysis of ARTEX exposure followed AI-agent attacks against South Korea's financial sector.

The through-line is that agentic tooling collapses reconnaissance and exploitation cost for a nation-state operator, and the same open framework is now reused across unrelated actors. Defenders should expect more of this pattern and watch for high-frequency, parallelized probing and ARTEX-specific indicators across cloud identity, VPN, and edge assets.
