CrowdStrike OverWatch identified a VAPOR PANDA campaign that used ARTEX, a Go-based agentic penetration-testing framework, together with the Chinese-developed DeepSeek large language model and a second commercial LLM, to run parallel intrusions against more than 200 organizations across at least 14 countries starting October 17, 2026. Targets included foreign ministries in Latin America, Europe, Asia, and the Middle East; semiconductor, defense, and think-tank organizations in the United States; and telecommunications providers in multiple regions. The operation is separate from the ARTEX activity reported under the Storm-0940 designation, and CrowdStrike confirmed post-exploitation at nine organizations, including deployment of the BLOODALCHEMY backdoor at a Middle East interior ministry and a Latin America foreign ministry.

The argument is not that a threat actor used an LLM, but that one operator could now run dozens of concurrent engagements that previously required a team, and the victim list shows state-directed collection rather than crime: foreign ministries, nuclear and defense contractors, and telecoms. The 1.4 billion input tokens consumed in a single intrusion, plus password spraying against at least 12 additional organizations, indicates reconnaissance at scale has already happened, so every target should be treated as potentially compromised even where no follow-on activity was confirmed. This is the first observed use of AI-driven intrusion tooling by VAPOR PANDA and a broadening of its historic government and think-tank targeting into pharmaceutical, semiconductor, nuclear, and defense sectors, which matters more than the model brand.

Defenders should hunt for ARTEX-related tooling and agent transcripts, web shells, and BLOODALCHEMY artifacts: a legitimate GameHook binary renamed svchost.exe, graphics-hook-filter64.dll, graphics-hook_config.bin, and the internal project path D:\ProjectX\Merman_New\, plus the associated command-and-control infrastructure in CrowdStrike's indicator table. Review exposure of internet-facing web applications, VPN, mail, and Active Directory, and look for credential and password spraying, network scanning, and hosts deliberately selected for missing endpoint sensors. There is no patch to apply because no CVE is involved; containment is configuration and detection work, including restricting outbound access to disposable VPS and proxy infrastructure.

Watch whether CrowdStrike publishes the full technical report and indicator set, whether other vendors corroborate the 200-plus target count, and whether activity expands beyond the initial target set; any of those would move this from a contained espionage disclosure to an ongoing campaign. Also watch for VAPOR PANDA changing models or for ARTEX forks spreading to additional China-nexus actors, which would make the framework rather than this single operator the story.
