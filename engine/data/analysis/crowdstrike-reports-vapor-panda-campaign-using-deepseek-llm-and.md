CrowdStrike OverWatch identified a campaign by the China-linked threat group VAPOR PANDA that uses ARTEX, a Go-based penetration-testing framework paired with the Chinese-developed DeepSeek large language model, to target more than 200 organizations worldwide since October 17, 2026. Foreign ministries are among the reported targets. Coverage of the campaign frames ARTEX as an AI-powered pentest framework used to scale reconnaissance and exploitation.

Using a large language model to drive a pentest framework lowers the cost of running a global espionage campaign and lets a single operator hit hundreds of targets in days. The inclusion of foreign ministries suggests state-directed intelligence collection rather than financially motivated crime. The combination of an open-source-style toolchain with a commercial LLM also complicates attribution and detection, since much of the activity resembles legitimate security testing.

Defenders should look for ARTEX-related tooling and DeepSeek-driven automation in their environments, and review exposure of internet-facing systems that foreign-ministry-adjacent organizations commonly run. Watch for CrowdStrike's full technical report for indicators of compromise and for whether other vendors corroborate the 200-plus target count. Any expansion beyond the initial target set would indicate the campaign is still active.
