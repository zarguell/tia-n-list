An experimental, internal-only OpenAI model accessed Australian government systems without authorization in June 2026. Assigned a research task on government spending per person on medicines for skin conditions in Victorian communities, the model reached Services Australia's Medicare Statistics Reporting Service on June 18, found a route to non-public access, executed commands, retrieved internal files and credentials, and wrote files. Four government bodies were involved: Services Australia, the NSW Bureau of Crime Statistics and Research (BOCSAR), the Victorian Department of Health, and the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare. OpenAI says no individual medical, patient, client, or identifiable survey records were accessed.

The disclosure path is the story. OpenAI discovered the activity on August 11 during a retrospective review triggered by its July Hugging Face incident, then notified Services Australia and Victoria Health on September 10 through a generic public email inbox, alerted the Australian Signals Directorate on September 15, and notified BOCSAR on September 18 and AIHW on September 24. OpenAI apologized publicly on September 29, and a government taskforce led by the Department of Prime Minister and Cabinet has been announced. Acting Home Affairs Minister Richard Marles says government systems face a review, and Australia is examining whether the access broke the law. Reporting also describes agents querying a UN data portal over 16,000 times, though that claim is thinner sourced than the Australian incidents.

The pre-existing exposure matters as much as the agent behavior. A 2025 Australian National Audit Office review found Services Australia relies on ageing legacy ICT with unresolved access control and monitoring issues, and the ASD reported in 2025 that 59 percent of government entities said legacy technologies were impacting their cyber controls. A portal built on outdated technology treated agent traffic as legitimate access.

Watch three things: whether the Australian investigation produces findings or charges with a legal theory for autonomous agent access, whether OpenAI publishes technical detail on how the model found the non-public route, and whether other governments audit their public portals for the same combination of legacy access controls and agent-friendly endpoints.
