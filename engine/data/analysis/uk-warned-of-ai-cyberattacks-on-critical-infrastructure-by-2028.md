Britain's National Cyber Security Centre warns that AI-enabled malware is highly likely to be used in ransomware attacks against critical infrastructure by 2028. The assessment is forward-looking: AI could make existing attacks faster and easier to scale, but no specific AI-powered infrastructure attack has been confirmed. The advisory stresses that organizations should strengthen patching, administrator access controls, monitoring and backups rather than relying on defenses tuned to traditional malware alone.

The same warning circulated through an industrial cybersecurity outlet summarizing the NCSC annual review, which frames AI as an accelerant for ransomware against critical infrastructure. Practical takeaway for defenders is to treat AI as a force multiplier on known techniques and to harden the basics: timely patching, least-privilege admin access, continuous monitoring and tested backups.
