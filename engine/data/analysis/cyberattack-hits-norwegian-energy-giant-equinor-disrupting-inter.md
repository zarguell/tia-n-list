Equinor said on 17 November 2026 that it had detected an ongoing cyberattack against certain IT systems. The Record reported that the Norwegian energy company was working to contain the intrusion and that oil and gas production had not been affected. A writeup by Pierluigi Paganini, carried by malware.news, describes Equinor as Europe's largest gas supplier and says the company had taken steps to neutralise the attack while insisting that operations and production were unaffected.

A 19 November post from KB Technologies lays out three Equinor statements in sequence: detection on Tuesday, a Wednesday statement calling this "not a virus attack," and a later statement saying the activity "does not appear to be specifically" targeted. The public wording shifted across those three statements, while the claim that production was uninterrupted stayed constant. The only concrete details available are the 17 November detection date and those specific quoted characterisations; no CVE, actor, malware name or indicator has been published in the event record.

What to watch is whether Equinor releases technical detail on the affected IT systems and whether the no-impact assessment changes. The absence of any published artifact so far is not evidence that the intrusion was trivial, only that the company has kept its disclosures at the level of status statements. Follow-on reporting from Norwegian authorities or from Equinor itself would be the next useful development, particularly if it names a toolset or a third-party entry point.
