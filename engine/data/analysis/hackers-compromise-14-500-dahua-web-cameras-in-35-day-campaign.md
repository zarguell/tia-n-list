Hunt.io uncovered a campaign dubbed CameraSwarm that compromised 14,530 Dahua IP cameras over 35 days (June 17 through July 22), primarily in Ukraine and Russia. The operator left an unprotected working directory on an HTTP server, exposing 407 MB of data including source code, logs, credentials, captured camera images, shell history, and exploitation results. Three parallel attack methods drove the campaign.

A brute-forcing system scanned TCP port 37777 and compromised devices at 12,324 unique IP addresses, capturing usable camera snapshots and exporting them for Dahua's SMART PSS platform. CVE-2021-33044 and CVE-2021-33045 were exploited using a tool called p2pwn, which installed a persistent backdoor account (p2pwn / p2password) on 1,923 cameras. This account survives password changes and, on most firmware versions, factory resets. A third method used cloud-relay exploitation to reach 283 cameras behind NAT using only serial numbers.

The geographic concentration in Ukraine and Russia suggests possible espionage or surveillance objectives. The persistent backdoor surviving factory resets is a serious remediation challenge: affected devices likely need full hardware replacement or firmware-level intervention. Both CVEs have known patches, but the campaign's scale indicates widespread unpatched deployments. Watch for attribution or additional campaigns targeting similar IoT platforms.
