Between August 21 and 24, 2026, the FBI and U.S. Coast Guard boarded two oil tankers in the Gulf of Mexico after their onboard networks were reportedly compromised. The initial report for one vessel was alarming, suggesting interference with navigation (ECDIS), propulsion, and cargo systems. However, authorities confirmed no operational disruptions, safety impacts, or environmental damage resulted from either incident. The source of the attacks has not been publicly attributed.

The boarded vessels included the VL Prosperity, a Very Large Crude Carrier carrying roughly two million barrels of Saudi crude bound for Texas, and an LNG carrier. Satellite communications on the VL Prosperity went dark for approximately 30 hours as it transited the Strait of Gibraltar. Iranian state media outlet Mehr News was first to give the story wide coverage, and a crew member testimonial alleged attackers manipulated engine parameters including RPMs and disabled fuel systems. U.S. authorities have not confirmed OT-level access and describe the attackers only as "foreign cyber actors."

The maritime attack surface is expanding: persistent satellite connectivity, increasing IT/OT integration, and third-party vendor remote access create multiple entry points. Potential vectors include SATCOM exploitation (T0884), phishing targeting crew (T1566), and poor network segmentation between IT and OT. The joint FBI-Coast Guard response signals how seriously U.S. authorities view threats to maritime critical infrastructure. For defenders, this underscores the need for genuine IT/OT network segmentation on vessels, active monitoring of satellite communication anomalies, and incident response plans that cover the "vessel underway" scenario with manual failover procedures.
