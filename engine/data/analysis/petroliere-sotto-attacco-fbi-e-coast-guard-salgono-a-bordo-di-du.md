On August 21, a U.S. Coast Guard and FBI team boarded the VL Prosperity, a Very Large Crude Carrier carrying roughly two million barrels of Saudi crude oil bound for Texas, after satellite and onboard communications went dark for approximately 30 hours as the vessel transited the Strait of Gibraltar. Three days later, a second boarding targeted an LNG carrier whose networks were also suspected compromised. The incidents, first reported September 16, represent one of the most documented cases of cyber intrusion against energy shipping headed to U.S. waters.

Iranian state media outlet Mehr News was the first to give the story wide coverage. U.S. authorities describe the attackers only as "foreign cyber actors" without official attribution, though investigators are reportedly examining possible links to Tehran. A crew member testimonial published by Mehr alleged the attackers manipulated engine parameters, potentially increasing RPMs and disabling fuel and lubricant oil tanks. If confirmed, that would indicate access not just to administrative IT but to operational technology systems closer to the ship's propulsion controls. U.S. authorities have not confirmed the OT access claim and say no operational damage, instability, crew danger, or environmental impact resulted.

The maritime attack surface is growing rapidly: persistent satellite connectivity for crew welfare and telemetry, increasing integration between IT networks (email, ERP, fleet management) and OT systems (ECDIS, navigation, engine monitoring), and third-party vendors accessing vessels remotely for predictive maintenance. This case arrives months after a U.S. Department of Transportation advisory on maritime cybersecurity best practices and International Maritime Organization guidelines on cyber risk management. For defenders, the incident underscores the need for genuine network segmentation between IT, crew communications, and OT propulsion systems, active monitoring of prolonged satellite communication anomalies, and incident response plans that explicitly cover the "vessel underway" scenario with manual failover procedures for critical propulsion systems.
