Anubis ransomware operators detailed an attack on Interim Healthcare, a Greece-based healthcare organization. The group claims to have encrypted 335,093 files totaling 530 GB, including backup systems. SuspectFile, an Italian cybersecurity outlet, reported the incident and noted the attackers discussed CVE-2025-5777 in their leak post, suggesting the vulnerability played a role in initial access.

The healthcare sector remains a primary ransomware target due to operational urgency and data sensitivity. Interim Healthcare operates across multiple facilities, and the encrypted volume indicates the attackers achieved broad lateral movement before detonation. The mention of encrypted backups suggests the organization had backup infrastructure that was not adequately isolated from production networks.

Healthcare organizations should verify that backup systems sit on separate networks with distinct credentials. Review exposure of systems vulnerable to CVE-2025-5777 and ensure patches are applied. The single-source reporting limits confidence in the full scope, but the details align with established Anubis TTPs of targeting healthcare and exfiltrating before encryption.
