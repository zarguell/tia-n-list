OpenAI has introduced GPT-6 Cyber, an advanced security model rated Critical for cybersecurity under the company's Preparedness Framework. The model is described as outperforming its predecessor in exploit testing, identifying and addressing software flaws, while incorporating stricter alignment controls intended to keep it within authorized boundaries and away from unauthorized targets. Researchers have used it to uncover vulnerabilities in major systems including the Linux kernel and Firefox, leading to timely patches from maintainers. Access is restricted to approved enterprise customers in specialized testing programs, with safeguards refined through feedback from industrial security experts.

The story matters because it marks a shift in how frontier AI is positioned for vulnerability research: a model capable of finding real flaws in core open-source infrastructure is being gated behind vetting rather than released broadly. That gating is itself a control, but it also concentrates a powerful capability among a small set of approved users and raises questions about how findings are disclosed and whether similar capabilities will spread to less restricted models.

What to watch next is how OpenAI defines and enforces the vetting and authorization process, whether independent researchers can access the model for defensive work, and whether the vulnerabilities found in the Linux kernel and Firefox represent the start of a larger wave of AI-assisted disclosures. The alignment controls will need real-world testing against prompt-injection and scope-confusion attempts.
