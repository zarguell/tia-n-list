Binance is warning iPhone users about worse_query, a publicly disclosed iOS sandbox-escape technique that lets a malicious app read private files belonging to other apps, including cryptocurrency seed phrases, private keys, and login credentials. Affected versions include iOS 26.7.1, 27.0.1, and 27.2. The technique was published by forcequitOS on November 8, 2026 as a proof of concept for iOS 26.0 through 26.7.2 and iOS 27.2, and it requires an App Group container to reach other apps' data paths.

Christoph Kolbicz released WQ Files 0.4 on November 9, 2026, a no-jailbreak file browser and app data manager built on worse_query that can browse, export, edit, and delete other apps' data; it was tested on iOS 27.0.1 and requires sideloading with an App Group entitlement. SlowMist warned that wallets storing mnemonic phrases in plaintext could be exposed, and Binance advises users to remove untrusted apps, update iOS and apps, and move funds to a new wallet on a clean device if compromise is suspected.

What to watch: the flaw is not a jailbreak and needs a malicious app installed, so the practical risk hinges on sideloading and untrusted app installs; Binance says no cryptocurrency losses are confirmed yet, but plaintext seed storage in wallets remains the key exposure. Apple has not been quoted in the coverage, so any iOS-level patch status is unconfirmed.
