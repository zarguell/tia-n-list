Barracuda Networks patched two flaws that can be chained for root-level compromise of its appliances. CVE-2026-116606 is a pre-authentication authorization bypass (CVSS 9.3) in Barracuda Email Security Gateway versions earlier than 9.4.0.030 that lets an unauthenticated remote attacker restore a crafted backup archive and modify the appliance's global configuration. CVE-2026-111560 is an OS command injection (CVSS 8.6) in the timezone configuration field affecting six products (ESG, ADC, WAF, WSG, BMA, WCC) that lets an authenticated administrator execute commands as root. Both require access to the administrative interface, and Barracuda released fixes on November 2, 2026. CISA added both to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog with a three-day remediation deadline, and Barracuda later warned of exploitation affecting a small number of organizations.

The chain mirrors the 2023 Barracuda ESG flaw CVE-2023-7102, which was exploited at scale, and watchTowr Labs researcher Sina Kheirkhah published technical details on November 5. Mandiant's disclosure timeline shows CVE-2026-111560 reported October 15 and CVE-2026-116606 on October 22, with patches shipping November 2. Ostorlab noted that no exploitation was known as of November 2, before Barracuda's update confirmed limited attacks.

Administrators should ensure appliances run the latest firmware (ESG 9.4.0.030 or later, ADC 6.4.0, WAF 11.0.1, WSG 16.1.100.100, BMA 5.5.4.0, WCC 2.2.1) and restrict management interfaces to trusted internal networks. Given the ESG line's attack history, treat any internet-exposed admin interface as already probed and review for signs of compromise, including unauthorized configuration changes.
