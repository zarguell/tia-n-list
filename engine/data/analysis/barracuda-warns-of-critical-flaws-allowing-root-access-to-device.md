Barracuda Networks published two vulnerabilities on 2026-11-02 that chain into pre-authentication root access on its Email Security Gateway. CVE-2026-116606 is a pre-authentication authorization bypass rated 9.3 (CWE-288) in ESG versions earlier than 9.4.0.030, allowing an unauthenticated remote attacker to invoke the configuration-import function and restore a crafted, compatible backup archive, which modifies the appliance's global configuration. CVE-2026-111560 is an OS command injection rated 8.6 (CWE-78) in the timezone configuration field, affecting ESG 9.4.0.27 and earlier plus five other products: Load Balancer ADC 6.4.0 and earlier, WAF 11.0.1 and earlier, Web Security Gateway 16.1.100.100 and earlier, Message Archiver 5.5.4.0 and earlier, and WAF Control Center 2.2.1 and earlier. An authenticated administrator can run commands as root. Mandiant recorded Barracuda reporting CVE-2026-111560 on 2026-10-15 and CVE-2026-116606 on 2026-10-22, with advisories published 2026-11-02.

Exploitation followed within a week. Barracuda updated its advisory on 2026-11-09 to say the two flaws are being exploited against a small number of organizations, and CISA added both CVEs to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, setting a three-day federal remediation deadline. watchTowr Labs researcher Sina Kheirkhah published technical details on 2026-11-05, and Ostorlab's 2026-11-02 analysis walked through the chain across the six affected product lines, noting that the 2023 ESG flaw CVE-2023-2868 was previously exploited at scale and gives this appliance line a live attack history. The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security tracked the issue as AV26-1112 with the same fixed versions.

Both bugs require reachability of the administrative interface, which is the controlling variable. Barracuda's guidance is to restrict management access to trusted internal networks and apply network access controls, on top of moving to the latest firmware: ESG 9.4.0.030 or later, ADC 6.4.0, WAF 11.0.1, WSG 16.1.100.100, BMA 5.5.4.0, WCC 2.2.1.

What to watch is internet-exposed management interfaces and the blast radius of a compromised ESG. An attacker with root on an email security gateway can read mail queues and credentials, alter configuration, and pivot into internal networks that trust the appliance. Inventory every Barracuda ESG, ADC, WAF, WSG, BMA and WCC instance, confirm firmware against the table above, and check admin UI exposure on all of them, including appliances that appear internal but are reachable through VPN or partner networks. Because exploitation was already observed at a small number of organizations, treat any unpatched internet-facing ESG as an incident candidate rather than a routine patch gap.
