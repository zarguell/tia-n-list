Barracuda Email Security Gateway carries two flaws that researchers demonstrated chaining from an unauthenticated position to full root on the device: CVE-2026-116606, a critical pre-authentication authorization bypass scored at 9.3, and CVE-2026-111560, with Mandiant disclosures MNDT-2026-0041 and MNDT-2026-0042 behind them and older entry CVE-2023-7102 also tracked in the cluster. Barracuda's own trust site advisories confirm both flaws affect ESG appliances, and BleepingComputer, SecurityWeek and The Hacker News each report the chain ending in root with no login. A November 6 HackerNews writeup describes the sequence plainly: the auth bypass lets an unauthenticated attacker restore a crafted configuration, which opens the path to root.

Independent researcher validation raised the credibility this week. WatchTowr Labs published a full technical analysis of the two ESG vulnerabilities on November 5, walking the pre-auth chain to remote root, and Ostorlab published its own pre-auth bypass analysis; The Perimeter Site flagged the pair as the most serious item on its desk on November 6. With two public teardowns plus vendor confirmation, working exploit tooling is a matter of time if it is not already circulating.

Patch ESG appliances to Barracuda's fixed builds immediately and verify device configurations for unknown changes, since the chain permits config overwrite. Watch for Barracuda confirmation of in-the-wild exploitation and for published IOCs or network signatures for the exploit traffic.
