Barracuda Networks disclosed two vulnerabilities on November 2, 2026 that can be chained to gain root access to affected appliances, though successful exploitation requires reaching the administrative interface. CVE-2026-116606 is a pre-authentication authorization bypass (CWE-288, CVSS 9.3) in Email Security Gateway versions earlier than 9.4.0.030 that lets an unauthenticated remote attacker restore a crafted backup archive and modify the appliance's global configuration. CVE-2026-111560 is an OS command injection (CWE-78, CVSS 8.6) in the timezone configuration field of six products, ESG 9.4.0.27 and earlier, ADC 6.4.0 and earlier, WAF 11.0.1 and earlier, WSG 16.1.100.100 and earlier, BMA 5.5.4.0 and earlier, and WCC 2.2.1 and earlier, that lets an authenticated administrator execute commands as root. Patches shipped November 2, and Mandiant and Ostorlab reported no known exploitation as of that date.

The risk is amplified by appliance history: Barracuda's Email Security Gateway line was previously compromised at scale through CVE-2023-2868 (and related CVE-2023-7102), making ESG a proven target for persistent attackers. The new bypass does not require credentials, so any ESG instance with an exposed management interface is reachable, and chaining it with the command injection yields unauthenticated root. The command injection's shared codebase widens the blast radius beyond ESG to ADC, WAF, WSG, archiver, and WAF control center deployments.

What to watch: whether in-the-wild exploitation emerges now that details are public, internet-facing Barracuda management interfaces in customer environments, and patch compliance across the six affected product lines. Administrators should apply the November 2 firmware, restrict management access to trusted internal networks, and treat ESG appliances as high-priority given the line's prior compromise history.
