Barracuda has patched two flaws that can be chained for root access on its Email Security Gateway and five other product lines. CVE-2026-116606 is a pre-authentication authorization bypass (CWE-288, CVSS 9.3) in ESG versions earlier than 9.4.0.030 that lets an unauthenticated remote attacker restore a crafted backup archive and modify the appliance's global configuration. CVE-2026-111560 is an OS command injection (CWE-78, CVSS 8.6) in the shared timezone configuration code that lets an authenticated administrator execute commands as root; it affects ESG up to 9.4.0.27, ADC up to 6.4.0, WAF up to 11.0.1, WSG up to 16.1.100.100, BMA up to 5.5.4.0, and WCC up to 2.2.1. Mandiant reported the command injection on October 15 and the bypass on October 22, with patches shipping November 2 and no known exploitation as of that date.

The chain is the risk: an attacker who can reach the management interface can bypass authorization and then inject commands that run as root, turning a single appliance into a pivot point. Barracuda stresses that successful exploitation requires access to the administrative interface, so exposure of that interface is the determining factor. The Canadian Centre advisory AV26-1112 lists all affected versions, and Ostorlab notes the 2023 Barracuda ESG flaw CVE-2023-2868 was previously exploited at scale, giving this appliance family a live attack history that raises the urgency beyond the CVSS numbers.

Operators should patch to the fixed versions immediately, restrict management interfaces to trusted internal networks, and review whether any ESG or related appliance was reachable from untrusted sources. The absence of reported exploitation as of November 2 is not a reason to delay, given the prior exploitation of the same product line.
