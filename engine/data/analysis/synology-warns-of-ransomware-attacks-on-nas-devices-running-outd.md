Synology warned customers of active C3RB3R ransomware attacks hitting NAS devices still running outdated DiskStation Manager versions. Its 29 October alert says attackers are exploiting a vulnerability patched in December 2024, and links advisory SA-24:20 for CVE-2024-10441 (arbitrary code execution), CVE-2024-50629 (arbitrary file read) and CVE-2024-10445 (arbitrary file write). Synology has not named which of the three is the entry point in these attacks; researchers disputed the entry point as of 2 November, with Qihoo 360 offering a separate attribution for the C3RB3R cases.

CISA added CVE-2024-10441 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 18 November 2026, and social posts picked up the listing on the same day. That changes the status of the Synology flaw from a long-known patching gap to a confirmed exploitation entry in the federal catalog, even though the link between that CVE and the C3RB3R encryptor still rests on Synology's advisory cross-reference. BleepingComputer reported that Synology urged users to block the management web interface from the internet, enable automatic updates and update DSM immediately. The flaws allow code execution and file read and write, which is enough for ransomware crews to encrypt data and extort victims; the December 2024 patches have been available for roughly a year, so exposure is driven by lagging updates rather than a fresh zero-day.

Administrators should treat any Synology NAS still on a pre-December 2024 DSM build as compromised until verified. Update to the latest DSM, enable automatic updates, restrict or disable internet access to the management interface, and check for signs of C3RB3R encryption or unauthorized accounts and scheduled tasks before reconnecting devices. Watch for a Synology follow-up naming the exploited CVE and for further KEV entries covering CVE-2024-10445 or CVE-2024-50629; either would narrow the hunting guidance and settle the entry-point dispute.
