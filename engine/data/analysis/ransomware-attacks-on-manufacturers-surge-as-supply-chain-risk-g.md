Black Kite's 2026 Manufacturing and Distribution Ransomware Report found that ransomware attacks on manufacturers increased 40% compared to the same period last year. The report identifies 5,237 disclosed ransomware victims across manufacturing and distribution from January 2023 to July 2026. The core finding: mid-sized manufacturers are the primary targets because they form the supplier layer that larger enterprises depend on, making their compromise a pathway into major companies.

The Jaguar Land Rover incident serves as the report's central case study. The September 2025 attack shut down UK plants, halted production of approximately 1,000 vehicles daily, affected over 5,000 other companies, and contributed to a national economic slowdown per the Bank of England. The UK's Cyber Monitoring Centre estimated a GBP 1.9 billion financial impact, calling it the most economically damaging cyberattack in UK history, surpassing WannaCry. Jaguar Land Rover has since announced 4,000 job cuts attributed to the attack.

The report's key insight is the "long tail" effect: manufacturing ransomware damage extends far beyond the initial incident. Supply chain disruption propagates upward to large enterprises and outward to dependent businesses. SecurityWeek's single-source coverage belies the significance of these findings for organisations assessing third-party risk in manufacturing supply chains.
