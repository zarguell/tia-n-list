Black Kite's 2026 Manufacturing & Distribution Ransomware Report finds that ransomware incidents targeting manufacturers are up 40% year-over-year, driven by the sector's long tail of consequences. The report points to Jaguar Land Rover's September 2025 attack as a defining example: the company shut down UK plants, halted production of roughly 1,000 luxury vehicles daily, and eventually announced 4,000 job cuts attributed to the cyberattack. The UK Cyber Monitoring Centre estimated a 1.9 billion pound financial impact, calling it the most economically damaging cyberattack in UK history, surpassing WannaCry.

The report argues that mid-sized manufacturers are the primary targets because they occupy the supplier layer from which large enterprises assemble products. When a mid-market manufacturer is hit, its entire customer base becomes an attack surface. From January 2023 through July 2026, Black Kite identified 5,237 disclosed ransomware victims across manufacturing and distribution sectors. The sustained targeting reflects the reality that manufacturing disruptions cascade through supply chains, giving attackers maximum leverage in extortion negotiations.

The data underscores that manufacturing ransomware is not just an IT problem but a systemic supply chain risk. Organizations should assess the ransomware resilience of critical suppliers, not just their own defenses, and consider contractual requirements for incident notification and recovery capabilities.
