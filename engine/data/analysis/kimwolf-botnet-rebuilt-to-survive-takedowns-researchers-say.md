Unit 42 reported that the Kimwolf botnet, also tracked as Aisuru and powered mostly by hijacked Android TV boxes, has been running a new version since February, a month before authorities seized infrastructure used by earlier versions. The biggest change is a flood method built on HTTP/2 that uses full Chrome browser fingerprints, copying header order and behavior, so attack traffic is hard to distinguish from ordinary web browsing. DDoS mitigation that drops raw packet floods does not catch it.

The second change targets takedown resilience: the rebuilt botnet keeps its command channels harder to seize, consistent with the operators' history of rebuilding after infrastructure loss. For defenders the lesson is that HTTP/2 floods with browser-like fingerprints defeat simple DDoS filtering, and sites need rate limiting and behavioral detection that does not rely on traffic looking non-browser. The botnet's scale, from cheap compromised TV boxes, makes this a durable capacity that will likely persist through future takedowns.
