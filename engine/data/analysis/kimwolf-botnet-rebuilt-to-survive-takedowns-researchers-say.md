Unit 42 tracks Kimwolf, also called Aisuru, a botnet powered mostly by hijacked Android TV boxes and set-top boxes. The current build, Kimwolf v7, was identified on February 3, 2026 and has been active since around February. The operation traces to the AISURU botnet, which focused on Linux IoT devices in 2024 before shifting to Android-based targets in 2025. Cyberpress coverage this week adds detail on the v7 rebuild.

The core change is the attack method: v7 adds an HTTP/2 flood built on the nghttp2 library that constructs a fuller set of browser headers designed to resemble Chrome traffic, copying header order and behavior. Attack requests look like ordinary browser sessions at the protocol and header level, so DDoS mitigation that drops raw packet floods does not catch them. Unit 42's own reporting also notes Ethereum ENS-based C2 resolution and Tor backup routing, further evidence of the takedown-resilience work.

v7 also removes the earlier scanner, exploit and brute-force components, suggesting separate tools or loaders now handle initial infection while Kimwolf runs the attack and command functions. The rebuilt botnet therefore focuses less on spreading and more on staying online and launching effective floods. Watch for rate limiting and behavioral detection that do not assume attack traffic looks non-browser, and expect the capacity to persist through future infrastructure seizures.
