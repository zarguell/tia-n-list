The bomb-threat emails that disrupted more than 6,000 schools in Italy on November 11, 2026 were sent from two university email accounts, one Egyptian and one Taiwanese, that had been compromised by infostealers years earlier. Hudson Rock traced both addresses in its stolen-credential holdings. The sender addresses were f0523219@cloud.dyu.edu.tw and Mosaad_Khalifa@den.kfs.edu.eg. Rather than a fresh infrastructure breach, the pattern points to threat actors hijacking long-exposed, unremediated identities to slip past outbound spam filters and cause public disruption.

The machine-level telemetry shows how old the exposure is. The Egyptian university account traces back to a Vidar infostealer infection dated October 1, 2021, whose logs show the malware pulled 112 credential records straight out of Google Chrome, including passwords saved against Microsoft authentication pages. The Taiwanese credentials were located in AlienTXTBASE, an aggregated infostealer credential collection, with data dated more than a year before the threats. Hudson Rock assesses both matches as infostealer-derived, placing the credential exposure well before the campaign.

The incident is a reminder that credentials stolen years ago retain value when victims never rotate them. Attackers used legitimate, aged accounts to bypass reputation-based filtering and amplify a mass disruption across a national school system. The defensive takeaway is unglamorous but concrete: audit for credentials circulating in infostealer logs and combo lists, force resets on anything found, and enforce phishing-resistant authentication on mail accounts so a leaked password alone is not enough to send mail as a trusted user.
