A campaign against Afghan telecom providers and South Asian critical infrastructure uses three previously undocumented malware families: PATCHCORD, SHEETCORD, and the HACKERAI C2 agent. Delivery relied on sector-specific lures, including a malicious Inno Setup package named TMS_AfghanTelecom.exe inside a ZIP archive masquerading as Afghan Telecom's telecom management software. The infrastructure is layered: custom implants, spoofed delivery portals, and cloud-backed command-and-control, with SuperShell hosting remote commands and webshell management for the PATCHCORD side.

Researchers assess a link to APT36, also known as Transparent Tribe, with moderate confidence. The basis is targeting patterns, shared infrastructure, malware similarities, and tools recovered from an exposed staging server. Targets span telecom, government, defense, and energy organizations in Afghanistan and India, which indicates the group is pushing beyond its traditional government and military focus into telecom, where access can mean subscriber data and communications systems.

Watch for confirmation or denial of the APT36 attribution as more samples surface, and for the HACKERAI component's LLM-assisted development angle: if the code style holds up, it is an early signal that this group is folding generative AI into implant writing. Defenders in Afghan and Indian telecom should treat ZIP-based lures referencing telecom management tools as high priority, and hunt for Inno Setup installers invoking webshell or C2 beaconing behavior.
