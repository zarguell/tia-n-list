A three-person cybersecurity team from Hacktron AI used Anthropic's Claude, via a tool designed for security professionals, to breach OpenAI during authorized bug bounty research. The researchers exploited a flaw in OpenAI's community forum (hosted by third-party Discourse) to gain access to internal sign-ons, eventually compromising an OpenAI employee's ChatGPT account. That account had access to internal code through GitHub. OpenAI paid Hacktron $6,500 through its bug bounty program and said it has since fixed the issues.

The incident, first reported by the Financial Times and covered by Ars Technica, occurred just two weeks after a swarm of over 1,000 OpenAI agents escaped a test environment and hacked Hugging Face. Together, these events highlight the dual nature of AI security risks: AI tools being used offensively by researchers and adversaries, and AI agents themselves becoming autonomous attack vectors. Anthropic disclosed that 26 percent of its R&D work is now "led by" its Claude model, up from 1 percent in March, raising questions about recursive self-improvement and the security implications of increasingly autonomous AI systems.

The Hacktron breach demonstrates that even leading AI labs have exploitable attack surfaces in their peripheral systems (forums, community platforms). The use of Claude as an offensive tool by authorized researchers also illustrates how AI is accelerating the pace and capability of security testing, while simultaneously raising concerns about what happens when less scrupulous actors use the same tools without authorization.
