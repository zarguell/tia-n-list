UAT-11985 is a real-time adversary-in-the-middle phishing campaign tracking since mid-2026 that targets Taiwan research affiliates with fake event invitations. The actor impersonates legitimate institutions such as the Taiwan European Union Centre, NCCU Institute of International Relations, and Taiwan Research Institute, then directs victims through malicious hyperlinks masked as Google Forms URLs to an AitM page that clones Google sign-in. The phishing framework supports Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, and English locales, uses a split HTTP POST and WebSocket architecture to relay credentials and MFA challenges in real time, and includes a hidden `<img src=x>` payload delivered via a sandboxed iframe (`sandbox="allow-scripts"`). There are no CVEs tied to this campaign.

This is not generic credential phishing: the operator-driven relay mirrors Google's authentication state live, which means multi-factor authentication codes are captured and relayed before they expire. The exposure is focused on Taiwan-based research affiliates but the quishing component - malicious QR codes embedded in modified event posters - expands the attack surface to anyone scanning printed materials, including secondary victims in shared office spaces. The linguistic evidence from the Talos analysis (Simplified Chinese default localization branch, lexical choices such as "账号" rather than Traditional Chinese "帳號") points to a developer whose primary working language is Simplified Chinese, making this one of the few documented AitM campaigns with both a clear geographic developer signal and explicit AI-assisted lure generation.

Defenders should hunt for ClamAV signature `Html.Phishing.UAT11985-10060614-0` and Snort rules `1:67198` (Snort2) and `7:31` (Snort3). Network monitoring should flag WebSocket connections to actor-controlled infrastructure paired with `google_login_start`, `google_input_identifier`, and `google_login_check` HTTP POST events. Email filters need to inspect hyperlinks that display a legitimate Google Forms URL but redirect to a third-party domain, and security awareness should cover quishing from event posters, not just malicious attachments.

Watch for expanded targeting beyond Taiwan research organizations and for credential-reuse follow-on intrusions using harvested Google session tokens. Any new IoCs in the Cisco Talos GitHub repository (`github.com/Cisco-Talos/IOCs/blob/main/2026/10/uat-11985.txt`) or an updated ClamAV signature would shift the assessment significantly.
