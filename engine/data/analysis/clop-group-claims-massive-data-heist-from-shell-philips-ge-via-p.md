The Clop extortion group is exploiting CVE-2026-12569, a critical improper input validation and unsafe deserialization flaw in PTC Windchill and FlexPLM, to steal engineering data from industrial targets. Clop has listed 43 organizations on its dark web leak site, including Shell, Philips, and General Electric, with claimed exfiltration volumes ranging from 1 GB to 391 GB (GE) and 89 GB (Shell) of engineering drawings, product blueprints, and project plans. The vulnerability allows unauthenticated remote code execution through malicious requests to vulnerable PTC instances, and attackers deploy persistent JSP web shells inside /Windchill/login/ directories with hexadecimal filenames such as dpr_0123abcd.jsp to maintain access and collect data.

This campaign is not ransomware encryption but a data-theft extortion operation that continues Clop's history of mass exploitation of enterprise platforms after MOVEit and Oracle EBS. PTC released patches on June 17, 2026, yet researchers at ReliaQuest confirmed active zero-day exploitation began in early June, weeks before disclosure. CISA added CVE-2026-12569 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on June 26, 2026, pushing federal civilian agencies onto an emergency patch timeline. The exposure profile is particularly dangerous for manufacturers and aerospace firms because PLM systems hold unreleased product designs, supplier contracts, and bills of materials rather than ordinary consumer records, making intellectual property loss a direct competitive and regulatory risk even without a conventional breach notification trigger.

Defenders running Windchill or FlexPLM must apply PTC's June 2026 patches immediately across all nodes, including file servers and replicas, and restrict internet exposure of login endpoints behind VPN or zero-trust access where business needs allow. Exact hunt actions include blocking attacker C2 IP 5.180.41.35 at the perimeter, searching web server logs for POST requests to /Windchill/login/*.jsp, scanning the filesystem for .jsp files matching the 16-character lowercase hex pattern under .../Windchill/codebase/login/, checking for the file flst.txt in /tmp or the Windchill working directory, and adding WAF rules blocking the header X-windchill-req: which has no legitimate use. PTC also published an Apache LocationMatch control to deny access to the servlet path com.ptc.wvs.server.publish.Publish and an IIS URL Rewrite equivalent for installations prior to the patch.

The assessment could shift sharply if Clop publishes additional victim names or if forensic reviews at GE, Philips, or Shell confirm personal or employee data mixed with engineering files. A second key development would be evidence of opportunistic scanning by actors beyond Clop, which typically follows once a severe RCE in a widely deployed enterprise platform is tied to confirmed extortion and public IOCs. Until either development occurs, the priority remains confirming patch coverage, removing internet-facing PLM portals, and preserving logs from the July 20-26 confirmed exploitation window and the weeks before it.
