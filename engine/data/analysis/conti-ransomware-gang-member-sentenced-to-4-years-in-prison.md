A Ukrainian national, 44-year-old Oleksii Oleksiyovych Lytvynenko, has been sentenced to four years in US prison for his role in Conti ransomware operations between 2020 and 2022. Lytvynenko was arrested by Irish police in July 2023 at US request, extradited, and pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit wire fraud in June 2026. He admitted to joining Conti in September 2021 as both an intruder and developer, personally harming at least 12 companies (8 US, 4 overseas), storing stolen victim data, and coding a loader malware for the gang.

The FBI estimates Conti victim payouts exceeded $150 million as of January 2022. The gang targeted over 1,000 victims across 47 US states and 31 countries, attacking healthcare, government, and enterprise networks. Conti emerged from the Ryuk group in 2020 with ties to TrickBot, controlled multiple malware operations including BazarBackdoor, and shut down in 2022 after internal chats leaked and law enforcement pressure mounted. The syndicate later splintered into BlackCat, Black Basta, ZEON, Hive, Quantum, BlackByte, Karakurt, and Silent Ransom Group.

The four-year sentence drew criticism as lenient given the scale of damage. Seven TrickBot/Conti members were sanctioned in February 2023, nine Russian nationals charged in September 2023 for attacks on 900+ victims, and German BKA doxed the alleged leader, Vitaly Nikolaevich Kovalev, in May 2025. This is one of the few Conti cases resulting in actual imprisonment rather than sanctions or indictments against unreachable Russian nationals.
