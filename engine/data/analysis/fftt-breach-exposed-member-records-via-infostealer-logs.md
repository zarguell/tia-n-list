The French table tennis federation, FFTT, has confirmed a new intrusion in which a compromised account, with infostealer logs in the hands of the attacker, exposed licensed member records including identity details, addresses and copies of documents. The federation's own account was the entry point, which is the pattern that infostealer infections produce: harvested session material buys a login without touching the authentication path.

Zataz frames the FFTT case inside a wider French sports problem. Its reporting puts the number at 34 French sports federations compromised in two years, with more than 20 million records claimed by an actor operating as Chimera, and FFTT confirming a fresh intrusion on top of that tally. The claim of volume is the actor's, not an independently audited figure, so it should be read as an upper bound on the market rather than a count of verified victims.

The consequence for FFTT members is identity document exposure, not just contact data, which raises the risk of targeted fraud and account takeover against people who hold federation licences. Watch for a CNIL notification and for whether the federation resets credentials for its membership base, since infostealer-derived access survives password changes unless the sessions themselves are revoked.
