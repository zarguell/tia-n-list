East Suffolk and North Essex NHS Foundation Trust has launched an urgent investigation into a data breach involving the digital medical records of three-year-old Noah Woods, and has apologised for the unauthorised access. Ten NHS staff have been removed from duty or suspended over the breach. Dr Martin Mansfield, the trust's deputy chief medical officer, announced the investigation, and the BBC reported the trust's apology on September 27.

The numbers are the story: one child's record, ten staff members pulled from duty. That ratio says either access controls allowed a wide circle of employees to open a single patient file, or the auditing that caught them was unusually good. Snooping on records by staff is a recurring NHS failure mode, and a paediatric file makes the privacy stakes sharper than a routine adult record.

Watch three things: whether the trust's investigation establishes that access was curiosity rather than disclosure to third parties, whether the case draws in the ICO or children's social care given the subject, and whether the suspensions indicate the trust's audit tooling flagged the access proactively or the family's complaint started the inquiry. The outcome will show whether NHS record-access auditing catches insider browsing before patients do.
