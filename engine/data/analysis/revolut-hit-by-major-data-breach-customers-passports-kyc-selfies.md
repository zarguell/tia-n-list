Revolut has confirmed a data breach affecting customers whose passports, KYC selfies, and Bitcoin transaction data were exposed. The company emailed affected users directly. The breach has escalated: Italy's data protection authority has opened an investigation, and the attacker is now claiming a $3 million ransom demand with new evidence to back it up.

The exposed data is particularly sensitive because KYC documents are irrevocable. A leaked passport or selfie cannot be rotated like a password. Revolut's single-source reporting through thecybersecguru.com limits visibility into the full scope, but the Italian investigation suggests regulatory bodies are treating this as serious.

Watch for Revolut's official breach disclosure, the number of affected users, and whether the ransom demand leads to negotiation or refusal. Financial regulators in other EU jurisdictions may follow Italy's lead. For affected users, the priority is monitoring for identity fraud, not just financial theft.
