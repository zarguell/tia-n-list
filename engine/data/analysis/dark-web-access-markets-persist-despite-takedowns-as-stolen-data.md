Dark web access markets remain active despite forum seizures, arrests, and disruption campaigns; operators migrate to copycat forums, encrypted channels, and private brokers. The pricing data offers a warning signal for defenders: low-cost stolen data is broadly available, with US Social Security numbers selling for a few dollars, basic personal information like name and email under $15, and complete identity packages, or fullz, commonly $20 to $100. Payment card data and enterprise access are traded alongside, and the pricing reflects where criminal investment is concentrating.

For enterprise defenders the intelligence value is directional: falling prices mean the data is abundant and the market is saturated, while rising prices for a category, such as enterprise network access, signal increasing demand from ransomware and extortion groups. The takeaway is that identity data has essentially no residual value as a secret, so organizations should treat identity verification as the control, not the secrecy of the data itself.
