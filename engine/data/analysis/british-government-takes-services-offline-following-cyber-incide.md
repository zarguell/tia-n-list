The UK Department for Work and Pensions took several online services offline on November 27, 2026 after what it described as a cyber incident. The department did not disclose the nature of the incident, whether personal data was accessed or when services would return. DWP said regular benefit and pension payments were unaffected and would be paid as normal.

Claimants lost access to Universal Credit online accounts, Personal Independence Payment applications, State Pension online services and the Tell Us Once service for reporting a death. DWP said it was investigating with the National Cyber Security Centre and the Government Cyber Coordination Centre. The department is the largest in the British government by spending and administers pensions and a range of benefits used by millions of people, so even a precautionary outage has immediate practical consequences.

The main operational risk is disruption to people who need these services for income, disability support or bereavement reporting, not a confirmed payment failure. Watch for restoration notices, a full list of affected services, any data-access statement and NCSC attribution or technical findings. The incident should not be linked to the NCSC's separate warning about a rise in AI-assisted attacks without evidence connecting the two.
