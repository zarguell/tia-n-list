Britain's Department for Work and Pensions took a wide range of online services offline on November 27, 2026 after identifying a cyber incident. Claimants could not sign in to Universal Credit accounts, apply online for Personal Independence Payment or the State Pension, or use Tell Us Once to report a death. The department said regular benefit and pension payments were not affected and would be paid normally. BBC live reporting quoted the work and pensions secretary describing the outage as a serious cyber incident.

A November 28 follow-up examines the government's 19:09 notice revision under the heading "The Sanctions That Still Stand," focusing on claimants affected by the outage. The captured excerpt ends before the full sentence, so it establishes the timing and subject of the revision but not the precise scope of any sanction exemption. The issue matters because DWP administers pensions and benefits used by millions of people, and the outage prevents claimants from managing accounts, reporting changes and contacting caseworkers even while scheduled payments continue. DWP said it was investigating with the National Cyber Security Centre and the Government Cyber Coordination Centre.

The department had not disclosed the incident type, whether personal data was accessed or when services would return, and it had not published a complete list of affected services. The full wording of the revised notice and its treatment of sanctions are therefore key watchpoints, alongside restoration of the named claimant services and any findings on data exposure or attribution. The available reporting does not establish a connection between this incident and the NCSC's separate warning about a rise in AI-assisted cyberattacks.
