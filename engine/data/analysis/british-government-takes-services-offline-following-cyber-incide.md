Britain's Department for Work and Pensions took a wide range of online services offline on November 27, 2026 after identifying a cyber incident. Claimants could not sign in to Universal Credit accounts, apply online for Personal Independence Payment or the State Pension, or use Tell Us Once to report a death. DWP said regular benefit and pension payments were not affected and would be paid normally. BBC live reporting quoted the work and pensions secretary describing the outage as a serious cyber incident.

On November 28, BBC reported that pension and benefits services remained down. DWP had not disclosed the incident type, whether personal data was accessed or when services would return, and said it was investigating with the National Cyber Security Centre and Government Cyber Coordination Centre. A November 28 follow-up on the government's 19:09 notice revision indicates claimant guidance changed during the outage, but the captured excerpt does not establish the precise scope of any sanctions treatment.

The disruption matters because DWP administers pensions and benefits used by millions of people, while the outage prevents account management, reporting changes and contacting caseworkers even as scheduled payments continue. Watch for restoration of Universal Credit, PIP, State Pension and Tell Us Once services, the full revised notice, findings on data exposure and any attribution. The available reporting does not establish a connection between this incident and the NCSC's separate warning about a rise in AI-assisted cyberattacks.
