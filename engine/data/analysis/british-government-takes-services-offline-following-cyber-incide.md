Britain's Department for Work and Pensions took a wide range of online services offline on November 27, 2026 after identifying a cyber incident. Claimants lost access to Universal Credit accounts, online applications for Personal Independence Payment and the State Pension, and the Tell Us Once service for reporting a death. The department said regular benefit and pension payments were not affected and would be paid normally. BBC live reporting quoted the work and pensions secretary describing the outage as a serious cyber incident.

The disruption is significant because DWP administers state pensions and a broad range of benefits used by millions of people. The outage prevents claimants from managing accounts, reporting changes and contacting caseworkers even though scheduled payments continue. DWP said it was investigating with the National Cyber Security Centre and the Government Cyber Coordination Centre, placing the response within the UK's central cyber incident structure.

The department had not disclosed the incident type, whether personal data was accessed or when services would return. It also had not published a complete list of affected services, so the reported failures may not define the full scope. The key watchpoints are restoration of the named claimant services, findings on data access and any technical attribution from the investigation. There is no basis in the available reporting to connect this incident to a specific vulnerability, threat actor or attack method.
