Ars Technica reports that Trump Mobile, the phone service tied to the Trump brand, was hacked, and that the service appears to lack FCC authorization to operate phone service at all, a point a US senator has raised publicly. The two issues together suggest a consumer telecom product that was both breached and operating outside the normal regulatory gate that would have examined its security posture.

Tia N. List separately tracks the service's earlier incidents: a May disclosure of customer data exposure and an October 6 BYOD breach that leaked 3,615 customer records. The new reporting adds a regulator gap on top of a pattern of data handling failures, which is what makes it more than a repeat story.

Watch for the FCC's position on whether the service required authorization and what happens if it did not have it, the senator's inquiry outcome, and whether the newly reported hack connects to the earlier breach disclosures or involves additional customer data.
