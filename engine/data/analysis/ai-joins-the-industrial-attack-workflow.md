Recorded Future's analysis links the October attack on retail refrigeration in France to the ARTEX offensive AI framework. On October 11 and 12, 2026, defrost settings were changed on refrigeration controllers at more than 700 French retail locations, causing fires, damaged equipment, and food losses estimated in the tens of millions of euros. France attributed the attack on November 9 to Shahid Kaveh, a unit of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Cyber Electronic Command.

The authors track a likely element of Shahid Kaveh as GreenJuliett, also known as CyberAv3ngers and Bauxite. The group builds infrastructure aimed at internet-facing PLCs, kept that work going through at least October 30 despite repeated public reporting, and widened its target set between August and October, with GreenJuliett-linked infrastructure scanning or attempting to exploit additional systems. The write-up argues that AI lets actors scale and speed up these operations, with tools such as ARTEX filling an operator role while humans stay in control.

The French incident turned controller access into physical damage at hundreds of sites, and the report sees AI-assisted tooling entering industrial attack chains used by both financially motivated and state-sponsored groups. Watch for further GreenJuliett scanning against internet-facing controllers, attribution tied to ARTEX activity, and any expansion of the retail refrigeration target set beyond France.
