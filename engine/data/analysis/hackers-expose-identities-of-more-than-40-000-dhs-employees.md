A threat actor using the handle "Department of Peace" leaked a database of more than 41,000 records containing personal and work information of US Department of Homeland Security employees and contractors. The attacker claims the data was stolen from an Okta single sign-on system used to access DHS's central environment, and stated "They have our information, so it's only fair that we have theirs." Okta denied any compromise of its systems. Most leaked records reportedly cover US Customs and Border Protection, Immigration and Customs Enforcement, and Homeland Security Investigations personnel, with roughly 1,000 CISA employees among those exposed. DHS acknowledged a potential breach and said it is working to address the situation.

The compromised data includes names, work email addresses and phone numbers, job titles, office locations, and records showing which systems and internal applications each account can access. An online search tool for the leaked data is also available. The story has drawn widespread coverage across outlets and social platforms, with over a dozen corroborating events tracked. The exposure of access-scope records alongside identity data raises the risk of targeted follow-on attacks against DHS staff and systems.
