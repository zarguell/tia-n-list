A threat actor using the handle Department of Peace leaked a database containing over 41,000 records of US Department of Homeland Security employees, including Customs and Border Protection, Immigration and Customs Enforcement, and Homeland Security Investigations staff, plus roughly 1,000 CISA employees. The data includes names, work email addresses, phone numbers, job titles, office locations, and system access records, and was allegedly stolen from an Okta single sign-on system used by DHS. Okta denied compromise of its own systems. DHS confirmed awareness and said it is actively addressing the situation.

The leak includes an online search tool for the data, increasing exposure risk. Most records cover DHS operational staff rather than high-level officials, but the inclusion of system-access information raises concerns about targeted follow-on attacks. Organizations with DHS partnerships should review access logs and assume that employee contact and role data is now public. The incident highlights the risk of SSO platforms as aggregation points for identity and access data.
