A threat actor using the handle "Department of Peace" leaked a database of more than 41,000 Department of Homeland Security employee records online. Most records reportedly cover U.S. Customs and Border Protection, Immigration and Customs Enforcement, and Homeland Security Investigations personnel, plus roughly 1,000 CISA employees and staff at several other DHS agencies. The compromised data includes names, work email addresses and phone numbers, job titles, office locations, and records showing which systems and internal applications each account can access. The attacker claims the data was stolen from an Okta single sign-on system used by DHS employees and contractors; Okta states it is secure and has no evidence its data or systems were compromised. DHS said it is aware of the potential breach and is working to protect employees.

An online search tool called Icepick allowed browsing the leaked data. DHS contractor ZeroFox asked Icelandic host FlokiNET to remove it; FlokiNET refused, but registrar CentralNic placed the icepi.cx domain on serverHold, taking the site offline. The site later returned under a different registrar. Separately, Nextgov/FCW confirmed the leak reaches DHS's Office of Intelligence and Analysis, with about 75 records tying personnel to that office, and verified two listed phone numbers belonged to I&A officials.

The full scope within I&A remains unclear, and the attribution of the breach to Okta is unconfirmed. Watch for DHS employee-safety measures, any change in the Okta attribution, and whether the Icepick mirror stays online.
