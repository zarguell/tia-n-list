A dark web marketplace called Nexus began selling scans of more than 153 million U.S. and Canadian driver's licenses on August 31, advertised on the Russian-language Exploit forum. The FBI has opened an investigation. The dataset includes over 153 million driver's licenses, 10 million ID cards, 3 million travel or international identity documents, and at least 579,000 medical cards. Operators claimed access to 170 million identity documents total. An unauthenticated blank search returned roughly 11.5 million result pages, each displaying about 15 records, consistent with the claimed scale.

The records may have originated from a Louisiana-based identity-verification provider, though this has not been confirmed. Canadian documents make up a smaller portion, with approximately 1.1 million driver's license results including 473,673 from Ontario. The listings include front-and-back license images, standard scans, infrared captures, ultraviolet images, photographs, and timestamps. This level of detail could enable identity theft, account takeover, and bypassing of KYC verification systems.

The scale is what makes this incident significant. Previous large identity breaches typically involved static PII like names and Social Security numbers. Driver's license scans include biometric photo data and document-specific visual features that many automated verification systems rely on. If the data is authentic, it represents a substantial expansion of what criminals can use to forge or duplicate government-issued identity documents at scale.
