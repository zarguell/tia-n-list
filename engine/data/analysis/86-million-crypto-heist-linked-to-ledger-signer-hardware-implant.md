A manually soldered hardware implant inside Ledger signer enclosures sold through Southeast Asia reseller CryptoBilis reads the 24-word recovery phrase from the device screen during setup, decodes it via an onboard microcontroller, and exfiltrates it over LTE. Ledger confirmed on October 13 that every confirmed fund loss linked to the incident involved a signer from CryptoBilis, and the initial disclosure was October 9. The implant is not a firmware vulnerability; it is a physical supply-chain modification inside the enclosure that intercepts the screen output, meaning conventional endpoint or network detection does not apply to setup-phase exposure.

The $86 million figure covers confirmed losses from multiple users, and the attacker specifically targeted the 24-word seed display rather than transaction authorization. Because the compromise is bounded to one reseller channel, the exposure map is narrow in vendor terms but deep for anyone who set up a device from CryptoBilis within the last 90 days. The story matters more than a generic crypto loss headline because it demonstrates that hardware wallet supply chains can be modified post-manufacture to harvest seeds silently, a mechanism that undermines the core security claim of air-gapped signers.

Defenders should treat any CryptoBilis-purchased Ledger signer as untrusted: do not initiate setup, and if already set up, migrate assets to a new Ledger signer with a fresh seed. Ledger instructed customers to stop using affected devices and to consider moving assets. Hunt focus should be on LTE exfiltration indicators tied to the implant's microcontroller rather than standard blockchain telemetry; review physical device provenance, check purchase dates within the 90-day window, and inspect circuit boards for unauthorized solder if forensics are available.

Watch for Ledger confirming the total number of affected devices and whether additional reseller channels beyond CryptoBilis emerge. A second development that would shift the assessment is evidence of the same implant design in devices sold outside Southeast Asia, which would expand the exposure from a single reseller compromise to a broader supply-chain incident.
