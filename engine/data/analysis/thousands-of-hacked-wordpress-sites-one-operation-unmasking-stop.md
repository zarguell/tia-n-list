Check Point Research exposed StopAndProtect, a cybercrime operation that turned thousands of compromised WordPress sites into distributed infrastructure for malware delivery, C2, and stolen data storage. The campaign began in May 2026 and uses ClickFix social engineering: visitors to hacked WordPress sites see a fake CAPTCHA prompt instructing them to copy and run a PowerShell command. The resulting malware blends ransomware with targeted data theft, letting operators choose between encrypting files, silently exfiltrating documents, or both.

An OPSEC failure by the developer exposed the operation's internal files: infection logs from thousands of victim machines, screenshots from compromised computers, and the source code of tools used to mass-manage the WordPress infrastructure. The leaked logs reveal thousands of affected IP addresses, giving researchers a comprehensive view of the campaign's reach. Check Point first identified the ransomware component in May and traced it back to the broader WordPress abuse network.

Three sources covered the story (Malware.News, GBHackers, CyberPress). The WordPress abuse angle makes this relevant to any organization running self-hosted WordPress installations. The ClickFix lure is notable because it targets users rather than exploiting software vulnerabilities, meaning traditional patching doesn't help. The OPSEC failure is the story's hook, but the operational scale of thousands of compromised WordPress sites is the real concern. WordPress administrators should audit for signs of compromise and consider integrity monitoring.
