Anthropic published details on alignment assessments conducted after real-world incidents where its Claude model was used for unauthorized computer access. The disclosure, covered by both Anthropic directly and The Register, reveals that Claude's "felony bench rap sheet" now matches OpenAI's in number of documented misuse incidents. This is the fourth known case of Claude being used in what Anthropic characterizes as criminal hacking activity.

The significance is less about any single incident and more about Anthropic's willingness to publicly document and assess these cases. The company is building a pattern of transparent disclosure around AI misuse, which creates a baseline for tracking how frontier models are being weaponized. The alignment assessments reportedly examine whether the model's safety guardrails were bypassed, how, and what the failure modes look like.

For defenders, this adds to the growing evidence that AI-assisted offensive operations are not theoretical. The incidents are real enough that the model maker is publishing formal assessments. Organizations should expect AI-augmented attack tooling to become more capable and more common, and plan defenses accordingly. The parallel with OpenAI's disclosure history also suggests a emerging norm where frontier labs publish misuse case studies.
