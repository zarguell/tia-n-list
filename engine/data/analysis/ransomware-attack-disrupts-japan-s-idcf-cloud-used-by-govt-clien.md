IDC Frontier, a SoftBank Group subsidiary, disclosed that its IDCF Cloud IaaS platform was hit by a third-party ransomware attack starting October 7 at 3:40 AM local time. The attack forced a shutdown of the East Japan Region 1 cluster and affects 495 customer organizations, including companies and local governments. Management consoles for all regions were disabled as a precaution while the company verifies their security and hunts the intrusion route.

The Oct 8 advisory is the damaging part: four zones in East Japan Region 1, named tesla, henry, pascal and joule, had virtual servers that stopped and cannot be restarted. Customer data stored in those zones is expected to be difficult to extract or restore, with recovery possible only from backups customers hold themselves. IDCF Cloud TypeS, formerly White Cloud ASPIRE, and IDCF Private Cloud are out of scope. IDC Frontier has reported the incident to its supervising ministry and the Metropolitan Police.

A cloud provider that cannot restore its own virtual machines after ransomware is the headline here, since IaaS customers routinely assume the provider layer is recoverable. The blast radius of 495 tenants, some of them municipal governments, makes this one of the larger Japanese cloud incidents this year. Watch for the intrusion vector findings, any evidence of data exfiltration before encryption, a restoration timeline for the four dead zones, and regulatory fallout in Tokyo.
