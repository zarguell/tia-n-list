IDC Frontier, a SoftBank subsidiary, disclosed that a ransomware attack hit its IDCF Cloud service starting around 03:40 JST on October 7, 2026, forcing the shutdown of East Japan Region 1 at the Shirakawa (Fukushima) data center. The second company advisory put the affected population at 495 companies and municipalities contracting the cloud, including government clients. By October 8 the company narrowed the damage to the tesla, henry, pascal, and joule zones, where virtual servers stopped and cannot be restarted, and warned that customer data stored there is expected to be difficult to extract or restore, recoverable only from backups customers hold themselves. IDCF Cloud TypeS, formerly White Cloud ASPIRE, and IDCF Private Cloud are out of scope, and management consoles in other regions were shut off pending safety checks. The company reported the incident to its supervising ministry and the Metropolitan Police.

Why it matters: this is a single cloud operator outage propagated into hundreds of downstream organizations at once, the cloud-era equivalent of an MSP compromise. JR East and View Card disclosed that up to about 6.09 million records held through an email delivery service may have been viewed or taken. A threat actor claim of 3.6 PB encrypted is circulating but unverified. Customer-facing effects are already visible: Peach Aviation reported email service disruption because its provider ran on the affected cloud.

Watch the intrusion-route identification IDC Frontier says is ongoing, whether other regions are cleared and consoles restored, and further customer disclosures in the mold of JR East, since 495 affected organizations imply a long tail of breach notifications. The likely unrecoverable-data warning is a blunt reminder that provider availability does not substitute for customer-held backups.
