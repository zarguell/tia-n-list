IDC Frontier, a SoftBank Group subsidiary, disclosed a ransomware attack on its IDCF Cloud service that began October 7 at 3:40 AM local time and forced the shutdown of East Japan Region 1. The company confirmed the outage was caused by a third-party ransomware attack and put the affected population at 495 companies and municipalities contracting the cloud service. Network isolation and system shutdown were completed, and management consoles were disabled across all regions pending safety verification.

The October 8 advisory narrowed the damage to part of East Japan Region 1, specifically the tesla, henry, pascal, and joule zones, where virtual servers stopped and could not be restarted. The company warned that customer data stored in those zones is expected to be difficult to extract or restore, with recovery possible only from backups customers hold themselves. IDCF Cloud TypeS, formerly White Cloud ASPIRE, and IDCF Private Cloud were declared out of scope. IDC Frontier reported the incident to its supervising ministry and the Metropolitan Police.

This is a cloud-provider event, not a single-tenant breach: the blast radius covers 495 organizations that rely on the provider's recovery posture. The warning that data may be effectively unrecoverable without customer-held backups is the sharpest part of the disclosure. Watch for identification of the intrusion route, ransomware group claims, whether management console access and the four affected zones are restored, and any regulatory follow-up in Japan.
