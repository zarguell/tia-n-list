CrowdStrike said on November 23 that it agreed to acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction valued at about $43.9 billion. The deal pairs one of the largest endpoint security vendors with a cloud-delivered zero trust access provider. The two companies already share more than 2,000 customers and have 16 product integrations. In September they announced plans to expand the partnership so Falcon could continuously assess user risk and pass that signal into Zscaler's access controls. CrowdStrike says it intends to bring the products onto a single platform with a common data layer and one customer contract after closing.

The stated logic is about agents as much as people. CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz said the network used to connect people to places and now connects agents to data. Chief business officer Daniel Bernard described the split as CrowdStrike software watching what an agent does on the device and Zscaler software setting what that agent can reach over the network. The acquisition also consolidates a run of Zscaler purchases: SquareX in February 2026, SPLX in November 2025, Red Canary in August 2025, and Avalor and Airgap Networks in 2024.

What to watch is execution on the integration rather than the price. The risk signals that Falcon already collects from endpoints and cloud workloads would become inputs to Zscaler access decisions if the shared data layer ships as described. Customers with both products should watch for changes to the September risk-sharing arrangement, contract migration details and any statement about how zero trust policy will be managed once the platforms merge.
