CrowdStrike has agreed to buy Zscaler in an all-stock deal valued at about $43.9 billion. The terms published in CrowdStrike's release offer 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each Zscaler share, an implied $260.41 per share based on the November 20 close and a 34 percent premium. Zscaler holders would own roughly 15.9 percent of the combined company, and closing is targeted for the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal year 2028, subject to shareholder and regulatory approval. The two firms say they share more than 2,000 customers and 16 product integrations, and CrowdStrike projects about $1 billion in annualized run-rate revenue synergies by 30 months after close.

The logic is product convergence around AI agents. CrowdStrike's Falcon software observes activity on endpoints and cloud workloads, while Zscaler's zero-trust service decides what users and programs may reach over the network. CEO George Kurtz framed it as a shift from a network that connects people to places to one that connects agents to data. Chief business officer Daniel Bernard said the combination was years in the making and described endpoint activity and network access rules as two halves of the same agent-security problem. Earlier reporting put the combined market value near $265 billion, with Zscaler around $32 billion and CrowdStrike around $233 billion; Reuters noted Zscaler shares jumped 17 percent after hours and CrowdStrike fell 8 percent.

Zscaler arrives with an acquisition history of its own: SquareX in February 2026, SPLX in November 2025, Red Canary in August 2025, Avalor for $350 million and Airgap Networks in 2024. It says it protects half of the Fortune 500 through more than 200 service locations. What to watch is approval risk and execution: a close more than a year out leaves time for regulatory challenge, and the promised single platform, common data layer and single contract have to survive integration across two large product estates.
