CrowdStrike has agreed to acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction valued at about $43.9 billion. The company's release sets an exchange ratio of 1.135 CrowdStrike shares per Zscaler share, implying $260.41 per share and a 34% premium to Zscaler's November 20 close. Zscaler holders would own about 15.9% of the combined company. Closing is expected in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal year 2028, subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals.

The deal combines CrowdStrike's endpoint and cloud-application visibility with Zscaler's zero-trust access controls. The companies say they already share more than 2,000 customers and have 16 product integrations. Their stated plan is one platform, one data layer and one customer contract, with run-rate revenue synergies projected at about $1 billion within 30 months after closing. The operational test is whether shared telemetry improves policy decisions for users, applications and AI agents while preserving the value of existing integrations.

The transaction remains subject to approvals and has not closed. Watch the shareholder and regulatory process, the timing against the stated fiscal 2028 window, and any details on product packaging or migration. For security teams, the immediate issue is continuity across Falcon and Zscaler deployments; the broader significance is concentrating endpoint detection and network access policy under one vendor.
