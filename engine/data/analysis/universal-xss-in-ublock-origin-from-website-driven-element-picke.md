uBlock Origin's Firefox MV2 element picker accepted `postMessage` traffic from the page embedding it, and GHSA-cmqw-9r2w-6wv2 rates the resulting universal XSS high with no CVE assigned. Affected builds are 1.75.2 and lower; the vendor shipped a verified secret token in 1.75.4 on November 3 and added filter validation plus WAR restrictions in 1.76.0 on November 8. Reporter syvb sent the bug on November 1. The chain recovers the per-install extension UUID through a WAR-backed `no-fetch-if` hook, drives the picker with `epickerStart` and `resultsetDetails` messages, and smuggles a trusted `+js(trusted-rpnt, ...)` filter through a newline in the `opt` field. One click on an overlaid or transparent Create control makes the injected filter trusted and executable on a chosen origin.

The impact is a privilege boundary failure, not an ad-blocking nuisance: a hostile page can plant a trusted scriptlet that runs JavaScript in the context of any origin the user visits next, including sites the extension is not blocking on. Picker-created filters stay trusted until a filter-list recompile, so editing custom filters after the click is a weak remedy when the attacker can open the target page immediately. Every Firefox MV2 install at 1.75.2 or older is in scope, and the MV3 uBO Lite variant is described as similar but less problematic. This story deserves desk attention despite thin public pickup: two source domains, four Mastodon events and no Reddit posts, with no CVE and no published exploitation telemetry. A low queue score here reflects reporting volume, not the reach of a successful click.

Patch to 1.75.4 or later and prefer 1.76.0, which adds filter parsing and blocks `war:`-related filters; verify the extension version on Firefox MV2 endpoints rather than assuming auto-update. Until rollout is confirmed, avoid the element picker on untrusted sites. Hunt the custom filter store for unexpected `+js(trusted-rpnt` entries, especially alongside the WAR-backed `no-fetch-if` filter named in the advisory; the test rule `##+js(prevent-xhr, leakme, war:noop.js)` is another reported artifact. No server log path is named in the reporting, so the practical telemetry is browser-side: extension message fields `what`, `opt` and `resultsetDetails`, plus page frames requesting `epicker-ui.html`.

Two developments would change the assessment: evidence of in-the-wild use of the published demo or other exploitation telemetry, and confirmation that the MV3 uBO Lite variant reaches the same trusted-filter outcome. A bypass of the 9859477 token check or the 1.76.0 parser would raise urgency further, but neither is reported in the sourced material.
