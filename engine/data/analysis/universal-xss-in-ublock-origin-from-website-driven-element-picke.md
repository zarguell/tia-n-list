A one-click universal cross-site scripting flaw in uBlock Origin's Firefox MV2 element picker was disclosed by Smitty van Bodegom, also known as loops. The chain recovers the per-install extension UUID through a WAR-backed fetch hook, embeds the element picker in a hidden iframe, and drives it with postMessage messages named epickerStart, showDialog, and resultsetDetails. A newline in the opt field turns the displayed network filter into a second trusted scriptlet filter for another domain. The published example uses the default rule lootdest.*##+js(no-fetch-if, adsbygoogle, war:googlesyndication_adsbygoogle.js) and a click on a transparent Create control. The writeup credits GPT-5.6 Sol with finding the bug and Claude with most of the PoC code.

The vendor advisory rates the issue high, assigns no CVE, and says uBlock Origin 1.75.2 and lower are affected. Fixes appear in 1.75.2, 1.75.4, and 1.76.0 through commits bf69cf7, 9859477, b1f7d27, and 150df12. The report reached the vendor on November 1. A less severe variant affects uBO Lite on MV3, and the advisory also notes extraction of the WAR secret on MV2 Chromium.

The fix passes a secret password from the content script to the picker so messages from outside pages are rejected. Tails removed uBlock Origin from its Tor Browser bundle in 7.17.1 as a downstream mitigation, and the archived researcher post recirculated on November 18. Watch for a CVE assignment and for confirmation that every affected branch is covered by the listed releases.
