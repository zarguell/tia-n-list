A targeted malware campaign is delivering the SNOWLIGHT stager and fileless VShell RAT to Chinese academic researchers using a convincing graduate-school resume as bait. The attack centers on a ZIP archive claiming to contain a resume from a Beijing Institute of Technology graduate named Zhang Yuguang, interested in AI-based power-grid diagnosis and renewable-energy control. The lure is written in Simplified Chinese and includes WPS Office metadata, suggesting it was crafted for mainland Chinese academic recipients.

The infection chain begins with a 32-bit Go loader that performs anti-analysis checks before executing. The loader specifically looks for the ThreatBook sandbox environment, exits on systems with fewer than four CPU cores, and uses the Windows Beep function as a timing test to detect virtualized environments. Once these checks pass, it opens a legitimate-looking Word document as a decoy while silently deploying SNOWLIGHT shellcode in memory. SNOWLIGHT then loads VShell, a fileless remote-access trojan, entirely in memory without writing to disk.

The campaign likely originates from a Chinese-speaking threat actor targeting academic and technical research environments. The specificity of the lure (academic research topics, mainland Chinese terminology) and the sophistication of the anti-sandbox techniques suggest an advanced operation. Organizations in Chinese academic and research sectors should be aware of this luring technique and ensure endpoint detection tools can identify SNOWLIGHT and VShell signatures.
