CNN reporters Sean Lyngaas and Davis Winkie, picked up via DataBreaches.net, report that unauthorized users gained access to a vulnerable computer server belonging to the Defense Manpower Data Center, the Pentagon's central HR system. The breach exposed Social Security numbers and other personal information of current and former military personnel. Reporting began surfacing around September 24 via Military Times, with CNN's fuller account on September 25.

The exposure matters beyond ordinary identity theft. National security experts cited in the reporting raise counterintelligence concerns: SSNs, service data, and contact details for military personnel are raw material for targeted phishing, coercion attempts, and profiling of people holding clearances. The access reportedly began through a vulnerable server, which points to an unpatched or misconfigured system in a high-value federal HR platform rather than a sophisticated novel technique.

The agency has warned affected personnel to monitor their accounts for phishing, and the CNN reporting urges military HR administrators to audit access logs and tighten controls. Watch for the scale of the notification effort, whether any foreign actor is attributed, congressional or DoD IG scrutiny of how a DMDC server was left vulnerable, and follow-on phishing campaigns targeting service members.
