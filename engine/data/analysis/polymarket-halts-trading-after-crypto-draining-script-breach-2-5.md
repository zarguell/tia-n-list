On October 29, 2026, Polymarket's international platform Polymarket.com served malicious JavaScript through a third-party performance-monitoring integration, DebugBear real-user monitoring, that drained user wallets. Hudson Rock retrieved the 15,187-byte script from a DebugBear URL at 18:12 UTC and again at 18:13 UTC; it searches for a logged-in user's trading client and signer, inspects the portfolio, and attempts to sell or transfer assets to an attacker-controlled wallet. Polymarket placed the exchange in cancel-only mode at 18:05 UTC, identified and fixed the issue by 18:37 UTC, and resumed trading around 19:05 UTC, while production build metadata recorded removal of the DebugBear RUM script with a build timestamp of 17:59:20 UTC. Loss figures remain inconsistent: Hudson Rock cites $800,000 reported and a separate screenshot showing over $2 million in holdings, BleepingComputer traces roughly $2.5 million to a suspected attacker wallet, and an early X report cited about $2.68 million. Polymarket has not disclosed how many users were affected or how reimbursement will work.

The exposure is anyone who loaded Polymarket.com while authenticated during the window the compromised script was served, because the payload ran in the victim's own browser session and used the platform's existing trading client and signer rather than stealing private keys. That means the blast radius is defined by page loads, not by a database breach, and no password reset closes it retroactively. The incident also repeats a pattern Polymarket itself acknowledged: a June 25, 2026 third-party vendor compromise injected malicious frontend code and stole about $3 million from fewer than 15 accounts, following a May 2026 internal top-up wallet compromise of about $700,000. Three frontend or wallet incidents in five months points to a control gap in third-party script governance and release verification rather than in the smart contracts, and defenders at similar trading platforms should treat any authenticated web app loading a third-party RUM or analytics script as a wallet-draining candidate.

Defenders should verify that no DebugBear or other third-party RUM script loads on authenticated trading pages, enforce a strict Content Security Policy that blocks unreviewed third-party hosts, and pin or hash external scripts with subresource integrity before deployment. On Polymarket specifically, affected users should monitor wallet 0x3F99139F1C5854bb628c2e576Aaf638e99dFfc18 and any Relay cross-chain swaps from USDC.e to POL, review Polygon transactions for unauthorized sells or transfers during the incident window, and await Polymarket's reimbursement process, which the company has promised but not detailed. Hunt telemetry should focus on page loads of Polymarket.com between roughly 17:59 UTC and 18:13 UTC on October 29, 2026, outbound requests to data.debugbear.com, and browser sessions that triggered position sells or asset transfers without user action.

The assessment changes if Polymarket publishes an affected-user count or final loss total, if the attacker's initial access into DebugBear or Polymarket's build pipeline is identified, or if the promised reimbursements are delayed or disputed. Watch also for copycat injection of the same DebugBear URL on other sites, since the script host and its Content Security Policy allowance may have been reused elsewhere.
