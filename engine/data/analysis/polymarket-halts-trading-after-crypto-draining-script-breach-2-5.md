On October 29, 2026, Polymarket placed its exchange in cancel-only mode at 2:05 p.m. ET and paused relayer actions after discovering that a third-party provider had been compromised and was distributing cryptocurrency-draining JavaScript through a performance-monitoring script. The payload, found by Hudson Rock in a DebugBear RUM script, reads a logged-in user's trading client and signer, inspects their portfolio, and attempts to sell or transfer assets. Polymarket identified and fixed the issue by 2:37 p.m. ET, resumed trading roughly an hour later, and announced full reimbursement for affected traders.

The incident shows that a performance-monitoring integration, not Polymarket's own code, became the theft vector, which means third-party script integrity is part of the exchange's attack surface. Reported losses range from $800,000 to over $2 million in holdings, with about $2.5 million traced to a suspected attacker wallet, though aggregate losses remain unverified. The platform's rapid containment and reimbursement promise limit user harm, but the long window of unauthorized script execution before detection is the key control failure.

Traders should verify reimbursement receipts and rotate any session credentials. Watch for law-enforcement updates on the third-party provider compromise and whether the attacker wallet is frozen. The case also warrants review of subresource integrity and script allowlisting for any platform that embeds third-party monitoring or analytics.
