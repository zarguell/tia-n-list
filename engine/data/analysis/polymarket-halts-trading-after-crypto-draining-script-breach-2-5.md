Polymarket suspended trading after attackers compromised a third-party service to distribute cryptocurrency-draining JavaScript, tracing roughly $2.5 million to a suspected attacker wallet. The incident started with cancel-only mode at 2:05 p.m. ET, a fix by 2:37 p.m., and full resumption around 3:05 p.m. ET; the company referred the breach to law enforcement and promised to make affected users whole.

Hudson Rock identified the malicious payload embedded in a performance-monitoring script (DebugBear RUM), which searched for logged-in trading clients, inspected portfolios, and attempted to sell or redirect assets. A production build explicitly records the integration's removal, corroborating remediation, and Hudson Rock linked a suspected attacker-controlled wallet to the theft. The separate recipient screenshot showed over $2 million in holdings, aligning with the reported $2.5 million traced.

The core risk is supply-chain compromise through a vendor script rather than a direct platform vulnerability. Polymarket has not disclosed the number of affected users or reimbursement mechanics, and aggregate losses remain unverified. Watch for the law-enforcement referral outcome, confirmation of reimbursement, and whether similar third-party script compromises surface at other trading platforms.
