Prediction market platform Polymarket temporarily suspended trading on Thursday after hackers compromised a third-party service to distribute cryptocurrency-draining JavaScript. The company placed its exchange into cancel-only mode at 2:05 p.m. ET and paused relayer actions while investigating, announced at 2:37 p.m. that the issue was fixed, and resumed trading around 3:05 p.m. ET. Polymarket said one of its third-party providers was compromised, referred the matter to law enforcement, and promised to fully reimburse affected traders.

Hudson Rock's technical analysis found the malicious code in a DebugBear real-user-monitoring script associated with Polymarket. The JavaScript reads account context, locates the page's trading client and signer, inspects the portfolio, and attempts to sell or transfer assets. Polymarket's production build records removal of the DebugBear RUM integration. Loss figures are inconsistent across sources: Hudson Rock cites $800,000 reported and a separate recipient-address screenshot showing over $2 million in holdings, while BleepingComputer traces roughly $2.5 million to a suspected attacker wallet. Aggregate losses remain unverified, and Polymarket has not disclosed how many users were affected or how reimbursement will work.

Because the compromise sat in a performance-monitoring integration rather than Polymarket's own code, similar third-party scripts on other platforms warrant review. Watch for disclosure of affected user counts, final loss tallies, and any law-enforcement or reimbursement updates.
