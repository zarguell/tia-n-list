UpGuard researchers found more than 16,000 misconfigured Supabase databases exposing readable tables to the open internet. Supabase is a PostgreSQL-based backend-as-a-service popular with rapid app development, and AI-assisted development now accounts for over 60 percent of newly created databases on the platform. Scanning about 300,000 domains showing signs of Supabase use, the researchers probed for accessible tables and used table schemas to infer what data types were exposed.

In more than half of the exposed databases the readable tables held personally identifiable information, with a smaller subset exposing passwords and authentication tokens, and what UpGuard believes is a very small set of credit card data. Notable cases include a U.S. valet service exposing over 100,000 customer records with contact details, license plates, and visit history, plus Canadian immigration-related data. None of this requires an exploit; the databases are simply readable as configured.

The story is a configuration hygiene problem amplified by AI-speed development: vibe-coded apps ship with public tables because nobody reviews the security defaults. Watch for the exposed token and password sets showing up in credential stuffing, for Supabase tightening defaults or adding exposure warnings, and for follow-up research quantifying how many of these databases get actively harvested.
