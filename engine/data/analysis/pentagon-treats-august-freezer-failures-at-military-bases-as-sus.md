The Pentagon is investigating a suspected cyberattack behind freezer malfunctions that spoiled food at military bases across the United States in August. The outages affected about six commissaries, the on-base grocery stores serving military personnel. At Fort Huachuca in Arizona, officials reported a freezer entered defrost mode and heated its contents, leaving food spoiled. Reuters first reported Thursday that investigators are treating the incidents as a possible hack. It remains unclear who might be responsible or whether investigators have conclusively determined what caused the failures. Cyber Command and various Defense Department law enforcement units are probing the incident, and French authorities are also working with officials examining the matter. The report also references a recent similar incident in France involving overridden temperature controls on commercial refrigeration equipment, though details were not available in the reporting.

Attribution is unconfirmed and the cause has not been conclusively determined, so the incident should be treated as suspected rather than confirmed. The cross-border involvement of French authorities suggests investigators are looking for links to the earlier refrigeration-control attack.
