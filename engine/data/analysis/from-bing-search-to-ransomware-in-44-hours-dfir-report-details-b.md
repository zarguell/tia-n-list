From Bing Search to Ransomware in 44 Hours: DFIR Report
The intrusion started with SEO poisoning: a user searching Bing for ManageEngine OpManager landed on a cloned download page at opmanager[.]pro, then downloaded a trojanized MSI signed by LLC Vector. The delivery infrastructure used Hostinger gateways (download-center[.]online) with shared certificates. Once inside, Bumblebee deployed an AdaptixC2 beacon (AdgNsy.exe) connecting to 172.96.137[.]160, followed by a reverse SSH tunnel and RustDesk for persistence. The actor harvested NTDS.dit via wbadmin.exe, decrypted Veeam credentials with DPAPI, dumped LSASS with lsassy, and systematically scanned password managers, browsers, cloud platforms and dev directories. About 77 GB was exfiltrated over two SFTP sessions via FileZilla to 185.174.100[.]203 in Ukraine, and Akira ransomware (locker.exe) targeted specific drive paths with partial encryption (-n=15) before deleting Shadow Copies via WMI.
A parallel Swisscom intrusion used near-identical TTPs: WMIC service termination, staged win.exe with netonly flag. Detection should focus on organic search traffic to cloned enterprise download pages, Event ID 5145 for systematic credential scanning, DGA DNS queries from non-browser processes, and FileZilla installations over RDP clipboard. The chain from search to ransomware in under two days shows how SEO-poisoned software downloads remain an effective initial access vector.
