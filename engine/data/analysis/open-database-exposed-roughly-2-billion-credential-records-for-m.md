Jeremiah Fowler's November 24, 2026 report, published by ExpressVPN, describes an unencrypted, password-free database holding about 2 billion credential records, the largest collection he has found and roughly 13 times the 149 million logins he reported earlier in 2026. The repository held 8,471 folders of plaintext credentials, some segmented by US, UK, FR, DE, ES and MX, and about 400,000 documents from more advanced infostealer malware that built per-victim folders named applications, cookies, edge, important files and operating systems. Records mixed passwords with browser cookies, session tokens, autofill data, device details and cryptocurrency wallet material, and thousands of Tor .onion addresses carried account names and passwords. Fowler notified the hosting provider, public access was restricted several days later, and no owner has been identified.

This is not a breach at Gmail, Facebook, Instagram, Amazon, OpenAI or ChatGPT, and the service counts are overlapping line matches with duplicates, not confirmed victims. That distinction does not lower the defensive stakes: 608 million Gmail records, 114.2 million Google service records, 88 million Facebook records, 32.8 million X records, 2.2 million OpenAI and ChatGPT records, 61.4 million .gov entries and 46 million .edu entries put consumer logins, government mailboxes and corporate portal accounts in one retrieval set. Because the logs include session tokens and cookies, an attacker can replay a live session instead of guessing a password, which makes a reset-only response inadequate while the originating endpoint is still compromised. This story outranks a routine credential dump because it packages account takeover material at 13 times the size of Fowler's prior 149 million record find, with no owner and no published exposure window.

No patch or hotfix version applies, since the failure was an access-control mistake on a storage repository rather than a shipped software flaw. Defenders should treat endpoints with unfamiliar browser extensions, disabled security tooling, odd processes or unexplained logins as probable infostealer hosts, isolate them, scan or rebuild them, then rotate credentials from a clean device, revoke active sessions and move MFA to an authenticator app or security key. The hunt has few hard indicators: use the exact folder names applications, cookies, edge, important files and operating systems, the text-file labels system, process, clipboard, brute force and passwords, and the .onion address and account pairs Fowler documented, because no C2 domains, file hashes or netblocks were released. Prioritize primary mail and corporate portals, since control of a mailbox drives password resets across the other services in the set.

The assessment changes if an owner is identified and the repository is shown to be a live criminal marketplace rather than a research or reseller archive, because that implies an active customer base already working from the same logs. It also changes if access logs, victim reports or a service investigation show that outside parties downloaded the data or used the exposed sessions, or if the exposure window and record freshness are published; stale duplicate logs are a different problem from current tokens.
