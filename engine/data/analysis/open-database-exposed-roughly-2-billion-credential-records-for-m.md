Security researcher Jeremiah Fowler found an unprotected database holding roughly 2 billion access credential records, with entries linked to Gmail, Facebook, Instagram, Amazon, ChatGPT and many other online services. The reports describe it as an open database rather than a breach at any single named provider, and the available text does not identify an owner. Multiple write-ups repeat the same count, so the figure should be traced to Fowler's original disclosure before it is treated as independently verified.

The figure counts records, not confirmed unique victims. No service has been tied to a specific exposure window in the material available, and no remediation step such as mass invalidation is described. That leaves the practical risk in credential reuse: any entry that pairs a service with a password or token could feed account-takeover attempts against users who repeat secrets.

Watch for a follow-up that names the database owner, states how long it was reachable and says whether the records are current or historical. Until then, the defensible response is to treat the report as an exposure of credential material at large scale and to look for password reuse rather than assume that every listed service suffered an intrusion.
