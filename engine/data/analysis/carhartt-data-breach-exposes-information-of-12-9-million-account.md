The ShinyHunters extortion group published a 50GB archive containing data from 12.9 million Carhartt accounts on August 27, 2026, after the workwear retailer refused to pay a $3.3 million ransom. The breach was first claimed on August 13. Have I Been Pwned founder Troy Hunt verified the archive's authenticity, confirming it contains customer PII, employee records, royalty metadata, and internal corporate documents. Carhartt, founded in 1889 with over 3,000 US and European employees, has not publicly confirmed the incident.

The dataset includes a broad range of sensitive information: customer personal data, employee files, passport-related documents, and corporate data spanning manufacturing operations in Kentucky and Tennessee. ShinyHunters released the data on their dark web leak site after a company negotiator stated Carhartt had "decided not to move forward with negotiations or further discussions." The group is known for mass-data extortion campaigns against retailers and consumer brands.

The breach ranks lower in severity because no CVE or zero-day is involved, but the sheer volume of affected accounts and the confirmed data release make it notable. Organizations with Carhartt vendor relationships or employee overlap should review for credential stuffing and phishing risks. The incident fits the ongoing ShinyHunters pattern of pressuring mid-market retailers through bulk data theft and public leak-site pressure.
