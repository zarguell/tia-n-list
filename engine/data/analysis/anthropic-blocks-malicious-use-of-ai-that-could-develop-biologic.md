Anthropic disclosed it identified and disrupted five cases of actors using its Claude models in ways that could support biological weapons development, calling this category "one of the most serious risks of frontier AI model." The findings appear in the company's September 2026 threat intelligence report, covering activity from December 2025 through August 2026. Anthropic stated that without appropriate safeguards, such capabilities "could have catastrophic consequences," while noting the dual-use nature of the underlying knowledge: the same information that could enable a biological weapon could also support vaccine or cure development.

The biological weapons cases sit within a broader report documenting misuse across multiple categories. Claude Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus models were implicated in the detected incidents. None involved the newer Fable or Mythos-class models, with one exception: a single distillation attempt where a smaller model was trained using outputs from a larger one. Beyond the biological weapons vector, Anthropic reported six cases where Claude was used to develop software for conventional weapons including firearms, missiles, armed drones, bombs, and their targeting systems.

The report also named specific threat actors. A hacking group whose infrastructure overlaps with the Russia-linked Midnight Blizzard allegedly used Claude to build an automated system that detected when its malware was flagged by security defenses and rewrote code to evade detection. Chinese-based labs, the ShinyHunters cybercriminal group, an Iranian propaganda institution, and various state-sponsored espionage operations were also identified. Anthropic said it incorporated findings into its internal processes and shared intelligence with authorities and industry partners. The disclosure comes amid escalating industry-wide concern: OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki recently called for voluntary development slowdowns, and U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders introduced legislation to ban AI superintelligence.
