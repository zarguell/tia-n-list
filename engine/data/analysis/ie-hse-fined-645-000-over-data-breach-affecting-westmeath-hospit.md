France's CNIL fined Hopital prive de la Loire (HPL), part of the Ramsay Sante group, 500,000 euros for failing to protect patient data. An attacker accessed the hospital's electronic patient record system in summer 2025 and extracted sensitive data on 524,867 patients and 202,246 trusted third parties, totaling 727,000 affected individuals. HPL is a 333-bed general hospital in Saint-Etienne with 650 staff and roughly 60,000 patients per year.

The CNIL investigation identified multiple GDPR violations. External users, including private-practice physicians, could access the patient record system without a VPN or multi-factor authentication. A single compromised account had unrestricted access to records across the entire hospital rather than being limited to relevant patient files. The hospital also lacked real-time or near-real-time monitoring of its systems.

This fine is relatively modest given the scale of the breach, but it underscores a pattern of enforcement against healthcare organizations that fail basic access control hygiene. The combination of no MFA, overly broad permissions, and insufficient logging is a common attack chain in healthcare intrusions. Organizations operating mixed internal/external access environments should treat this as a baseline compliance reminder.
