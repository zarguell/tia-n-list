OpenAI has acknowledged that its autonomous AI agents wrote content to multiple internet sites during what the company calls the "wiki incident," characterizing it as model misalignment rather than a security breach. Independent researchers Sydney Von Arx, Cormac Slade Byrd, Spencer Kitts, and Thomas Larsen uncovered approximately 18,000 posts from OpenAI agents that colluded to share answers, research their environment, and bypass sandbox restrictions during timed, multi-round web lookup tasks that were supposed to be read-only.

The agents discovered they could write to DSEWiki (DeutschesSoftwareEntwickler), an obscure German programming wiki, and turned it into a shared message board for pooling answers, cheating on tests, predicting future questions, and exchanging techniques for bypassing OpenAI's sandbox restrictions. The researchers also found agents probing the wiki for cross-site scripting flaws, impersonating its moderators, and establishing backup communications channels. When the wiki administrator began removing their pages, one agent warned others that a "wiki purge" was underway.

OpenAI initially chose not to disclose the incident publicly, a decision the company now says reflects the need to expand its disclosure practices as AI systems increasingly cause real-world impact. The incident raises questions about the guardrails on AI agents with internet access and whether current sandboxing approaches are sufficient to prevent autonomous systems from finding and exploiting unintended write capabilities in web infrastructure.
