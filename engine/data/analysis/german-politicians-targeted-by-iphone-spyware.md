In late October and early November 2026, Apple's threat notification system flagged members of Germany's Bundestag and the federal government as targets of mercenary spyware, with alerts reaching affected phones from October 29 and the BSI issuing its warning on November 2. The October wave covered 99 countries and Apple described it as distinct from the August wave that reached 110 countries, implying separate campaigns rather than a widened sweep of the same one. Apple attributes nothing and confirms no compromise, so the BSI told recipients to treat their devices as already compromised. No CVE or vendor has been named for this round.

The significance is not the German headlines but the tempo: two global waves in eleven weeks, each spanning roughly a hundred countries, against a backdrop of more than 150 countries notified since 2021. That cadence suggests mercenary spyware operations are running in parallel rather than as isolated incidents, and elected officials are now routinely inside the target set alongside journalists and activists. For a parliamentarian, a successful compromise means message history, contacts, location, and microphone or camera access, with the added risk that a legislator's device bridges into party and government networks. The story matters more than a routine alert cycle because it converts an abstract consumer warning into a documented exposure of a sitting parliament and a federal executive, with the BSI's assume-compromised guidance effectively conceding that detection arrives after the fact.

Recipients should put the device in Lockdown Mode immediately, which Apple says it is not aware of any spyware compromising, and contact the Access Now Digital Security Helpline, available around the clock. Treat the handset as compromised: do not use it for sensitive communications, rotate credentials from a clean device, and verify any alert only through the lock screen, email, or the banner at account.apple.com, since genuine Apple threat notifications never request clicks, passwords, or verification codes. Hunt for the alert itself across those three channels and confirm iOS is current; the BSI's guidance is to assume compromise, so forensic triage by a qualified lab is warranted for anyone who received the October 29 notification. Germany's own BKA reportedly bought access to Pegasus in 2019, a reminder that mercenary tools are already inside European procurement channels and that attribution will be politically fraught.

Watch for the BSI's findings on scope and whether any classified material was accessed, and for any linkage between this wave and a known vendor such as NSO Group or Paragon. Also watch whether other European parliaments received the same October alerts, which would move this from a national incident to a cross-border targeting pattern.
