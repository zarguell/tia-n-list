Crédit Mutuel Arkéa, a French cooperative bank with more than 5.8 million members and 211.5 billion euros in assets, disclosed on November 4, 2026 that attackers initiated fraudulent transactions of up to 133 million dollars through its own accounts to other banking organizations. The bank said customer assets were unaffected and that the incident was closed and under control, with operations gradually returning to normal, though certain transfer transactions faced delays. Reports of the intrusion emerged on November 2.

On-chain investigator ZachXBT first flagged the theft on November 2 as more than 106 million dollars stolen from an unidentified European entity, with outflows beginning on Sunday, November 1. Bitquery traced 108.8 million dollars of the movement and found 61.0 million dollars still on Ethereum. KuCoin reported on November 3 that ZachXBT raised the estimate to more than 133 million dollars after tracing roughly 27 million dollars in additional transactions. The stolen funds were in USDC and USDT, swapped into ETH, routed through the instant exchanges WellWallet and Kyrrex, converted into Monero, and bridged across multiple blockchains. ZachXBT said about 30 million dollars had been laundered by November 3, and stated with high confidence that North Korea's Lazarus Group was not involved.

The story to watch is whether law enforcement or stablecoin issuers can freeze or recover any of the bridged and converted funds, and whether Crédit Mutuel Arkéa faces regulatory scrutiny for the delay between the November 1 outflows and its November 4 disclosure. Attribution remains open beyond the Lazarus exclusion.
