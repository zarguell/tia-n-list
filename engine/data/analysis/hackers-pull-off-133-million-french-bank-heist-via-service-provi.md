French cooperative banking group Credit Mutuel Arkea was hit by a cyberattack that enabled fraudulent transactions of up to 133 million dollars, forcing the bank to suspend certain systems. The attacker used the bank's own account to initiate transactions to other banking organizations, while customer assets were unaffected, according to the bank. Arkea counts more than 5.8 million members and 211.5 billion euros in assets, and disclosed on November 4 that the incident was closed and under control.

The intrusion surfaced publicly on November 2, when blockchain fraud investigator ZachXBT flagged large-scale unauthorized withdrawals from multiple centralized exchange accounts without naming the entity. ZachXBT estimated up to 118 million euros, about 133 million dollars, withdrawn as USDC and USDT stablecoins, and said the lack of public disclosure let the attacker launder 30 million dollars by November 3 that EU law enforcement might otherwise have frozen.

Watch for whether Arkea revises the loss figure, whether any of the USDC and USDT was frozen by stablecoin issuers, and whether the November 3 laundering window draws enforcement action. The case also shows how abusing a bank's own account can move eight figures without any malware.
