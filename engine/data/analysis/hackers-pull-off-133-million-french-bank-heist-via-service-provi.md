Crédit Mutuel Arkéa, a French cooperative banking group with 5.8 million members and 211.5 billion euros in assets, was hit by a cyberattack that enabled fraudulent transactions of up to $133 million. The attacker entered through the platform of an unnamed service provider rather than the bank's own systems, then used the bank's own account to initiate transfers to other banking organizations. Blockchain investigator ZachXBT traced the theft beginning November 1, 2026, watching the attacker convert stolen USDC and USDT into ETH, route funds through instant exchanges WellWallet and Kyrrex, convert ETH into Monero, and bridge assets across multiple blockchains. ZachXBT stated with high confidence that North Korea's Lazarus Group was not involved.

The bank disclosed the incident on November 4, 2026, saying it was closed and under control, that customer assets were unaffected, and that only certain transfer transactions were delayed. It acknowledged the stolen amount matched the order of magnitude of $133 million without confirming the figure, and did not name the service provider or the exploited platform. ZachXBT had warned earlier that the lack of public disclosure let the attacker launder $30 million that EU law enforcement could otherwise have frozen.

What to watch is whether the service provider is identified and held accountable, whether French or EU authorities recover any of the laundered funds, and whether other banks using the same provider face similar exposure. The incident is a clear example of third-party platform risk reaching a major bank through a vendor path.
