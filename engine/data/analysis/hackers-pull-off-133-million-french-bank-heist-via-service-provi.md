Crédit Mutuel Arkéa, a French cooperative banking group with more than 5.8 million members and 211.5 billion euros in assets, said a cyberattack enabled fraudulent transactions of up to $133 million and forced it to suspend certain systems. The attacker used the bank's own account to initiate transactions to other banking organizations. The bank stated that customer assets remain unaffected and that the incident is now closed and under control.

Why it matters: Reports emerged on November 2 from blockchain fraud investigator ZachXBT, who warned of unauthorized withdrawals from multiple centralized exchange accounts and estimated up to 118 million euros moved as USDC and USDT stablecoins, with $30 million laundered by November 3 before EU law enforcement could freeze it. Crédit Mutuel Arkéa disclosed the incident on November 4.

What to watch: whether law enforcement recovers additional stablecoin funds, any attribution of the exchange account compromise, and follow-up disclosures from Arkéa on how the attacker gained access.
