Crédit Mutuel Arkéa, a French cooperative bank with 5.8 million members and 211.5 billion euros in assets, lost up to $133 million (about 118 million euros) in fraudulent crypto withdrawals after attackers entered through a service provider's platform rather than through the bank's own systems. The attacker used the bank's own account to initiate transfers to other banking organizations, with funds leaving as USDC and USDT stablecoins.

Blockchain investigator ZachXBT first flagged the outflows on November 1, initially estimating more than $106 million stolen, then raised the figure to more than $133 million by November 3 after tracing roughly $27 million in additional transactions. The attacker swapped stolen stablecoins into ETH, routed them through the instant exchanges WellWallet and Kyrrex, converted the proceeds to Monero and bridged assets across multiple blockchains. ZachXBT stated with high confidence that North Korea's Lazarus Group was not involved, and warned that the delayed public disclosure let the attacker launder about $30 million that EU law enforcement might otherwise have frozen.

The bank disclosed the incident on November 4, saying it was closed and under control with operations gradually returning to normal. It stated the incident had no impact on customer assets and caused only delays in certain transfers. The bank acknowledged the amounts were of the same order of magnitude as reported without confirming the $133 million figure, and said the service provider's identity and the exploited platform were not disclosed. The entry point was the provider's platform rather than Fortuneo.
