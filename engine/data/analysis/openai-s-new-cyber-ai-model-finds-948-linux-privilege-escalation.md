OpenAI disclosed on November 4, 2026 that an autonomous pipeline powered by an unreleased cybersecurity model found and exploited 948 distinct Linux kernel local privilege escalation flaws in a single mid-October 2026 research run. The pipeline validated exploits on unmodified desktop Linux with KASLR, SMEP and SMAP enabled.

The run cost $1,213,430 in API credits, about $1,279 per exploit. It started from 2,308 candidate findings produced in 12 hours for $316,549, reproduced 1,724 flaws in 30 hours for $73,330, and produced 1,169 working local privilege escalation exploits plus 291 information-leak proofs of concept; OpenAI's figure of 948 reflects the separate patches created. The company said its agents used at least 10 previously undocumented exploitation techniques unknown to outside kernel experts.

Ten days after the scan, 61 local privilege escalation flaws had been patched upstream, 43 had staging patches and 299 were under public discussion. An earlier July scan using GPT-5.6 Sol yielded 166 unique local privilege escalation flaws at $513,690. Watch for upstream patch status of the remaining flaws and for disclosure of the novel techniques.
