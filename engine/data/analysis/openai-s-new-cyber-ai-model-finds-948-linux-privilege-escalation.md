OpenAI says an autonomous pipeline built around an unreleased cyber model generated working local privilege escalation exploits for 948 unique Linux kernel vulnerabilities in a single mid October 2026 run. The pipeline narrowed kernel code to areas reachable by low privileged users, flagged 2,308 candidate bugs in 12 hours, reproduced 1,724 of them in 30 hours, and produced 1,169 working LPE exploits plus 291 information leak proofs of concept, consolidated into 948 patches. Exploits were validated on unmodified desktop Linux VMs with common protections including KASLR, SMEP and SMAP enabled. Total API spend for the run was 1,213,430 dollars, about 1,279 dollars per exploit.

The disclosure includes remediation progress and a comparison point. Ten days after the scan, 61 of the LPEs had been patched upstream, 43 had staging patches, and 299 were in public reporting and discussion. An earlier late July run powered by GPT-5.6 Sol found 166 unique LPEs at a cost of 513,690 dollars, so the new model represents roughly a sixfold yield jump. OpenAI says the agents discovered at least 10 novel Linux exploitation techniques unknown even to outside kernel experts consulted, and full technical details are being withheld for safety.

What to watch is the patch pipeline rather than the headline count. Kernel maintainers now face hundreds of incoming reports from one lab, and the quality of the generated patches will determine how fast they land. Defenders should track upstream stable releases over the coming weeks and expect copycat autonomous pipelines from other labs, since the demonstrated cost per working LPE makes large scale kernel bug hunting economically routine.
