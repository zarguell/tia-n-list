Kaspersky GReAT reports a new BlackLotus-like UEFI bootkit used in an APT41 campaign targeting diplomatic organizations in Central Asia in early 2026. The malware bypasses Secure Boot through the Baton Drop vulnerability (CVE-2022-21894), which Microsoft patched in 2023 but remains relevant for unpatched systems. This is the first known revival of BlackLotus since ESET confirmed the bootkit in 2023.

The sample includes new payloads not seen in earlier BlackLotus advertisements, which were originally priced at $5,000 in 2022. The campaign marks a return of the bootkit to active APT operations after years of silence.

Watch for confirmation of which diplomatic entities were targeted and whether the new payload variants include persistence mechanisms beyond the original Baton Drop exploit. The revival of a previously quiet UEFI bootkit indicates APT41 is investing in low-level persistence for long-term access.
