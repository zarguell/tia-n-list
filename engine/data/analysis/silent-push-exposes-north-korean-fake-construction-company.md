Silent Push uncovered North Korean remote workers running a fake construction and engineering company called The Great House at thegreathouse[.]us, advertised from Lynchburg, Virginia. On the first call the operator kept the camera off; on the second, real-time deepfake software produced layering artifacts and facial distortion before the feed was cut. The domain was registered with devtop0109[@]gmail[.]com, an address on a publicly reported list of 1,400 emails attributed to North Korean IT workers, and it registered no other domain. State phone numbers and the listed address contradict the site's contact details. GBHackers carried the finding and Silent Push assessed that the operation sought revenue for North Korea.

The tradecraft matches fake companies used by Contagious Interview against developers, but the target has shifted. Here no malware is delivered and the operator pursues building contracts directly from U.S. businesses instead of luring job applicants. That matters because it pushes the same deepfake interview technique into procurement and vendor onboarding, where a fake firm can win work without any code being shipped.

Watch for additional domains registered to the same email cluster, more state-linked phone numbers and addresses, and any contract awards tied to The Great House. The deepfake artifacts on the second call are a concrete detection detail for interview teams: camera presence alone is not identity verification.
