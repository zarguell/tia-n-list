Google expanded access to Gemini 4 Carbon, previously limited to select cyber defenders in its Fairwind Program, to paying developers and businesses via the Gemini API and Gemini for business, plus Google AI Ultra subscribers, with introductory pricing of $2/$10 per million input/output tokens until November 30. Separately, Google DeepMind introduced AlphaProof Code, which pairs human experts with AI agents to formally verify software, and applied it to libsignal, the Signal protocol's reference implementation, producing 6.5 million lines of proof in four weeks.

Google says agents built on Carbon helped formally verify core security guarantees in the Signal protocol's code, a notable application of AI to cryptographic assurance. Google's Cloud Vulnerability Research team used the model to confirm that two bugs in a popular open source project were exploitable, with severity comparable to PHP2Shell, and to build a proof of concept that helped maintainers re-classify one bug as critical and issue a patch. Wiz reportedly used Carbon for cyber to find zero-days at two major SaaS providers within two hours. Carbon for cyber remains gated behind Fairwind membership.

Carbon for cyber tops key benchmarks for discovering, exploiting, and fixing vulnerabilities, so broader access raises dual-use questions even though Google gates the cyber variant. Watch for independent replication of the Signal verification claims and for whether other vendors adopt formal verification pipelines. Pricing doubles after November 30, which may limit adoption.
