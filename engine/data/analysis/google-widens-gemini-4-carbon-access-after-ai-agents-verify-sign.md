Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on October 28, 2026, a frontier model aimed at cyber defense, and on November 10 widened access beyond vetted Fairwind defenders to paying developers, businesses, and Google AI Ultra subscribers at introductory rates of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens until November 30. Carbon scored 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro, 54.8% on ExploitGym, and 71.0% on Collinear's CWE-bench, topping GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 in Google's comparison, and Google attributes the fast release to researchers and models working together.

The practical claims are the substance: Google says Wiz used Carbon for cyber to find zero-day vulnerabilities at two major SaaS providers within two hours, and its Cloud Vulnerability Research team used the model to confirm two bugs in a popular open-source project were exploitable, with severity comparable to PHP2Shell, helping maintainers reclassify one bug as critical and ship a patch. Separately, DeepMind introduced AlphaProof Code, which generated over six million lines of Lean proof in four weeks to verify key security properties of libsignal, the Signal protocol's reference implementation, and distinguished correct from buggy code by reintroducing two previously fixed bugs; Signal CTO Ehren Kret called the approach a major breakthrough and DeepMind open-sourced the specification and proofs.

What to watch: Fairwind membership remains required for Carbon for cyber, so the broadly available tier is not the full defensive capability, and pricing doubles after November 30. Independent review of the open-sourced Signal proofs will test whether the verification holds, and disclosures of the two SaaS zero-days will show whether the two-hour claim translates into real-world findings. The benchmark figures come from Google's own comparisons, so third-party reproduction matters.
