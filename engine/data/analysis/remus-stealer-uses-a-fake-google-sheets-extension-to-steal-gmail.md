Remus Stealer's advertised Gmail access is a browser-compromise mechanism, not a demonstrated Gmail zero-day. Invoke RE and Gen Threat Labs found a malicious Chrome extension disguised as Google Sheets that reads mail through the victim's existing Gmail session and sends message contents to an attacker-controlled endpoint. The extension can resume collection when the victim signs back in, explaining claims of access after password changes and session revocation without proving a flaw in Gmail.

Invoke RE's technical account describes a ZIP expanded from the user's temp directory by PowerShell and edits to Chrome's Secure Preferences that register extension ID cfmepkojgnkahbmgablgdahdomjdppgf. The extension checks Gmail account slots 0 through 9 through the unread Atom feed every minute and exfiltrates to prem1um[.]onl/. These are concrete artifacts for endpoint hunts and mail-session investigations.

Remus version 1.3.9 marketed the Mail capability, including verification codes and recovery links, but Hudson Rock and Malwarebytes said controlled testing had not shown fresh mail retrieval after full account recovery. Response requires both account and endpoint work: use a trusted device, review signed-in devices and app access, inspect forwarding, filters, delegation and recovery settings, and remove the extension from the infected Chrome profile.
