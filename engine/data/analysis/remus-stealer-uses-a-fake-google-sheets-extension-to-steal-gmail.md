Hudson Rock's November 24 research update, crediting Invoke RE and Gen Threat Labs, identifies a malicious Chrome extension disguised as Google Sheets inside Remus Stealer operations. The extension uses the victim's existing Gmail session to read messages and send their contents to an attacker-controlled endpoint. Operators began advertising the capability on November 8, 2026, and the malware's developers claimed a Gmail zero-day; the technical analysis instead points to browser compromise through an extension left in the Chrome profile.

That extension explains how Remus could claim continued mailbox access after its original executable stopped running, and it sets a two-part response: revoke the Google account session and clean the endpoint, since changing the account password alone does not remove the browser component. The collected mail includes verification codes and sensitive correspondence, giving operators material for further account takeover. Watch for the indicators published by Invoke RE and for other stealer families copying the same extension-based persistence trick.
