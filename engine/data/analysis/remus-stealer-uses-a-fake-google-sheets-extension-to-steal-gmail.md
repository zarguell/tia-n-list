Remus Stealer's advertised Gmail "zero-day" was a malicious Chrome extension, according to Gen Threat Labs, Invoke RE and GenDigital research published November 24, 2026. Remus version 1.3.9 distributed a fake Google Sheets extension that read Gmail through the browser's existing signed-in session and sent message contents to an attacker-controlled endpoint. The researchers found browser compromise, not a flaw in Gmail that survives full account recovery.

Invoke RE described a ZIP expanded from the user's temporary directory by PowerShell, followed by edits to Chrome's Secure Preferences to register extension ID cfmepkojgnkahbmgablgdahdomjdppgf. The extension checked Gmail account slots 0 through 9 through the unread Atom feed every minute and exfiltrated to prem1um[.]onl/m. Because the add-on remains in the compromised Chrome profile, it can regain mailbox access when the victim signs back in, which explains the seller's persistence claims without requiring a Gmail zero-day.

Response has to cover both the endpoint and the Google account. Malwarebytes recommends a separate trusted device, disconnecting and cleaning the infected machine, reviewing signed-in devices and app access, and checking Gmail forwarding, filters, delegation and recovery settings. Watch for the extension ID and exfiltration domain in browser and proxy telemetry, and treat verification codes or recovery links exposed in collected mail as credentials at risk.
