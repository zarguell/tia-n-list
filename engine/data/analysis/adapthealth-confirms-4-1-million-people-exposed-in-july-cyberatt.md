AdaptHealth has confirmed that 4.1 million people were exposed in a cyberattack discovered in July 2026 and attributed to the ShinyHunters threat group. The company, which provides home medical devices including sleep-apnea equipment, oxygen therapy, and mobility products, first disclosed the incident in an SEC filing on July 2, 2026. The breach occurred on June 5 through social engineering that compromised a third-party contractor's privileged account, granting access to cloud-based patient management systems, document storage, and electronic health record portals.

Exposed data includes full names, contact information, demographic details, health insurance information, and medical records. AdaptHealth received a ransom demand on June 15 from an unnamed threat actor who threatened to leak stolen data. The company has notified the Florida Office of the Attorney General and is providing affected individuals with 12-month credit monitoring. ShinyHunters, the same group behind the recent Florida DMV breach, has been linked to multiple healthcare and government data theft operations in 2026.

The breach highlights the ongoing risk of third-party contractor access to sensitive healthcare systems. Social engineering targeting contractor credentials remains an effective initial access vector for healthcare organizations. The combination of personal identifiers and protected health information makes this data highly valuable on underground markets. Healthcare providers should review third-party access controls and implement zero-trust verification for contractor connections to clinical systems.
