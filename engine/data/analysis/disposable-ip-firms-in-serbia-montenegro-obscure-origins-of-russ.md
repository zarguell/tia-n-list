A BIRN investigation has mapped how Russian-owned bulletproof hosting providers use disposable front companies in Serbia and Montenegro to launder IP address ranges and obscure the origins of cyber operations targeting Europe. The investigation centers on Maxim Azarov, who registered eServer in the central Serbian town of Krusevac in April 2022, months after Russia's invasion of Ukraine. eServer links to Aeza Group, sanctioned by the US in July 2025 for supporting ransomware, malware distribution, and black-market drug sales, and to Stark Industries Solutions, sanctioned by the EU in May 2025 for providing infrastructure to Russian and Belarusian state-aligned cyber actors.

The scheme works by rotating IP address blocks between shell companies across multiple jurisdictions. After the EU sanctioned Stark Industries Solutions, it rebranded as PQ Hosting and transferred its IP resources under Dutch WorkTitans, whose owners were arrested by Dutch police in May 2026. Archived routing data show Azarov served as an upstream provider for the rebranded entity. BIRN identified at least eight IP ranges that WorkTitans listed as Serbian, with former eServer ranges now advertised by providers linked to Aeza Group, FIN7, GRU operations, and the Doppelganger disinformation campaign. Among current users of former eServer IP ranges are Russian providers Rost and Timeweb, where researchers found over 300 active C2 servers, and US-based Baxet Group, linked to GRU cyberattacks by Arctic Wolf Networks.

Another Russian national, Alexei Fedorov, runs kvmka.ru from the same Krusevac apartment as eServer. In Russia, Fedorov directs Coloded and Alex Group, both identified by Qurium as Doppelganger infrastructure providers. His Serbian-registered IP ranges include ones previously held by Aeza Group, Dutch MIRhosting, and Media Land, which was sanctioned by the US, UK, Australia, EU, and Canada. A third participant, Aleksandr Shmalko, registered Castles in Novi Sad as a DDoS protection provider. His Serbian company advertises an IP range belonging to IPX-FZCO, linked by Qurium to Russian cyberattacks and the Qilin ransomware group that hacked Serbia's state power utility EPS in December 2023. The pattern shows how bulletproof hosting operators establish small, quiet organizations that can be abandoned when convenient, making attacks appear to originate from within the EU.
