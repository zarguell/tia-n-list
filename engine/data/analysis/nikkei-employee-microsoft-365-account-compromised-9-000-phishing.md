Nikkei disclosed that an employee's Microsoft 365 account was compromised in a cyberattack and used to send roughly 9,000 spoofed emails on September 30. Recipients included contacts inside and outside the company, among them news sources who had previously communicated with multiple Nikkei employees. The company says leaked information likely includes recipients' names, email addresses, and the content of some emails.

The report went to Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission, and Nikkei is still investigating the scope and the number of affected records. The account password was changed and no further unauthorized logins have been detected; the company says it contacted recipients individually and asked them to delete the messages.

The real exposure is source confidentiality. Phishing sent from a journalist's legitimate mailbox arrives with full context and trust, and recipients who reply or click may expose both themselves and the reporting relationships in the mailbox history.
