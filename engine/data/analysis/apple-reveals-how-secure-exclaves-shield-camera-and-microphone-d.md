Apple has published a detailed account of Secure Exclaves, silicon-backed isolated runtimes that process camera and microphone data outside the main operating system. The design covers ambient sensing such as listening for Hey Siri and face and voice recognition on the HomeView display. Apple states that data processed inside an exclave cannot be read or altered by the main OS, apps, or Apple. In background sensing only metadata reaches the rest of the system; on HomeView the recognition result is a person identifier, position and confidence level, without audio or video.

When an app requests full sensor access, a dedicated exclave acts as gatekeeper. Apple sets out three requirements: the main OS cannot extract or change exclave data, only a narrow set of information crosses the boundary, and users can tell when a sensor session is active.

The architecture matters to iOS exploitation because Exclaves now sit in the chain alongside KASLR, Pointer Authentication, kalloc_type with zone_require assertions, PPL and its SPTM and TXM successors, and trust-cache code signing. A public reversing article argues these stacked layers push attackers toward data-only techniques. This is a design disclosure, not an incident or patch.
