The Florida Department of Education confirms a breach of its student financial aid website that may have exposed data for approximately 4.9 million people. The intrusion was discovered on October 19. Attackers potentially accessed names, Social Security numbers, dates of birth, home addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, and gender codes. The department has contained the breach and is working with the Florida Digital Service and state law enforcement. No threat group has claimed the attack, and the intrusion path has not been disclosed.

The dataset is a complete identity profile for a large student-aid applicant population, making it a high-value target for identity fraud, tax fraud, and targeted phishing. Because the data sets include Social Security numbers paired with verified addresses and phone numbers, affected individuals face long-term exposure rather than a simple password-reset cycle. The absence of a disclosed vulnerability or intrusion path limits immediate defensive action for other organizations.

Affected individuals should freeze credit reports and monitor for fraudulent use of personal information. Organizations in the education and government sectors should review their own financial-aid application platforms for similar exposure and confirm that breach detection covers web applications that process high-volume PII. Watch for updates from FLDOE disclosing the intrusion mechanism.
