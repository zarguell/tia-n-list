An updated joint advisory from CISA, FBI, and HHS details Medusa ransomware's expansion to hundreds of new victims in the past year, with significant changes to the group's access acquisition model. Medusa now pays access brokers between $100 and $1 million, with premium pricing for brokers who work exclusively with the group. In practice, most brokers serve multiple ransomware operations simultaneously, creating cross-contamination risk for victims.

The advisory, which updates a March 2025 version, draws on ongoing FBI investigations and details Medusa's evolving tactics, techniques, and procedures. The RaaS operation has matured its affiliate model and is targeting healthcare and critical infrastructure alongside its traditional enterprise victims.

The broker economy detail is notable: Medusa's willingness to pay up to $1 million for exclusive access indicates significant revenue per victim. Organizations should assume that initial access brokers are shopping credentials and vulnerabilities to multiple groups simultaneously. Detection should focus on broker-sold access patterns (stolen VPN credentials, compromised RDP, exploited edge appliances) rather than waiting for the ransomware payload.
