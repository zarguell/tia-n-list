Ryohin Keikaku, the operator of MUJI, disclosed a data breach in stages. Its first report said email addresses, birth dates and gender of up to 27 million app users may have leaked. A second report adds online store data, and a separate disclosure covers 1.09 million MUJI Card members.

The story matters because two distinct customer bases were exposed with personal data including birth dates, and payment card numbers are part of the original app-user disclosure. Disclosure figures were revised upward across reports rather than stated fully at once.

Watch for whether the MUJI Card member figure is revised again, whether payment card data is confirmed exposed rather than potentially exposed, and any action by Japanese regulators or card brands.
