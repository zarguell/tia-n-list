A flaw in eMudhra’s Key Generation Ceremony allowed compromise of eight intermediate CA private keys: six TLS intermediates (DV, OV, EV) and two code-signing intermediates. The event details note the Go Project assigned CVE-2026-111943 and released Go 1.26.10 and 1.27.3 with a denylist in crypto/x509 that rejects chains containing the known untrusted intermediate keys. Apple blocked the certificates on October 19 and Microsoft on October 20; CERT-In also issued an official statement. The Register reported one private key was literally the number “4”, underscoring how broken the ceremony was.

The exposure is not endpoint malware but trust inheritance: any attacker holding an intermediate CA private key can mint certificates that clients treat as valid under the affected root. With eight intermediates in play the attacker can issue domain-validated, organization-validated, extended-validation, and code-signing certificates at will, making this a supply-chain trust failure rather than a single-site breach. The first_seen of 2026-10-21 and rapid browser and runtime response show the industry treats this as an active infrastructure-level event, not a hypothetical vulnerability.

Defenders should apply Go 1.26.10 or 1.27.3 immediately for the denylist, verify browser updates that block the intermediates, and hunt TLS certificate chains and certificate stores for unexpected intermediates tied to eMudhra. Monitor for unauthorized certificates signed under the affected chain rather than conventional endpoint IoCs, because exploitation here is silent certificate issuance rather than payload delivery.

Watch for additional vendor blocks beyond Apple and Microsoft, and for any disclosure of forged certificates observed in the wild, which would confirm active abuse of the compromised keys.
