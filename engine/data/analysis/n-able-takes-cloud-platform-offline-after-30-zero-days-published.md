N-able took its hosted N-central environments offline on November 9, 2026, and urged on-premises customers to shut down their servers after security researcher Sean Bales publicly disclosed more than 30 alleged zero-day vulnerabilities under the name Able2Shell, publishing proof-of-concept exploits on GitHub. N-central is a remote monitoring and management platform used by managed service providers and IT teams to administer endpoints and infrastructure, so compromise of an RMM server puts downstream managed environments at risk. Bales tested against N-central Appliance 2026.4.0.31, a pre-release build, and believes most or all flaws also affect earlier versions; he confirmed to BleepingComputer that the discovery was AI-assisted. The disclosure covers 33 issues, including authentication bypasses, credential disclosure, arbitrary file writes, directory traversal, cross-site scripting, XML external entity injection, and remote code execution. One critical chain abuses an unauthenticated XXE flaw in N-central's service-import functionality to steal a trusted local session, reset an account password, and execute commands on the server as the nable user, and can also create an enabled Default Administrator account.

N-able opened Incident 202561 on November 9 at 15:15 UTC, rating it a Major Outage, called the disclosure non-responsible, and said it had no evidence that the vulnerabilities were exploited against its environments. As of November 10 the company reported scanning activity associated with the published proof-of-concept code but still no evidence of successful exploitation. It said engineering was making progress on an N-central hotfix and validation, on track with no setbacks, expected around midday on November 10, and advised keeping on-premises deployments offline until patches release. Hosted N-central classic environments remain offline. Rapid7 said it was triaging the newly published details. No CVE identifiers or CVSS scores have been assigned to the Able2Shell flaws yet.

This disclosure lands on top of N-central 2026.4 HF1, shipped November 2 with build 2026.4.0.32 and fixes for CVE-2026-98432, a critical Linux kernel flaw rated CVSS 9.8, plus CVE-2026-111497 and CVE-2026-114406. N-central does not use the Brotli decoder affected by CVE-2026-111497 and is not vulnerable to it.

What to watch: whether the hotfix ships and validates on schedule, since MSPs face an extended outage window; whether CVEs and CVSS scores are assigned, which will clarify severity; whether the observed scanning converts into actual exploitation of internet-exposed N-central instances; and the blast radius across MSP downstream customers if any server was compromised before takedown. The flaw count and exploitability claims remain the researcher's allegations until N-able confirms them.
