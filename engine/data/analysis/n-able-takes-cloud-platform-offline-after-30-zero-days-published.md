N-able shut down its hosted N-central environments and told on-premises customers to take their servers offline after security researcher Sean Bales published proof-of-concept exploits for more than 30 alleged zero-day vulnerabilities in the RMM platform. The flaws, tested against N-central Appliance 2026.4.0.31 and believed to affect earlier versions, span authentication bypasses, credential disclosure, arbitrary file writes, directory traversal, cross-site scripting, XXE injection, and remote code execution. One critical chain abuses an unauthenticated XXE flaw in N-central's service-import functionality to steal a trusted local session, reset an account password, and execute commands on the server as the nable user, and can also create an enabled Default Administrator account.

The disclosure matters because N-central is an RMM platform used by managed service providers and IT teams to manage customer devices and networks, so a compromise cascades to many downstream clients; the public PoC repository, named Able2Shell, lowers the barrier to exploitation. Bales confirmed the discovery was AI-assisted, and N-able is building a hotfix while hosted environments remain offline. A separate N-central 2026.4 HF1 release (build 2026.4.0.32) shipped November 2 with fixes for CVE-2026-98432 and two other CVEs, but that update does not address the newly disclosed flaws.

Watch for N-able's hotfix and any confirmed in-the-wild exploitation against MSP environments; the XXE-to-RCE chain against internet-exposed N-central consoles is the highest-risk path. Defenders running on-premises N-central should keep servers offline until patched, audit for unauthorized Default Administrator accounts and unexpected command execution as the nable user, and review session and credential logs for the November 8-9 window.
