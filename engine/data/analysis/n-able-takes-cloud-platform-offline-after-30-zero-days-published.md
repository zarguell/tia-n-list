N-able shut down its hosted N-central environments and told on-premises customers to take their servers offline after security researcher Sean Bales published proof-of-concept exploits for more than 30 alleged zero-day vulnerabilities in the RMM platform. The flaws, tested against N-central Appliance 2026.4.0.31 and believed to affect earlier versions, span authentication bypasses, credential disclosure, arbitrary file writes, directory traversal, cross-site scripting, XXE injection, and remote code execution. One critical chain abuses an unauthenticated XXE flaw in N-central's service-import functionality to steal a trusted local session, reset an account password, and execute commands on the server as the nable user, and can also create an enabled Default Administrator account. Bales confirmed the discovery was AI-assisted, and N-able opened Incident 202561 on November 9, 2026 at 15:15 UTC, rating it a Major Outage with status Investigating, and described the disclosure as non-responsible.

The disclosure matters because N-central is an RMM platform used by managed service providers and IT teams to manage customer devices and networks, so a compromise cascades to many downstream clients. The public PoC repository, named Able2Shell, lowers the barrier to exploitation. A separate N-central 2026.4 HF1 release (build 2026.4.0.32) shipped November 2 with fixes for CVE-2026-98432 and two other CVEs, but that update does not address the newly disclosed flaws. N-able is building a hotfix while hosted environments remain offline.

Watch for N-able's hotfix and any confirmed in-the-wild exploitation against MSP environments; the XXE-to-RCE chain against internet-exposed N-central consoles is the highest-risk path. Defenders running on-premises N-central should keep servers offline until patched, audit for unauthorized Default Administrator accounts and unexpected command execution as the nable user, and review session and credential logs for the November 8-9 window.
