N-able took its hosted N-central environments offline on November 9, 2026, and urged on-premises customers to shut down their servers after security researcher Sean Bales publicly disclosed more than 30 alleged zero-day vulnerabilities under the name Able2Shell, publishing proof-of-concept exploits on GitHub. N-central is a remote monitoring and management platform used by managed service providers and IT teams to administer endpoints and infrastructure, so compromise of an RMM server puts downstream managed environments at risk. The researcher tested against N-central Appliance 2026.4.0.31, a pre-release build, and believes most or all flaws also affect earlier versions; the discovery was AI-assisted. The disclosed flaws include authentication bypasses, credential disclosure, arbitrary file writes, directory traversal, cross-site scripting, XML external entity injection, and remote code execution. One critical chain abuses an unauthenticated XXE flaw in N-central's service-import functionality to steal a trusted local session, reset an account password, and execute commands on the server as the nable user, and can also create an enabled Default Administrator account.

N-able opened Incident 202561 on November 9 at 15:15 UTC, rating it a Major Outage, called the disclosure non-responsible, and said it had no evidence the vulnerabilities were exploited against its environments, though scanning activity tied to the published proof-of-concept code was observed. The company expects a hotfix around midday on November 10 and advises keeping deployments offline until patches release. No CVE identifiers or CVSS scores have been assigned yet. This disclosure lands on top of N-central 2026.4 HF1, shipped November 2 with fixes for CVE-2026-98432, a critical Linux kernel flaw rated CVSS 9.8, plus CVE-2026-111497 and CVE-2026-114406, though N-central is not vulnerable to the Brotli decoder issue in CVE-2026-111497. Rapid7 is triaging the newly published details.

What to watch: whether the hotfix ships and validates on schedule, since MSPs face an extended outage window; whether CVEs and CVSS scores are assigned, which will clarify severity; whether scanning activity converts into actual exploitation of internet-exposed N-central instances; and the blast radius across MSP downstream customers if any server was compromised before takedown. The disclosure is unverified in full, so the flaw count and exploitability claims should be treated as alleged until N-able confirms.
