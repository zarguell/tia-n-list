N-able took its hosted N-central environments offline and urged on-premises customers to shut down their servers after security researcher Sean Bales publicly disclosed more than 30 alleged zero-day vulnerabilities in the remote monitoring and management platform on November 8, 2026, publishing proof-of-concept exploits in a GitHub repository called Able2Shell. The flaws were tested against N-central Appliance 2026.4.0.31, a pre-release build, and include authentication bypasses, credential disclosure, arbitrary file writes, directory traversal, cross-site scripting, XML external entity injection, and remote code execution. The most critical chain abuses an unauthenticated XXE flaw in N-central's service-import functionality to steal a trusted local session, reset an account password, and execute commands on the server as the nable user.

N-able opened Incident 202561 on November 9, 2026 at 15:15 UTC, rating it a Major Outage with status Investigating, and called the disclosure non-responsible. Bales confirmed to BleepingComputer that the discovery was AI-assisted. N-central is used by managed service providers and IT teams to monitor and manage customer devices and networks, so a flaw in the platform carries supply-chain implications for downstream customers. Separately, N-able shipped N-central 2026.4 HF1 (build 2026.4.0.32) with fixes for CVE-2026-98432, CVE-2026-111497, and CVE-2026-114406, and noted that N-central is not affected by the Brotli decoder issue in CVE-2026-111497.

What to watch: whether N-able validates and patches the 30-plus disclosed flaws, the timeline for restoring hosted N-central service, upgrade guidance for on-premises customers, and whether any of the published exploit chains are used against MSP environments before fixes ship.
