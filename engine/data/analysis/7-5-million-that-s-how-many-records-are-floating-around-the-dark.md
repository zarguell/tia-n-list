A threat actor using the alias "4d722e4d656f77" told BleepingComputer they exfiltrated 7.49 million CenterPoint Energy customer records by iterating IDs through a public API that lacked rate limiting, WAF protection, or automated-access defenses. The attacker released a 2.5 GB archive on September 12 containing roughly 3 million phone numbers, and the company confirmed to the Texas Attorney General that 1,062,060 state residents were affected, with names, addresses, Social Security numbers, and driver license numbers exposed. CenterPoint serves about 7 million metered customers across Indiana, Minnesota, Ohio, and Texas, employs roughly 8,300 people, and reported over $9.3 billion in annual revenue, so the 7.49 million claim exceeds the Texas-only count and leaves multi-state exposure unresolved.

This is not another routine disclosure: the gap between the forum post claiming 7.5 million records and the official Texas filing of 1,062,060 suggests the full scope is still being reconciled, and the attacker explicitly threatened to move from data theft to infrastructure attacks. For defenders at utilities and critical-infrastructure operators, the mechanism matters less than the exposure scale; partial Social Security numbers paired with billing amounts and service addresses are sufficient for identity fraud and targeted social engineering against a customer base that spans four states.

Patch and restrict immediately: audit all public-facing APIs for missing rate limits and WAF rules, enforce authentication on endpoints that expose customer IDs, and restrict download of bulk archives. Hunt for the IoC patterns mentioned in the events: automated ID iteration against the external-facing API, absence of rate-limiting logs, and the 2.5 GB archive hash or download source from September 12. Monitor for notification letters to Indiana, Minnesota, and Ohio residents, since only Texas has confirmed impact so far.

Watch whether multi-state notifications expand beyond the 1,062,060 figure and whether the SEC filing is updated with a confirmed total. If CenterPoint releases the exact compromised data types for all states, or if the actor fulfills the threat to target operational infrastructure rather than just data, reassess from identity-theft risk to operational-disruption risk.
