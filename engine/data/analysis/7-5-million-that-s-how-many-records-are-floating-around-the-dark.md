CenterPoint Energy, a major Texas electric and gas utility serving approximately 7 million metered homes and businesses, has confirmed a data breach affecting 7.5 million records. The stolen data appeared on a dark web hacker forum before CenterPoint publicly acknowledged the incident. The company said it was "verifying the incident" after the data surfaced.

The compromised records reportedly include personal information of customers and possibly employees. CenterPoint is one of the largest investor-owned utilities in the US, operating in Texas, Indiana, Ohio, Minnesota, Mississippi, and Louisiana. The breach raises questions about data minimization practices at critical infrastructure providers, since utilities collect far more personal data than needed to deliver power.

The disclosure timeline is notable: CenterPoint did not proactively notify customers or regulators before the data appeared on underground forums. This pattern of delayed disclosure after dark web exposure has become common among large organizations, but it carries particular weight for a utility provider that holds sensitive customer data and operates critical energy infrastructure. Customers have no realistic option to switch providers, making the data stewardship asymmetry especially stark.
