CVE-2026-113380 (CVSS 9.3) affects Signal RingRTC releases before 2.72.0. A malicious call peer can saturate the internal FSM event queue and block the local hangup teardown, which leaves audio (and possibly video) active after the victim believes the call ended. The vulnerability concerns only call shutdown logic in RingRTC, not the Signal encryption layer, and no in-the-wild exploitation has been reported. The fix is present in RingRTC 2.72.0; developers integrating the library must upgrade rather than rely on application-level settings.

The exposure is narrow but severe: any Signal user on an affected version who accepts a call from an adversarial peer risks post-hangup audio leakage, which includes background conversations and unintended remarks. Because RingRTC powers Signal's mobile and desktop calling, the affected population spans the full user base, yet the attack requires an active malicious participant rather than unauthenticated remote access; that distinction is why this flaw demands attention despite lower visibility than network-facing RCEs. It is a privacy failure with direct physical-world impact, not just a cryptographic or authentication gap.

Defenders should upgrade to RingRTC 2.72.0 immediately and verify Signal clients pull the updated library. Hunt for anomalous call teardown behavior in application logs and monitor for reports of audio continuing after hangup; no specific IoC names appear in the disclosure, but event-queue saturation on the peer side is the mechanism. Restrict untrusted peer connections where organizational policy allows, and treat future RingRTC release notes as priority reading until the fix is widely deployed.

Watch for confirmation of video persistence (the disclosure notes it as unconfirmed) and for any telemetry showing active exploitation. A vendor advisory with exact timestamp or additional technical detail would sharpen the timeline; absence of exploitation reports so far is reassuring but not conclusive given the privacy-sensitive failure mode.
