On October 29, 2026, attackers stole approximately $6.34 billion from Binance by moving from compromised engineering workstations to the systems that sign wallet transactions. The FBI attributed the intrusion to North Korea's TraderTraitor unit. BitGet lost $388 million through a vulnerability in a third-party security product. Since September, at least three cryptocurrency companies have been hit with the same pattern: after initial access, intruders used legitimate credentials and permissions to move laterally toward production systems. Only the initial access vector changed. The bug is not a novel exploit but credential theft from developer environments, followed by abuse of valid access.

The scale of loss and the low recovery rate make this story distinct. As of November 3, only $2.85 billion of the $6.34 billion had been frozen or intercepted, leaving roughly $3.49 billion still unaccounted for. Forty-four percent of compromised machines held more than ten secrets, which means developer environments are not just an entry point but a treasure chest of standing access. This matters more than the other thirty stories today because the playbook repeats across victims with the same actor and the same method, and defenders who only patch CVEs will miss an intrusion that walks in with valid credentials.

Binance cleared the EIP-7702 delegation from the Binance14 hot wallet at 00:10 UTC on October 30. Defenders should revoke unexpected EIP-7702 authorizations, separate signing infrastructure by chain and wallet tier, and monitor treasury wallets for new delegations that should not exist. Hunt for credential access in developer environments, inventory which machines hold more than ten secrets, and establish tested, pre-approved revocation paths before an attacker needs them.

Watch whether the attacker moves the remaining funds through cross-chain bridges and OTC desks, and whether more exchanges report similar credential-based intrusions from the same cluster since September. A fourth victim with the same lateral movement pattern would confirm this is an ongoing campaign rather than a discrete incident.
