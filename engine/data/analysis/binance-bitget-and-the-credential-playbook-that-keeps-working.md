On October 29, 2026, Binance disclosed an attack affecting approximately $6.34 billion in crypto assets, of which $2.85 billion had been frozen or intercepted as of November 3. The FBI attributed the intrusion to the Democratic People's Republic of Korea and identified the activity as TraderTraitor. According to Binance's initial investigation findings, attackers moved from compromised engineering workstations to the systems that sign wallet transactions.

The reporting places Binance in a cluster of at least three cryptocurrency companies attacked since September, sharing one pattern: after initial access, intruders used legitimate credentials and permissions to move laterally toward production systems, and only the initial access vector changed. BitGet lost $388 million after attackers exploited a vulnerability in a third-party security product; a third company was also hit.

Developer environments are the recurring weak point: 44 percent of compromised machines held more than ten secrets. The reporting says blast radius is limited by knowing where credentials live, what they access, and how quickly they can be revoked. A Swedish-language follow-up on redaktionen.net repeats the same Binance theft figures.
