BTCPay Server, the self-hosted Bitcoin and Lightning payment processor, is warning that attackers are draining hot wallets on instances running version 2.4.1 (released July 23, 2026) or earlier. The threat actor logs in as an administrator, and the project has confirmed the active vector relies on flaws present in releases older than 2.4.2. Version 2.4.7, published October 29, 2026 as an emergency update after a critical finding from Anthropic's Frontier OSS scanner, fixes 85 security findings and is the release the project now requires; the project reports no credible hot-wallet drains on 2.4.7. The earlier August 7, 2026 advisory documented a separate but related pre-authentication flaw in all versions before 2.4.2 that exposed LND macaroon credentials, with confirmed exploitation and stolen funds.

Because BTCPay Server holds live funds in server-side hot wallets and verifies invoices locally, administrator access converts directly into spendable balances rather than mere data exposure. Operators running 2.4.1 or older are the exposed population, including multi-merchant shared servers that the 2.4.7 notes call out as highest priority. This incident is worth separating from the routine CVE churn: it is an actively exploited, money-moving compromise of self-hosted payment infrastructure, and it traces to a codebase whose maintainers have now twice in three months shipped emergency fixes for authentication or access-control failures in pre-2.4.2 code.

Administrators should upgrade to 2.4.7 or later immediately and take any instance that ever ran 2.4.1 or earlier offline until patched. Review administrative logins and outbound wallet movements since late October, rotate LND macaroons and any credentials reachable from the host, and check LND nodes for unexpected payments, channel closures, or unfamiliar peers. Keep excess funds in cold storage and treat a patched instance as potentially compromised if it was exposed unpatched.

Watch for the project's post-incident report naming the specific flawed component and whether the admin-access vector is a variant of the pre-2.4.2 issue, plus any confirmation of broader impact beyond LND-using deployments or evidence of the same actor pivoting to other self-hosted wallet software.
