BTCPay Server, the self-hosted Bitcoin and Lightning payment processor, warned that attackers have been draining hot wallets on instances running version 2.4.1 (July 23, 2026) or earlier, logging in as administrators and stealing BTC and LTC. Operators were initially told to shut down servers pending investigation. The maintainer identified the attack vector against older versions and urged an immediate upgrade to version 2.4.7, which shipped as an emergency security update after a critical vulnerability surfaced through Anthropic's Frontier OSS scanner on October 29. Founder Nicolas Dorier said the attacks did not target public servers but focused on instances older than 2.4.2, and clues indicate the threat actor used vulnerabilities present in those earlier releases.

The incident shows how a self-hosted payment stack becomes a direct theft target when an authentication or access flaw lets an attacker act as an administrator: because hot wallets hold live funds and invoices are verified server-side, admin access translates into direct wallet drainage rather than data exfiltration. The project's response combined a version bump with guidance to audit instances and rotate credentials, though the specific flawed component was not disclosed.

Administrators running BTCPay Server should upgrade to 2.4.7 or later, review administrative logins and wallet movements since late October, and treat any instance that ran 2.4.1 or earlier as potentially compromised even after patching. Watch for a post-incident report naming the underlying flaw and whether the same vector affects other self-hosted payment or wallet software.
