Belarusian Cyber Partisans, through a backdoor called Vasily, maintained unauthorized access inside a Russian healthcare organization from early 2024 through December 2025, roughly a two-year intrusion. Solar's reporting ties the access to the Vasily backdoor and notes Telegram-based command and control. The group used 35 indicators of compromise and triggered 9 detections during the campaign.

This matters because healthcare infrastructure in Russia is a strategic target, and a year-plus dwell time shows the attackers had sustained, low-noise access rather than a quick smash. The Cyber Partisans are politically motivated, which changes the threat profile from pure cybercrime to activist-driven espionage and potential data exposure. Organizations tracking Russian-targeting actors should review the published IOCs and compare against health-sector telemetry.

Watch for follow-on Solar updates that clarify what data was accessed, whether patient records were taken, and if the intrusion connects to other Russian healthcare targets. The Telegram C2 channel and the 35 IOCs are concrete hunting leads.
