Federal agencies warned that hackers are targeting refrigeration and cold-chain systems worldwide. CISA, the FBI, and the Departments of Health and Human Services, Agriculture and Transportation issued joint guidance saying attackers are going after electronic temperature controls at grocery stores and cold storage facilities. The warning follows Nextgov/FCW reporting that U.S. officials suspect Iran-linked hackers in freezer malfunctions at military bases, with incidents beginning around late August and roughly six commissaries affected; Reuters reported the Pentagon treated the freezer events as possible cyberattacks. In France, CERT-FR tied a cyberattack to resets of refrigeration systems in about 500 stores, with defrost cycles driven to 50 degrees Celsius for one to eight hours, creating physical damage and fire risk.

The targeting matters because the consequences extend past IT into physical safety: spoiled food, damaged equipment, and possible fires. Forescout's analysis frames exploitation of internet-exposed refrigeration systems as a global risk landscape and tracks CVEs CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451, and CVE-2025-41452, which indicates the issue spans multiple products and exposures rather than one vendor.

Operators of grocery and cold-storage infrastructure should apply the CISA joint guidance on hardening industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems and verify that temperature-control devices are not exposed to the internet. Further incidents are likely as more agencies and countries attribute the activity, and the France CERT-FR case shows the campaign is already causing physical consequences.
