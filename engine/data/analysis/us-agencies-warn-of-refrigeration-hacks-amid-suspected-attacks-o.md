U.S. agencies issued joint guidance warning that hackers are targeting electronic temperature controls in industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems at grocery stores and cold-storage facilities worldwide. CISA, the FBI, and the Departments of Health and Human Services, Agriculture, and Transportation joined the alert after recent failures spoiled food and created fire hazards. The warning follows Nextgov/FCW reporting that U.S. officials suspect Iran-linked hackers in freezer malfunctions at military bases beginning around late August, affecting roughly six commissaries, with Reuters reporting the Pentagon treated the incidents as possible cyberattacks.

The same week, separate reporting described cyberattacks against several French blood banks on November 2, 2026, and Russian officials denied Kremlin involvement in cyberattacks against supermarket refrigerators. The physical consequences make this unlike a typical data-theft story: temperature-control manipulation can spoil stored goods, endanger blood supplies, and create fire hazards. Operators should watch for follow-on guidance from CISA, any confirmed attribution to Iranian or other actors, and further reports of cold-chain disruption in Europe.
