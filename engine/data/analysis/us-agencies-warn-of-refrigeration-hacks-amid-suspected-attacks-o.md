On November 5, 2026, CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA and the Department of Transportation issued a joint fact sheet warning that attackers are targeting electronic temperature controllers and commercial refrigeration systems in retail grocery, cold storage, healthcare, chemical and transportation facilities. The advisory names no products, victims or attackers, but says actors exploit unpatched or internet-reachable controllers, including authentication bypass and arbitrary code injection weaknesses, to alter temperature settings and force equipment into continuous heating or defrost cycles. CISA Acting Executive Assistant Director Chris Butera said every organization that depends on refrigeration and monitoring systems should assume it could be a target.

The warning follows weeks of incidents. Hundreds of European supermarkets were hit on October 11 when attackers pushed fridge temperatures to 50 degrees Celsius and freezers to defrost, spoiling food and in some cases causing fires. A second wave this week hit more than 100 supermarkets and a French blood bank, though staff prevented compromise of blood products. Separately, U.S. officials suspect Iran-linked hackers in freezer malfunctions at about six military commissaries since late August, and French and Dutch small retailers using older Danfoss controllers with default or unsecured connections were compromised. Russian hacktivist groups claimed responsibility, but nothing has been confirmed.

Operators should harden controllers, remove internet exposure, change default credentials and patch. The case shows how low-value IoT equipment in food and medical cold chains can be used for disruption and physical damage, and attribution remains unconfirmed.
