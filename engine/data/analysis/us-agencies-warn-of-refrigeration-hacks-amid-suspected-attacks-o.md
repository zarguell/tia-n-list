CISA, the FBI, and the Departments of Health and Human Services, Agriculture, and Transportation issued joint guidance warning that attackers are targeting electronic temperature controls at grocery stores and cold storage facilities worldwide. The agencies say malicious actors exploit authentication bypass flaws, arbitrary code injection, and weak configurations in unpatched or internet-accessible refrigeration controllers to modify temperature settings and force equipment into continuous heating or cooling, risking food spoilage, equipment damage, and fire hazards. The affected controllers are tracked under CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451, and CVE-2025-41452. Operators across healthcare, food production, chemical, and transportation sectors are urged to harden these systems.

The advisory follows reports that U.S. officials suspect Iran-linked hackers were behind freezer malfunctions at roughly six military base commissaries beginning around late August, incidents the Pentagon treated as possible cyberattacks. A separate November 2 cyberattack hit several French blood banks. Chris Butera, CISA's Acting Executive Assistant Director for Cybersecurity, said every organization that depends on refrigeration and monitoring systems should assume it could be a target.

The concrete exposure is unpatched or internet-facing commercial refrigeration management systems whose remote temperature monitoring and defrost controls can be altered by attackers. The guidance is a hardening advisory rather than a single patched CVE, so defenders should inventory internet-accessible controllers, restrict remote access, and apply vendor updates where available.
