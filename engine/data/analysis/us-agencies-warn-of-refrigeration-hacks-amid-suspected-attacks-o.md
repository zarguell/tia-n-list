US agencies have issued a joint warning that hackers are actively targeting industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems worldwide. CISA, the FBI, and the Departments of Health and Human Services, Agriculture, and Transportation published guidance on Thursday urging operators to secure electronic temperature controllers and commercial refrigeration management systems used in grocery retail, cold storage, healthcare, chemical facilities, and transportation. The agencies said attackers exploit authentication bypass flaws, arbitrary code injection bugs, and weak configurations to change temperature settings and force equipment into continuous heating or defrost cycles, risking food spoilage, equipment damage, and fire hazards. CISA Acting Executive Assistant Director for Cybersecurity Chris Butera said every organization relying on refrigeration and monitoring systems should assume it could be a target.

The suspected espionage angle adds urgency. US officials suspect Iran-linked hackers in freezer malfunctions at military bases across the country, with incidents starting around late August and roughly six commissaries affected; the Pentagon has been treating the events as possible cyberattacks. A second wave hit European supermarkets on November 2, with stores in Belgium, Ireland, Luxembourg, and Sweden affected, including an ICA store in Sjoebo, Sweden where fridges and freezers were set to 50 degrees Celsius. Days earlier, hacks across eight European countries forced grocery stores to discard spoiled food, and a French cyberattack reset refrigeration systems in about 500 stores, with defrost cycles running at 50 degrees Celsius for one to eight hours, according to CERT-FR.

Operators should treat cold-chain controllers as exposed OT assets: restrict internet access, patch authentication and injection flaws, and monitor temperature setpoints for unauthorized changes. What to watch next is whether US attribution of the military-base incidents is confirmed, and whether attackers move from defrost manipulation to wider physical damage. CISA and its partners are continuing to track internet-exposed refrigeration controllers, so further advisories and IOCs are likely as the campaign develops.
