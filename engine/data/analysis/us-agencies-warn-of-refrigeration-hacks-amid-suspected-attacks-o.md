CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA and the Department of Transportation published a joint fact sheet on 5 November 2026 warning that attackers are targeting internet-accessible and unpatched electronic temperature controllers and commercial refrigeration controllers. The agencies say intruders can change setpoints and force continuous heating or defrost cycles, causing spoiled food, equipment damage and fire risk in grocery, cold storage, healthcare, chemical and transport settings. The warning followed reports of freezer failures at six or more US military commissaries starting in late August, which Nextgov/FCW said officials suspect were the work of Iran-linked hackers.

The campaign already has physical impacts in Europe. About 700 French supermarkets were hit on 11 October, with refrigeration units set to 50 degrees Celsius and freezers put into defrost; a second wave on 2 November reached more than 100 stores across 13 countries, including an ICA store in Sjoebo, Sweden, with fridges and freezers at 50 degrees. France's blood service EFS said cold rooms at three sites had temperature thresholds altered to 110 degrees before staff intervened. Danfoss told Dutch customers on 13 October to disconnect controllers from the public internet, and Reuters found at least 4,500 Danfoss interfaces exposed; the affected units were older models whose security software could not be updated, with unsecured connections and default passwords.

CISA has now added several Danfoss AK-SM 800A flaws to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog: CVE-2025-41450 improper authentication, CVE-2023-25915 improper input validation and CVE-2023-25914 path traversal. Those entries tie the refrigeration campaign to named defects rather than only weak configuration. Russian hacktivist groups Noname057(16) and Z-Pentest Alliance claimed responsibility for hacking Danfoss, but no attribution has been confirmed and Iranian actors remain another possibility. Watch for federal patch deadlines, updated exposure counts, and vendor notices for controllers that cannot be patched.
