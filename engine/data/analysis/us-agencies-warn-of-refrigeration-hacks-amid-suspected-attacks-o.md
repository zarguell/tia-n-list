US federal agencies issued a joint warning that hackers are targeting industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems worldwide. CISA, the FBI, and the Departments of Health and Human Services, Agriculture and Transportation published the guidance after recent failures spoiled food and created fire hazards. The cluster tracks CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451 and CVE-2025-41452. Attackers exploit authentication bypass, arbitrary code injection or weak configurations to change temperature settings and force refrigeration equipment into continuous heating or defrost cycles.

The suspected espionage angle adds urgency. US officials suspect Iran-linked hackers in freezer malfunctions at military bases dating to late August, with roughly six commissaries affected. A second wave on November 2 hit supermarkets in Belgium, Ireland, Luxembourg and Sweden, including an ICA store in Sjoebo where fridges and freezers were set to 50 degrees Celsius, and hacks across eight European countries forced stores to discard spoiled food. In France, attackers hit about 500 stores, running defrost cycles at 50 degrees for one to eight hours per CERT-FR. On November 6, the BSI confirmed German supermarkets in a second wave of more than 100 European stores, and a French blood bank attack altered temperature thresholds on EFS plasma refrigeration units before emergency alarms intervened.

Operators should treat cold-chain controllers as exposed OT assets: restrict internet access, patch authentication and injection flaws, and monitor temperature setpoints for unauthorized changes. Watch for confirmed US attribution of the military-base incidents, further BSI guidance on the German supermarket wave, and whether CISA moves any of the three CVEs into its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog.
