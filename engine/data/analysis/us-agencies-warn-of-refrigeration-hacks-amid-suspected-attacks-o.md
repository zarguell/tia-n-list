CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA and the Department of Transportation published a joint fact sheet on 5 November 2026 warning that attackers target internet-accessible refrigeration controllers and electronic temperature controls in grocery, cold storage, healthcare, chemical and transport sites. The agencies say intruders alter setpoints and force continuous heating or defrost cycles, with outcomes that include spoiled food, equipment damage and fire risk. Chris Butera is quoted saying every organization that depends on refrigeration should assume it could be a target.

Physical damage is documented. About 700 French supermarkets were hit on 11 October, with refrigeration units pushed to 50 degrees Celsius and defrost runs lasting one to eight hours. A second wave on 2 November reached more than 100 stores across Belgium, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Sweden, Estonia, Ireland and Portugal. France's blood service EFS said cold rooms at three sites had thresholds set to 110 degrees before staff intervened; SOCRadar records three UK meat suppliers reached through Copeland XWEB controllers. Reuters found at least 4,500 exposed Danfoss interfaces, and Danfoss said older affected models could not be patched and were reachable with default passwords.

The campaign now has named defects. CISA added CVE-2025-41450 improper authentication, CVE-2023-25915 improper input validation and CVE-2023-25914 path traversal in Danfoss AK-SM 800A controllers to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 25 November. Sekoia counted more than 2,700 exposed Danfoss systems on 11 October, about 1,700 in France, and logged unauthenticated read_units and xml.cgi requests from 26 unique IPs, password spraying with 12345, and an attempt on 1 November to rename user ID 1 from 178.83.226[.]215 and 37.16.75[.]128.

US involvement predates the European wave. Officials suspect Iran-linked actors in freezer failures at roughly six military commissaries starting in late August, and the Pentagon treated them as possible cyberattacks. French authorities attributed the campaign on 9 November to the IRGC unit Shahid Kaveh, known as CyberAveng3rs, while Noname057(16) and Z-Pentest Alliance also claimed it. Watch for controller exposure counts, patch deadlines on the KEV entries and whether operators remove internet-facing management interfaces.
