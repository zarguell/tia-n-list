Five US agencies published a joint TLP:CLEAR fact sheet on November 5 titled "US Government Partners Urge Hardening Industrial Refrigeration and Cold Chain Monitoring Systems," warning that attackers are hitting refrigeration controllers and electronic temperature controllers in retail grocery and cold storage worldwide. CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA and Transportation say actors exploit unpatched or internet-exposed controllers, using authentication bypass, code injection or weak configurations to alter setpoints and force units into continuous heating and defrost cycles. CISA Acting Executive Assistant Director Chris Butera said every organization depending on refrigeration should assume it could be a target. The sheet names no products, victims or attackers and carries no advisory number; the cluster tracks CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451 and CVE-2025-41452.

The incident record behind the warning keeps growing. US officials suspect Iran-linked actors in freezer malfunctions at military bases dating to late August, with about six commissaries affected. In France, attackers hit roughly 500 stores and ran defrost cycles at 50 degrees for one to eight hours per CERT-FR, and a separate attack on French EFS blood bank plasma refrigeration units altered temperature thresholds before emergency alarms intervened. A second wave starting November 2 struck supermarkets in Belgium, Ireland, Luxembourg and Sweden, including an ICA store in Sjoebo with units set to 50 C, and on November 6 the BSI confirmed German supermarkets were hit in a wave covering more than 100 European stores, with spoiled food discarded across eight countries.

Treat cold-chain controllers as exposed OT: pull them off the internet, patch authentication and injection flaws, and alert on unauthorized setpoint changes. Watch for US attribution on the military-base incidents, further BSI detail on the German wave, and whether CISA adds any of the three tracked CVEs to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog.
