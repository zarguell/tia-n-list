CISA, the FBI and the Departments of Health and Human Services, Agriculture and Transportation issued joint guidance warning that attackers are targeting electronic temperature controllers and commercial refrigeration management systems at grocery stores and cold storage facilities, with the potential to spoil food, damage equipment and create fire hazards. The advisory points to unpatched or internet-exposed controllers and says actors may use authentication bypass or arbitrary code injection to change temperature settings and force units into continuous heating or defrost. The November 5, 2026 fact sheet references weaknesses such as CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2025-41451 and CVE-2025-41452 among the authentication bypass and code injection flaws being targeted.

The warning follows a suspected Iran-linked campaign that caused freezer malfunctions at about six commissaries on U.S. military bases beginning around late August, which Reuters and Nextgov/FCW reported the Pentagon treated as possible cyberattacks. Blood storage sites in France were also hit.

Operators in healthcare, food, agriculture, chemical and transportation sectors should harden controllers, restrict internet exposure and patch promptly. The fact sheet names no specific products or attackers, so asset inventories and monitoring for anomalous temperature changes are the immediate defensive steps.
