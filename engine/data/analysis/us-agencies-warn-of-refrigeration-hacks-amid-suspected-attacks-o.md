US federal agencies warned on November 5, 2026 that attackers are targeting industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems worldwide, urging operators in healthcare, food, chemical, and transportation sectors to harden exposed controllers. The joint advisory from CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA, and DOT followed weeks of incidents: US officials suspect Iran-linked hackers caused freezer malfunctions at about six military commissaries starting in late August, while European supermarkets saw two waves of attacks that reset fridges to 50 degrees Celsius, spoiling food and causing fires. France's national blood service (EFS) reported three affected cold-storage sites, though staff prevented compromise of blood products.

Attribution remains unresolved. Russian hacktivist groups Noname057(16) and Z-Pentest Alliance claimed responsibility for Danfoss-related attacks, but nothing is confirmed and Iranian actors are also suspected. The affected systems were largely older Danfoss controllers still reachable over unsecured connections and using default passwords; Reuters found at least 4,500 Danfoss interfaces exposed. Censys continues to monitor internet-exposed refrigeration controllers, and Forescout published an analysis of the global risk landscape.

What to watch: operators should patch or disconnect controllers, eliminate default credentials, and monitor for anomalous temperature changes. The advisory names no products, victims, or confirmed attackers, so the true scope of exposure may be larger than reported. Any organization relying on refrigeration should assume it could be targeted.
