U.S. federal agencies issued joint guidance warning that hackers are targeting refrigeration systems and electronic temperature controls at grocery stores and cold storage facilities worldwide. CISA, the FBI, and the departments of Health and Human Services, Agriculture, and Transportation released the advisory after food spoilage and fire hazards were linked to recent equipment failures. The guidance urges operators to harden industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems.

The suspected activity traces to late August, when U.S. officials began suspecting Iran-linked hackers of freezer malfunctions at military bases across the country. Roughly six commissaries, the on-base grocery stores serving military personnel, were affected, and the Pentagon treated the incidents as possible cyberattacks. Defense One picked up the Nextgov/FCW reporting on November 2, three days before the federal warning. A second wave hit supermarket refrigerators across Europe the same day, affecting stores in Belgium, Ireland, Luxembourg, and Sweden; an ICA store in Sjoebo, Sweden had its fridges and freezers raised to 50 degrees Celsius, and grocery stores across eight European countries were forced to discard spoiled food.

Censys ARC is monitoring Internet-exposed refrigeration controllers and published a SITREP on European exposure following the attacks. The Kremlin denied involvement in the supermarket refrigerator cyberattacks. Operators should watch for further targeting of cold-chain and temperature-control equipment, with food safety and fire risk as the likely impact.
