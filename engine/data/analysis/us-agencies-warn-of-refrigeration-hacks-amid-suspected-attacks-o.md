On November 5, 2026, CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA, and the Department of Transportation issued joint guidance warning that threat actors are actively targeting industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems worldwide. The advisory says attackers exploit vulnerabilities and weaknesses in unpatched or internet-accessible electronic temperature controllers and commercial refrigeration controllers, using authentication bypass, arbitrary code injection, and weak configurations to alter temperature settings and force equipment into continuous heating or defrost. The result can be food spoilage, equipment damage, and fire hazards across healthcare, food and agriculture, chemical, and transportation sectors.

The warning caps weeks of incidents. On October 11, attackers turned fridge temperatures to 50 degrees Celsius and set freezers to defrost at hundreds of supermarkets across Europe, causing millions in losses and some fires. A second wave beginning November 2 hit more than 100 stores in Belgium, Ireland, Luxembourg, Sweden, and Germany, plus at least 17 small businesses in the Netherlands that still used unsecured connections and default passwords. France's blood service (EFS) saw three cold-storage sites affected, though staff intervened before blood products were compromised. Separately, about six U.S. military commissaries have experienced freezer malfunctions since late August, with U.S. officials suspecting Iran-linked hackers; Reuters reported the Pentagon treating them as possible cyberattacks. Russian hacktivist groups claimed involvement via Danfoss, but nothing is confirmed, and Iranian actors remain possible. The Kremlin denied Russian involvement.

Operators should assume they are targets: remove controllers from the public internet, change default passwords, patch, and monitor for anomalous temperature changes. Danfoss advised customers to disconnect and reconnect only through secure networks, and Censys is tracking exposed controllers. Watch for copycat attacks on cold-chain systems, especially healthcare and blood storage, and for any confirmed attribution or new vulnerability details.
