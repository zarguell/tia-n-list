CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA and the Department of Transportation issued a joint fact sheet on 5 November 2026 warning that attackers target internet-accessible and unpatched electronic temperature controllers and commercial refrigeration controllers. The agencies say intruders can alter setpoints and drive continuous heating or defrost cycles, with consequences that include spoiled food, equipment damage and fire risk across grocery, cold storage, healthcare, chemical and transport sites. The warning followed suspected Iran-linked freezer failures at roughly six US military commissaries that began in late August, and Reuters reporting that the Pentagon treated them as possible cyberattacks.

The physical damage is already documented in Europe. About 700 French supermarkets were hit on 11 October, with refrigeration units pushed to 50 degrees Celsius and freezers put into defrost. A second wave on 2 November reached more than 100 stores across Belgium, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Sweden, Estonia, Ireland and Portugal, including an ICA store in Sjoebo, Sweden. France's blood service EFS said cold rooms at three sites had temperature thresholds set to 110 degrees before staff intervened; SOCRadar also records three UK meat suppliers reached through Copeland XWEB controllers. Reuters found at least 4,500 exposed Danfoss interfaces, and Danfoss said affected older models could not receive security updates and were reachable through unsecured connections with default passwords.

The catalog now ties part of this activity to named defects. CISA added CVE-2025-41450 improper authentication, CVE-2023-25915 improper input validation and CVE-2023-25914 path traversal in Danfoss AK-SM 800A controllers to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, with CVE-2023-25914 listed on 25 November. Sekoia logged unauthenticated read_units and xml.cgi requests from 26 unique IPs, password spraying with 12345, and a 1 November attempt to rename user ID 1 from 178.83.226[.]215 and 37.16.75[.]128. French authorities attributed the campaign on 9 November to the IRGC unit Shahid Kaveh, known behind the CyberAveng3rs persona, while Russian groups Noname057(16) and Z-Pentest Alliance also claimed it. Watch for patch deadlines and exposure counts on controllers that cannot be updated.
