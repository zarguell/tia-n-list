The Hacker News reported on September 24 that newly discovered flaws in OnePlus devices let an already-installed malicious app silently take complete control of the phone, with the reporting indicating the flaws are unpatched at publication. The capability described requires no user interaction beyond an app being present on the device, which is what makes silent full control the operative risk rather than simple privilege abuse.

OnePlus ships a heavily customized Android build, so fixes of this type typically arrive through the vendor's OxygenOS update channel rather than stock Android patching, and no CVE identifiers were attached to the reporting at the time of collection. Until a vendor advisory names affected models and patch levels, there is no reliable way for users or enterprises to check exposure, and app-level defenses cannot be assumed to hold against a flaw that ends in complete device control.

Watch for three things: a CVE assignment and technical writeup that clarifies the affected component, a OnePlus security advisory with a fix timeline and affected device list, and any evidence of the flaws being exploited in the wild. Enterprises that allow OnePlus devices should treat unmanaged sideloaded app installation as the primary risk amplifier while the vendor response is pending.
