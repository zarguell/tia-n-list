An unpatched authentication bypass vulnerability (CVE-2026-75501) in Calix GS7 XGS (GS5239XG) residential routers lets remote, unauthenticated attackers create port-forwarding rules via the exposed MiniUPnPd control endpoint on TCP port 5000 of the WAN interface. This effectively bypasses NAT, exposing any device on the customer's local network to the public internet. The affected firmware is EXOS/6.6.47, and no patch is available from the vendor.

The scope is significant because Calix supplies gateway hardware to major US broadband providers including Cox Communications, Brightspeed, ALLO, CityFibre, and Conexon. The GS5239XG, also marketed as the GigaSpire 7u10txg, is a premium Wi-Fi 7 gateway with integrated XGS-PON fiber terminal, meaning the exposed devices are likely high-traffic residential and small-business connections.

Researcher Brian Khan Quintana discovered the flaw and attempted vendor notification on June 7 with no response. After further failed outreach, he reported to CERT/CC at Carnegie Mellon, which coordinated public disclosure. The lack of vendor engagement and the absence of a patch five months after initial discovery is notable. Operators using Calix GS7 XGS gateways should restrict WAN-side access to TCP port 5000 if possible, and monitor for EXOS firmware updates addressing this issue.
