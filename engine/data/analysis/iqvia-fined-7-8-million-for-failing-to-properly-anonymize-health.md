Italy's Data Protection Authority fined IQVIA 7 million euros, about $7.8 million, over health-data processing that the regulator says risked exposing and de-anonymizing roughly one million patients. IQVIA is a multinational healthcare analytics and clinical research company that claims to handle 68 petabytes of data and 1.2 billion patient records across more than 100 countries. The Italian division was found to have built a database of health information on about one million patients by aggregating records from 800 general practitioners.

The core failure is a cautionary tale on pseudonymization. IQVIA replaced patient names with a unique per-patient code and treated the result as anonymized. The authority found the code made it possible to track individuals over time, and combined with a detailed attribute set (year of birth, sex, diagnoses, symptoms, prescriptions, tests, vaccinations, plus location data) it allowed patients to be singled out and reidentified using reasonable means. The investigation started in April 2025 and the announcement also cites additional unlawful processing beyond the anonymization failure.

Watch for two things. First, whether other EU regulators reuse this reasoning against health-data brokers that equate pseudonymization with anonymization; the ruling sets a practical bar for what "anonymous" means under GDPR. Second, whether IQVIA changes how its aggregated GP datasets are built and sold in Europe, and whether the fine draws appeals or further enforcement against similar secondary-use health databases.
