Icelandic hosting provider 1984 ehf shut down every service on November 20 after what it described as a large and serious cyberattack on its IT infrastructure. A joint notice from the company and CERT-IS said the scope was still under investigation and that initial findings pointed to a well-organised, technically advanced attack. Managing director Mörður Áslaugarson told RÚV the attack was backed by well-funded actors and moved at a speed he had not seen before. The company hosts a large share of Icelandic websites, so the decision to stop all services was a containment step to preserve the investigation and avoid restoring systems before their security could be verified.

Email and DNS were expected back first under special monitoring, with other services to return gradually and no date given for full restoration. A follow-up report says customer data is safe but restoration has been delayed. Investigators also found that the attackers first damaged seven websites whose domain names indicated support for Palestine, and the National Commissioner of the Icelandic Police is now investigating, including whether the attack was highly coordinated. Those details make this more than a routine outage, but no actor has been named and no technical intrusion path has been published.

What to watch is the forensic finding and the restoration sequence. Customers of a hosting provider cannot inspect the underlying compromise themselves, so the useful signals will be the CERT-IS update, the police assessment, and whether any restored sites show signs of persistent access. The deliberate targeting of a small set of customer sites also raises the question of why the rest of the platform was hit, which matters for other hosting operators facing similar politically motivated destruction.
