Icelandic hosting company 1984 ehf shut every service on 20 November after what its managers and CERT-IS described as a large, well-organised and technically advanced attack on its IT infrastructure. RÚV quoted managing director Mörður Áslaugarson saying the attack was extremely serious, backed by well-funded actors and moving faster than he had seen before. The company hosts a large share of Icelandic websites, so the shutdown took DNS, email and customer sites offline together. The stop was presented as a safety step to limit damage and preserve the investigation, not as a completed recovery.

Initial reporting put a political target on the attack. Vísir and Daily Northern reported that the intruders first damaged seven websites whose domains indicated support for Palestine, with Áslaugarson telling Vísir those sites were hit before the wider intrusion became clear. Recovery was staged: email and DNS were expected back first under special monitoring, and other services would return only after data and customer security were confirmed. No date was given for full restoration, and the company later sought government help, according to Daily Northern.

By 23 November the National Commissioner of the Icelandic Police had opened an investigation, with RÚV saying it was too early to identify who was behind the attack. Attribution remains absent from the public record, as does a technical account of how the attackers got in. Watch for findings from CERT-IS or the police, confirmation of the initial access path, and whether restored services show any further tampering. The seven damaged pro-Palestinian websites remain the clearest concrete target detail reported so far.
