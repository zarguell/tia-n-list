1984 ehf, the Icelandic host trading as 1984.hosting, shut down every service on 20 November 2026 after what it and CERT-IS described as a large, well-planned and technically sophisticated attack on its IT infrastructure. The joint notice said scope was still under investigation and did not rule out that more systems were affected. Chief executive Mörður Áslaugarson told RÚV that front-end services were the target and that the pace was unlike anything he had heard of; Vísir reported that sites whose domain names indicated support for Palestine were damaged first. That outlet also reported smaller intrusion attempts beginning on 14 November and peaking on 19 November, with defenders partly pushing the attackers out before the decision to go dark. RÚV had published at 10:58 UTC and 11:34 UTC before the story was first seen at 12:42 UTC.

This is an availability event with a control-plane tail, and that is why it outranks a routine defacement. RÚV put the customer count in the tens of thousands and called 1984 probably the largest Icelandic web and email host, while Vísir says about 65 percent of customers are abroad. Email and DNS were prioritised for return under special monitoring while web hosting, VPS and the control panel stayed down, so every hosted domain inherited a partial failure even when its own server was intact. The free FreeDNS service uses the same nameservers, which reaches domains hosted elsewhere. The Palestine-linked first damage points to selection rather than indiscriminate ransomware, but no actor, ransom demand, CVE, entry vector or data-theft confirmation has been published, so an extortion or leak claim would materially change the case.

No patch version or CVE exists to chase because no product or flaw has been named. Customers should read the 1984 status page and CERT-IS notices rather than treat restoration as proof of integrity, rotate passwords and API tokens reused elsewhere, and check MX and NS records for their own domains against the published service list. The available hunt is narrow: look for unauthorised DNS, mail-forwarding and site-file changes, and review logs for probing of public-facing services in the 14 to 19 November window. VSX reported only two of five nameservers and one of three mail servers answering at 13:00 UTC on 21 November, with webmail, the control panel and the main site still down. No IOC or log path has been released, so any feed offering one for this incident should be treated as unverified.

Two developments would change the assessment: CERT-IS or 1984 publishing how the attackers got in and whether customer websites, mailboxes or VPS disks were read, and a confirmed actor or extortion claim. The Wagyu operator's allegation that a key on a deleted 1984 server enabled a $13.5 million XMR1 theft on 20 November remains unconfirmed, and Bitquery says onchain data alone cannot show a stolen key. Clean restoration of DNS, email and the control panel without a data-breach finding would lower the outage risk but would not answer what the intruders reached in the days before the shutdown.
