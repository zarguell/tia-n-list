Icelandic hosting provider 1984 ehf shut down all services on November 20, 2026 after what it and CERT-IS described in a joint notice as a large, well-organised and technically advanced attack on its IT infrastructure. Managing director Mörður Áslaugarson told RÚV the attack was extremely serious, backed by well-funded actors and carried out at a speed he had not seen before. The company hosts a large proportion of Icelandic websites, and it framed the full shutdown as a safety step to limit impact, preserve the investigation and avoid restoring systems before their security could be verified.

Email and DNS were the first services expected back, under special monitoring, with no date given for full restoration. A later update from Visir quoted the CEO saying the attackers first damaged websites whose domain names indicated support for Palestine. Daily Northern reported that the hosting firm sought government help after the cyberattack, and a separate report said hackers destroyed seven pro-Palestinian websites in the attack on 1984. Those reports add a destructive and targeted element to an incident that initially looked like a broad infrastructure compromise.

The case matters because a single hosting provider carries a large share of Iceland's web presence, so a containment shutdown has national reach. The reported selection of targets by domain name also suggests an ideological motive, which changes the risk calculus for customers whose sites carry political signals. Watch for CERT-IS findings on the initial access path, confirmation of how many sites were destroyed, the outcome of government assistance, and whether the staged return of email and DNS uncovers further malicious access.
