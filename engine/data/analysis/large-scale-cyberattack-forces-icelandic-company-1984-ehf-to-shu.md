Icelandic hosting company 1984 ehf shut down its systems on Friday, 20 November 2026 after what it described as a large-scale cyberattack. RUV reported that the company saw no other option than to turn everything off. A follow-up report says all services were stopped, with email and DNS given priority for restoration. The company's own account of the incident, quoted in that coverage, treats keeping those two services available as the immediate goal for customers.

The available reports do not name an actor, claim ransomware, give an entry vector, or state how much data was affected. Nor do they put a date on full service restoration. For a hosting provider, the decision to take everything offline is itself the main fact: it suggests the operator could not isolate the affected systems quickly and chose a broad outage over continued exposure. Email and DNS are the first services back because they carry customer identity and routing functions.

Watch for a forensic statement from 1984 ehf on the attack vector and scope, any customer data notification, and whether other Icelandic providers see related activity. Until then the incident is a service-availability event with unknown data impact, not a confirmed exfiltration case.
