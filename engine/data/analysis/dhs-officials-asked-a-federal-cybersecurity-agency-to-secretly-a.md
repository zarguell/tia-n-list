Political appointees at the Department of Homeland Security asked the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency to secretly acquire a voting machine and attempt to hack it, according to ProPublica and Defense One reporting. The request, made early in the Trump administration's second term, alarmed career staff because it involved covertly obtaining election equipment without the manufacturer's knowledge.

The episode is less a technical vulnerability than a governance and election-security concern: CISA's election-security mission depends on trust with state officials and vendors, and a covert acquisition plan could undermine that cooperation. Watch items include whether DHS formalizes any voting-machine testing program, how state election officials respond, and whether the request leads to oversight or policy changes at CISA.
