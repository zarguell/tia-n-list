Palo Alto's advisory wave began with 13 fixes across PAN-OS, GlobalProtect and Prisma Cloud products. The flaw that became an incident was CVE-2026-0322, a CVSS 8.6 code injection in the PAN-OS management interface. An attacker with credentials and network access could run code as root on PA-Series and VM-Series firewalls and Panorama virtual or M-Series appliances. Palo Alto said very limited exploitation had been observed and published IP indicators. CISA added the flaw to KEV on 14 October 2026 with a federal remediation deadline of 17 October. Cloud NGFW and Prisma Access were not affected.

Patched builds in the October advisory included PAN-OS 12.2.4, 12.1.4-h12, 12.1.7-h8, 12.1.10-h2, 11.2.4-h22, 11.2.7-h21, 11.2.10-h15 and 11.2.13-h3, with 11.2.16 expected on 15 October. On 11 November Palo Alto fixed CVE-2026-0330, another authenticated root-level RCE, in PAN-OS on Panorama. The vendor rated that issue medium severity, but root execution on the management plane still matters. Restricting management access to trusted internal IPs was the stated mitigation for CVE-2026-0322, and no workaround was available.

The 25 November report added an unauthenticated route to the same exposed interface. The chain combined PHP2Shell, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, which give code execution as the PHP web worker, with Fragnesia, CVE-2026-46300, a Linux kernel XFRM ESP-in-TCP local privilege escalation to root. CISA had warned about that chain on 28 October, and Dream reported targeting in the United States, Japan, Taiwan and South Korea, including a US government affiliate. Palo Alto confirmed that every Fragnesia exploitation it saw on PAN-OS involved a management interface exposed to the public internet. Watch for new fixed builds, scans for exposed interfaces and evidence that the KEV deadlines are driving patching.
