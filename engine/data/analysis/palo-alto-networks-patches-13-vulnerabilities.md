Palo Alto Networks patched 13 vulnerabilities in a batch first reported July 10 with no critical rating and no disclosed exploitation at the time, and the October 14 Canadian Centre advisory AV26-1041 re-surfaced the set with the product scope spelled out. The named item is CVE-2026-0322, an authenticated remote code execution flaw in the PAN-OS management interface tracked as PAN-SA-2026-0013, alongside the October 2026 Chromium monthly update folded into Prisma Browser. Affected trains span PAN-OS 12.2, 12.1, 11.2, 11.1 and 10.2, plus Prisma Browser before 152.8.5.83.

The word authenticated in CVE-2026-0322 reads as a mitigator but functions as a thin one. Management interfaces on perimeter firewalls are reached with stolen or phished credentials routinely, and a firewall that falls gives the attacker the choke point itself: inspection policy, VPN termination, and logging all sit on the same box that is now untrustworthy. The advisory's vague "multiple versions" wording for the 11.1 and 10.2 trains is the other tell, since it marks shops running old code with no clean fixed build called out by number. Among the day's patch batches this one matters because edge appliances draw targeted exploitation within weeks of disclosure, and this batch has now been public since July.

Operators should move to the fixed builds the advisory names, including 12.2.4, the 12.1 hotfix lines 12.1.4-h12, 12.1.7-h8 and 12.1.10-h2, and the 11.2 lines 11.2.4-h22, 11.2.7-h21, 11.2.10-h15 and 11.2.13-h3, and push Prisma Browser past 152.8.5.83. Limit management-interface reachability to jump hosts and trusted sources, enforce multifactor authentication on all administrative accounts, and audit recent administrative access for sessions that do not match known operators. The two developments that change this assessment are exploitation reporting or a severity revision against CVE-2026-0322, and confirmation that any of the Chromium flaws in the October update were exploited in the wild before the patch.
