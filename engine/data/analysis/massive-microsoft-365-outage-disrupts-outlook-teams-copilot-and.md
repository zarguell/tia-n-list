Microsoft 365 suffered a global outage beginning the afternoon of November 12, 2026 that disrupted Outlook, Teams, Copilot, and Defender XDR, with Microsoft 365 drawing roughly 50,000 Downdetector reports. Microsoft first scoped the problem to Exchange Online mailbox access in the Americas, then expanded it to Microsoft 365 service connectivity for users in any region. The company said the elevated load was not driven by a traffic increase but by a number of servers becoming unexpectedly unavailable and straining the remaining infrastructure, traced to failures of automated service health checks originating from a component responsible for processing server health. Microsoft found no recent change correlated with the start of impact, was testing a configuration change, and was working an alternate health-check method to return affected servers to service under incidents EX1550543 and MO1550821. The outage passed 12 hours and disrupted AI services and businesses worldwide; Microsoft continued restoration work into November 13.

The key detail is that a routine service-health component failed in a way that cascaded into broad productivity loss across mail, chat, AI, and security tooling, with no correlated deployment to roll back. That makes mitigation dependent on changing health-check behavior and returning servers to service rather than reverting a release, which lengthens recovery. The exposure of Defender XDR alongside mail and chat also means security monitoring and response capabilities were impaired during the incident.

Administrators should track Microsoft's status updates under EX1550543 and MO1550821, verify service restoration in their tenants, and review whether any security alerts or XDR telemetry were missed during the window. Watch for Microsoft's post-incident root-cause report and for any recurrence of the health-check failure mode.
