A global Microsoft 365 outage on November 12-13, 2026 started as Exchange Online mailbox access failures in the Americas and widened into a worldwide degradation of Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, Copilot, Defender XDR, Purview, and Intune. Microsoft attributes the incident to several servers becoming unexpectedly unavailable, which overloaded the remaining infrastructure and an overwhelmed secure-connection process. The company says it has no indication the outage was caused by malicious activity, though user reports exceeded 130,000 and service was still being restored region by region roughly 20 hours after the first incident opened.

The blast radius is large because Microsoft 365 has more than 450 million paid commercial subscriptions, and the failure hit hospitals, airlines, courts, and government agencies that depend on Exchange Online for email and Teams for operations. The practical risk during a long outage is not only lost productivity but shadow IT: staff working around the failure with personal email and unmanaged tools, which raises data-exposure and compliance concerns for security teams.

Microsoft reported early stabilization and incremental recovery, but problems persisted across regions. IT teams should track tenant status, defer risky workarounds, and review what data moved outside managed channels during the downtime. The incident is a reminder that a single cloud control plane can take down multiple security products at once, including Defender XDR, so offline detection and incident-response playbooks need to function without Microsoft 365 connectivity.
