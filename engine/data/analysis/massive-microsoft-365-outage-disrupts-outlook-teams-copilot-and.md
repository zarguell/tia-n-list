A global Microsoft 365 outage that began on November 12, 2026 as an Exchange Online mailbox access problem in the Americas widened into a worldwide degradation affecting Outlook, Teams, Copilot, Defender XDR, SharePoint Online, Intune, Purview, and administrative tools, running past 12 hours and up to roughly 20 hours. Microsoft attributed the incident to servers becoming unexpectedly unavailable and straining the remaining infrastructure: a core process on some servers became overwhelmed, causing servers to fail automated health checks and go offline. The company said it had not identified any recent change correlated with the start of the impact and that the failed health checks may originate from a specific component responsible for processing server health; it was testing a configuration change and an alternate health-check method. Microsoft said it had no indication the outage was caused by malicious activity. The outage drew nearly 50,000 Downdetector reports and affected government, hospital, and airline operations, with nearly 450 million paid commercial subscriptions on the platform.

The incident is a service-side infrastructure failure rather than a security event, but it carries security implications: shadow IT and workaround behavior during prolonged email outages can expose company data, and prolonged unavailability of Defender XDR and Purview reduces visibility. No root cause was published during the reporting window.

Watch for Microsoft's post-incident root cause analysis and whether the health-check component failure is fully remediated. Organizations should review fallback communications and offline access plans, and monitor for credential or data exposure from staff workarounds during the outage.
