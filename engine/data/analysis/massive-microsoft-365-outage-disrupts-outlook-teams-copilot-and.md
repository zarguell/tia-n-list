A global Microsoft 365 outage that began on November 12, 2026 as an Exchange Online mailbox access problem in the Americas widened into a worldwide degradation affecting Outlook, Teams, Copilot, Defender XDR, SharePoint Online, Intune, Purview, and administrative tools. Microsoft attributed the incident to servers becoming unexpectedly unavailable and straining the remaining infrastructure: a core process on some servers became overwhelmed, causing servers to fail automated health checks and go offline. The company said it had not identified any recent change correlated with the start of the impact and that the failed health checks may originate from a specific component responsible for processing server health. Microsoft said it had no indication the outage was caused by malicious activity. The incident drew nearly 50,000 Downdetector reports and affected government, hospital, and airline operations, with nearly 450 million paid commercial subscriptions on the platform.

Recovery did not hold. The outage ran past 20 hours and then past 27, tracked by Microsoft under incident IDs EX1550543 and MO1550821, and the company reported partial recovery across some regions that did not become sustained recovery. Microsoft said servers returned by an alternate health-check method could become unresponsive again once they began receiving traffic, and that machines stuck in a bad state were blocking a network change from syncing. Administrators spent the outage redistributing traffic and scaling virtual machines while the same machines fell over.

The incident is a service-side infrastructure failure rather than a confirmed security event, but it carries security implications: shadow IT and workaround behavior during a prolonged email outage can expose company data, and the prolonged unavailability of Defender XDR and Purview reduced visibility at exactly the moment administrators were improvising. No root cause was published during the reporting window.

Watch for Microsoft's post-incident root cause analysis, whether the health-check component and the stuck machines are fully remediated, and whether the SLA and regulatory reporting clocks that started during the outage produce customer filings.
