A Microsoft 365 outage that began on November 12, 2026 as an Exchange Online mailbox access problem in the Americas spread into a global degradation affecting Outlook, Teams, SharePoint Online, Copilot, Defender XDR, Purview, and Intune. Microsoft confirmed users in any region could be affected and tracked the incident under EX1550543 and MO1550821. Downdetector showed nearly 50,000 reports and major outlets covered the disruption, which ran past 12 hours. Microsoft attributed the outage to multiple servers becoming unexpectedly unavailable and failing automated health checks, which strained the remaining infrastructure; a core process on some servers became overwhelmed, and the company said it had not identified any recent change correlated with the start of the impact. Engineers tested a configuration change and an alternate health check method while reporting early stabilization and capacity improvements roughly 20 hours after the first incident was opened.

The blast radius was large: Microsoft had more than 450 million paid Microsoft 365 commercial subscriptions, and sheriff's offices, courts, state agencies, and hospitals lost email, with staff workarounds during the outage creating shadow IT and data exposure risk that insurers flagged. Microsoft stated it had no indication the outage was caused by malicious activity and framed it as an infrastructure failure rather than a DDoS attack, though the distinction is hard for users to verify while services are down.

What to watch: whether Microsoft publishes a root cause, whether the configuration and health check changes fully resolve the capacity shortfall, and whether follow-on advisories change the security posture for tenants that lost Defender XDR and Purview visibility during the window. Organizations should treat the gap in monitoring and email security as a review item even though no compromise was reported, and confirm that staff workarounds did not expose company data.
