The Microsoft 365 outage began at 11:20 AM ET on November 12 as an Exchange Online mailbox access problem in the Americas and widened into a global service degradation. Outlook, Teams, Copilot, Defender XDR, Purview, Intune and the Microsoft 365 Apps Admin Center were affected, with nearly 50,000 Downdetector reports at the peak. Microsoft tracked the incident under EX1550543 and MO1550821 and said users in any region could be affected. Service was reported fully restored at 11:10 AM ET on November 13.

Microsoft initially described several servers becoming unexpectedly unavailable after failing automated health checks, overloading the remaining infrastructure; it said traffic was not elevated and found no recent change correlated with the start. Its preliminary post-incident report later identified a latent lock contention bug introduced in July through an update for Microsoft 365 networking gateway servers. More than 80% of those gateways became unresponsive after a Teams failover changed the traffic mix. Microsoft said lab load tests used less powerful hardware and a different traffic mix, so the bug was not caught during three months of deployment.

The outage disabled email and collaboration for courts, sheriff's offices, state agencies and hospitals, and phishers used the extended disruption as cover. Microsoft's final statement called the cause technical and not related to cyber activity or AI agents. Watch for the completed post-incident review on the flawed health checks and load testing, plus phishing campaigns that reference delivery delays from the incident.
