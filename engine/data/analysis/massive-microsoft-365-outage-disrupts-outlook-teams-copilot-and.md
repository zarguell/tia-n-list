A global Microsoft 365 outage on 12-13 November 2026 began as Exchange Online mailbox access failures in the Americas and widened into a worldwide degradation of Outlook, Teams, SharePoint Online, Copilot, Defender XDR, Purview, and Intune. Microsoft tracked the incident under EX1550543 and MO1550821 and reported nearly 50,000 Downdetector reports for Microsoft 365. The root cause was a technical infrastructure failure, not an attack: a number of servers became unexpectedly unavailable, straining the remaining infrastructure, with a core process on some servers overwhelmed so that servers failed automated health checks and went offline. Microsoft said it had not identified any recent change correlated with the start of the impact and attributed the failed health checks to a specific component responsible for processing server health, testing a configuration change and an alternate health check method to return affected servers to service. The outage ran past 12 hours and about 20 hours after the first incident was opened Microsoft was still restoring server capacity region by region.

Microsoft said it had no indication the outage was caused by malicious activity and treated the incident with urgency until full resolution, with early stabilization and incremental improvement across workloads reported on Friday. Government operations, hospitals, courts, and sheriff's offices lost email during the failure, and brokers raised coverage questions about employee workaround risk. Microsoft's official statement said the issue was a technical problem not related to cyber or AI and agents, apologized to customers and partners, and reported broad recovery and stability. Phishing campaigns were observed exploiting the extended outage.

For defenders and IT teams the lessons are operational: Exchange Online dependency fans out to Teams, Copilot, Defender XDR, Purview, and Intune, so a single mailbox-layer failure cascades across security tooling. Microsoft's measured recovery approach and the absence of a confirmed root cause for roughly 20 hours show the value of tenant-side resilience planning and offline fallback procedures for email and identity-dependent workflows.
