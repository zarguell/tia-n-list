The n0n extortion group listed Visa and JPMorgan Chase on its leak site on 16 November with a 48-hour publication deadline, then posted data tied to all three financial firms. JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard separately confirmed incidents at third-party providers. JPMorgan said attackers reached marketing data held by vendors, possibly customer names and email addresses; Visa pointed to a provider in Asia Pacific and said no payment data such as card numbers was involved; Mastercard described a limited set of email addresses for a small subset of cardholders in a specific benefit program.

Newsweek reported roughly 16,000 customer email addresses, phone numbers and JPMorgan account IDs, apparently connected to the invitation-only Reserve program for clients with at least $10 million in assets under management. Cybernews cited more than 6,000 affected Visa premium cardholders and a 502-kilobyte leaked file whose completeness researchers could not establish. No samples or file descriptions had been posted when the listing first appeared. JPMorgan said it would contact affected customers and offer free credit monitoring.

The disclosure also corrected an earlier explanation: the bank had attributed transaction delays reported on 16 November across several institutions to a software bug in on-premise hardware and said there was no evidence of a cyberattack. It later said the vendor breach was unrelated to that disruption. SecureBlink described n0n as financially motivated, with prior documented intrusions using weak or reused credentials, including credential stuffing against more than 300 FortiGate SSL-VPN appliances. Watch for further leak postings and for the identity of the unnamed vendors.
