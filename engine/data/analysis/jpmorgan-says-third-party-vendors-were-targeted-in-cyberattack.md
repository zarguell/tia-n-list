JPMorgan Chase said on 19 November that attackers targeted third-party vendors and reached certain marketing data held by them, possibly customer names and email addresses, with no disruption to its own services. The dataset published by n0n after a 48-hour extortion deadline contains about 16,000 customer email addresses, phone numbers and account IDs linked to the invitation-only Reserve card program, and a separate dump covers more than 6,000 Visa premium cardholders. Visa traced its incident to an unnamed Asia Pacific service provider and said no card numbers or payment data were involved. SecureBlink's 17 November account describes n0n's typical access as valid accounts (T1078) rather than exploited bugs: credential stuffing against more than 300 FortiGate SSL-VPN appliances, weak RDP and WinRM administrator credentials, long-lived AWS IAM keys and stolen hosting-panel sessions.

The absence of card numbers does not make this minor. Names, emails, phone numbers and account IDs of Reserve holders, a program generally requiring at least $10 million in assets under management, give a caller enough detail to clear routine verification and make high-value phishing or account-takeover attempts credible. The 502-kilobyte file reviewed by Cybernews may be a sample of the hundreds of thousands of marketing interactions and the membership directory with customer categories and postal codes that n0n claimed, so 16,000 is a count of what has been seen, not a confirmed victim total. The 16 November transaction delays were blamed by the bank on a software bug in on-premise hardware and are a separate event, but three days of outage noise gave the extortion deadline cover and left customers primed for support-themed lures. That overlap in timing and pretext, not a shared root cause, is what separates this story from the other vendor-breach items today.

No CVE or patch version applies; the exposure is data held outside the bank. Defenders should inventory vendors with access to premium-card marketing stores, suspend nonessential export of names, email addresses, phone numbers and account IDs, and rotate long-lived AWS IAM keys, FortiGate SSL-VPN credentials and hosting-panel administrator sessions tied to those providers. Hunt in vendor export and API access logs for bulk reads between 16 and 19 November of exactly those fields, plus marketing interactions, customer categories and postal codes, and treat any account ID presented on a call or email as an identifier rather than proof of identity. Support teams should be briefed before the promised credit monitoring notices go out, since those notices create a second wave of plausible contact.

Watch for the vendor name, a dataset larger than the 502-kilobyte sample, or regulator notices tying the marketing records to other banks or to the earlier outage. Any of those would change the assessment from a contained vendor leak to a multi-bank supply-chain incident and would put the outage-denial line back under review.
