The n0n extortion group listed Visa and JPMorgan Chase on its leak site on 16 November with a 48-hour publication deadline, then posted data tied to all three financial firms. JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard separately confirmed incidents at third-party providers. JPMorgan said attackers reached marketing data held by vendors, possibly customer names and email addresses; Visa pointed to a provider in Asia Pacific and said no payment data such as card numbers was involved; Mastercard described a limited set of email addresses for a small subset of cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit program. BleepingComputer's report notes JPMorgan is the largest U.S. bank by assets, about $4.1 trillion as of June 2026.

Newsweek reported roughly 16,000 customer email addresses, phone numbers and JPMorgan account IDs, apparently connected to the invitation-only Reserve program for clients with at least $10 million in assets under management. Cybernews cited more than 6,000 affected Visa premium cardholders and a 502-kilobyte leaked file whose completeness researchers could not establish. Other coverage described the data as including travel or dining bookings and said no card numbers had been reported. No samples or file descriptions had been posted when the listing first appeared. JPMorgan said it would contact affected customers and offer free credit monitoring.

One important separation is between this breach and the banking outages earlier that week. JPMorgan attributed transaction delays on 16 November to a software bug in on-premise hardware and told CNBC there was no evidence of a cyberattack; after the vendor disclosure, the bank said the two matters were unrelated and that its own services were not disrupted. SecureBlink described n0n as financially motivated, with prior intrusions using weak or reused credentials rather than vulnerability exploitation, including credential stuffing against more than 300 FortiGate SSL-VPN appliances. That entry path is not confirmed for the providers serving the three firms here. What to watch is whether the providers are named and whether the leaked files match the customer counts JPMorgan and Visa have described.
