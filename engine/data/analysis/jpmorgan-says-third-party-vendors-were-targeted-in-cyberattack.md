The n0n extortion group listed Visa and JPMorgan Chase on its leak site on November 16 with a 48-hour publication countdown. JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard later acknowledged incidents at outside providers. JPMorgan said attackers reached marketing data held by vendors, possibly including customer names and email addresses. Visa tied the claims to a third-party provider in Asia Pacific and said payment data and its own systems were untouched. Mastercard described a limited number of email addresses for a small subset of cardholders in a specific benefit program, with its payment network unaffected.

Newsweek reported roughly 16,000 JPMorgan customer email addresses, phone numbers and account IDs, apparently tied to the invitation-only Reserve program for clients with at least $10 million in assets under management. Cybernews cited more than 6,000 affected Visa premium cardholders and a 502-kilobyte file whose completeness researchers could not establish. A later malware.news item said the leaked material may also include travel or dining bookings. No card numbers have been reported. JPMorgan said its services were not disrupted and that the vendor incident was unrelated to the bank outages reported on November 16, which it attributed to a software bug.

SecureBlink described n0n as financially motivated, with financial services making up 23 percent of its claimed victims. Its documented intrusions used weak or reused credentials rather than vulnerability exploitation, including credential stuffing against more than 300 FortiGate SSL-VPN appliances. That description of the group's methods is not a confirmed entry path for these vendors.

This is marketing and contact data, not a confirmed payment-card breach, but the records point at high-net-worth programs and are useful for targeted phishing. JPMorgan said it would contact affected customers and offer free credit monitoring. Watch for the identities of the vendors, a full inventory of n0n's files, and any evidence that the account identifiers are being used in follow-on fraud.
