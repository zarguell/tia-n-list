The n0n extortion group claimed on November 16 that it had JPMorgan-related engagement data, listed Visa and JPMorgan Chase on its leak site on November 17 with a 48-hour publication deadline, then published files it says come from all three companies. Newsweek reported roughly 16,000 JPMorgan customer email addresses, phone numbers and account IDs, apparently tied to the invitation-only Reserve program for clients with at least $10 million in assets under management. Cybernews was cited on more than 6,000 affected Visa premium cardholders and a 502-kilobyte leaked file whose completeness researchers could not verify.

All three companies now describe vendor incidents rather than breaches of their own systems. JPMorgan said attackers hit some service providers and reached marketing data that may contain customer names and email addresses, with no service disruption and free credit monitoring where appropriate. Visa tied the claims to an unnamed third-party provider in Asia Pacific and said neither its systems nor card numbers were affected. Mastercard told Reuters that a third-party provider exposed a limited number of non-payment email addresses for a small subset of cardholders in a specific benefit program, and that its payment network was unaffected. SecurityWeek repeated those statements on Thursday. A French-language post on November 20 restated the extortion claim without adding a vendor name or new data count.

Watch for the vendor names, the final file contents, and whether the benefit-program data matches the Tailored Benefits records n0n claimed to hold. Keep the November 16 JPMorgan transaction delays separate: the bank told CNBC and Yahoo Finance those came from a temporary software bug in on-premise hardware, and it has repeatedly said the vendor incident is unrelated.
