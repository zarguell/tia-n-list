The n0n extortion group listed Visa and JPMorgan Chase on its leak site on November 16 with a 48-hour countdown, then published data it claims came from the firms. Newsweek reported roughly 16,000 customer email addresses, phone numbers and JPMorgan account IDs, apparently tied to the invitation-only Reserve program for clients with at least $10 million in assets under management. Other reporting cited more than 6,000 affected Visa premium cardholders and a 502-kilobyte leaked file whose completeness researchers could not establish. No payment card numbers have been reported in the leaked material.

On November 19 the three companies confirmed incidents at outside providers. JPMorgan said vendors were targeted and attackers reached marketing data that may include customer names and email addresses, with no service disruption and plans for credit monitoring where appropriate. Visa said the claims concern a third-party service provider in Asia Pacific and that its own systems and payment data were not involved. Mastercard said a third-party provider exposed a limited number of email addresses for a small subset of cardholders in a benefit program, and that its payment network was unaffected.

JPMorgan had earlier told CNBC that transaction delays on November 16 came from a temporary software bug in on-premise hardware, with no evidence of a cyber cause. The later vendor-breach disclosure said the two matters are unrelated.

The concrete details to watch are the unnamed Asia Pacific provider, confirmation of the 16,000-record set, and any follow-up leak from n0n. The exposed data is marketing and contact information, which is still useful for targeted phishing against high-net-worth banking clients.
