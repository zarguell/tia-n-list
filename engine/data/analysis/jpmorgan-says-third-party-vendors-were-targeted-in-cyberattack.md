The n0n extortion group listed Visa and JPMorgan Chase on its leak site on 17 November with a 48-hour publication deadline, after claiming on 16 November that it had obtained JPMorgan related engagement data. SecureBlink reported that financial services make up 23 percent of n0n's claimed victims and that documented intrusions used weak or reused credentials rather than vulnerability exploitation, including credential stuffing against more than 300 FortiGate SSL-VPN appliances. The group is described as financially motivated.

The disclosures now cover all three card brands. JPMorgan said in a post on X that attackers hit some of its service providers and reached marketing data that may contain customer names and email addresses, with no disruption to its services and free credit monitoring for affected customers where appropriate. Visa tied the claims to an unnamed third-party provider in Asia Pacific and said neither its systems nor payment card data such as card numbers were affected. Mastercard told Reuters that a third-party provider exposed a limited number of email addresses, non-payments information, for a small subset of cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit program, and that its payment network was not affected. Securityweek reported the same statements on Thursday.

Newsweek reported that n0n published about 16,000 customer email addresses, phone numbers and JPMorgan account IDs, apparently tied to the invitation-only Reserve program for clients with at least $10 million in assets under management. It also cited Cybernews on more than 6,000 affected Visa premium cardholders and a 502-kilobyte leaked file whose completeness researchers could not verify. Earlier reporting noted that no sample was posted when the countdown began, so the leaked fields and the scale remain a moving target.

Watch for the vendor names, the final file contents, and whether the benefit program data disclosed by Mastercard matches the Tailored Benefits records that n0n claimed to have stolen on 16 November. Keep the 16 November transaction delays separate from the extortion. JPMorgan told CNBC and Yahoo Finance those delays came from a temporary software bug in on-premise hardware, with no evidence of a cyberattack, and the bank has repeatedly said the vendor incident is unrelated.
