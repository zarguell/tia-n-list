JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard have each acknowledged incidents at outside service providers after the n0n extortion group claimed on November 16 that it had stolen their customers' data. JPMorgan said attackers reached marketing data held by vendors, possibly including customer names and email addresses, with no disruption to bank services. Visa tied the claims to a third-party provider in Asia Pacific and said its own systems and payment data, including card numbers, were not involved. Mastercard described exposure of a limited number of email addresses for a small subset of cardholders in a specific benefit program and said its payment network was unaffected.

The leak site timeline is doing much of the driving. SecureBlink and TechRadar reported that n0n listed Visa and JPMorgan with a 48-hour publication countdown before posting samples; Newsweek then reported roughly 16,000 customer email addresses, phone numbers and JPMorgan account IDs, apparently tied to the invitation-only Reserve program for clients with at least $10 million in assets under management. Cybernews cited more than 6,000 affected Visa premium cardholders and a 502-kilobyte file whose completeness researchers could not establish. The bank's earlier service disruptions on November 16, reported across Chase, Bank of America, U.S. Bank, Wells Fargo, PNC, Citigroup, Capital One and Zelle, were initially blamed on a temporary software bug in on-premise hardware, and JPMorgan later said the vendor breach was unrelated.

This is a marketing-data incident with card-brand names attached, not a confirmed payment-card breach. Contact details plus account identifiers are still useful for targeted phishing and account takeover attempts, especially when the records point at high-net-worth programs. SecureBlink described n0n as financially motivated, with financial services making up 23 percent of its claimed victims, and said documented intrusions used weak or reused credentials rather than vulnerability exploitation, including credential stuffing against more than 300 FortiGate SSL-VPN appliances. That description of the group's methods is not a confirmed entry path for these three vendors.

Watch for the vendor identities, a full file inventory from n0n, and any correction to the affected counts. JPMorgan said it would contact impacted customers and offer free credit monitoring. The key open questions are whether the leaked account IDs and contact details are being used in follow-on fraud and whether the Asia Pacific provider and the other vendors share any infrastructure or credentials.
