JPMorgan Chase confirmed on 19 November 2026 that third-party service providers were targeted in a cyberattack and that attackers accessed marketing data held by those vendors, possibly including customer names and email addresses. Visa tied the claims to an incident at a third-party provider in Asia Pacific and said its own systems and payment data such as card numbers were not affected. Mastercard described exposure of a limited number of email addresses for a small subset of cardholders enrolled in a benefit program, with its payment network untouched.

The n0n extortion group listed JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard on 16 November with a 48-hour deadline and later published roughly 16,000 customer email addresses, phone numbers and JPMorgan account IDs apparently tied to the invitation-only Reserve program for clients with at least $10 million in assets under management. Reporting also cited more than 6,000 affected Visa premium cardholders and a 502-kilobyte leaked file whose completeness researchers could not establish. No samples or intrusion method were posted when the countdown started.

The bank said its services were not disrupted and that the vendor breach was unrelated to the 16 November transaction delays it attributed to a software bug in on-premise hardware. Watch for identification of the vendors, expansion of the leaked data set and use of the marketing records in targeted fraud against high-net-worth clients.
