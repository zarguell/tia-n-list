Data from thousands of Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan Chase customers was leaked after an attack on third-party providers. The n0n extortion gang listed Visa and JPMorgan on its leak site on November 17 with a 48-hour publication countdown, then released roughly 16,000 customer email addresses, phone numbers and JPMorgan account IDs. Newsweek tied the records to the invitation-only Reserve program for clients with at least $10 million in assets under management. Cybernews cited more than 6,000 affected Visa premium cardholders and a 502-kilobyte leaked file whose completeness researchers could not establish.

The banks' statements narrow the exposure. Visa said the activity began at an unnamed third-party provider in Asia Pacific, that its own systems were not involved, and that no payment card data was affected. JPMorgan told Reuters that vendors were targeted and that attackers reached marketing data that may include names and email addresses. The bank separately attributed the week's service disruptions to a software bug in on-premise hardware and said there was no connection to the leak. SecureBlink described n0n as financially motivated, with financial services making up 23 percent of its claimed victims, and linked documented intrusions to weak or reused credentials rather than vulnerability exploitation, including credential stuffing against more than 300 FortiGate SSL-VPN appliances.

The open questions are which vendor or vendors were breached and whether the published files are complete. The 48-hour countdown preceded the sample dump, and no payment card numbers have appeared in the reporting. Watch for additional n0n postings, notifications to Reserve clients, and any bank correction of the earlier software-bug explanation for the outage.
