TheGentlemen, a ransomware group that emerged in mid-2025, claimed two Italian victims in quick succession during September 2026. On September 15 the group hit ACA Pescara, a public housing agency in the Abruzzo region, threatening to publish stolen data unless the agency negotiated. A separate claim targeted an insurance company in Lodi, near Milan, reported by Italian security outlet Diggita. Both incidents follow the same playbook: data theft followed by extortion deadlines with no apparent encryption payload mentioned.

The pattern matters more than any single claim. TheGentlemen has been steadily building a victim list concentrated in Italy, hitting both public-sector entities and private companies. The group operates a leak site on Tor and uses standard double-extortion pressure. Neither ACA Pescara nor the Lodi insurer have publicly disclosed the scope of data taken, but Italian public agencies typically hold resident personal data, employment records, and financial information that would be high-value for identity fraud.

Watch for follow-up data dumps on the group's leak site. If the agency does not engage, TheGentlemen has historically followed through on publication within a week. The concentration of Italian targets suggests either a language-capable affiliate or a deliberate focus on organizations that may lack mature incident response capabilities.
