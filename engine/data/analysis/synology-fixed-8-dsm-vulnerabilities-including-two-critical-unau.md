Synology patched eight vulnerabilities in DiskStation Manager, including two critical unauthenticated flaws, CVE-2026-13684 and CVE-2026-13639, that allow remote file access on affected NAS devices. A third tracked CVE, CVE-2024-10441, is also associated with the advisory. Alerts from multiple outlets flagged the pair as major security issues for Synology NAS owners. Administrators should consult Synology security advisory SA_26_13 for the affected DSM versions and fixed releases, since the social posts do not enumerate patch versions.

The timing raises the stakes: separately, the C3RB3R ransomware has been encrypting Synology NAS data since October, appending the .L0CK3D extension and dropping a read-me3.txt ransom note, and Synology issued an official warning. The available posts do not establish that C3RB3R exploits CVE-2026-13684 or CVE-2026-13639, but the overlap of unauthenticated remote-file-access flaws with active ransomware against the same platform makes patching and exposure reduction urgent.

NAS owners should apply the DSM security update, restrict management interfaces from the internet, verify backups are offline and tested, and scan for .L0CK3D files or read-me3.txt notes. Watch for Synology's full advisory details and any confirmed link between the patched flaws and the C3RB3R campaign.
