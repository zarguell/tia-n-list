German-led Operation KillSwitch dismantled the KillSec ransomware operation on September 30, with support from nine other countries, Europol, and Eurojust. Investigators seized the group's leak site and extortion servers and shut down five systems. Three suspects were arrested in Spain, the UK, and Romania, including a 16-year-old in Alicante suspected of being the group's administrator and primary operator. The leak site was taken over by authorities. The action was announced October 1.

KillSec had been a visible operator for over a year. Halcyon's profile recorded the group formalizing as ransomware-as-a-service in June 2024 with a $250 entry fee and 88 percent affiliate retention, more than 250 claimed victims concentrated in India, the US, and Latin America, and infection vectors that included misconfigured AWS S3 buckets and CVE-2025-31161. Resecurity documented a September 2025 attack on MedicSolution, a Brazilian healthcare software vendor, that exposed over 34 GB of data across 94,818 files, including unredacted patient records relating to minors.

A teenage administrator running a RaaS brand with hundreds of victims says the barrier to running extortion infrastructure has collapsed to near zero. Takedowns of this shape, servers seized and affiliates arrested, rarely remove the affiliate talent pool, so reconstitution under a new brand is the expected outcome. Watch for claimed victims reappearing on successor leak sites and for whether the suspect's operational data, seized servers, and chat logs produce follow-on arrests.
