Group-IB attributed the Iran-linked Handala Hack persona to a Telegram-based surveillance backdoor called HEAVYGRAM and a Delphi-based utility called CRUDEEXCLUDE. HEAVYGRAM is a Python-based malware that uses Telegram for command-and-control, supporting remote command execution, screenshot capture, file exfiltration, microphone activation, and theft of Telegram and WhatsApp data, saved passwords, and browser data. CRUDEEXCLUDE prepares environments for HEAVYGRAM deployment by configuring Microsoft Defender exclusion paths for defense evasion.

The FBI first issued alerts about Handala Hack in March 2026 and again in September, accusing Iranian actors operating on behalf of the Ministry of Intelligence and Security (MOIS) of targeting dissidents, journalists, and opposition groups. The U.K. NCSC tracks the same malware family under the moniker CHOSEN BRICK. Handala Hack, which emerged after the October 7, 2023 attacks, is assessed to be operated by Void Manticore (also known as Banished Kitten or Storm-0842), an MOIS-affiliated threat actor with a history of destructive wiping operations and hack-and-leak campaigns.

Delivery typically involves social engineering via Telegram, WhatsApp, and Instagram, where targets are lured into installing malware disguised as legitimate applications like Pictory, KeePass, or Telegram. HEAVYGRAM was first detected in September 2023 and has been delivered through WSF/VBS scripts, VBScripts, HTA files, and executables with embedded archives. The use of Telegram as both a social engineering vector and a C2 channel provides operators with an encrypted, low-cost communication infrastructure that is difficult to disrupt.
