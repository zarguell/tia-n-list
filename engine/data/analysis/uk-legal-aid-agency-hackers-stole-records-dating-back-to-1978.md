Hackers stole records from the UK Legal Aid Agency dating back to 1978, exposing a very long historical dataset of client and case information. The breach underscores the risk of retaining decades of sensitive legal aid data on systems without commensurate access controls.

Affected data subjects face elevated risk of identity fraud and targeted phishing. The agency and oversight bodies should review retention policies, access logging, and legacy data stores. Incident response should prioritize notifying affected individuals and determining whether the intrusion was contained.
