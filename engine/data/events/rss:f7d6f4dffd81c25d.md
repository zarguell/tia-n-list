Anthropic on Monday announced the public beta of Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS), a system designed to help organizations detect malicious use of AI. Unveiled in September, EFS has been developed with input from over 100 Anthropic customers, with companies such as Stripe and Salesforce evaluating it in private beta. A quarter of Fortune 100 companies helped develop EFS. According to Anthropic , existing Claude protections can identify potentially harmful requests made individually, but EFS adds a monitoring layer that can detect suspicious patterns across multiple requests that may appear benign when analyzed individually. The system targets organizations that use Claude under zero data retention agreements and have so far relied only on classifiers that analyze each request in isolation. EFS is designed to detect both deliberate misuse of AI, such as AI-powered cyberattacks, and incidents where AI agents go beyond what their operator intended, such as the cases where agents exploited vulnerabilities to gain access to real-world systems. With EFS, organizations store conversation data in their own cloud and protect it with their own encryption keys and access policies. Neither Anthropic nor its cloud providers retain copies of the conversation data after it’s transferred to the customer’s storage.
