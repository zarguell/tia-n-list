The General Services Administration did not effectively protect physical security, personally identifiable data and general proprietary business information stored within its Google Drive, according to an https://www.gsaig.gov/sites/default/files/audit-reports/A250043-1%20Final%20Report%20Redacted.pdf ”>audit released Tuesday by the agency’s Office of Inspector General. In a limited search of GSA’s Google Drive, the OIG found 116 files containing controlled unclassified information, or CUI, that were improperly shared with employees and contractors who did not have a legitimate business reason to know those details. The exposed data included Social Security numbers, phone numbers, proprietary research, vendor proposals and site plans for a Washington, D.C. building. GSA IT’s review determined that the identified documents were “not exposed externally” and found “no indication of malicious intent,” according to a response letter from David Shive, GSA’s chief information officer. The watchdog attributed the improper exposure of sensitive information to “deficiencies” in GSA’s oversight.
