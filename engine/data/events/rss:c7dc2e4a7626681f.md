As Android Central reports , users have received unexpected emails telling them their phone number has been verified for secondary Google accounts. Like me and many others, you may have one Google account for everyday use, another for work, and a third for things you would rather keep separate. But if you’re using an Android phone, those accounts may have something in common that you didn’t intend or even know about: your phone number. Google’s own documentation confirms that phone number verification is on by default and adds your number to all Google accounts signed in on the device. This applies to both existing accounts and any you add later. Google says phone number verification helps support features such as signing in, account recovery, Rich Communications Services (RCS) messaging, and finding or securing a lost device. But it also means that a number you intended to use with your main account can become associated with a secondary account simply because both are signed in on the same Android device. If you deliberately left your phone number off a secondary account, this links it to your real identity without you knowingly making that connection. Your number doesn’t automatically become public, but Google gains another way to connect accounts you wanted to keep separate.
