Microsoft’s streak of record-breaking vulnerability disclosures continued with a mega Patch Tuesday , containing 1,413 defects affecting the company’s various enterprise products and services. The monthly batch of patches surpassed the company’s previous annual record of 1,261 common vulnerabilities and exposures (CVEs) set in 2020. “Hundreds of CVEs in a single release used to be the exception — now it’s the baseline,” Dustin Childs, head of threat awareness at TrendAI’s Zero Day Initiative, wrote in an email. AI is partly to blame, he added, but it “isn’t creating these bugs out of thin air; it’s just shortening the time between a vulnerability existing and someone noticing it.” Yet, for defenders understandably overwhelmed by the surge of defects found in Microsoft products, actively exploited zero-day vulnerabilities aren’t growing in kind. “If there’s solace to be had, it’s in the fact that only one of these bugs is under active attack,” Childs wrote in a blog post . “If you go back to the hot 0-day summer of 2023, there were 17 Microsoft 0-days between January and October as opposed to 15 this year during the same period.” Microsoft disclosed one actively exploited zero-day affecting Windows Shell — CVE-2026-96951 — that allows unauthorized attackers to bypass a security feature.
