Microsoft says Teams will begin using AI-powered phishing detection in early November 2026 to detect malicious behavior in messages. The rollout is part of a targeted release phase expected to end by mid-November, with the new security feature likely reaching general availability worldwide between early and mid-December. Microsoft first revealed in a Microsoft 365 Roadmap entry last week that the AI-powered phishing detection will use conversation context (without retaining message content) to identify phishing and suspicious behavior in higher-risk messaging scenarios, such as external first-contact chats. "Microsoft Teams is introducing AI-powered, context-aware phishing detection to help identify phishing, social engineering, impersonation attempts, and deceptive intent," the company said in a Microsoft 365 Message Center update on Wednesday. "The capability analyzes conversation context to identify suspicious behavior that may not include traditional indicators such as malicious links or files." Microsoft says IT admins won't need to take any action to enable this new security protection feature, which will focus on external first-contact chats for most users. ​It also added that while the new Teams feature will analyze conversation context to discover suspicious behavior, it will not retain customer data for further AI training or other uses.
