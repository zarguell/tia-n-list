The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has added two critical PHP vulnerabilities collectively tracked as PHP2Shell to its catalog of actively exploited security flaws. Threat actors without privileges can exploit the two security issues (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619) in high-complexity attacks to gain remote code execution (RCE) on unpatched systems. ​ The PHP security team patched the two flaws on Wednesday, October 21, one day after urging security teams to install the security updates as soon as they became available. Although details on the PHP2Shell security flaws (including the CVE IDs and mitigation measures shared with critical infrastructure providers) leaked and some proof-of-concept (PoC) exploits were published online before patches went live, the PHP security team said that none were functional. Multiple cybersecurity firms (including Cloudflare , Amazon , Hadrian , watchTowr , Previdian , and Patchstack ) also detected widespread exploitation attempts before public disclosure, but they all tagged them as unsuccessful. Amazon Threat Intelligence security researchers also said that attackers began targeting the PHP2Shell vulnerabilities four days before security patches were released . CISA added the vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog on Monday and ordered Federal Civilian Executive Branch (FCEB) agencies to secure their systems by Thursday, as mandated by Binding Operational Directive 26-04.
