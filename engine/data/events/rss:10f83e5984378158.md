For decades, classical public-key cryptosystems have provided robust protection against unauthorized access and tampering when evaluated against classical computational capabilities. However, the eventual deployment of a Cryptanalytically Relevant Quantum Computer (CRQC) — a quantum computer capable of breaking real-world public-key cryptosystems — presents a single-point failure vector for modern enterprise security architectures. Transitioning to Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC), which encompasses public-key cryptographic algorithms engineered to resist cryptanalytic attacks mounted by both conventional and quantum systems, is an immediate strategic imperative for federal agencies, technology vendors, and critical infrastructure operators. To govern and accelerate this global cryptographic transition, a binding framework of executive orders, policy directives, and operational mandates has been established. This regulatory environment mandates strict timelines, risk-prioritized resource allocation, and specific protocol adoption schedules
