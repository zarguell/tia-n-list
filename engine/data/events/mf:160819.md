T-Mobile’s cybersecurity team reportedly used an unusually direct response to evict suspected Chinese state-backed intruders in 2024: it physically cut a network cable connecting compromised infrastructure to the outside world. According to Bloomberg, the move followed months of investigation into activity linked to Salt Typhoon, the China-associated espionage group accused of infiltrating telecommunications and internet providers worldwide. The incident illustrates the severity of the campaign and the need for defenders to act quickly when a sophisticated actor gains a foothold inside critical network environments. T-Mobile’s team had reportedly been hunting the intruders across its environment without a clear breakthrough. T-Mobile Cuts Network Cable to Kick Salt Typhoon Hackers The decisive clue emerged after analysts identified anomalous activity on an internal system that appeared to originate from a router operated by another, unnamed telecommunications provider. The finding pointed to a potential pathway into T-Mobile’s network through interconnected carrier infrastructure. Jeff Simon, T-Mobile’s chief security officer, and three colleagues reportedly traveled to a data center near the company’s Bellevue, Washington headquarters to locate the affected equipment.
