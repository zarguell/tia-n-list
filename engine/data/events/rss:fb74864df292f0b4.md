The European Commission (EC) on Monday confirmed that a recently disclosed AI-assisted cyberattack resulted in the compromise of personal information. The attack , disclosed last week, prompted the temporary shutdown of several applications. At the time, the organization said it had found no evidence that personal information belonging to Commission staff had been compromised. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier revealed during a press briefing on Monday that the investigation has since uncovered a data breach affecting EU Academy, an online learning platform used by people outside the EC to take courses and learn about European Union policies. Compromised information includes names and email addresses of EU Academy users, but the full extent of the breach is still being investigated. Access to EU Academy has been suspended during the investigation and the European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS) has been notified. Potentially affected individuals have not been informed directly. “Important to keep in mind at this point in time that the exact scale of the data leak is still subject to the investigation, so there was no reach-out to individual users, but rather a general message to all users on the website out of transparency,” Regnier explained .
