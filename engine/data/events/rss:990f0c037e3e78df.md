Malware is the umbrella term for any malicious software. Spyware is one type of malware, a virus is another, and adware is a third; what separates them is what each one does and how it spreads. This guide compares spyware with malware, viruses, worms, adware, trojans, and ransomware, shows the differences side by side, and explains how to remove and prevent all of them. Is Spyware Malware? (Yes, and Here’s How They Relate) Yes, spyware is malware. Malware is the broad category, and spyware is a specific kind of malware built to secretly collect information about you and send it to someone else. What Is Malware? The Umbrella Term Malware, short for malicious software, is any program designed to harm a device, steal data, spy on users, or disrupt systems without the owner’s permission. Viruses, worms, trojans, ransomware, spyware, and adware are all types of malware, and the term describes intent rather than a specific technique. What Type of Malware Is Spyware? Spyware is surveillance malware. It hides on a device, records activity such as keystrokes, passwords, browsing, and messages, and reports the data to an operator.
