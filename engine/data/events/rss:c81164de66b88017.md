Ride-hailing company Uber is notifying nearly 150,000 drivers that attackers stole their data after breaching AI infrastructure provider Fireworks in early October. In breach notification letters sent to affected individuals on November 10, Uber said threat actors accessed their personal information after breaching an Uber account with a third-party service provider. While Uber didn't share the total number of affected drivers in these letters, it revealed in a filing with Maine's Office of the Attorney General that the data breach has exposed the information of 149,346 drivers, including their names, driver’s license numbers, dates of birth, and addresses. After being informed of the breach, Uber launched an investigation and severed data connections with the service provider to block further malicious access to its data. "On October 9, 2026, a service provider notified Uber that an unauthorized third party had compromised the service provider's internal credentials and used them to access Uber's account with the service provider," Uber said. "After being notified of this incident, Uber immediately severed data connections with the service provider and launched an investigation. On October 13, 2026, our investigation determined that the unauthorized party had used the compromised credentials to access Uber data connected to the service provider."
