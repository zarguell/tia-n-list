WhatsApp is one of the world’s most widely used messaging apps, connecting billions of people every day. Because it’s so widely used, cybercriminals also often target WhatsApp users . Account takeovers, phishing attempts, and unwanted contact are constant threats. Meta knows that adequate security matters, and last month it began rolling out changes that make it harder for unauthorized people to access chats and give WhatsApp users more context before they answer a call. These updates focus on three main security areas . Stronger two-step verification, better information about calls from unknown numbers (on Android), and more flexible passkey use. Key Takeaways WhatsApp is replacing its multi-digit two-step verification PIN with a stronger full password to better resist guessing and brute-force attacks. On Android, WhatsApp now shows useful extra details—such as the caller’s country and any shared groups. Users can add multiple passkeys to the same account (including separate ones for Android and iOS), making secure biometric logins more practical across devices. WhatsApp users should enable the new features promptly, treat unexpected contacts with caution, keep apps updated, and use reliable antivirus
