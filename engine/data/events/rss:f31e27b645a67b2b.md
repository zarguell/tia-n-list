A Kosovo man accused of selling stolen credit card and personal information over the internet, who was allegedly found with millions of payment card numbers in his possession, was extradited to the United States, the Justice Department announced Monday. In October 2025, a federal grand jury in Wisconsin returned an eight-count indictment charging Hektor Avdyli, 21 — reportedly the nephew of a former member of Kosovo’s parliament — with wire fraud, access device fraud and aggravated identity theft. Kosovar authorities arrested him in July at the behest of the FBI and DOJ, and he’s now in Milwaukee awaiting trial. “Unfortunately, there is nothing remotely unusual about American consumers having their identities and financial information stolen online, often by foreign actors,” said First Assistant U.S. Attorney Brad Schimel for the Eastern District of Wisconsin. “It happens countless times every day, and that theft costs American consumers billions of dollars.” Avdyli allegedly used online criminal forums to ply his illicit trade, advertising and selling stolen credit card data and personal information to help customers commit fraud. Court documents say that he claimed to steal the information from shopping websites using malware. As part of the operation to apprehend him, the FBI secretly bought more than 27,000 credit card numbers and related personal information from a seller.
