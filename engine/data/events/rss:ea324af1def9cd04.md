Flipper Devices, the company behind the popular Flipper Zero pentesting device, has launched a crowdfunding campaign for Flipper One, a portable Linux computer and networking multi-tool. Despite global RAM shortages and unpredictable price fluctuations, the company said Flipper One will launch at $399 , a price that reportedly covers production costs and leaves no profit margin. Flipper Devices said the device’s RAM and UFS storage account for half of the total hardware cost, but after years of development, they decided not to hold back now. “Instead of waiting for perfect market conditions or raising the price, we’re moving forward with the Kickstarter campaign,” reads the press release Flipper Devices shared with BleepingComputer. “We aim to make Flipper One as accessible as possible without building a profit margin into the hardware's price.” Flipper One is a portable computer that runs a Debian-based system called Flipper OS , and features built-in Wi-Fi 6E, Gigabit Ethernet, and an eSIM. It can manage up to five network uplinks, act as a hotspot, run VPNs and firewalls, and connect to an external display over USB-C. It is a separate product from Flipper Zero , focused on IP networking and on-device computing. In May, Flipper Devices asked the community to help build it , highlighting the project's complexity and listing several ambitious goals.
