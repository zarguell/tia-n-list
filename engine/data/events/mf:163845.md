The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) and the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) released final technical guidance to prevent attackers from forging, stealing, replaying, or misusing identity and access tokens . Published as NIST Interagency Report (IR) 8587 on September 15, 2026, the guidance provides federal agencies and cloud service providers with a detailed roadmap for securing single sign-on, identity federation, API authorization, and machine-to-machine authentication deployments. Identity tokens and signed assertions enable applications to rely on authentication already performed by an identity provider, reducing repeated credential prompts. NIST Urges Cloud Providers to Strengthen Identity Tokens However, a stolen or forged token can let an attacker impersonate a trusted user and access connected systems without the victim’s password or an additional multifactor authentication challenge. This risk has become increasingly visible in attacks involving forged SAML assertions, compromised signing certificates, and weak token-validation controls. NIST noted that improperly scoped signing keys have contributed to major incidents, including one that exposed more than 60,000 emails from a federal agency. According to the NIST IR 8587 guidance , the publication expands on the IA-13 “Identity Providers and Authorization Servers” control in NIST SP 800-53 Release 5.1.1.
