Attackers compromised third-party registry operators for .gh (Ghana), .sl (Sierra Leone), and .as (American Samoa) and altered authoritative DNS records. By controlling registry-level DNS, they passed automated domain control validation and obtained unauthorized HTTPS certificates for Google domains and other leading global brands. Google confirmed its systems were not breached; it blocked identified certificates in Chrome via CRLSets and coordinated revocation with certificate authorities. The incident exposes dependency of TLS issuance on DNS-based validation at the registry layer.
