A Delta Air Lines flight carrying passengers home from the world’s largest hacking conference became the center of a rapidly unfolding cybersecurity scare on Monday after crew members reported that someone on board had jammed the aircraft’s official Wi-Fi. The incident occurred aboard Delta Flight 591, a Boeing 757 that departed Las Vegas Harry Reid International Airport around 8:30 a.m. on August 10, following an overnight delay, and flew roughly three and a half hours toward Atlanta. About sixty minutes into the journey, pilots messaged corporate security via ACARS that a passenger had created a “scam Wi-Fi” network named Delta WiFi Fast. DEF CON Attendees Accused of Hacking In-Flight Wi-Fi A follow-up transmission stated that “a bunch of pax that were at a cyber conference in Vegas… were able to jam our Wi-Fi and broadcast their signal,” a message independently picked up by two ground-based ACARS receivers. The flight departed shortly after DEF CON 34 concluded, as part of the annual “Hacker Summer Camp” cluster, which also includes Black Hat and BSides Las Vegas. Security researchers describe the reported activity as a textbook evil twin attack , in which an attacker broadcasts a rogue access point whose SSID mimics a trusted network, luring users onto a fraudulent captive portal that harvests credentials.
