Password manager mSecure has warned current and former customers to protect the confidential information stored in its app after detecting “suspicious activity.” But its advice about what users should do comes without an explanation of what happened or whose information might be at risk. The warning leaves users to decide whether to replace credentials—and whether it’s safe to store replacements in mSecure. While those are perfectly reasonable questions, mSecure’s warning does not answer them. In an email published by The Hacker News and sent to customers on November 3, mSecure’s developer, mSeven Software, said: “mSeven Software (‘mSeven’) recently learned of suspicious activity associated with the mSecure password management application. In response, mSeven quickly launched an investigation, took steps to mitigate the issue, and engaged external cybersecurity experts to assist.” The notice recommends that customers “immediately take steps to protect all confidential information” stored in mSecure. Those steps: “may include changing passwords, resetting or re-enrolling two-factor authentication methods, or removing existing passkeys.” But the notice does not identify the suspicious activity, when it occurred, which systems were involved, or whether anyone accessed customers’ stored information .
