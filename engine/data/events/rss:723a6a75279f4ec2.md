As companies running refrigeration units in the U.S. and Europe recover from recent hacking incidents, threat intelligence firms have disclosed more than a dozen new serious security vulnerabilities in popular products, while others continue to track intense interest from potential threat actors in similar internet-connected devices. On Friday, cybersecurity firm VulnCheck published details on 14 vulnerabilities discovered earlier this year in Dixell refrigeration units, which are manufactured by refrigeration technology firm Copeland. According to lab testing with rebuilt versions of the vendor’s firmware, each flaw would have given an attacker “root” or administrative access to the infected unit. Many of the vulnerabilities have severity scores between 8 and 9.8, and at least two are known to be under active exploitation in the wild. The vulnerabilities all affect Dixell and Copeland’s XWEB EVO supervision system, which is one of four known to have been affected in the October hack of refrigeration controllers in 700 supermarkets across France. VulnCheck security researcher Valentin Lobstein wrote that he began looking into XWEB’s cybersecurity profile after reading that an affected device from another vendor was “almost insulting” in its lapses, including setting the password of the administrative account to the current day’s date.
