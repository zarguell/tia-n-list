Australia’s move from the Essential Eight to an outcomes-based cybersecurity model will push organizations from conducting periodic point-in-time, checklist compliance assessments to having continuous evidence of a solid security posture. Key takeaways The Australian Signals Directorate (ASD) is moving from the Essential Eight cybersecurity framework to a new outcomes-focused Essentials series covering enterprise IT, cloud, operational technology (OT), and potentially agentic AI. The Essential Eight itself only ever covered on-premises enterprise IT, built around eight named technical controls, such as application control and patching. It never extended to the security of cloud, identity, or OT. The shift challenges the traditional checklist approach to cybersecurity, where organizations demonstrate compliance through periodic assessments and point-in-time reports. In dynamic environments spanning IT, cloud, identity, and OT, security posture can change quickly and repeatedly between assessments. Exposure management can help organizations continuously understand where they are exposed, prioritize the most critical weaknesses, and provide evidence of their current security posture. ASD’s strategic shift to active security posture validation Can you prove your security posture is solid, right now, on demand?
