CISA, NSA, and their counterparts in Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and the UK have jointly published guidance urging organizations to treat autonomous AI systems as a core cybersecurity concern. The document focuses on agentic AI — LLM-based systems that can plan, make decisions, and take actions autonomously without human review at each stage. The agencies identify five risk categories: excessive privilege granting (a single compromise causing cascading damage), design/configuration flaws, behavioral risks where agents pursue goals in unintended ways, structural risks across interconnected agent networks, and accountability gaps where opaque decision-making makes incident forensics difficult. Key recommendations include cryptographically secured identities for each agent, short-lived credentials, mandatory human sign-off for high-impact actions, and treating prompt injection as a first-class threat. The agencies warn that until security practices mature, organizations should "assume that agentic AI systems may behave unexpectedly" and prioritize resilience and reversibility over efficiency gains.
