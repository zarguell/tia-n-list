Removing standing local-admin rights is one of the highest-impact things any organization can do to blunt ransomware and lateral movement and endpoint privilege management (EPM) is how you do it without grinding productivity to a halt. EPM lets users run approved applications and perform approved tasks with elevated rights on demand, while stripping away the permanent admin privileges attackers love. The catch is operational: too strict and helpdesk tickets explode; too loose and you’ve achieved nothing. The right tool depends on your size, your appetite for policy tuning, and whether you want EPM standalone or unified with broader privileged access management (PAM) . This playbook walks through eight leading EPM tools in depth features, best fit, pros, and cons and shows how to roll them out by business size. Pricing is described by model only. Stage 1 — Understand the EPM Job EPM sits between “everyone is a local admin” (dangerous) and “no one can do anything” (unworkable). It grants just-in-time, application- or task-level elevation based on policy, logs privileged actions, and ideally learns your environment to minimize prompts. Done well, it removes local admin from the vast majority of users while keeping them productive a foundational least-privilege control and a common cyber-insurance requirement.
