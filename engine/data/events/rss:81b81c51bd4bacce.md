Reddit announced in a post by co-founder Steve Huffman that the site will gradually become a "logged-in service," offering limited access to visitors without accounts. For the past 20 years, the discussion platform has let users browse almost all its content without logging in, but according to Huffman, Reddit has now grown to a size where this is no longer sustainable. The two main points for this upcoming change are the need to offer users more personalization and to address the increasing problem of content scrapers. To tackle this, a new pilot test will begin in selected markets outside the United States in the coming months, requiring users to log in to access the site's full content. However, what content will be restricted, what countries will be impacted first, and rollout timelines have not been determined at this time. Huffman said they currently believe it’s important to keep the front page accessible to all, and allow people arriving from search results or other external sources to see at least the page the URL points to. With users logged in, Reddit expects to gather more data from upvotes to help users find relevant content, join communities, and participate in discussions.
