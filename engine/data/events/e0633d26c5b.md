New technical details from Unit 42's incident response show that exploitation of CVE-2026-0300 began with unsuccessful attempts on April 9, 2026, with successful unauthenticated RCE achieved one week later. The attackers injected shellcode into an nginx worker process, immediately cleaned crash logs and core dumps to evade detection, then waited four days before deploying tools. Post-exploitation activity included EarthWorm (a multi-platform SOCKS5 tunneling tool previously used by Volt Typhoon and APT41) and ReverseSocks5 for persistent network pivoting. Critically, the attackers leveraged the firewall's service account credentials to enumerate Active Directory, targeting domain root and DomainDnsZones. Unit 42 tracks the cluster as CL-STA-1132 and assesses it as likely state-sponsored. On April 29, the attackers conducted a SAML flood to force a second device to become Active, then achieved RCE on that device as well — demonstrating a deliberate dual-device compromise strategy. Palo Alto's Advanced Threat Prevention can block attacks with Threat ID 510019 from content version 9097-10022. Patches are not expected until staggered rollout between May 13 and May 28. The interim mitigation remains restricting the User-ID Authentication Portal to trusted internal zones or disabling it entirely.
