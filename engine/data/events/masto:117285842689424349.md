Chinese hackers used publicly available AI tools to conduct the first observed fully autonomous cyberattack on a government target. According to a Financial Times report from August 12, citing researchers from Israeli cybersecurity firm Dream, the attack compromised at least 85 user accounts and stole over 2,500 personnel records from Taiwanese government systems.

The attackers built an autonomous hacking platform using open-source AI agent frameworks, allowing multiple agents to simultaneously map networks, analyze vulnerabilities, attempt intrusions, and modify tactics when a particular attack path failed. The campaign reportedly lasted four days in early July, deploying up to eight autonomous agents in parallel.

Dream reported that the system mapped 21 government systems before compromising user accounts and stealing employee personal data. The attackers then expanded their operations to target Taiwan's nuclear safety agency, at least seven energy companies, government service providers, and other government systems.

Dream said it found evidence of the attack in a 160 MB internet archive encountered during broader threat monitoring. The archive contained 1,395 files indicating the tool was built on two open-source AI agent systems, Hermes and OpenClaw. Both are freely downloadable and designed to allow large language models to independently execute multi-step tasks.

Researchers were unable to determine which base model powered the agents, but the data indicated the model's safeguards were bypassed by presenting the intrusion as an authorized penetration test rather than an actual attack.
