Something I've seen consistently, from the very beginning when I first brushed up against this business of "digital forensics", is the default focus on, "...this is how you analyze this artifact...", and rarely extending beyond that. I'm guilty of this, too. My first 8 books took this approach, listing data sources and artifacts, describing what they were in a general sense, and then maybe showing how to parse them. Each of those efforts basically left it up to the reading to stitch together their investigation on their own, and I justified this with the adage that each investigation is different. But then, I realized that what makes each investigation different isn't the incident itself, or the data sources. No, it's the investigator. Everyone of us approaches an incident investigation supported by our own knowledge and experience, and that's really the only thing that makes each investigation different. So, yes, while it's important to understand the value and nuances of the various data sources available on a Windows endpoint, something I can to realize is that this must be done in the context of an overall investigation. I recently ran across a blog post titled, How Do You Investigate Windows Event Logs? , with the subtitle, Windows Eventlog Analysis 101 .
