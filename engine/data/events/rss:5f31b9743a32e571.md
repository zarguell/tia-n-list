What happened On Thursday, November 12, millions of people around the world woke up to emails that wouldn’t load, video calls that kept dropping, and inaccessible cloud documents. The outage hit Outlook, Teams, Microsoft 365, and Copilot. According to Microsoft, the root cause was technical: several of its servers went offline without warning, overloading the remaining infrastructure. Still, how can you tell whether an outage like this stems from an internal glitch or a cyberattack? How to tell an infrastructure failure apart from a DDoS attack To the average user, the symptoms of a technical glitch and a Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attack look virtually identical: unresponsive apps, connection issues, and overloaded servers. Both can trigger bottlenecks, service disruptions, and connectivity drops. The key difference lies in what’s driving the problem. In a DDoS attack , cybercriminals use networks of compromised devices or systems to flood a service with massive amounts of traffic, aiming to exhaust its capacity to respond.
