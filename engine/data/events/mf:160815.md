The campaign, tracked as “Offside Wallet Theft Factory,” has been active since at least March 2026. It relies on cloned code, misleading listings, reused Firefox add-on identities, and fake wallet branding to trick users. The extensions impersonated popular Web3 brands such as OKX, Rabby Wallet, and TronLink. A recovery phrase or private key gives an attacker full control over a crypto wallet. Once stolen, the attacker can restore the wallet on another device and transfer assets without needing the victim’s password. Removing the malicious extension after the theft does not make the wallet safe again. 40 Firefox Extensions Steal Crypto Seven malicious extensions used attacker-controlled Supabase projects as remote switches for phishing pages. One example, named “0KX WEB3,” copied the appearance of the OKX wallet brand by replacing the letter “O” with a zero. It claimed to be a Web3 wallet but had no real wallet functions. Instead, it loaded a remote phishing page inside its extension window. The phishing page asked users to create or import a wallet. During the import process, victims were prompted to enter a recovery phrase or private key.
