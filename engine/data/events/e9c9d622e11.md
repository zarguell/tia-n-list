Google Project Zero's Seth Jenkins and Jann Horn published a full zero-click exploit chain against Pixel 10 devices, progressing from a crafted Dolby Digital Plus audio stream (CVE-2025-54957, previously used against Pixel 9) to complete kernel control via a newly discovered vulnerability in the VPU driver for the Chips&Media Wave677DV block on the Tensor G5 chip. The VPU `mmap` handler performs zero bounds checking on the requested mapping size, allowing an attacker to map arbitrary physical memory including the entire kernel image. Because Pixel 10 places the kernel at a fixed physical address, no KASLR bruteforcing is required. Exploit was built in under a day. Google shipped a fix 71 days after reporting — the first time one of Jenkins' Android driver bugs was patched in under 90 days. However, the research underscores that shallow, easily discoverable vulnerabilities persist in vendor-maintained kernel drivers despite years of bug bounty programs. The Dolby UDC exploit works on devices that have not applied December 2025/January 2026 Android security patches.
