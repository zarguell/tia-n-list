Picture a customer-support chatbot at a mid-sized Indian e-commerce company. It answers questions about orders and returns. It runs on a third-party large language model. Nobody thinks of it as high-risk. Now the product team gives it one new permission: it can issue refunds up to ₹5,000 without human sign-off. It's a small feature that saves agents hours a week. Under the Artificial Intelligence (AI) Security Auditing Guidelines that CERT-In issued on 4 November 2026, that one change can be enough to move the chatbot into a higher risk tier. And the tier decides how the system gets audited, how often, and by whom. The 107-page framework scores every AI system on eight risk factors. Most coverage so far has stopped at "four risk tiers, from low to critical." This post goes further: it explains how the scoring actually works, walks through the chatbot example factor by factor, and sets out what security teams should do now. Key Takeaways CERT-In's AI Security Auditing Guidelines (v1.0, 4 November 2026) cover AI systems developed, procured, deployed, or operated in India, plus systems that process Indian data or serve Indian users.
