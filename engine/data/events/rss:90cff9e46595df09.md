Here are this week’s highlights: Iran suspected in US military freezer hacks US officials suspect Iran-linked hackers caused freezer malfunctions that spoiled food at around six military base commissaries starting in late August, Nextgov reports, citing people familiar with the matter. At Fort Huachuca in Arizona, a freezer entered defrost mode and heated its contents, and Cyber Command and Defense Department law enforcement units are investigating with French authorities, after hackers overrode refrigeration controls at some 350 French establishments earlier this month. Most servers rely on UEFI code hit by critical boot flaw A critical 16-year-old buffer overflow (CVE-2026-97631) in TianoCore EDK II, open source code that most of the world’s server vendors and data centers use through firmware suppliers such as AMI, Insyde and Phoenix, means the flaw affects most servers worldwide, Ars Technica reports.
