Registration campaigns, Temporary Access Pass, protecting security-info registration, and a help-desk recovery process that doesn't become a bypass MFA to Passkeys in Microsoft Entra ID series · Part 4 of 7 Passkeys make the sign-in ceremony very hard to phish, so attackers go after the process that issues new credentials instead. The most damaging identity intrusions of the last few years didn't break cryptography. The attackers called the service desk, impersonated an employee, sometimes with a spoofed number or an AI-generated voice, and asked for an MFA reset or a new device registration. Once the help desk registered a method the attacker controlled, every phishing-resistant control downstream worked as designed and protected the attacker's access. So for passkeys, the security of the account equals the security of the weakest way to get a new credential onto it. This part covers four such ways, and how to close each: Self-service registration (Security info), protected by Conditional Access. This includes the BYOD trap where your own policies block registration. The first credential for new hires and users with no strong method, issued as a Temporary Access Pass.
