MOIS on Telegram: CHOSEN BRICK spies on Iranian dissidents and journalists. NCSC, FBI and AIVD attribute to Iranian Ministry of Intelligence.

For years, Tehran has refined a low-cost, high-impact surveillance model: no expensive zero-day exploits, no dedicated C2 infrastructure to dismantle, just one Telegram bot per victim and an executable file disguised as a medical report or innocuous software. A joint advisory published on September 15, 2026 by NCSC (UK), FBI (US) and AIVD (Netherlands) confirms that this espionage machine, attributed to the Iranian Ministry of Intelligence and Security (MOIS), is still fully operational and has hit dissidents, activists and journalists in at least three Western countries.

**A pattern repeating since 2023:**

The malware at the center of the advisory, dubbed CHOSEN BRICK (with a related variant indicated in some sources as HEAVYGRAM), is not entirely new: the FBI had already flagged a Telegram-based setup with similar characteristics in fall 2023, and in March 2026 there was a further public warning accompanied by a State Department bounty linked to a hack-and-leak operation from July 2025. The mid-September 2026 advisory is therefore the most recent point of a multi-year campaign, not an isolated incident, and it is the first time three Western intelligence agencies have jointly certified it, indicating that the number of confirmed victims on their territory has reached a threshold justifying coordinated public attribution.

What strikes analysts is not the sophistication of the code -- CHOSEN BRICK runs only on Windows and does not exploit known vulnerabilities -- but the operational discipline with which it is distributed. The entry vector is almost always the same: a long social engineering campaign on WhatsApp or Telegram, often lasting weeks, where the MOIS operator builds a trust relationship with the target before sending the payload. In one case documented by Recorded Future, the bait was a falsified MRI report -- an image of a herniated disc -- sent to a person identified by the regime as an "enemy," attempting to exploit the natural willingness to download an apparently innocuous medical attachment.

**Fake software as Trojan horses:**

Beyond bait documents, Iranian operators disguise the malware as widely used legitimate applications: fake clones of Pictory, RunwayML, Norton Antivirus, Adobe Flash Player, and even KeePass -- the choice of a password manager as bait is not accidental, given the attackers' interest in the victim's credentials. An operational detail emerging from the advisory concerns the transition from corporate to personal devices: targets, often NGO professionals, newsroom staff, or human rights organizations, are convinced to continue the conversation on their private devices, outside the employer's IT security monitoring perimeter.

**Command and control: one Telegram bot per victim:**

The most characteristic element of the campaign remains the C2 architecture. Instead of managing dedicated servers that are easily identifiable and seizable, CHOSEN BRICK communicates exclusively through Telegram's public API, with a unique bot ID assigned to each victim. This approach ensures near-perfect operational segmentation: the compromise of a single setup does not expose the infrastructure used against other targets, and traffic to api.telegram.org blends into the background noise of a legitimate service used by billions of people. For exfiltrating larger files, operators rely on inexpensive, low-monitoring cloud storage providers like Backblaze B2, Vultr Object Storage, and Storj.

Once installed, CHOSEN BRICK provides operators with near-total access to the target's digital life:
- Theft of contacts, message history, and conversations from Telegram and WhatsApp
- Periodic screen capture
- Microphone activation for ambient audio recording
- Email and email content extraction
- File deletion and wiping capabilities to cover tracks
- Download and installation of additional payloads on request

The stated objective, according to NCSC, is to build a true "pattern of life" of the victim: schedules, frequent contacts, movements, professional relationships -- information that in the Iranian context can translate into direct physical consequences for the surveilled person and their contact network back home.

**Persistence and evasion techniques:**

On the technical side, the malware relies on classic but effective persistence mechanisms: Run registry keys in HKCU\Software\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run, with observed values like SMQDService and winappx (non-exclusive, so treat as weak indicators). For evasion, operators add custom exclusions to Windows Defender and register dedicated mutexes to prevent multiple executions that might alert the user or generate anomalous telemetry.

**Two lines for Western defenders:**

The coordination between NCSC, FBI and AIVD signals a step-change in the threat: it is no longer isolated incidents against individual Iranian activists in exile, but a systemic campaign affecting citizens and residents of at least three Western countries, often through their personal devices connected to corporate or institutional networks. Newsrooms, human rights NGOs, and organizations hosting dissidents should consider CHOSEN BRICK a concrete risk in their threat analysis, not a distant geopolitical problem outside their IT perimeter.

The three agencies' recommendations are pragmatic: distrust software received via link or attachment even from apparently trusted contacts, keep systems and antivirus updated, enable SmartScreen and equivalent protections on other OSes, and for organizations implement phishing-resistant MFA, application allowlisting, and active monitoring of endpoints and network traffic to anomalous cloud domains. Traffic to api.telegram.org from a corporate endpoint that should not use that service, or unexpected connections to backblazeb2.com, vultrobjects.com, and storjshare.io, warrant immediate investigation.

**Indicators of Compromise:**

Observed C2 and exfiltration domains/services:
- api.telegram.org (C2 channel, unique bot ID per victim)
- backblazeb2.com (file exfiltration)
- vultrobjects.com (file exfiltration)
- storjshare.io (file exfiltration)

Persistence registry keys (weak indicators, non-exclusive):
- HKCU\Software\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run\SMQDService
- HKCU\Software\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run\winappx

Known distribution vectors:
- Fake applications: Pictory, RunwayML, Norton Antivirus, Adobe Flash Player, KeePass
- Bait documents: falsified medical reports (e.g., MRI)
- Lure channel: WhatsApp, Telegram
