The Remus infostealer is stealing Gmail messages through a stealthy malicious Chrome extension, a capability the malware operators marketed as a zero-day exploit in Google's email service. On November 8, the Remus infostealer developer started advertising the alleged Gmail zero-day flaw that allowed the malware to collect emails even if victims changed their passwords and revoked all sessions. Threat intelligence company Hudson Rock investigated the claims but came to inconclusive results. The researchers noted that evidence from the malware customers matched the seller's pitch, although there was no technical proof that a zero-day vulnerability was being exploited. GenDigital and Invoke RE researchers published their independent findings yesterday, explaining that the advertised “zero-day” was just a malicious Chrome extension installed on the victims' browsers. The extension is designed to steal unread emails from signed-in Gmail accounts. It can also survive password changes and session revocations because it remains installed in the victim's Chrome browser. Its access to Gmail stops when the active session ends, and resumes again when the victim signs back in. GenDigital discovered the extension after reverse-engineering the Remus infostealer.
