A newly identified macOS infostealer , dubbed AmnesiaStealer, is using a polished ClickFix campaign to trick users into installing malware that steals credentials and silently commandeers active Chromium browser sessions. Jamf Threat Labs uncovered the operation after investigating a counterfeit GitHub download portal hosted at github.aoitour[.]com. The site closely imitates GitHub’s dark interface, Octocat branding, and “Verified Publisher” badge, giving visitors a convincing impression of legitimacy. Rather than providing a normal application download, the fake page presents a “Terminal installation” section with a one-click copy button. It instructs victims to open Terminal, paste a supplied command, press Return, and enter their macOS password. AmnesiaStealer macOS Malware Uses ClickFix This technique, known as ClickFix , shifts the final execution step to the user, bypassing many conventional warnings because the victim manually launches the malicious command. ClickFix has become increasingly common in macOS-focused social-engineering campaigns . Threat actors have previously used similar lures to distribute malware families, including Atomic Stealer (also known as AMOS) and MacSync. AmnesiaStealer demonstrates how attackers can reuse a familiar phishing template while replacing the backend infrastructure, malware payloads, and post-compromise objectives.
