Defense-industrial access can form before a conventional security workflow has a stable employee, device, or account to monitor. DPRK IT-worker schemes, suspected Iran-nexus recruitment operations, and supplier compromise remain distinct threats, but they expose the same defensive problem: workforce and delegated authority are often created from evidence scattered across teams that do not routinely compare records. AlphaHunt’s latest game-theory analysis maps the players, incentives, countermoves, and trust transitions behind that problem. It shows why stronger assurance belongs at high-consequence handoffs—not uniformly across every applicant—and gives defenders a practical authority register spanning HR, vendor management, payroll, assets, identity, endpoint, and remote access.
