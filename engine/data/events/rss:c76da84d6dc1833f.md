The Department of Commerce is rushing to put in place new export rules that prevent American companies from using third-party data center deals in other countries to evade restrictions on AI chip sales. On Monday, the Bureau of Industry and Security announced a 132-page interim final rule that limits the ability of American chipmakers to sell AI chips to third-party data centers abroad. The rule adds new restrictions on exports, reexports and transfers to overseas data centers in countries under U.S. arms embargoes. That list currently covers more than 20 nations, including China, Cuba, Iran, Libya, North Korea, and Sudan. “These restrictions will undermine the ability of companies headquartered in countries of concern from using U.S. advanced Super Intelligence (SI) semiconductors to train advanced SI models,” the final interim rule states. “Data centers, foreign foundries, and chip exporters are required to perform robust due diligence on their customers, including any direct or indirect ownership or control by persons [in those countries], as part of complying with this new control.” The rule also requires new licenses for sales of advanced AI chips to five other countries not on the arms embargo list: – Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has determined these countries present a “higher-than-normal risk” of chips being diverted to other parts of the world.
