Technische Universität Berlin researchers submitted the Hello Again paper on November 13, describing the first practical security analysis of Replay Protected Monotonic Counters in shipping x86 systems. They show that AMD's NV controller does not verify RPMC increments, allowing an interposer to suppress them unnoticed, and that Winbond SPI flash leaks its HMAC key under voltage glitching. On two AMD desktops without RPMC flash, reflashing an older image reset the firmware TPM lockout counters. On locked, encrypted hardware they recovered a four-digit Windows Hello PIN in 40 hours instead of 69 days and reduced the worst case for a default six-digit BitLocker TPM+PIN from 19 years to 20 days.
