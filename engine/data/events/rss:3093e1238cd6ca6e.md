Threat actors exploited several Mitel MiCollab vulnerabilities as zero-days in a data theft campaign, according to Accenture’s Apex Labs. CISA on Wednesday added five MiCollab flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog. In one attack observed by Accenture, hackers gained privileged access to a MiCollab server and quickly reached the targeted organization’s identity infrastructure, ultimately stealing sensitive business information. The MiCollab business collaboration platform enables employees to communicate through calls, messages, and virtual meetings. Accenture noted that the product’s frequent internet exposure and integration with Active Directory make it particularly attractive to hackers. Mitel on November 16 announced patches for five vulnerabilities affecting the product’s audio, web and video conferencing component, including arbitrary file upload, XML injection, unauthorized access, command injection, and authentication bypass. They can all be exploited by unauthenticated attackers, and two critical bugs allow arbitrary command execution. The security holes are tracked as CVE-2026-133275, CVE-2026-133303, CVE-2026-133311, CVE-2026-133323, and CVE-2026-133324. Accenture found evidence that the vulnerabilities were exploited in the wild before Mitel published its advisory.
