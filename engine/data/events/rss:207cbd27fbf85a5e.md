Facebook’s Cambridge Analytica scandal may feel like very old news . But the consequences are still arriving, and the latest comes with a $25 billion price tag. Reuters reports that a New Mexico judge has ordered Meta to pay $25 billion in civil penalties after a jury found that Facebook misled consumers about its data practices and its handling of hate speech and misinformation. Meta says it expects to win on appeal. This case is about more than information ending up in the wrong hands. It concerns what Facebook told people about who could access their information, how much control users had, and how the company enforced its own rules. What Facebook told its users In September, jurors found that the company and its leadership made 26 misleading statements on data privacy, hate speech, and other subjects. For example , Mark Zuckerberg’s 2010 statement that: “We do not share your personal information with people or services you don’t want.” That assurance appeared in a statement the jury found misleading. The judge found that choosing who could see a Facebook post did not necessarily control what outside app developers could access. That access was governed by separate settings that many users did not know existed. New Mexico’s attorney general filed the lawsuit in 2021, following revelations that Cambridge Analytica obtained personal information from as many as 87 million Facebook users through a third-party app. But New Mexico’s case went beyond Cambridge Analytica.
