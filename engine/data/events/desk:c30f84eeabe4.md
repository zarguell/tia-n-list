Let's Encrypt committed to Merkle Tree Certificates as its post-quantum path, targeting a staging MTC environment in late 2026 and production in 2027. The post quantifies the size problem: an ML-DSA-44 signature is roughly 2,420 bytes against 64 bytes for ECDSA P-256, a typical handshake carries five signatures and two public keys, and naive substitution would push handshakes past 10 kilobytes, citing Cloudflare research that a meaningful share of TLS connections then fail on real networks. It also cites NIST draft guidance that would disallow RSA-2048 and P-256 after 2035.
