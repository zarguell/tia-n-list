Britain has again delayed the launch of its National Cyber Action Plan, the government’s long-awaited strategy for defending the wider economy against state-backed and criminal hackers, according to multiple people familiar with the matter. The document, originally conceived as a national cyber strategy, was first due to be published by the end of 2025. After initial delays it was rebranded as an action plan and was close to publication in July this year before being postponed amid Keir Starmer’s resignation. Officials, who had planned to publish the document in mid-November, now aim to unveil it on December 9 according to several sources, speaking to Recorded Future News on condition of anonymity. The reasons for the latest delay are unclear. A government spokesperson declined to comment on the timing but said ministers remained committed to publishing the plan. The latest delay follows an influential cross-party House of Lords committee warning that the government “appears complacent” about “potentially catastrophic risks,” including cyberattacks from Russia, China and Iran. In a report published last week, the National Resilience Committee found Britain was dangerously underprepared for crises which could cascade across the economy, with disruption spilling over multiple sectors and disrupting the everyday lives of millions of people. Baroness Northover, a Liberal Democrat member of the committee, told Recorded Future News that the country could not afford more uncertainty
