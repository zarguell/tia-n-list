The Colorado Governor’s Office of Information Technology (OIT) has disclosed a data breach impacting data that residents uploaded to the myColorado application. Colorado’s official mobile application, myColorado is used for digital identification, state services, and document storage. It has approximately 2.1 million users. OIT took the application offline last week, after identifying unauthorized access to platform data, and immediately initiated incident response and an investigation into the incident. One week after the outage began, OIT said it discovered the incident after the attackers triggered the automated threat detection systems deployed within its Amazon Web Services (AWS) environment. The investigation has determined that the attackers accessed documents stored within the ‘myCards and Documents’ section of the application, which includes user-uploaded data, such as PDF files and photos of government IDs, vehicle registrations, and insurance cards. According to Colorado, approximately 100,000 individuals might have been affected, but no payment information was accessed. “Staff are working around the clock to match the inappropriately accessed documents to individual users. As that matching continues, OIT will further refine estimates on the number of people whose data was involved,” Colorado said.
