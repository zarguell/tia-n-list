Cybersecurity researcher targeted by criminals, but they picked the wrong adversary. Allison Nixon, research director at cyber investigation firm Unit 221B, has helped the FBI identify and arrest more than two dozen members of a loose anarchist hacker collective known as "The Com." In spring 2024, a person operating under the pseudonyms "Waifu" and "Judische" began posting death threats against Nixon on Telegram and Discord channels, including threats of garrote execution and beheading. AI-generated nude images of Nixon were also circulated.

The threats escalated because Nixon had become a direct threat to the group's operations. Working undercover in online chat channels under pseudonyms, she gathers intelligence on cybercriminals by monitoring their unguarded statements and operational details. Her work has contributed to the arrest of members of The Com, a youth-driven cybercrime community whose subgroups, including one called "764," have been linked to animal cruelty, knife attacks, and school shootings in addition to cryptocurrency theft, data theft, and extortion. Members have also engaged in physical violence against researchers tracking them, including "bricking" (throwing bricks through victims' windows) and swatting.

FBI Special Agent Ryan Brogan stated that Nixon's ability to unmask these individuals is unparalleled: "No matter how much effort you put into digital anonymity and camouflage, if you end up on Allison's and my radar, you're finished. It's only a matter of time." The article, written by journalist Kim Zetter for MIT Technology Review, details how Nixon began tracking these communities over a decade ago, using scraping tools to systematically search internet forums and leveraging 50 billion AT&T call records that played a key role in investigations. The criminal groups have increasingly adopted counterintelligence tactics against researchers.
