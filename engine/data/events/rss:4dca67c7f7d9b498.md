Spyware has been detected on devices used by some of Serbia’s most prominent figures connected to the country’s political upheaval, according to researchers who announced their findings Monday. Vladan Ðokić, the rector of the University of Belgrade and prominent figure in the country’s student protest moment, and Rade Bajić, a lawyer with Serbia’s Public Prosecutor’s Office for Organized Crime, were targeted, according to research from the SHARE Foundation, a Serbian digital rights group, and The Citizen Lab at the University of Toronto. The new cases follow what the SHARE Foundation described in September as the largest documented wave of spyware surveillance in the country to date. That wave included 14 people, among them a member of parliament and a local government official, targeted with Pegasus and a new version of the NoviSpy Android spyware. He has publicly backed the student-led anti-corruption protests that followed the 2024 Novi Sad railway station canopy collapse, and government officials and pro-government news outlets have portrayed him as a driving force behind the movement. His tenure has included direct confrontations with the government. In March, Serbian authorities raided his offices, and reportedly seized computers and servers.
