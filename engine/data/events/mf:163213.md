A lost or stolen laptop is a data breach only if the disk wasn’t encrypted which is why full-disk encryption is both a baseline control and a near-universal compliance requirement. The operating systems already include strong encryption (BitLocker on Windows, FileVault on macOS); the real question for most organizations is management: centrally enforcing encryption, escrowing recovery keys, proving compliance to auditors, and covering mixed fleets through comprehensive data security policies . This guide scores eight endpoint encryption options on five weighted criteria, then examines each in depth features, best fit, pros, and cons. It’s an editorial assessment to aid shortlisting, not a lab benchmark . Pricing is described by model only. Scoring Methodology Five criteria, weighted: Encryption strength & coverage (25%) FDE, removable, file/folder; Central management & key escrow (25%) ; Compliance reporting (20%) ; Cross-platform support (15%) ; Value & fit (15%) . Scores reflect fit for a shortlist, not a benchmark.
