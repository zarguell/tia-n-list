Kaspersky's consumer-facing post explains that attackers re-registered expired Retadup C2 names such as alphanoob and radnewage, allowing dormant USB copies of the 2017 worm to receive fresh commands. CoffeeLoader then uses graphics-card decryption to hinder sandbox analysis, adds itself to startup and blocks removal through the registry before fetching signed commands that can download infostealers or ransomware. The post reports about 15,000 blocked Retadup attempts in the first nine months of 2026, mainly in Peru, Angola and Russia.
