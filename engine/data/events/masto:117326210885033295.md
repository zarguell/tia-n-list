Data from CERT Polska shows telecom operators in Poland blocked almost 1.9 million malicious SMS messages in 2025: 1,883,610, more than 400,000 above the prior year, stopped using 790 patterns prepared by CERT Polska. Simulations run by security awareness vendor Nimblr across four Nordic markets show why the channel matters: the average click rate on links in simulated smishing messages was 29.4%, nearly seven times the 4.5% for phishing emails. Sweden and Denmark were highest at about 32%, Finland lowest at 18.5%, while email click rates on the same markets ranged from 4.3% to 5.6%.

Mundane messages worked best. A fake electronics store promotion in Sweden drew 69.4% clicks, a medicine-ready-for-pickup message in Denmark 58.3%, a free e-book offer 46.7%, a fake Amazon support notice 39.1%, and a suspicious bank transaction alert 32%. The pattern: a small plausible promise or a routine request beats alarming content, because it fits the recipient's daily rhythm and leaves little time to judge authenticity. Local branding and familiar scenarios add credibility.

Blocking improvements are reshaping attacker tradecraft. Since malicious links are filtered faster, messages increasingly aim only to provoke a reply, a phone call, or a move to a messenger app, with the actual credential or payment theft happening in that later contact. Researchers at Hunt.io documented a 2026 smishing campaign spanning 19 countries, impersonating public institutions, postal operators, and telecoms, with 1,628 malicious URLs tied to a single operation and fake pages localized per country. Recommendations: verify any message through independently sourced contact details, and in Poland forward suspicious SMS to 8080 run by CERT Polska. Source data: Nimblr Smishing Report 2026.
