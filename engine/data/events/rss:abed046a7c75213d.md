Investing heavily in cybersecurity is a positive trend observed across the EU. Companies implement SIEM, EDR, threat intelligence and dedicated SOC teams. Everything seems to be in place, the budgets have been spent. But what does it take for security leaders to conclude that these investments are well-spent and keep their organization protected? With the EU’s NIS2 Directive putting greater emphasis on effective cybersecurity risk management, this question carries more weight than ever. Key Takeaways As the EU’s NIS2 Directive raises expectations for cybersecurity risk management, security leaders need to look beyond the solutions they have deployed and evaluate how effectively they work together. Having a SIEM, EDR, threat intelligence providers, and a dedicated SOC does not automatically mean your organization has complete visibility into cyber threats. Independent threat intelligence and behavioral analysis can help identify potential blind spots, strengthen existing security controls, and support more defensible security decisions.
