Frontline Education is notifying over 1.3 million people that their personal information was compromised in a data breach. The incident occurred on August 14 and involved the exploitation of a vulnerability in a third-party software product to gain unauthorized access to Frontline’s environment. Frontline, which provides administration software purpose-built for K-12 school districts, addressed the security defect and reinforced security. The attack was fully contained by August 15, the company says in an incident notice . The investigation into the data breach determined that the hackers stole the personal information of both school district staff and students from the Frontline Central and School Health Management products. While the stolen data varies by individual, it may include names, Social Security numbers, dates of birth, driver’s license numbers, passport and other government ID numbers, health insurance information such as Medicaid IDs, and Student IDs. “Frontline has notified affected school districts and, in cooperation with those districts, has begun notifying individuals whose personal information was affected via email or by letter sent via the US Postal Service, as required and where available,” the company says.
