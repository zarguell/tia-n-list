First seen in July 2025, the group has already listed more than 80 organizations on its leak site, known as the DeadLock blog . More than half of the claimed victims are located in Europe, but attacks have also affected organizations across Asia, North America, South America, and Africa. Targeted sectors include IT, mining, manufacturing, transportation, logistics, hospitality, and consumer goods. DeadLock has also been linked to multiple cybercriminal groups, including an affiliate associated with the Lynx and INC ransomware ecosystems. The operation follows the familiar double-extortion model: attackers first steal sensitive information, then lock files and use the threat of a public data leak to increase pressure on victims. What makes DeadLock notable is its decentralized infrastructure. Instead of depending entirely on traditional websites and servers that law enforcement or hosting providers can disrupt, the ransomware uses blockchain-backed services and the Session messaging network to support negotiation, leak publication, and victim communication. DeadLock Steals Encrypts Extorts The DeadLock encryptor is written in Rust and begins by decoding an embedded configuration file with an XOR key . This configuration includes the victim ID, attacker public key, lists of processes and services to stop, excluded files and directories, ransom-note content, and geofencing settings.
