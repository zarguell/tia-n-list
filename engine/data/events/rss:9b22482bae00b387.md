The Container Store on Friday said it is recovering from a cyberattack that forced it to shut down its online ordering systems and limited in-store transaction processing. The firm’s parent company, Neighborhood Intelligence, said in a statement that The Container Store has “restored its website and systems following a cybersecurity incident this past weekend.” Neighborhood Intelligence — a rebrand of Bed Bath & Beyond — purchased The Container Store in July for $150 million. While the stores remained open throughout the week, they had to use “alternative transaction-processing procedures,” according to the statement. The Texas-founded company has about 100 stores across the U.S. Neighborhood Intelligence said the incident only impacted The Container Store and did not affect its other brands including Bed Bath & Beyond, Overstock, buybuy BABY or Kirkland’s. Neighborhood Intelligence reported $509 million in revenue for the third quarter earlier this month, with the increases driven primarily by the acquisition of The Container Store. The company said it hired cybersecurity experts to contain the incident and restore the affected systems. So far, it does not appear the hackers stole customer financial data, but did not address claims made by the cybercriminal group n0n, which listed the company on its leak site earlier this month.
