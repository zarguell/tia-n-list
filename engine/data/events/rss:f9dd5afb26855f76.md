Applebee’s has fixed a security flaw that exposed customers’ personal information after a researcher discovered a database password embedded in the restaurant chain’s Android app. The researcher, Robert Wiggins, found the password with help from AI . It gave him access to an internal Applebee’s customer database. A password written directly into an app’s code or configuration is known as a hardcoded credential. Anyone who extracts it may be able to use it to access the system. The exposed database contained: Names, email addresses, and email-verification status Dates of birth, phone numbers, and ZIP codes Veteran status Account-creation dates and login counts IP addresses from customers’ most recent logins, which can indicate their approximate location TechCrunch independently checked Wiggins’ findings. It created an Applebee’s account with dummy information, which Wiggins successfully retrieved from the database. According to TechCrunch, Wiggins could not find a suitable way to report the issue to Applebee’s or its parent company, Dine Brands, so he asked the publication to help contact them. TechCrunch held its story until the fix was confirmed. Applebee’s confirmed on October 29 that it had addressed the issue. It’s unclear how many customers’ records were in the database. Dine Brands says it has not found evidence of unauthorized access, but its investigation is ongoing.
