A joint U.S. and South Korean government advisory has warned that the Gunra ransomware operation is exploiting vulnerable Fortinet VPN appliances, weak remote-access controls, and exposed infrastructure to enter enterprise networks. The ransomware-as-a-service, or RaaS, group has targeted organizations across government, healthcare, finance, manufacturing, transportation, utilities, education, media, and retail. Gunra first appeared in April 2025 and is believed to be derived from the leaked Conti ransomware source code. By early 2026, the operators had expanded into a structured affiliate program, advertising a ransomware builder, management panel, cross-platform payloads, and documentation on dark web forums. The group follows a double-extortion model. Its affiliates steal sensitive information before encrypting files, then threaten to publish or sell the data through a dedicated leak site if the victim refuses to pay. Negotiations are conducted through a Tor-based portal and the qTox encrypted messaging application, often with victims given five to seven days to respond.
