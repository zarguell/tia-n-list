When discussing IT infrastructure security, the focus is usually on firewalls, EDR, backup, vulnerability management, and network segmentation. However, all of them are effective only under one condition — if the infrastructure itself is under control. Incidents often begin not with the exploitation of a vulnerability or bypassing protection systems, but with shortcomings in basic infrastructure management. Every virtual machine, server, access, and IP address must have an owner, a lifetime, and a controlled path for creation and deletion. When management processes are opaque, the infrastructure itself becomes a source of information security risks, and security tools have to operate over an unknown environment and merely contain the consequences. This principle is equally critical for enterprise companies and service providers — regardless of whether the platform serves internal services, development teams, or external customers, a loss of control over administration can lead to the compromise of the entire environment. In modern IT infrastructure, hundreds of systems run on the same resources, and the environment itself is constantly changing — dozens of services are created, scaled, and deleted daily. In such conditions, provisioning comes to the fore. Deploying a new virtual machine without a regulation and recording the owner leaves "forgotten" VMs in the environment.
