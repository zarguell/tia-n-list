Even OpenAI is admitting there is a lot of AI slop in the security space. An executive for the frontier AI company said Tuesday that AI -generated vulnerability reports are overwhelming open-source maintainers, leading some to stop accepting submissions as the workload exceeds their capacity to review them. Michael Aiello, OpenAI’s cyber executive in residence, described the problem during a question-and-answer session Tuesday at the DataTribe Challenge in Washington, D.C., when he was asked how he viewed OpenAI ’s Patch the Planet program versus open-source maintainers banning AI-based submissions because of concerns about quality and clarity. “It’s an ecosystem problem where that [maintainer] is just not going to respond to hundreds and hundreds of AI-submitted vulnerabilities,” Aiello said. He contrasted the volume against his company’s own program, which partners with security research organizations to identify vulnerabilities, develop fixes and help bring them into the affected projects. According to the company’s website , 661 patches have been developed and tested, with 458 of them accepted by maintainers for inclusion in the projects they oversee. However, Aiello said those involved with Daybreak often coordinate with maintainers, as opposed to just sending a message into GitHub and hoping for the best.
