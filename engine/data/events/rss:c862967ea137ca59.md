AI can help you send more job applications. But when you lose track of where you’ve applied, a fake recruiter’s message can start to look like the reply you’ve been waiting for. Job hunting can feel like shouting into the void. You send applications, rewrite cover letters, and wait for replies that may never arrive. Then a message lands on your phone: someone wants to talk about a job. But was it one of the jobs you applied for? It is hard to keep track when you’ve sent out a lot of them. Two reports highlight an uncomfortable overlap. The BBC describes jobseekers using AI to apply for large numbers of roles, a strategy known as “spray and pray.” Meanwhile, research reported by The Independent suggests many young adults are receiving unsolicited job offers and worry that ignoring those offers could mean missing a real opportunity. Neither report establishes that mass applications cause people to fall for recruitment scams. But together, they point to a useful security question: When you’re expecting replies from practically everywhere, how do you recognize a message from someone you never contacted?
