The US cybersecurity agency CISA has added the Brotli vulnerability known as Node2Shell to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog. Separately, two security firms say they have recorded exploitation attempts against their honeypots. Tracked as CVE-2026-111497 and also known as HTTP2Shell and Broccoli2Shell, the flaw is an out-of-bounds write in the decoder of Brotli, the data compression library developed by Google. It impacts Brotli versions 1.0.3 through 1.2.0. With specially crafted compressed data, an attacker could crash the application, expose the contents of its memory, and potentially execute arbitrary code. The bug was reported by researchers at OpenAI, and Node.js and Google released fixes on October 21. Microsoft, the maintainers of two Python packages, Bun, Envoy and Istio have also released fixes, and Palo Alto Networks is rolling out hotfixes for PAN-OS. Imperva has demonstrated that the vulnerability can be exploited for code execution on an official Node.js image. CISA added CVE-2026-111497 to the KEV catalog on October 27, but provided no information on the attacks. Federal civilian agencies have been instructed to address the flaw by November 10. Previdian, which monitors exploitation activity through its own network of sensors, says it recorded one exploitation attempt targeting CVE-2026-111497 on October 27, coming from a US-based IP address.
