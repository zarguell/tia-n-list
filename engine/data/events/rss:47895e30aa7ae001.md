An attacker reportedly used a leaked password to abuse a company’s legitimate access to Denmark’s population register (CPR). The weak password is remarkable, but the failure to spot millions of unusual searches made the breach worse. The CPR administration states it identified a serious security incident. By abusing a Danish company’s authorized access to the CPR system, unauthorized people gained access to the names, addresses, CPR numbers, and other information belonging to around 8.8 million people registered in the system. CPR numbers are Denmark’s personal identification numbers. How could a password so weak that it should have been rejected by the system open the door to so much personal information ? The attacker reportedly gained access through Pays ApS, an IT company based in Odense. According to TV 2 , Pays has confirmed that its authorized access to search the CPR system was abused. Pays offers services including data updates and “CPR cleansing,” allowing customers to check records against the register to update addresses, identify people who have died, and obtain information about changes of address. The attacker reportedly used a leaked password associated with a former Pays employee. TV 2 says an email containing the password 123456 had appeared in a larger data leak, and that this account of the initial access matches its own research.
