Intro Recent security incidents revealed that malicious actors successfully compromised three ccTLD registries — specifically targeting .gh for Ghana, .sl for Sierra Leone, and .as for American Samoa — to fraudulently acquire HTTPS certificates for Google and other global entities. By manipulating authoritative DNS records , the attackers bypassed standard domain-control verifications without actually breaching the internal infrastructure of the targeted organizations. In response to these breaches, Google utilized its Chrome browser mechanisms to swiftly block the unauthorized certificates and collaborated with certificate authorities to revoke them. Furthermore, the discovery highlighted broader vulnerabilities in web security where DNS infrastructure compromises undermine HTTPS trust , prompting experts to advise stricter certificate monitoring and enhanced authorization policies. Ultimately, the episode demonstrates that securing domain name systems and tracking transparency logs are critical measures for preventing future unauthorized certificate generation across the global web ecosystem. ccTLD Registry Hijacking Enterprise reliance on external country-code top-level domain (ccTLD) registry infrastructure represents a critical third-party trust dependency within modern digital architecture.
