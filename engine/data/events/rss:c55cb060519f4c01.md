It would be a “betrayal of the democratic process” if Congress included a straightforward renewal of a controversial lapsed surveillance law in the annual defense policy bill, a collection of civil liberties groups wrote to Hill leaders Thursday. Lawmakers weren’t able to agree this summer to a long-term extension of Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act, under which the government can use Americans’ personal information without a warrant to search for their communications with foreign targets. A court has authorized such searches through March despite the law’s lapse. Congress in 2023 authorized a short-term extension of Section 702 through the annual, “must-pass” National Defense Authorization Act in a move protested by civil liberties groups and some lawmakers at the time.
