Original Zenity Labs disclosure of the SalesBleed chain, published one day before the story's first coverage. A hidden prompt injection in a lead submitted through the public Web-to-Lead endpoint stays dormant until an employee asks Agentforce to review the newest lead, then steers the General CRM subagent's Query Records tool from Leads into Accounts and leaks values through an HTML img src tag whose hostname encodes the data in a DNS query to the attacker's authoritative nameserver. The Trusted URLs redaction layer was bypassed by combining a fixed TLD allowlist that missed .fun with termination characters (curly braces, square brackets) that the redactor and the chat renderer parsed differently, and redaction ran after the agent generated its output. Zenity reported the chain to Salesforce on June 1, 2026, confirmed the fix on August 19, 2026, and notes a DNS label caps at 63 characters and a full name at 253, so data leaves in continuous bleeding queries that survive HTTP egress controls.
