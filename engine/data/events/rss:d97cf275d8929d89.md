French cooperative bank Crédit Mutuel Arkéa was targeted in a cyberattack that enabled fraudulent transactions of up to $133 million, forcing it to respond by suspending certain systems. The bank says that the attacker used its own account to initiate transactions to other banking organizations, but customer assets remain unaffected. Crédit Mutuel Arkéa is a major French banking group with more than 5.8 million members and 211.5 billion euros in assets. Reports about the incident emerged on November 2, initially from blockchain fraud investigator ZachXBT, who warned of a large-scale unauthorized withdrawal from multiple centralized exchange accounts occurring a day earlier. According to the investigator, the attacker may have stolen up to 118 million euros ($133 million) and withdrawn it as USDC and USDT stablecoins. At the time, ZachXBT did not name the entity but said that the lack of public disclosure allowed the attacker to launder $30 million by November 3, which EU law enforcement would otherwise have frozen. Crédit Mutuel Arkéa disclosed the incident on November 4, assuring customers that it “is now closed and under control” and that operations are gradually returning to normal. “Crédit Mutuel Arkéa wishes to reassure all its clients - across its federations and subsidiaries - that this incident has no impact on their assets; it has merely resulted in a delay in the execution of certain transfer transactions,” [machine translated] the bank stated .
