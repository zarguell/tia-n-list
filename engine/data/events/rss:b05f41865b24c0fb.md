Prince of Persia, a threat group sponsored by the Iranian government, ran one of its command-and-control (C&C) servers from the United States this year, after a months-long lull in its operations, according to Semperis. The APT actor, also tracked as Infy, has been operating for close to 20 years. Its targets are mostly Iranian dissidents, along with government and private entities in the US, Europe, and the Middle East. Tomer Bar, AVP of security research at Semperis, tracked the group in his previous role at SafeBreach. He says that within a week of SafeBreach’s February 2026 report , the hackers took a C&C server offline and later relocated the rest of their servers. Prince of Persia then halted operations for three months, a longer silence than any other in its nearly two-decade history. The pause overlapped with the war between Iran and the US and Israel , which broke out on February 28. Semperis told SecurityWeek that this is the longest downtime it has confirmed, as its researchers were monitoring the group’s infrastructure the entire time. Earlier quiet periods reflected gaps in researchers’ visibility into the group’s operations rather than confirmed inactivity. Prince of Persia’s operations restarted on May 27 with the deployment of a new C&C server, and data theft resumed.
