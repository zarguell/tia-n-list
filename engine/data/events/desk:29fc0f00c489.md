Group-IB identified a previously undocumented kit it calls MadApe, delivered through PDF attachments that impersonate SharePoint file-sharing notices and refer to payments, invoices and contracts. The pages present a dynamically supplied device code, copy it to the clipboard on click, and send the victim to the genuine microsoft.com/devicelogin portal; backend status tracking uses paths such as /api/code/status/ followed by a numeric ID and /api/code/entered. Group-IB found 155 root domains, with phishing components on 97, and says 117 of the 154 domains registered in 2026 were registered between mid-June and late August, 133 through one registrar. Lures appeared in at least nine languages, the earliest operator panel was seen on August 19, and the infrastructure counts are not victim counts.
