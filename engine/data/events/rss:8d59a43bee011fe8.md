When Nick Woodhead spoke with Business Insider on October 23 , he had been awake for nearly 48 hours responding to critical PHP vulnerabilities. The CTO of Rothco and Compubox was helping his teams update systems, review logs for signs of compromise, and protect older installations while they waited for backported fixes. His organization already had infrastructure for deploying patches, yet getting through the response still required substantial manual effort. This experience will be familiar to anyone responsible for securing a portfolio of applications: when a critical vulnerability is disclosed, teams must establish where it exists, identify who owns the affected systems, and work out how to make and verify the necessary changes. Older systems can further add to the workload because teams may need to obtain a backported fix or upgrade to a supported version, then test whether existing application code and dependencies still work. Coordinating those changes across connected applications takes time, especially when testing capacity is limited and services need to remain available.
