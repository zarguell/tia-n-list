CrowdStrike said Monday it agreed to acquire Zscaler in an all-stock deal valued at about $43.9 billion, giving the cybersecurity company a network security business alongside its software for protecting devices and cloud applications. The acquisition would bring two kinds of security under one owner: tools that monitor what happens on a device or in a cloud application, and tools that control what those systems can connect to. That distinction matters as employees, applications and artificial intelligence agents connect directly to cloud services without necessarily routing traffic through a company data center. Zscaler’s approach, known as zero trust, checks whether a user or program is authorized to access a specific resource rather than trusting it based on where it connects from. “The network used to connect people to places. Now it connects agents to data,” CrowdStrike founder and CEO George Kurtz said in the announcement. Daniel Bernard, CrowdStrike’s chief business officer, said in an interview that “this isn’t a shotgun wedding” and the companies’ years of working together gave them a view of how customers were already using their products to secure AI agents. “The network really starts on the endpoint, and that’s the genesis of how we view this transaction,” Bernard said.
