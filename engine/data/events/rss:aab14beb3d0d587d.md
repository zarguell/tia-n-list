A cavalcade of tech outages is underscoring the need to build systems with resilience and outcomes in mind — regardless of whether it’s to withstand a cyberattack or something else, the head of the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency said Tuesday. Acting director Nick Andersen said “it’s been a highly disruptive last couple of days” marked by flight ground stops , the Microsoft Outlook global outage and banking slowdowns , but that “I do think it’s a it’s a good illustration of the types of things we’ve been talking about over the course of the last year or so.” “Ultimately being able to prevail by being able to demonstrate resilience against disruptions, whether they occur because of things like an IT outage or a bad patch, or a modernization update gone awry, or because of a malicious malicious cyber incident — we want to make sure we’re engineering in the resilience that is going to be able to anticipate for and ultimately overcome those outages and those impacts,” Andersen said, speaking at FedTalks, presented by FedScoop. The cause matters less than trying to think about what the final picture ought to be, he said. “At the end of the day, people just care about the outcomes,” Andersen said. “They care about clean drinking water continuing to flow into their houses. … They care about groceries being on grocery store shelves.
