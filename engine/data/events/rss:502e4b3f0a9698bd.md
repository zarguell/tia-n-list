The Treasury Department’s Financial Crimes Enforcement Network is investigating a cybersecurity incident in which an unauthorized party gained access to a limited subset of its systems, the agency said in a notice posted to its Bank Secrecy Act filing portal. FinCEN, the federal government’s financial intelligence unit, said it took some systems offline as a precaution while the investigation continues. The agency did not say which systems were affected, how the intrusion occurred, how long it lasted or who was behind it. The BSA E-Filing System, the portal at the center of the disruption, is FinCEN’s web-based platform for submitting reports required under the Bank Secrecy Act, the 1970 law that forms the backbone of U.S. anti-money-laundering policy. Banks, credit unions, brokerages, casinos and other financial institutions use it to transmit suspicious activity reports, currency transaction reports and related filings electronically through a secure network. FinCEN said the filing website itself remains operational, though some services may be temporarily unavailable. The agency said it is extending deadlines for filers affected by the disruption. CyberScoop has reached out to the Treasury for additional information. The agency also said its Financial Institutions Hotline is unaffected by the disruption and remains available to financial institutions that need to expedite delivery of suspicious activity information to law enforcement.
