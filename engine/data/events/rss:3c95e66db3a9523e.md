Google said Monday that Russian-speaking hackers carried out what its analysts believe is the first cyberattack run end-to-end by artificial intelligence, without a human in the loop at any stage of the intrusion. The operation appears in a quarterly report from Google’s Threat Intelligence Group and its Mandiant incident response unit. Researchers stopped short of naming a group or state. Their attribution rests on Russian-language comments in the operation’s code, including a configuration file instructing its user to enter API keys for “auto-exploitation” there rather than in a chat. Victim data on the staging server pointed to targeting of French websites in the media, entertainment and education sectors. “Given the low barrier to entry, ease of integrating new exploits, and massive scaling potential, we believe it is highly likely that threat actors will continue to use these frameworks in campaigns targeting organizations globally,” the report reads. The centerpiece is a system the researchers dubbed “exploit_agent.” It chains together multiple AI agents with frontier and locally hosted language models to continuously compare code in widely used software before and after vendors publish patches. When a fix appears, the system infers what the change reveals, drafts an exploit and executes it automatically.
