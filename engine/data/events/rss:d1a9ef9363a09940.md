Artificial Intelligence technologies have made a huge impact on the security industry. But as with any public technology, adversaries have access to AI as well. They’re wasting no time incorporating AI into their malicious workflows and ransomware operators have been able to leverage it - unfortunately, this has led to some of their successes.Fortunately, there is plenty of potential for defenders to leverage similar AI technologies to improve their own game, neutralizing the adversaries’ advantage to keep users and resources safe.This blog post covers adversaries’ uses of Generative AI (GenAI) for both social engineering pretexts and technical development, as well as how defenders can spot and mitigate those efforts. For the full story, be sure to check out the complete analysis in the Zscaler ThreatLabz 2026 Ransomware Report. Key TakeawaysRansomware attacks continue to be a major driver of cybercrime incidents and attacker’s corresponding financial gains. While there are numerous means of compromising a victim, ransomware threat actors typically rely on social engineering as their first step toward compromising a target. The more believable a social engineering ruse, the more likely it will be successful. Attackers are already using GenAI to help overcome some of their traditional shortcomings.After they gain an initial foothold, time is of the essence.
