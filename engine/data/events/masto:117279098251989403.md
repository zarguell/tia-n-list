The hacker asked Revolut for user data. And Revolut handed it over. Fintech company Revolut has confirmed a data breach caused by attackers who sent fake government requests via email. Because the emails came from a legitimate government domain, Revolut employees treated them as authentic and disclosed the requested information.

According to TechCrunch, which obtained Revolut's breach notification, the disclosed data included dates of birth, postal and email addresses, phone numbers, and copies of identity documents including passports and driver's licenses. The attackers may also have obtained selfies used for identity verification, bank statements, and transaction history.

Revolut said the incident affected a "limited" number of customers and all affected users were contacted directly. The company stated that its internal systems were not compromised and customer funds remain secure. Blockchain analyst ZachXBT published Revolut's notification and suggested the attackers specifically targeted high-net-worth users. Revolut currently serves over 80 million customers worldwide and holds banking licenses in more than 30 countries.
