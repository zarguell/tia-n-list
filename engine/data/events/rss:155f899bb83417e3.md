Frontier AI labs are very clear about their responsibilities: making the model more robust, better guarded, and harder to hijack. They are also clear about what they can’t do, which is understand the business context of an action. An agent deleting files from Google Drive could be malicious or perfectly normal. They can't tell. That raises questions about the shared responsibility model for Agentic AI. In cloud security, there is a clear separation of responsibilities between cloud vendors and their customers. If you copy data from AWS into SFDC, each vendor is responsible for the data's infrastructure security while they have it, but the customer is responsible for the action. The AWS Shared Responsibility Model is a classic example. The separation isn’t nearly as clean for AI agents. Agents act as intermediaries between you and the request, and that intermediary isn't deterministic by nature. It sends authenticated, authorized requests that can be compromised or rogue. The first scenario is somewhat clear and goes back to what the AI labs try to prevent: a rogue agent has either misunderstood something or acted in a way it shouldn’t. A human mistake, only not human and not necessarily a mistake.
