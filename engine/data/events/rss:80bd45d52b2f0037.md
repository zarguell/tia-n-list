Michael LoSapio, the Palantir executive and Trump administration nominee for Director of the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, struck a combative tone with Democrats on the Homeland Security and Government Affairs Committee and took sides in several partisan disputes that are likely to complicate his path to confirmation. LoSapio’s hearing lasted less than an hour, far less time than Congress has spent scrutinizing previous CISA nominees, as he was considered alongside a large tranche of other Trump administration nominees that Republicans are attempting to move through Congress during the lame duck session. CISA has lacked a confirmed director for the first two years of the Trump administration, and policymakers have fresh concerns about cyber insecurity in U.S. critical infrastructure, artificial intelligence and other threats. Despite that, LoSapio and senators on the committee spent little time discussing the specifics of his vision for the federal government’s primary defensive cybersecurity agency.
