Adding 10 new clients sounds like exactly the kind of problem an MSSP wants to have. More revenue, more accounts, more room to grow. But growth also brings more alerts, suspicious files and URLs, investigations, escalations, reports, and SLA commitments. And if too much of that work still depends on manual analyst effort, those 10 new clients can put pressure on the SOC faster than expected. That is usually where the cracks start to show: analysts spend more time on each case, escalations pile up, and keeping the same level of service gets harder. 1. Alert Volume Grows Faster Than Analyst Capacity The first pressure usually shows up in the investigation queue. Every new client adds another stream of alerts to the SOC. Some are easy to dismiss, while others send analysts into a longer process: opening files, checking URLs, following redirects, collecting IOCs, and figuring out whether the activity is actually malicious. The workload grows especially fast when analysts have to keep stepping in just to move an investigation forward.
