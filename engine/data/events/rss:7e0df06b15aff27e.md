*SOCFortress technical research · October 2026 · Synthetic test data* Executive summary Can a fast, structured AI decision model provide a useful maliciousness score for endpoint telemetry? We evaluated TypeSafe Jev 1.13.0 using a Python harness and synthetic Windows Sysmon-style JSON events. Across three experiments — 12 baseline events, 12 matched A/B pairs (24 event submissions), and eight contextual triplets (24 submissions) — Jev demonstrated coherent responses to suspicious event attributes and corroborating context. The key findings are 11 of 12 A/B pairs increased in score when a single attribute was changed to a more suspicious variant; the remaining pair (process integrity level) was unchanged. All eight contextual triplets followed the hypothesized ordering: verified-legitimate context not estimates of detection accuracy or proof of production readiness. Why this matters Traditional detection rules identify behaviours or indicators, but an alert’s priority often depends on surrounding evidence: process ancestry, command line, reputation, asset role, and whether activity is approved. Jev offers typed decision outputs rather than narrative prose. We tested a five-level maliciousness rubric (0–4), normalized to 0–100 for readability, alongside activity classification and a separate investigation signal. A normalized score of 80 does not mean an 80% probability of maliciousness . Methodology and safeguards All samples were synthetic. The same rubric was used throughout
