Cybersecurity company Radware says attackers gained access to some of its customers' account details and cloud access keys through an unsecured internal Cloud WAF (Web Application Firewall) management API that was left exposed online. Founded in 1996 and headquartered in Tel Aviv, Radware now provides application delivery and DDoS protection services to more than 12,500 enterprise and telecom carrier customers worldwide. In a root cause analysis report shared by an anonymous source with BleepingComputer, Radware says it blocked the originating IP address and access to the internal management API. The company also revoked and replaced all internal account credentials after detecting the breach on Thursday, ten hours after the initial unauthorized API access. "On 19 November 2026, an unauthorized party accessed an internal-only Cloud WAF management API that was reachable from the Public Internet without requiring authentication," Radware said. "The confirmed information potentially accessed includes customer contact details, and for a subset of customers, the credentials configured for log export into their self-managed storage (AWS S3, Azure Blob Storage, or SFTP)." The company added that the breach did not expose WAF traffic inspection, certificates, end-users’ data, customer application traffic, or cloud DDoS services and data.
