State-sponsored and cybercrime groups are increasingly adopting generative AI, including multi-agent offensive frameworks, to increase the speed and scale of their operations, Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) reports. For the first time, GTIG has identified threat actors using an AI framework to automate both vulnerability discovery and exploitation without human intervention. The multi-agent framework, called exploit_agent, was discovered on an exposed staging server. It was designed to compare patched and unpatched web application code, identify vulnerabilities, develop exploits, and execute them automatically. The server also contained data stolen from victims and scripts for post-compromise activities, including reconnaissance and webshell deployment. According to GTIG, evidence pointed to an interest in French media and entertainment websites, as well as the education sector. Attackers are using frameworks such as Artex, Cairn, CyberStrikeAI, and PentAGI to increase the velocity of their multi-stage intrusions, and rely on LLMs and agentic frameworks to reverse engineer patches at machine speed and rapidly weaponize n-day vulnerabilities. “Threat actor adoption of these frameworks represents a paradigm shift towards intrusions occurring at machine speed, with the automated chaining of complex attack paths scaling operations significantly,” GTIG notes in its latest AI threat tracker report.
