Satellite imagery obtained by Bellingcat lays bare the extent of tree-felling at a Washington DC golf course that President Trump is reportedly seeking to transform into a championship-level course, despite moves to protect the site by conservationists. The extent and circumstances surrounding the rapid changes prompted a judge to call an emergency hearing earlier this week, banning any further changes until later this month. Reports on Wednesday detailed that 430 trees were cut down at the East Potomac Golf Links which is owned by the Federal government and managed by the National Park Service (NPS) . This is despite a judge’s order issued earlier this year that required court approval to cut down more than 10 trees at the course. U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes was reported to have said that the government’s actions had cost it “all of its credibility with me in this case.” She is also reported to have said she was “ furious ” with the Justice Department for the lack of time afforded the court and plaintiffs before the tree removal was carried out. NPR reported that officials notified the court just before 5pm on Friday Oct. 9 of plans to remove up to 529 trees in phases starting the very next day on Saturday Oct. 10. That prompted Reyes to call an emergency meeting on Oct. 12. By that time, 430 trees had reportedly been cut down.
