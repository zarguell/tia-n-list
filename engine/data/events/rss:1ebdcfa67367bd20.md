In the context of AI-assisted vulnerability discovery and exploitation, Google Project Zero is significantly shrinking bug disclosure windows. So far, Project Zero’s policy has been a well-known one: vendors were given 90 days to patch vulnerabilities before public disclosure and granted a 30-day grace period for patch roll-out. Starting November 13, that grace period is no longer in the books, Project Zero security engineer Natalie Silvanovich announced . Under the new ‘Full X’ policy, Project Zero will publish vulnerability details 90 days after reporting them to the vendor, regardless of whether, or when, a fix is released. Beginning February 2, 2027, Project Zero will disclose vulnerabilities publicly after 60 days, and the disclosure window will shrink to 30 days on January 3, 2028. Furthermore, Project Zero is removing most ‘special cases’ from its disclosure policy, except for security defects actively exploited in the wild (which have a 7-day disclosure window, per Google’s policy ) and flaws already made public by others. If a vendor requests it, Project Zero may publish details on a security hole ahead of the deadline, Silvanovich says. However, serious vulnerabilities may be granted exceptions. Project Zero has done it on rare occasions, such as Spectre and Meltdown, and that will not change.
