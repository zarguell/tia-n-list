WhatsApp has confirmed that more than 1 billion people now use passkeys to access the messaging platform, marking one of the largest passwordless authentication rollouts in consumer technology. The milestone coincides with broader account-security improvements, including a replacement for the platform’s six-digit two-step-verification PIN and new caller-context signals intended to reduce fraud. Passkeys enable WhatsApp users to authenticate through a device-bound credential rather than a reusable password or code. Users can approve access using a fingerprint sensor, Face ID, or the device’s screen-lock method. WhatsApp Passkeys Now Protect More Than 1 Billion Users The feature is available through Settings > Account > Passkeys, where users can add and review passkeys associated with their account. The security advantage stems from the underlying cryptographic design. A passkey uses a private key stored and protected on the user’s device, while authentication relies on a corresponding public key held by the service. Because there is no reusable secret for a victim to type into a phishing page or disclose to a scammer, passkeys are more resistant to credential theft, phishing, credential stuffing , and some SIM-swap-enabled takeover scenarios. WhatsApp is also expanding passkey support for people who use both Android and iPhone devices.
