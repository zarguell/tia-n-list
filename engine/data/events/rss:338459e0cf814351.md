The new space race isn't just rockets and lunar bases. From the 2022 Viasat attack to hacktivists blinding Iran's tanker fleet, cyberattacks on ground stations, vendors, and terminals are a serious threat. As Earth’s orbit fills with satellites and possibly even AI data centers, securing space matters as much as reaching it. Background There were 329 orbital launch attempts in 2025, up from 263 the year before. In the same year, a record 4,517 satellites were placed in orbit. 87% of these satellites were commercially owned ; they are part of a space economy that reached $686 billion in 2025 and is set to skyrocket further. In April 2026, Artemis II completed a crewed lunar flyby, the first humans beyond low Earth orbit since 1972. In March, NASA paused the Gateway station and pivoted to building a lunar base instead. Beijing expects taikonauts on the Moon's surface by 2030 and a functioning International Lunar Research Station by 2035. The first space race ended with the Cold War, but now that geopolitical competition is escalating, the second space race has begun , and cybersecurity will play a vital role in the outcomes. This is because thousands of new satellites are computers running software written by people. So is every lander, rover, and relay that follows them to the Moon.
