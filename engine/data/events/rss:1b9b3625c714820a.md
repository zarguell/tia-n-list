For agencies responsible for protecting Criminal Justice Information (CJI), meeting CJIS requirements means more than putting security controls in place. Those controls need to work across the systems staff use every day, and agencies need to be able to prove they're effective. But, between legacy systems, stretched IT teams, and constantly changing access needs, even agencies with strong security practices can struggle to apply controls consistently and keep the evidence auditors expect. So, where should you focus your efforts? In this article, we'll walk through five key areas agencies should be paying attention to under CJIS v6.1 , with practical advice to make compliance easier to manage. CJIS v6.1 requires MFA for both privileged and non-privileged accounts under controls IA-2(1) and IA-2(2), covering local, network, and remote access. The challenge is making sure no accounts slip through the cracks. Start by reviewing MFA coverage across your environment, paying particular attention to legacy applications and administrative accounts. When implementing MFA, consider phishing-resistant methods such as FIDO2 security keys. You'll also need reliable processes for enrolling, replacing, and revoking authenticators.
