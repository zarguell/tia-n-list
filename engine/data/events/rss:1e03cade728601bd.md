Cisco Talos is aware of eight intermediate certificates belonging to the intermediate certificate authority, eMudhra . Rather than possessing the mandatory 384 bits of pseudo-random security, several private keys evaluated single-digit and double-digit integers. This misconfiguration allows an attacker with the private key of any of the compromised intermediate CAs to impersonate any website or service. Six of the TLS certificates, trusted by Chrome, Mozilla, and Apple have been blocked by these vendors already. Two other certificates, used for codesigning by Microsoft, have also been blocked. Talos expects there will be additional patches available across a variety of technologies as software applications work to remediate the certificates’ exposure. Following the disclosure, eMudhra has revoked the compromised certificates and stopped issuing new certificates under its G3 root. Compromise details The cause of this issue, originated during an April 30, 2025 key generation activity conducted by eMudhra under its emSign Root TLS CA - G3 and cross-signed Comodo AAA Certificate Services. During a key generation ceremony for 24 subordinate CAs, a flaw in eMudhra’s key generation process created a second set of weak P-384 keys during a wrap/unwrap step. Those keys were mistakenly used for eight intermediate CA certificates. This flaw took the encryption from 384 bytes of entropy down to 3 – 8, making recovering the private keys trivial.
