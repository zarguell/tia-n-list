What Happened In late July 2026, Beacon CRM, a UK-based customer relationship management platform serving over 1,500 non-profit and charitable organisations, suffered a major supply chain cyber incident that resulted in the unauthorised exfiltration of its database backups. The compromise began around 27 July 2026, when the unknown attackers gained initial access by leveraging an AWS access key that was inadvertently exposed in publicly accessible JavaScript build artifacts. Over a two-day period of 27–28 July, the attacker used these credentials to download full database backups containing records from over 1,000 UK charity customers. Beacon CRM detected the unauthorised activity on 29 July, revoked the exposed credentials, and began notifying affected organisations between 30 July and 3 August. Public announcements and regulatory notifications followed, including press releases from the UK Charity Commission. While payment card numbers and live bank details were not stored within the affected Beacon CRM database, exported backups included names, email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses, donation histories, and uploaded attachments (including Gift Aid declarations and user notes). Several charities noted that historical records and data previously requested for deletion were retained within stored system backups, exposing older records belonging to past donors and service users.
