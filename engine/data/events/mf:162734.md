Security researchers have uncovered fresh evidence that North Korean-linked Kimsuky hackers are using artificial intelligence to rapidly create phishing documents that look like routine business files. The campaign, tracked as Operation GitPower, combines AI-made decoys with malicious Windows shortcut files, hidden PowerShell scripts, GitHub-hosted payloads, and scheduled tasks that maintain access to compromised machines. Genians Security Center examined 13 malicious LNK files identified between August 11 and August 19, 2026. The files were packaged in ZIP archives and disguised as notices about fund disbursements, insurance payments, interest, certificate renewals, customer records, retail operations, and Visa payments. This wider set of lures suggests the attackers are seeking victims in financial institutions and corporate environments, in addition to the diplomatic, security, and academic targets commonly associated with Kimsuky. North Korea AI Phishing Surge The researchers collected 29 downloaded decoy files but found only 11 unique documents, showing that the group repeatedly reused material under randomized filenames. Several PDFs listed “opencode,” an AI coding agent, as both creator and producer. Their author field was “anonymous,” while some pages still included text such as “(temporary value),” a sign that drafts were likely generated and distributed without careful human review.
