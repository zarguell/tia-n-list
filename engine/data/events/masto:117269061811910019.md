Revolut leaks passports and bitcoin data from wealthy customers through fake government email

The digital bank fell for an email from a hacked government domain. Although no money was stolen, passports and crypto transaction history of wealthy customers are now exposed.

The Revolut data breach: the fintech gave passports, selfies and bitcoin transaction histories to a threat actor impersonating a government agency. The attacker used a compromised government email domain to trick Revolut into sharing customer data. While no financial losses were reported, the exposed data includes passport scans, selfies, and cryptocurrency transaction records belonging to high-net-worth customers.

The breach was confirmed by Revolut on September 12, 2026. The company stated it notified affected customers and alerted the relevant government agency. The incident involved social engineering through fake government requests, not a technical vulnerability in Revolut's systems.
