President Donald Trump’s nominee to lead the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency denied Thursday that the U.S. Capitol building was attacked on Jan. 6, 2021, and refused to say who won the 2020 presidential election. Michael LoSapio, who is currently Palantir’s chief information security officer, made the remarks during his confirmation hearing before the Senate Homeland Security Committee, where Democrats also pressed him on whether he would seek to reverse staffing and budget cuts at the government’s main civilian cyberdefense agency. “No, I don’t believe it was an attack,” LoSapio told Sen. Richard Blumenthal, D-Conn., after initially saying he did not know. When Blumenthal asked again whether the Capitol had been attacked, LoSapio answered no. “I wasn’t involved in politics,” he said. “I know what I saw on the news.” Trump supporters stormed the Capitol that day, assaulting police officers and interrupting Congress’ certification of then-President Joe Biden’s victory. Biden won both the popular vote and the Electoral College in 2020.
