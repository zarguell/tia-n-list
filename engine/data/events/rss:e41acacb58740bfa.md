The Verge reports that Eiliko, a small AI robot companion made by Energize Lab, has serious security flaws that expose private conversations and let an attacker remotely change what the toy says. According to The Verge, researcher Sammy Azdoufal could access 13 million conversations and what appeared to be the entire active worldwide fleet of 68,000 robots. The publication reports that the US is the largest market, with more than 31,000 profiles belonging to children there. Azdoufal found multiple security flaws in Energize Lab’s service: The service analyzes conversations to build profiles containing names, ages, birthdays, relationships, occupations, and assessments of personality and emotional vulnerability. They are stored alongside email addresses, IP addresses , and searchable conversation transcripts, potentially giving scammers detailed information to exploit. Some photos, including apparent images of children from a related camera-equipped device, and short audio recordings were accessible at public web addresses. Retaining audio conflicts with the company’s privacy policy, which says recordings are deleted immediately after processing. It is possible to remotely manipulate the AI: Azdoufal altered The Verge reporter Sean Hollister’s robot’s memory and behavior, making it invent personal details, insist the Earth is flat, and later deliver sexually explicit, abusive dialogue.
