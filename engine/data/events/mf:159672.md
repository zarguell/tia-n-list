Anthropic has announced plans to introduce invisible text watermarks and cryptographically signed metadata for content generated or processed by its Claude AI models, expanding transparency controls intended to help users identify AI involvement in digital content. The initiative follows Anthropic’s decision to sign the European Union AI Act’s Article 50(2) Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content . Under the plan, Claude models released in the European Union on or after August 2, 2026, will include machine-readable content-marking from the date they become available. Anthropic Adds Invisible Watermarks and C2PA Metadata Although prompted by European transparency requirements, Anthropic said the marking capabilities will be deployed globally. The controls are expected to apply across supported Claude models accessed through the Claude website, API platform, Claude Code , Claude Cowork, Claude Tag, and supported cloud-hosted services. Anthropic’s approach consists of two separate technical mechanisms: invisible watermarking for text and C2PA-based provenance metadata for files. The first mechanism embeds an invisible, machine-readable watermark directly into Claude-generated text. Unlike a visible disclosure label, the watermark is not intended to affect readability, meaning, style, or output quality.
