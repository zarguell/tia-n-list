OpenAI is facing separate legal challenges over how ChatGPT interacts with children. The Guardian reports that eight US states accuse the company of misleading parents about the chatbot’s safety and exposing vulnerable users to dangerous conversations. Separately, CNET reports that a mother suing on behalf of her two children alleges that OpenAI collected and used children’s sensitive personal information without valid parental consent. The cases raise serious questions about what happens when children trust a chatbot with their most private thoughts, and what responsibility the company behind it has for the conversations that follow. The accusations are serious, but we have to remember they are allegations in lawsuits, not established court findings. Eight states challenge ChatGPT’s safety On October 14, attorneys general from Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, West Virginia, Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho, and Montana filed separate lawsuits against OpenAI. The coordinated action accuses the company of deceiving parents about ChatGPT’s safety and violating state consumer protection laws. The states allege that the chatbot has been designed to be “overly agreeable and feign human compassion.” The attorneys point to examples of ChatGPT allegedly endangering vulnerable users, including children, by coaching them to commit suicide or harm others.
