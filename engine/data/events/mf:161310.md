EvilTokens is a phishing-as-a-service (PhaaS) platform that expands the role of stolen Microsoft 365 accounts in financial fraud. Instead of stopping after token theft, it helps cybercriminals analyze compromised inboxes, identify high-value payment conversations, and create convincing fraud messages with AI. First documented in February 2026, EvilTokens was promoted through Telegram. Its Microsoft-focused Office 365 capture service reportedly cost $1,500 for panel access, followed by a $500 monthly fee to keep phishing links and backend services active. Researchers have linked the operation to a 16-day campaign affecting 344 organizations in five countries. Unlike older phishing kits, EvilTokens offers a complete post-compromise playbook. Attackers can capture access tokens, search email content, find suppliers and payment approvers, and prepare business email compromise (BEC) messages. This turns a single hijacked identity into an opportunity for invoice fraud, payment redirection, and targeted scams. EvilTokens Fuels Microsoft Fraud EvilTokens commonly abuses Microsoft’s legitimate OAuth 2.0 device authorization flow. This sign-in method is designed for devices that cannot easily display a normal browser-based login page. The attack begins with a phishing lure that directs a target to an EvilTokens-controlled page.
