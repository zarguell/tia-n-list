TL;DR Three attack surfaces: An AI agent exposes its host, its identity, and itself. Nine public incidents between May 2025 and July 2026 hit all three; the agent surface appears in five, always combined with the host or the identity. The credential is the target: In the injection incidents, the agent reads an issue, an email, a settings file, or a web page that carries instructions. The instruction opened the door, and the credential is what the attacker came for. Six controls limit the blast radius: Sandbox the agent, scope its credentials, keep it out of its own configuration, record every call, scan the workspace for secrets first, and deny by default. None of them prevent prompt injection. Abstract An AI agent runs with your credentials and acts before you can stop it. Nine incidents show what that costs. The AI agent attack surface: host, identity, and the agent itself An agent exposes three distinct surfaces. There is the host it touches, the identity it acts as, and the agent itself. The host is everything the process can reach: the filesystem, process execution, the network, environment variables, and hardware such as the camera or microphone.
