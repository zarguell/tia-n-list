Russian government-connected hackers compromised two defense firms and a law enforcement agency in North Atlantic Treaty Organization countries as part of an espionage campaign, Symantec researchers said in a blog post Tuesday. The infamous group behind hacks of the Democratic National Committee during the 2016 election cycle was behind the attacks that Symantec identified in recent months: Swallowtail, also known as APT28, Fancy Bear and Forest Blizzard, notorious for its links to the Russian military agency GRU. The hackers apparently chose the targets of their attacks carefully, including a naval shipbuilder, a manufacturer of military aircraft engines and the law enforcement agency. In the attack on the shipbuilder, Symantec saw “hands-on-keyboard” activity as the hackers repeatedly returned to a compromised host over the course of a week to steal credentialed material. “Hands-on-keyboard activity means a human operator is choosing what to do next, rather than malware running a fixed routine,” Dick O’Brien, principal intelligence analyst at the Symantec Threat Hunter Team, told CyberScoop. “It’s essentially confirmation that the organization is a genuine target for the operators, not an accidental infection. It also confirms that further exploitation beyond the initial infection was at least attempted.” O’Brien said the campaign reflected broader intelligence collection aimed at European government and defense targets, but its motive wasn’t certain.
