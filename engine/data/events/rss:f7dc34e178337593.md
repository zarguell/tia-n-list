The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) is warning about a critical vulnerability in Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA Series controllers used by food retailers to manage commercial refrigeration, HVAC, and lighting systems. Danfoss controllers have been compromised recently in attacks at various supermarkets and food retailers across Europe where hackers remotely shut down cooling systems or raised their temperatures. The security issue is tracked as CVE-2026-129713 and received a severity score of 9.8. It is a stack-based buffer overflow in the controller’s server. CISA says an attacker can trigger the flaw without authentication, cause the server to restart, and potentially achieve remote code execution with the web server's privileges. Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA controllers are central control units for supermarket and convenience-store refrigeration. They are used to manage equipment such as refrigerated display cases and compressor racks, to log temperatures, and to alert when changes occur. Danfoss calls the product line the AK-SM 800A Series, which includes the 820A, 850A, and 880A models. Depending on the model, they can also control store lighting and HVAC. CISA notes that the controllers are deployed worldwide, but their exact number remains unclear. On November 20, Danfoss published a security advisory saying that the CVE-2026-129713 vulnerability would be addressed in the upcoming software version 5.5.x for Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA Series.
