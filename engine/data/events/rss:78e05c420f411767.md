There is little doubt that 2026 has been marked by a significant increase in threat activity across both the cyber and physical domains. For European organizations, much of this increase can be traced back to the wider confrontation between Russia and Europe following Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine. More than four years into the conflict, its effects continue to extend far beyond the battlefield and have become a defining factor in the threat landscape facing organizations across the continent. At the same time, another topic has dominated industry discussions: artificial intelligence. Depending on who you ask, AI is either about to unleash a wave of autonomous cyberattacks, fundamentally transform cybercrime, or become an existential threat to humanity itself. While these debates attract attention, they can also make it difficult to focus on the threats organizations are dealing with today. If we step back from the noise and look at what defenders are actually facing, a more nuanced picture emerges.
