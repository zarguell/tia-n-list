Researchers assess the activity as China-nexus with medium confidence, citing victim selection, shared malware tooling, infrastructure links, and tradecraft commonly associated with Chinese espionage activity. The campaign used seven remote access trojan (RAT) families. Five were previously undocumented and named DriveSilkRAT, CookiETagRAT, NomadRAT, GoginRAT, and NodeEdgeRAT. The other two families were SpiceRAT and BloodAlchemy, which is linked to the ShadowPad and Deed RAT lineage. The operation was discovered after a suspicious infection at a Central Asian government organization involved in economic decisions in late 2025. Months of investigation revealed a maintained campaign that had likely operated for nearly a year. SilkParasite Targets Central Asia SilkParasite targeted entities in Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Kazakhstan, and Georgia. Attackers used phishing emails containing malicious Microsoft Office documents, often hidden inside password-protected RAR archives. The documents launched macros that deployed malware through DLL sideloading. DLL sideloading lets attackers abuse trusted, signed applications to load a malicious DLL file stored in the same folder.
