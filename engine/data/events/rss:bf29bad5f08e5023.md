Two new lawsuits accuse SpaceX and its subsidiaries of violating state consumer and trade laws by allowing its AI tool, Grok, to create deepfake nudes of millions of people earlier this year. Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones and Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield, both Democrats, filed the lawsuits. They allege SpaceX and owner Elon Musk repeatedly failed to block Grok’s nudification feature after it became clear the tool was being used to break the law by creating nude or defamatory images of real people. The attorneys general want a court to bar SpaceX from advertising or profiting from the tool’s capabilities, pay restitution to victims and impose civil penalties. “Time and again, xAI and X told consumers they were being protected from abuses. That was false,” Jones said in a statement. “Instead, the tool permitted users to routinely create and circulate non-consensual intimate imagery and sexualized images, and child sexual abuse material (CSAM), causing harm to individuals and children. Companies that deploy powerful tools don’t get to ignore consumer safety and call it innovation. The law applies to everyone, and this office will hold big tech accountable when they break the law.” The lawsuits say Grok launched when other AI companies already knew about the harms of deepfake pornography to adults and children.
