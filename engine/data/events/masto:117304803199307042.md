North Korean Cyber Group Steals from IT Professionals Seeking Jobs

German intelligence services warn IT job seekers about a cyber group that targets software developers and other IT specialists using social engineering tactics. The campaign, dubbed "Contagious Interview" in the IT security community, operates on behalf of the North Korean state to steal cryptocurrency and bypass international sanctions.

The attackers first contact targets through social media, job platforms, or freelance marketplaces, posing as representatives of AI companies or cryptocurrency firms. During the fake online application process, candidates are asked to download a file for a task, which contains malware. Once installed, the attackers steal sensitive data and cryptocurrency, while trojans maintain persistent access for further attacks.

Simultaneously, North Korean IT specialists obtain remote developer jobs at foreign companies under false identities, using "laptop farms" in other countries to hide their location. Employers should be suspicious when applicants invent excuses to avoid in-person meetings, request payment in cryptocurrency, or frequently look sideways during video calls as if reading from a second monitor.

North Korea has been under extensive UN sanctions for roughly two decades due to its nuclear and missile programs. The group, identified as "Waterplum," aims to generate funds for the Kim regime's salaries and military, though espionage and intellectual property theft may also play a role.
