Spain's data protection authority (AEPD) has reported the first data breach caused by an AI agent. According to the AEPD, an AI agent based on a known language model autonomously searched for vulnerabilities in generic files, successfully logged in, located further weaknesses, modified personal data, and accessed invoices. The agency described this as a qualitatively new type of cyberattack.

The AEPD emphasized that the information came from the affected organization's breach notification, not from a compromised AI provider. The key concern is that a third party used an AI agent as a tool to chain together different attack phases successfully. The agency noted that AI agents can receive a target, plan intermediate steps, deploy tools, execute code, query sources, interpret results, and autonomously adapt their approach based on findings. The primary novelty is the speed of such attacks.

AEPD deputy director Francisco Perez Bes wrote that AI-assisted attacks must be explicitly included in risk assessments, noting that general references to malware, phishing, or unauthorized access are insufficient. He warned that defenses designed for manually executed attacks may be too slow against AI-driven ones, and that digital credentials and API keys gain critical importance since compromised keys let attackers move at machine speed.
