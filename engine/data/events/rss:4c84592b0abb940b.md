AI infrastructure startup Fireworks has disclosed a new security breach in which attackers accessed its development machines after breaching a third-party cloud provider. FireWorks provides a high-performance inference platform for developers who want to deploy and run open-source artificial intelligence models (such as Mistral, Meta's Llama, or Google's Gemma) without worrying about hardware bottlenecks and complex infrastructure tasks. The company notified law enforcement and customers of another security incident in early October after attackers accessed environment variables containing API keys and tokens stored as secrets for a limited number of accounts. "The evidence indicates links to a cybercriminal threat actor group, which is financially motivated and aimed at selling tokens from unauthorized access to Large Language Models (LLMs) to illicit routing gateways, known as 'LLMJacking'," Fireworks said at the time. After revoking compromised internal credentials, affected Fireworks API keys, and keys created by the attackers, Fireworks discovered another security incident on October 23. In the new breach, the threat actors accessed resource metadata (e.g., Fireworks resource identifiers, display names, descriptions, creation time), billing information (e.g., billing and shipping addresses, invoice and payment history, account balance, and credit usage), and incomplete model weights and datasets for some customers.
