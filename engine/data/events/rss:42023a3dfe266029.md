Dozens of MSP ScreenConnect servers have been targeted in a month-long campaign exploiting a vulnerability patched in September, cybersecurity firm Huntress reports. The attacks, ongoing since at least October 20, aim to exploit CVE-2026-84869 (CVSS score of 9.9), a critical missing authorization and improper privilege management bug that allows for files to be transmitted and executed during a connection without authorization or confirmation. ConnectWise patched the security defect on September 8, and CISA added it to the KEV catalog on September 11. Huntress warned at the time that exploitation started on August 20 . Now, the cybersecurity firm says a new wave of attacks is targeting ScreenConnect instances, with a focus on managed service provider (MSP) servers. The hackers exploit CVE-2026-84869 via requests to run a guest-pushed batch file that connects to a Telegram bot and installs a rogue ScreenConnect client. “Attackers appear to be spraying servers blindly: Since October 20, we’ve seen at least 688,000 guest ‘run file’ requests for update*.bat hit 42 MSP ScreenConnect servers from 29 IP addresses, waiting for any technician to join,” Huntress says . According to the cybersecurity firm, the activity is different from the recently observed campaigns targeting ScreenConnect instances with the SCKit malware toolkit and with unauthorized sessions from machines posing as crypto exchanges.
