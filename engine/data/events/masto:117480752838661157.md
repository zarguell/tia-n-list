PHP2Shell: defense starts before the patch. The critical vulnerabilities announced for PHP require reducing exposure immediately. For systems still on PHP 8.1 and earlier, the problem also includes the absence of official maintenance.

An announced patch does not protect an exposed server. Between the pre-announcement of a critical vulnerability and the actual installation of the fix there is an operational window in which risk has to be managed with the tools already available: inventory, configuration, access control and, where necessary, isolation. That is the priority that emerges from the PHP2Shell case, the name used publicly for the vulnerabilities associated with CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619. Waiting for your distribution's package without acting on exposure means letting the distribution calendar set your defensive capability.

CERT-EU also recalls the possibility of remote code execution, citing the leaked information and pointing to possible exposure extending to historical versions from PHP 5 onward. That "possible" should be kept: verifying the perimeter requires the vendor's details. The operational recommendation is explicit instead: identify PHP applications, prepare immediate patching, and consider isolating public services when they cannot be fixed in time.

The operational sequence is therefore immediate: inventory services, reduce exposure, verify measures and prepare the update. When the fixes become available they should be installed without delay, and the update must also reach the images, replicas and templates used to recreate the services, otherwise a later deployment could reintroduce the vulnerable runtime. If signs of compromise emerge, patching should be accompanied by an incident review: fixing the defect does not remove persistent access already obtained. The patches were released at 16:00 Italian time, which is 14:00 GMT, the coordinated publication window.
