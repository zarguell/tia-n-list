AI access becomes a commodity: How stolen session tokens fuel a black market for frontier models.

via Mastodon https://www.infopoint-security.de/ki-zugaenge-werden-zur-ware-wie-gestohlene-session-tokens-einen-schwarzmarkt-fuer-frontier-modelle-antreiben/a46386/

Okta Threat Intelligence warns of a rapidly professionalizing black market for stolen AI accounts and session tokens. Cybercriminals sell stolen or fraudulently obtained access to powerful AI models at 70 to 90 percent discounts off official subscription prices. In one documented case, unauthorized AI usage costs totaled nearly one million US dollars.

The primary attack vector is account takeover via infostealer malware. Attackers steal active session tokens or API keys directly from browsers and applications. When these tokens are reused through anti-detect browsers and proxy infrastructure, an already-authenticated session can be hijacked without needing the user's password or multi-factor authentication. From the target system's perspective, the session appears legitimate.

The scale of available stolen credentials is substantial. A roughly seven-gigabyte infostealer dataset from August 2, 2026 contained logs from 5,871 compromised machines across 162 countries. Researchers found thousands of valid session tokens for major technology and AI providers including Google, Microsoft, Anthropic, and Amazon, along with thousands of JSON Web Tokens. Nearly 18 percent of the JWTs contained personally identifiable information in plaintext, including names, phone numbers, and email addresses.

The second attack vector targets legitimate promotional and startup credit programs. Gray and black market sellers offer seemingly unlimited token quotas at prices far below official rates, backed by mass-registered fake accounts and abused startup or promotional credits. Google provides qualifying startups with up to $350,000 in credits; Anthropic offers up to $100,000. When these programs are systematically harvested through bot networks and fake identities, innovation funding instruments become resources for criminal arbitrage.

In one investigated case, a KI video service provider recorded over 105,000 automated registration attempts in a single month from just 251 IP addresses, using disposable email addresses and automated account creation. These methods can also circumvent regional restrictions or regulatory access blocks.

Okta recommends continuous session monitoring to detect context parameter changes during active sessions, centralized logout mechanisms for cross-application session termination, API keys with restrictive permissions, regular rotation, and IP binding where technically feasible. Phishing-resistant authentication like Passkeys can help prevent automated account farming.
