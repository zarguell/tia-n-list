FBI arrested three American cybersecurity experts who allegedly planted their own ransomware on US companies over two years, then offered their services as ransom negotiators to the same victims. The suspects, including Kevin Martin who worked for DigitalMint, a Chicago-based ransom negotiation firm, used their access and expertise to deploy malware before positioning themselves as rescuers.

The scheme exploited trust in the incident response industry. When companies faced ransomware attacks, they turned to external specialists for help recovering data. The FBI alleges these experts betrayed that trust by creating the very emergencies they were paid to resolve. This represents a fundamental conflict of interest in the ransomware response business model.

US cybersecurity salaries are substantial, with median analyst pay at $124,910 annually and senior roles reaching $150,000-$220,000. CISOs at large organizations earn $300,000-$600,000, with Fortune 100 CISOs making up to $5 million. The FBI suggests pure greed, not financial desperation, motivated the scheme. The case highlights risks in the ransomware negotiation industry, where companies pay criminals ransoms that fund future attacks, creating a cycle that benefits both criminals and the intermediaries facilitating payments.
