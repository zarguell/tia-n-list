ZRON logo from a company presentation file In Part 1 and Part 2 of our ZRON leak reports, we evaluated the sources of the leaked data and cross-checked Chinese-language open-source information about ZRON. We then analyzed ZRON’s full product catalogue, customer accounts, and use of artificial intelligence (AI). In particular, we highlighted which provincial Public Security Bureaus and related Ministry of Public Security entities were interested in ZRON’s products and services. Finally, we assessed that the ecosystem connecting government and intelligence agencies with China’s commercial hackers for hire — for-profit cybersecurity companies — largely resembles what we identified in the 2024 i-SOON leak . At the same time, we argued, this environment is evolving, as competition among vendors intensifies and government agencies face uncertain budgets, tighter procurement processes, and organizational restructuring. How did ZRON navigate this complex environment? As the Natto Team previously noted , the i-SOON leak – whose documents dated from 2020 to 2022 – vividly depicted the intricate relationships among information security companies, China’s public security and state security agencies, other government bodies, and the military. Within this landscape, information security firms have cultivated an ecosystem in which they engage with diverse government entities, operating both collaboratively and independently.
