Threat actors are now relying on agentic workflows powered by AI-based offensive frameworks to automate vulnerability discovery and exploitation, accelerating attacks to machine speed. Although AI was initially used experimentally in the attack flow, Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) has observed its large-scale adoption by both financially motivated and state-sponsored threat groups. A notable example is the use of CAIRN , an open-source multi-agent penetration-testing framework, in an attack against the U.S. aerospace sector, and suspected North Korean hackers using Claude Code to deliver malware. The researchers estimate that in the next three months threat actors will be able to launch unsophisticated attacks that require "little to no human interaction" and rely mostly on multiple AI agents. Their projection is that "sophisticated, fully autonomous" agentic attacks are likely to emerge in the next six months. In an advisory last week, the FBI warned that multiple threat actors started weaponizing the ARTEX AI pentesting framework since August and launched attacks against organizations in the financial, government, technology, defense, healthcare, and other sectors. Google says attackers now systematically abuse open-source pentesting suites to run fully automated attacks, including ARTEX, CAIRN, CyberStrikeAI, and HexStrike AI.
