ShinyHunters still active: details of the FBI data theft. The cybercrime group ShinyHunters claimed last week that it stole data from the FBI, and the claim appears to be no bluff: the data seems real. The gang infiltrated the FBI's job portal and claims to have stolen data on almost all FBI agents, backing the claim with a sample dataset of 5,000 entries. Reuters compared sample records with other databases and judged them plausible, and the BBC reviewed samples from FBI agent health screenings that included blood and urine test results and specific allergy information. Further data includes full names and addresses, exposing agents to fraud, blackmail, and targeted attacks, and could be used to forge law-enforcement identities.

According to the BBC, ShinyHunters says it exploited a vulnerability in an Oracle cloud storage system, possibly Oracle PeopleSoft, a flaw the group has attacked since mid-June. Over the weekend Google's Mandiant renewed its warning that ShinyHunters is mass-exploiting the PeopleSoft vulnerability worldwide, bypassing Web Application Firewall rules with URL encoding such as /%50SEMHUB/ instead of /PSEMHUB/. Organizations that have not yet applied the June patches for CVE-2026-35273 (CVSS 9.8, critical) will find protective guidance in Mandiant's analysis.

The group had threatened to publish the full dataset, said to cover around 60,000 current and former FBI employees and total two to three terabytes, within five days. In a statement to 404 Media on Monday, ShinyHunters said it never intended to publish and that its sole objective was to force the deletion or correction of an FBI report attributing sextortion to the group. The report also notes a possible motive shift: Dutch police arrested a 24-year-old suspected of involvement in ShinyHunters data theft and extortion. He had been convicted in 2023 under the handle "Umbreon" for multiple data thefts and extortion attempts earning 1.5 to 2.7 million euros, received a four-year sentence with one year suspended, and was released in December 2025 before being arrested again in mid-September. Dutch police confirmed the arrest of the 24-year-old from Amsterdam on Monday. Dutch investigators had also sought help identifying a Dutch-speaking caller who socially engineered his way into mobile provider Odido in February 2026, a breach that exposed data on more than 6.2 million Dutch citizens. The FBI job portal was left defaced with ASCII art of Umbreon, the Pokemon character.
