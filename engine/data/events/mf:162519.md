Threat actors can bypass Microsoft 365’s RejectDirectSend protection by using an empty SMTP envelope sender, allowing phishing messages to appear as if they were sent by trusted internal users, according to ReliaQuest. RejectDirectSend is an Exchange Online control designed to stop unauthenticated Direct Send emails that claim to originate from an organization’s own Microsoft 365 domain . Direct Send is commonly used by printers, scanners, applications, and other devices to send messages to users within the same tenant without needing sign-in credentials. However, ReliaQuest found that the control checks the domain in the SMTP envelope sender, also called the MAIL FROM address. When attackers send a message using an empty envelope sender, represented as MAIL FROM:<> , there is no domain available for RejectDirectSend to evaluate. The visible From address can still display a trusted internal identity, such as “IT Support” or an executive email address. This creates a phishing risk because recipients may trust the sender identity shown in their email client, even though the message originated externally and without authentication. Empty Sender Bypasses RejectDirectSend In controlled testing, ReliaQuest sent two unauthenticated messages directly to a Microsoft 365 tenant with RejectDirectSend enabled.
