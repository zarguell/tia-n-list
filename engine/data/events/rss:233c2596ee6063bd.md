As AI agents become increasingly autonomous, access sensitive data, interact with tools and take actions across enterprise systems, securing the infrastructure that powers them becomes more important than ever. AI security extends beyond models and applications. It encompasses the compute, networking, storage, firmware and management systems that execute workloads and enforce security controls. Trusted AI must therefore be anchored in trusted infrastructure. That means moving from assumed trust to verifiable trust: evidence that critical infrastructure is in a known-good state, remains correctly configured and has not drifted because of vulnerabilities, unauthorized modifications, supply-chain compromise or persistent threats.
