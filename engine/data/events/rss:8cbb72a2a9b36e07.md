Russian state-backed hackers are using information stolen from cloud accounts to impersonate trusted contacts and launch new espionage attacks against people in their victims' professional networks, according to new research. The hacking group, known as Cold Relic or ColdRiver , has previously targeted government officials, defense contractors and nonprofit organizations across Europe and North America. U.S. authorities have linked the group to Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB). In a report published Wednesday, Google's cybersecurity unit Mandiant described how the hackers used data stolen from one U.S. organization in late 2025 to help compromise a second entity. During the first attack, the hackers took control of an administrator account and used it to download the organization's entire cloud drive and employee directories. They also searched for emails, passwords and personnel records, apparently looking for information they could use in future operations. The hackers remained inside the organization's systems for several months. They continued reading emails through third-party applications and found ways to regain access after the organization changed passwords and reset user sessions. Even after losing access to the network, the hackers continued targeting the organization and its contacts by sending emails that appeared to come from employees.
