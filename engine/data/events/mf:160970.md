This article was co-authored by Gary Katz and Jason Deyalsingh Recent studies have shown that genAI is commoditizing the creation of detection rules. This elevates the minimal qualifications of the detection engineer from simply creating detections to identifying where to focus efforts, implementing more robust detection capabilities and performing the research and tooling to support them. This article will focus on the first of these skillsets, identifying opportunities to create detections which align to your organization’s needs. In the previous article, Critical Asset Analysis for Detection Engineering , we looked at one approach for prioritizing tasking for a detection engineering team. In this article, we will expand upon that analysis to examine four information sources of detection engineering and how they each can be either a starting point or a filter mechanism for prioritization. In the follow on article, we will identify different detection approaches, their limitations, and their data sources. This provides a guide for starting either from the data source perspective or from the detection mechanism perspective to prioritize tasking which aligns to your organization, skillset or detection capabilities. Teams should take a balanced approach across each of these options, using the right tool in the toolbox to achieve their goal.
