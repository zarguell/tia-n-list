In this article Attack chain overview Mitigation and protection guidance Learn More Microsoft Threat Intelligence has identified an evolving fake-download campaign targeting users searching for cracked commercial software, free games, activators, plugins, and unofficial utilities. The campaign uses trojanized Inno Setup-style installers to distribute multiple monetized software payloads, referred to by the delivery system as “offers”, during a single installation session. Our investigation found that the installers use a per-offer callback and status-tracking model to manage delivery and record installation outcomes. We confirmed three downstream monetization branches that transformed affected devices into monetized assets, including by routing third-party internet traffic through the device, enrolling it in a distributed-computing platform, or installing additional utility-style software, often without users’ informed knowledge or consent. These products and components may have legitimate uses, but their silent installation through deceptive software bundles without informed user consent introduces security and privacy risks. Organizations can reduce their exposure to this activity by preventing the use of untrusted software sources, enabling potentially unwanted application protection, and monitoring for unexpected software installations and persistence mechanisms.
