IonQ announced Monday that it will acquire Elisity, a zero-trust network security firm, in an all-stock transaction valued at up to $1.3 billion that folds enterprise access control into the quantum computing company’s expanding cybersecurity business. Elisity, founded in 2019, sells a software policy layer that governs which users, devices, workloads and AI agents can communicate across a network, independent of the underlying network technology. The California-based company reports more than 100 global deployments and says revenue and customer counts have doubled year over year, with no churn among deployed customers. Maryland-based IonQ, whose core business is quantum computing, has been building a security arm around post-quantum cryptography and quantum key distribution, technologies intended to replace or supplement encryption that future quantum computers may be able to break. The combination addresses what the companies describe as a two-front problem: conventional attacks that operate at machine speed, and the risk that encrypted data could be retained until quantum decryption becomes practical, a concept known as “harvest now, decrypt later.” “Our customers need to contain attacks today, put AI to work safely, and prepare for quantum risk,” Elisity CEO James Winebrenner wrote in a blog post announcing the deal.
