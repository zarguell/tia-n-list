Chinese state-sponsored hacking group Volt Typhoon has been using a framework that relies on rotating chains of connected devices compromised on demand, including for zero-day attacks targeting high-value systems. The framework has been dubbed Silent Train because the threat actor uses it against select targets, cleans it up after each operation, and refreshes it continuously. Researchers at Black Lotus Labs, the threat research division of communications company Lumen, have been tracking Silent Train for three years and confirmed that Volt Typhoon exploited at least four zero-day vulnerabilities in attacks targeting edge and management infrastructure. Targets include telecommunication firms, government agencies, defense contractors, educational institutes, utilities/critical infrastructure, satellite service providers, IT firms, MSPs, transportation, aviation, and emergency services. Black Lotus Labs’ observations indicate the most targeted regions are California, Texas, and Hawaii, suggesting a focus on Indo-Pacific matters. Volt Typhoon previously targeted U.S. critical infrastructure, including communications, energy, transportation, and water systems, with CISA warning that it seeks to establish access for potential disruption during a future crisis or conflict. According to the researchers, Silent Train's deployment was partly fueled by the FBI's disruption of KV-Botnet , a botnet that Volt Typhoon used to evade detection when attacking critical infrastructure in the US
