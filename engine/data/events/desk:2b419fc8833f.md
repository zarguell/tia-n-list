PrivacyAffairs reported on 21 November that GL.iNet owners began receiving notice emails that day and that fixed Comet KVM firmware appeared as beta builds. The article points out that a compromised Comet sits between the owner and a computer, able to see its screen and type on its keyboard, and that GL.iNet's KVM cloud service is enabled by default. At publication there was no new router firmware, and the company had not said how many devices were hit or when the attacks began.
