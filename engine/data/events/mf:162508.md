OpenAI has launched GPT-6 Astra, a frontier AI model the company says has reached its Critical cybersecurity-capability threshold, intensifying debate over automated vulnerability research and exploit development. Announced September 3, Astra is initially available to a limited group of organizations, followed by planned access for ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise users, API customers, and AWS users. OpenAI says Astra can identify previously unknown security flaws and develop ways to exploit them when it has appropriate tools and authorized access. OpenAI GPT-6 Astra Reaches Critical Cyber Capability With Zero-Day The company reported that the model achieved a 100% score on ExploitBench, a benchmark measuring exploit-development performance against known vulnerabilities. It also said Astra discovered and used two zero-day vulnerabilities as part of an exploit chain during internal testing. Those results require careful interpretation. A controlled benchmark does not demonstrate that a model can reliably compromise arbitrary production systems, nor does it establish that every generated exploit will be stable, safe, or operationally useful. However, the result highlights a major shift: AI agents are increasingly able to combine code analysis, browser use, terminal interaction, debugging, and iterative software engineering in a single workflow.
