PHP2Shell: Two Critical PHP Vulnerabilities (CVE-2026–109618 and CVE-2026–109619) — Are Your Servers Ready? ( + KQL Queries) Two critical vulnerabilities affecting PHP have been disclosed ahead of their scheduled security release. The vulnerabilities, tracked as CVE-2026–109618 and CVE-2026–109619 and commonly referred to as PHP2Shell, have received a CVSS v4.0 score of 9.2 from the PHP security team. An emergency security release is scheduled for October 21, 2026, at 14:00 UTC. At the time of writing, the official affected version ranges and complete technical details have not yet been published. Initial reports suggest that older PHP versions may also be affected. While waiting for the official advisory, I wanted to explore what we can already investigate using Microsoft Defender XDR and KQL. The objective is not to detect these two vulnerabilities directly. Without confirmed exploitation details, that would be premature. Instead, we can identify PHP installations, investigate suspicious process execution, review network connections, and establish a baseline of PHP-related activity. Here are a few hunting queries worth exploring. 1. Identifying PHP installations and versions Before investigating potential exploitation, we need to understand where PHP is installed. Microsoft Defender Vulnerability Management provides software inventory information through the DeviceTvmSoftwareInventory table.
