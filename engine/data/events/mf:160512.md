A newly identified phishing-as-a-service (PhaaS) framework named JWR is targeting banking and payment-service customers through convincing SMS scams. Cisco Talos found that the toolkit lets fraud operators watch victims type in real time, then guide them through fake login, payment, and verification pages designed to steal cards, credentials, identity data, and one-time passcodes. The activity uses familiar smishing lures: unpaid road-toll notices, postal charges, and parcel-delivery fees. Victims who open the malicious link land on a cloned website that resembles a transport authority, courier, bank, or payment platform. Talos observed lures tied to Singapore, the UAE, and regional services operating across Southeast Asia and the Middle East. JWR PhaaS Hits Banking JWR is more dangerous than a typical phishing kit that simply collects a submitted form. Its browser-side client maintains an AES-CTR-encrypted WebSocket connection to the attacker’s command-and-control server. This gives an operator a live view of information as it is entered, including partial card numbers, passwords, and verification codes. The operator can then react before the victim submits a form. For example, after collecting a card number, the attacker can show a fake “card declined” alert and ask for a second card.
