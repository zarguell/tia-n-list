A massive cybercriminal ecosystem facilitating more than a billion malicious messages targeting over 75,000 organizations across 150 countries has been disrupted by law enforcement agencies and partners worldwide. Since the start of the year, more than 750 million malicious messages have been aimed at Microsoft customers via email and SMS texts. Following an investigation initiated by Microsoft and Japan’s National Police Agency (NPA) into a wave of fraudulent activity, "a sprawling Chinese-language cybercrime marketplace" was discovered, with tools and services for phishing campaigns. The investigators traced the malicious activity to YAM Store (Storm-3119), an underground infrastructure marketplace, then linked it to the CoGUI, Haozi, and Iron Man System phishing kits, collectively tracked as Storm-2990. In a report today, Microsoft says that YAM Store sold compromised or fraudulently obtained Microsoft and Google environments, accounts, licenses, and storage. Its operators acquired expired domains of real organizations, rebuilt websites to make defunct entities appear active, and then used those identities to obtain cloud accounts. Another observed method was to generate fake organizations, complete with content and documentation, using AI tools. In compromised Microsoft environments, the operators used stolen application credentials to manage accounts and licenses, and abused SharePoint and OneDrive storage for hosting.
