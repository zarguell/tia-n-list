A Russian national recruited through Telegram has been charged in the United States with acting as a Moscow agent in a surveillance campaign targeting employees of a defense technology company, understood to be Palantir Technologies. The alleged spying operation involved electronic surveillance, detailed dossiers on employees and their families, and, in one case, a nearly 30-mile pursuit of a female worker who ultimately sought help from a police patrol car. Igor Andreevich D’iu was “recruited, directed, and funded by the Russian government,” according to Roman Rozhavsky, an assistant director in the FBI’s Counterintelligence and Espionage Division. D’iu entered the U.S. illegally from Mexico — after travelling there from Russia via Istanbul — and was charged this week with conspiring to act as an agent of Russia, acting as an agent of a foreign government without notifying the attorney general, interstate stalking and identity document fraud. The indictment does not identify Palantir by name, but describes the target company as a large, publicly traded technology and defense business whose software Ukraine is using during its war with Russia. Palantir did not respond to a request for comment. Images released by the Department of Justice show photographs which D’iu took of laptop screens at outdoor café tables at a Starbucks and Verve Coffee Roasters, both of which are a short walk from Palantir’s offices in Palo Alto.
