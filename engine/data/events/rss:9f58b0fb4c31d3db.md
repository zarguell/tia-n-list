Executive Summary A critical certificate authority incident disclosed on October 17, 2026, impacts eight eMudhra intermediate CA certificates. A flaw in the key generation process could allow attackers to recover private keys and issue fraudulent TLS or code-signing certificates. The affected intermediates have been revoked and blocked by major platform vendors, including Chrome, Mozilla, Apple, and Microsoft. The issue is tracked as CVE-2026-111943 and CVE-2026-111944. The eMudhra Certificate Authority Compromise On October 17, 2026, a researcher reported recovering the private keys for eight eMudhra intermediate CA certificates after discovering that the underlying cryptographic keys contained only a fraction of the expected entropy. Browser and OS vendors have since blocked the certificates, and eMudhra has revoked them. Security teams now face an immediate question: Where do certificates issued by the compromised eMudhra intermediates exist within my environment? Qualys VMDR and Qualys Certificate View already collect the data needed to answer that. This post explains how to use them, with QQL queries you can run today. What is eMudhra? eMudhra, a digital trust and cybersecurity provider, offers solutions spanning Public Key Infrastructure (PKI), digital certificates, identity management, and secure digital transactions.
