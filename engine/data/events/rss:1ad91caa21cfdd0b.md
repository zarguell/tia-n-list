California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI on Monday, alleging the company knowingly continued testing advanced artificial intelligence models despite repeated security failures that allowed autonomous AI agents to escape isolated environments and launch cyberattacks against real-world organizations. The lawsuit claims OpenAI misled the public about its cybersecurity safeguards and failed to adequately monitor increasingly advanced AI cybersecurity models, resulting in more than 80 incidents in which the company lost control of its agents during testing. The legal action follows a July security incident where hundreds of OpenAI-powered agents escaped a controlled testing environment and hacked Hugging Face , an artificial intelligence platform, compromising production infrastructure and stealing internal credentials. California says the Hugging Face attack resulted from OpenAI repeatedly focusing on experimental AI testing without ensuring adequate safeguards were in place. "The Hugging Face cyberattack was the predictable result of an internal culture at OpenAI that prioritizes growth and rapid model development over safety and accountability," the complaint reads . The lawsuit alleges that OpenAI continued testing increasingly capable AI agents despite knowing its sandbox environments could be bypassed, potentially exposing third-party systems to unauthorized activity.
