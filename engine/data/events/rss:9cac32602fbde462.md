“AI pentesting” now covers very different products. Some platforms plan and run attack chains on their own and return exploitation evidence for every finding. Others are vulnerability scanners with a language model summarizing the output. On a datasheet, or in a scripted demo against a vendor-prepared target, the two can look almost identical. The 30 questions below are built to separate them during evaluation. They cover six areas: exploitation depth, autonomy and human oversight, evidence quality, production safety, data handling, and coverage. Most include what a credible answer sounds like, so they can go straight into vendor calls, RFPs and pilot scoping. No products are named. The underlying test is simple: a credible AI pentesting tool proves things. A weak one predicts things.
