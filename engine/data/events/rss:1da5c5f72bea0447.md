Japan is facing a dense wave of breach disclosures. Since late September, at least 20 major Japanese companies have reported breaches, including Times Car, Lawson, Yakiniku King, Sourcenext and Burger King Japan, and a ransomware attack on SoftBank’s IDC Frontier cloud took 495 customer organizations offline. Between September 25 and October 8 alone, at least 36 Japanese organizations issued a first breach notice, and new notices kept arriving after mid-October from Yamato Transport, Nikkei and email delivery provider Ymirlink, among others. On October 19, a Japanese security firm tied part of the wave to a Chinese-developed AI penetration-testing tool. SOCRadar’s own Dark Web News data reflects the same spike. Items on Japan reached 134 in the first 15 days of October, already more than all of September, at twice September’s daily rate. This post breaks down what happened, how attackers got in, why the wave is hitting now, what the new AI findings show, where ransomware fits, and what defenders in Japan should do next.
