Intel and AMD have published more than 50 new security advisories that describe over 200 vulnerabilities affecting their products. Fixes are available for most of the flaws, but not all. Some AMD mitigations were released months or even years ago, others are still in the works, and Intel has discontinued one of the impacted products. Intel publishes 24 advisories covering more than 100 flaws Intel’s 24 new advisories describe just over 100 vulnerabilities. Three of them are rated ‘critical’ and 20 are rated ‘high severity’. The largest advisory covers 30 security holes in the TDX Migration Trust Domain (MigTD) reference software. They include three critical authentication issues that can be exploited for privilege escalation. One of them, CVE-2026-61887 , could also allow remote code execution. High-severity privilege escalation vulnerabilities have been addressed in Slim Bootloader, the UEFI firmware of the D50DNP and M50FCP server boards, and the VTune Profiler software. Intel also fixed high-severity flaws that can be exploited for denial-of-service (DoS) attacks in its PROSet/Wireless WiFi and Bluetooth products, and in the Performance Counter Monitor (PCM) software. No patches are being released for three medium-severity privilege escalation bugs in BigDL, which has been discontinued.
