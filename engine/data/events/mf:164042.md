Cybersecurity researchers at Silent Push have uncovered a suspected North Korean IT worker operation that recruits foreign nationals to act as job-interview proxies. The scheme uses people in the United States, Europe, and Latin America to appear on camera, pass identity checks, receive payments, and hide the real worker’s location. The activity was discovered through a suspicious job advertisement posted in the “Mouse Review” Discord server. The account, identified as “tecguru113,” promoted an offer seeking individuals who could become the public face of an IT job candidate. Silent Push researchers contacted the linked Telegram account , “Tecguru0618,” using a controlled persona to investigate the offer. The operator, known as “Tec Guru,” allegedly offered a revenue-sharing arrangement. The foreign proxy would receive 35% of the income, while 65% would go to the hidden worker. The proxy was expected to join video interviews, communicate with clients, and present technical skills that may not actually belong to them. Silent Push assessed with moderate to high confidence that the operator was a North Korean IT worker. The assessment was based on technical reference indicators, operational behavior, language patterns, and infrastructure-related evidence. During the conversation, the operator recommended Astrill VPN, a service previously linked by researchers to North Korean threat activity.
