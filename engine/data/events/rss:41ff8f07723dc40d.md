A security researcher has released a proof-of-concept exploit for a recently patched Android vulnerability, dubbed "SMS of Death" and tracked as CVE-2026-58882, that lets attackers remotely disrupt cellular service or crash phones by sending a specially crafted text message. The zero-click denial-of-service flaw can interrupt ongoing calls, including emergency calls, without requiring any interaction from the target. What's worse, Android processes the message before it reaches the SMS inbox, leaving victims with no visible indication of what caused their phones to crash. According to the vulnerability researcher, Mark Blah, Google reportedly patched the vulnerability in its October 2026 Android security updates for Android 14 through 17. However, the flaw is not listed in Google's October security bulletin, and older Android 12 and 13 devices remain vulnerable because they did not receive the fix. Blah published a technical analysis of the flaw, caused by improper validation of a header-length value in Android's WapPushOverSms.decodeWapPdu function. To exploit the flaw, an attacker can send the following malformed eight-byte WAP Push message to targeted phones: 01 06 88 80 80 80 00 BE → sent as a data SMS to port 2948 When this value is translated into a signed 32-bit integer without a bounds check, it becomes -2147483648.
