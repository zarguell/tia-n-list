Three men concocted an elaborate, yearslong scheme that defrauded companies of at least $129 million by using AI to impersonate executives and lawyers, tricking targeted victims into transferring large payments to criminal-controlled accounts, the Justice Department said Thursday. Jacob Soall, Sharon Hazan and Guy Sheetrit allegedly used AI to impersonate the voices and images of chief executive officers at companies and lawyers at major international law firms. The trio are accused of targeting thousands of victims between July 2024 and February 2026, according to court records. Victims include a U.K.-based financial tech company that lost almost $11.6 million, a Florida-based pet food company that lost $5.2 million, a Missouri-based hospital system that lost $4.8 million, a Massachusetts-based mutual fund company that lost $4.6 million, a California-based hospital system that lost nearly $3 million, a New York-based investment company duped out of $2.8 million, and a Rhode Island-based bank that transferred nearly $2.7 million. The trio’s scheme typically started by targeting accounting staff, pretending to be the CEO, and requesting help on a financial transaction, officials said. Using AI to create deepfakes pretending to be CEOs, the crew allegedly directed employees to contact attorneys who they would also impersonate to trick the employee into thinking the financial transaction was legitimate.
