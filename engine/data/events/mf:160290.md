As the US-Iran conflict oscillates between combat and ceasefire, urgent questions persist about how Russian technology, knowledge, talent, infrastructure, and other capabilities and resources are supporting Iranian unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) activity—and vice versa. Media investigations and open-source research have well-publicized the many touchpoints between the UAV sectors in Russia and in Iran. By August 2022, shortly after Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine, Iranian transport planes delivered Iranian-made combat drones to Russia, according to The Washington Post . Russia has subsequently launched thousands of Iran-made Shahed attack drones against Ukrainian targets. The US government publicly stated in June 2023 that Iran had “offered to provide UAV production technology and facilities” to Russia (as well as Tajikistan), on top of already having “transferred hundreds of Shahed- and Mohajer-series UAVs to Russia.” The Russian government then built such a UAV plant in Russia. Now, Russia is sending upgraded drones back to Iran, including with improved navigation capabilities, developed amid Russia’s full-scale war on Ukraine. These UAV touchpoints matter for the security activities and threats posed by the Russian and Iranian governments. In the former case, the Putin regime continues to rapidly innovate with UAV technology—including in response to Ukrainian innovation and adaptation—to continue its slaughter in Ukraine and against the Ukrainian people.
