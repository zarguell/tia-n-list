Allegations that Chinese intelligence targeted Nvidia’s chief executive are raising questions about how American technology companies can protect themselves from foreign spies and whether Washington is doing enough to help. Military investigative agencies told Nextgov/FCW they brief commercial AI firms, review research funding proposals for security risks and share warnings with defense contractors. But interviews with executives and current and former U.S. officials also highlight that government counterintelligence assistance is often uneven and one-sided, leaving businesses involved in economic and national security work to recognize threats they may be ill-equipped to detect. Some members of Congress expect to examine counterintelligence risks to the AI and tech industry next year, according to a Capitol Hill aide granted anonymity to discuss the planning. Scrutiny of the U.S. counterintelligence posture comes on the heels of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang, a business executive linked to Nvidia customer Megaspeed.
