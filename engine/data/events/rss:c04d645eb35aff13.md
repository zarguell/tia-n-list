In an industrial network, malicious activity can be hard to tell apart from normal data exchange between devices. Malicious actors routinely rely on legitimate tools and the Living-off-the-Land technique, so a single network connection may show no known signs of an attack: the device uses a familiar protocol and transmits ordinary data. An anomaly can hide in subtle details: the data source, the time of access, or the volume of transferred data may be atypical for a particular device. However, to detect this kind of anomaly, you need to understand the roles of the devices at both ends of the connection and know which network activity patterns are typical for them. But an organization’s asset inventory data is often incomplete, which makes it harder to catch unauthorized connections in time. Suppose monitoring detects a host that regularly exchanges data with programmable logic controllers (PLCs). Its IP address is known, but the inventory does not specify what role it plays: is it an engineering workstation, a process data server, or a piece of contractor’s equipment?
