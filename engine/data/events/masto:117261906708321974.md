Cybercriminals have published large amounts of data from the Berlin administration. Affected citizens can now access information and assistance on a services page.

The state of Berlin has established a central contact point for victims following the cyberattack with extensive data theft, the Senate Chancellery announced. Questions can be submitted via the email address Cyberangriff@senatskanzlei.berlin.de. The state portal berlin.de provides an information page for citizens who believe they are affected by the illegal publication of data by the criminals.

The offering includes background information on the cyberattack, behavioral guidance, and a FAQ section. The information page is continuously updated.

Additional email addresses are available for specific concerns: The cyberattack particularly affected the Senate Departments for Transport and Environment and for Urban Development, Building and Housing. Citizens with specific concerns related to these two administrations can contact Datenschutzvorfall@senmvku.berlin.de and Datenschutzvorfall@SenStadt.berlin.de.

The cyberattack on parts of the Berlin administration's data network was made public on August 14. The Rhysida group extorted the state, demanding 30 bitcoins, approximately two million euros. The Senate did not pay, and the cybercriminals published at least 1.2 million data records in the darknet after their deadline expired.
