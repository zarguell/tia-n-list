A Syrian man who prosecutors say was a central figure in a hacking operation run from former President Bashar Assad’s inner circle was sentenced Thursday to 42 months in federal prison. Ahmad Umar Agha, 42, carried out the conspiracy from as early as Aug. 24, 2011, until Jan. 31, 2024, according to the Justice Department. The operation was organized and led by members of Assad’s circle, including the commander of Syria’s General Intelligence Directorate. Agha hacked approximately 47 media entities, businesses, nongovernmental organizations and U.S. and allied government bodies, prosecutors said. The motives included punishing news organizations for reporting on Syrian government atrocities during the Arab Spring and the civil war that followed, spreading fear and confusion among the regime’s opponents, collecting intelligence and trying to persuade members of the U.S. military to refuse their orders. “Agha deployed his hacking skills in the service of Assad’s regime to silence reporting on the regime’s atrocities, intimidate its opponents, and incite U.S. servicemembers to refuse their orders,” said Assistant Attorney General for National Security John A. Eisenberg. The most damaging intrusion came April 23, 2013. Prosecutors said Agha accessed the Twitter account belonging to the Associated Press to post a false claim that President Barack Obama had been injured in two explosions at the White House.
