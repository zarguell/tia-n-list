Hackers are using fake Zoom installers, PDF reader updates, and meeting invitations to trick Windows users into installing legitimate remote management software. Microsoft Defender Experts observed these phishing campaigns in July 2026, targeting organizations across multiple industries. The attacks delivered MSP360 Remote Monitoring and Management (RMM) version 2.5.0.67 under misleading filenames. Although the installer was digitally signed and legitimate, attackers used it to gain unauthorized access to victim devices. After establishing access, the attackers installed ConnectWise ScreenConnect to create a second remote administration channel. Microsoft did not observe attackers exploiting a ScreenConnect vulnerability. Instead, they abused legitimate software to maintain access and carry out further operations. Fake Installers Deploy RATs The phishing emails directed victims to websites impersonating document portals, Adobe Reader download pages, Zoom installation pages, and business collaboration services. Other lures included job offers, delivery notices, invitations, and document signature requests. Payloads were hosted on attacker infrastructure, compromised websites, and legitimate services, including Amazon S3, Cloudflare R2, Dropbox , GitLab, and Supabase. Different filenames often concealed the same MSP360 installer. Once a victim opened the executable, the installer requested administrator privileges through Windows User Account Control.
