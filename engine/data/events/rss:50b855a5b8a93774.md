If you’ve been paying attention to enterprise edge security lately, the September 2026 Citrix NetScaler zero-day advisories probably gave you a familiar case of heartburn. But before everyone panics, let’s step back and look at the bigger picture: what we are seeing right now isn’t a collapse of edge defense. It is, in fact, exactly how modern active defense is supposed to work when high-value, internet-facing infrastructure comes under heavy fire. First, a quick refresher on why Citrix NetScaler ADC (Application Delivery Controller) and Gateway sit directly in the crosshairs of global threat actors. NetScaler is an everything-at-the-edge solution. It acts as a reverse proxy, load balancer, Web Application Firewall (WAF), traditional firewall, and SSL VPN all rolled into one appliance. Pretty much every major vendor in the perimeter space (F5, Cisco, Palo Alto Networks, Juniper, and more) went down this path of consolidation over the last decade or so. Instead of managing a bunch of standalone boxes chaining together these individual security controls, larger organizations clearly are showing a preference for a single platform to handle edge traffic. After all, it tends to be cheaper and easier to deploy just the one physical or virtual box from one vendor than to manage a constellation of security control devices.
