Ireland’s Data Protection Commission (DPC) has fined Google €403 million after concluding that the company violated the EU’s General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) through its handling of users’ location data. The enforcement action follows an own-volition inquiry launched in February 2020, after the regulator received complaints from European consumer-rights organizations, including BEUC. The DPC acted as Google Ireland Limited’s lead supervisory authority under the GDPR’s one-stop-shop mechanism. Google Fined €403 Million Over GDPR Violations The inquiry examined Google’s processing of location information between 25 May 2018, when the GDPR took effect, and 4 February 2020. It focused on three features: Web & App Activity, Location History, and Android’s Location Accuracy setting. According to the DPC , Google infringed GDPR requirements governing the lawfulness and fairness of processing related to Web & App Activity and Location History. The regulator also found that Google failed to demonstrate compliance with accountability obligations concerning Location Accuracy, particularly its ability to prove that its processing met the GDPR’s lawfulness, fairness, and transparency standards. The decision additionally identifies transparency failures across all three products. The DPC said users may not have understood the extent to which Google collected, retained, or used their location information to infer interests and influence advertising.
