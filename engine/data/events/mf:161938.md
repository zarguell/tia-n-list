Google has unveiled Android 17 network-security controls intended to reduce privacy leakage, restrict unauthorized discovery on home Wi-Fi, strengthen certificate validation, and blunt phishing campaigns delivered through rogue 2G cell sites. Announced August 27, the changes target weaknesses that remain exploitable even when users rely on encrypted apps, HTTPS browsing, and modern 5G service. The platform update combines protocol-level privacy improvements with application permission controls and carrier-managed cellular protections, extending defenses beyond individual user settings. Android 17 Adds New Network Security Protections At the transport layer, Android 17 adds support for Encrypted Client Hello (ECH) , a TLS mechanism designed to conceal a connection’s intended hostname during the initial handshake. HTTPS normally encrypts page content, but exposed domain-name metadata can still give network operators or local eavesdroppers visibility into destinations. When paired with Private DNS, ECH obscures that metadata for compatible sites and apps, reducing opportunities for profiling and more tailored phishing. Google said Android 17 is the first major mobile operating system to enable broad ECH support. Developers using OkHttp are advised to upgrade to version 5.5.0 and explicitly enable ECH, while teams operating custom networking stacks should validate HTTPS record-query behavior and deployment performance.
