In recent years, cyberthreats have increasingly taken a hybrid approach, combining time-tested techniques for advancing through infrastructure, modern methods of evading network filters, and automation powered by large language models. This is also the approach of Citrus Ransomware, a previously undocumented group whose toolset includes VLESS tunnels (used to disguise traffic as regular HTTPS) and a ransomware Trojan from the Sn00py family. The attackers also integrate LLMs (large language models) into the process of infrastructure compromise. In this article, we review the tactics and techniques the group used in recent incidents. Initial Access: attacks through contractors The attackers gain initial access to victims’ infrastructure through compromised contractors. They use credentials stolen from these contractors’ employees to connect to corporate VPNs or directly to internet-facing terminal servers over RDP. Execution: commands, LLMs, and Impacket The attackers run commands on compromised systems through the standard Windows command interpreter and PowerShell.
