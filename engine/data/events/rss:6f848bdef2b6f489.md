Two leading members of the Senate urged the White House on Tuesday to launch a national effort to more robustly confront AI-enabled cyber threats in light of recent security incidents involving artificial intelligence. In a letter to Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, Sens. Maria Cantwell (D-Wash.) and Mike Rounds (R-S.D.) warned that AI is “rapidly increasing the ability to discover and exploit weaknesses in software” and that the country’s defenses “are not keeping pace.” “The fundamental problem is that AI can discover software weaknesses and help attackers exploit them faster than many organizations can respond,” the senators said. “Even when fixes are available, testing and installing patches across interconnected systems can take weeks or months, potentially disrupting essential services.” The letter cites recent incidents where AI was used to discover obscure bugs and power cyber intrusions in Japan . The senators also raised concerns from banks about AI agents, which are increasingly accessing bank accounts. Several U.S. banks have said they are unable to tell whether AI agents are rogue or are accessing data on behalf of customers. Chinese-developed AI models are also being used widely for a variety of nefarious purposes, the lawmakers said. Rounds is the chairman of the Senate Armed Services subcommittee on cybersecurity and has repeatedly called on the White House to do more to confront risks posed by AI.
