An alternative to hard blocks is adapting protective mechanisms based on user behavior. That means: if you act according to company policy, you shouldn’t face very hard restrictions on what you can do with sensitive company information. But if you act irresponsibly, you should face the consequences. Below I’ll describe an approach on how this can be made possible in a few steps. Step 1: Discovery Most organizations have far more sensitive information than they realize (often in places they were not expecting to find it), so our first step is discovery (automatic and manual). Example: A colleague opens a Word document titled document1.docx and finds it contains negotiation points around an M&A target. Step 2: Sensitivity Classification Let’s start step 2 by acknowledging the fact that different types of information require different types of protection. A credit card number on its own is less sensitive than a credit card number alongside an expiration date and CVV, a product brochure is less sensitive than an email containing a termination notice, etc. Sensitivity classification is how we provide context into how much protection an information asset needs.
