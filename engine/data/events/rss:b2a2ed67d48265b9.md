The CEO of a ransomware recovery firm was indicted by a federal grand jury after prosecutors accused him of lying about his services and simply paying out ransoms to cybercriminals. Colombian national Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, 52, is the co-founder of a company based in Florida that marketed itself as a data recovery service for ransomware victims. Palacio claimed he had decryption tools that allowed him to help victims without negotiating or paying ransomware gangs. Instead, Palacio allegedly paid ransoms and charged his customers enough to make a profit. Federal agencies have increasingly sought to crack down on the murky industry of ransomware recovery and cybercriminal mediation in recent months. “As alleged, the defendant preyed on ransomware victims by falsely marketing a solution he did not possess, secretly negotiating with cybercriminals, and profiting from the difference,” said Charles Calenda, first assistant U.S. attorney. “Businesses and individuals seeking to recover from a cyberattack should not be victimized for a second time by those they turn to for help.” He is facing three counts of wire fraud, which carry a maximum sentence of 60 years in prison. Before the DOJ announced the charges, the news outlet 404 Media and independent journalist Brian Krebs published details of Escobar’s operation, writing that he ran a company called Ransomwarehelp.com.
