Threat actors have been hammering servers vulnerable to PHP2Shell for over a week, but only a few successful compromises have been confirmed to date. Standing out are Palo Alto Networks firewalls, which have been rooted by a China-linked hacking group in attacks chaining PHP2Shell with the Fragnesia kernel privilege escalation bug. Assisted by AI, the hackers chained two separate exploits: one specifically targeting the PHP2Shell flaws CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 on Palo Alto Networks’ PAN-OS devices, and another one for Fragnesia to deploy webshells with root privileges. Cybersecurity outfit Dream flagged the campaign earlier this week, noting that at least 25 firewalls out of a pool of hundreds were hacked. Palo Alto Networks has since confirmed the findings. “Dream Research has reported a campaign in which attackers chained PHP2Shell with a Linux kernel privilege-escalation vulnerability to gain root access. Unit 42 has independently verified the same attacker’s tooling and confirmed the reported results,” Palo Alto Networks said . The company has rolled out patches that resolve the PHP2Shell flaws in PAN-OS 12.2, 12.1, 11.2, 11.1, and 10.2 for PA-Series and VM-Series firewalls, and for Panorama (virtual and M-Series). “No special configuration is required to be affected by this issue.
