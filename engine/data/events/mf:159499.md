Ransomware operations are increasingly targeting the people behind critical business processes, not just privileged IT administrators. Over a one-month observation period, ThreatLabz identified 351 victims across 334 organizations connected to a single ransomware campaign. More than a dozen of those organizations had multiple employees compromised, indicating that an initial account takeover may be only the beginning of a coordinated intrusion rather than an isolated security event. The victims were not randomly selected. Nearly 62% held manager-level positions or above, while about 75% worked in accounting and finance, sales, operations, human resources, or marketing. These functions provide what researchers describe as business privilege: authority and routine access to systems, records, approvals, relationships, and information that can be just as useful to ransomware operators as traditional administrator privileges. A regional sales manager, for example, may routinely access customer accounts, commercial contracts, pricing data, forecasts, and sensitive communications. An accounts payable manager may handle vendor records, invoices, payment information, and approval workflows. A project manager may have access to budgets, roadmaps, internal documents, and systems tied to key product or expansion initiatives.
