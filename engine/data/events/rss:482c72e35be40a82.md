In this article Phishing attack overview Attack chain overview Mitigation and protection guidance References Learn More On November 13, 2026, Microsoft Threat Intelligence observed a coordinated, high-volume phishing campaign targeting organizations across multiple sectors worldwide. The threat actors used phishing emails disguised as legitimate Microsoft 365 service-health notifications to lure recipients into clicking a malicious link. The messages created a false sense of urgency by claiming that emails had been held during a service disruption and would be deleted within 24 hours unless the recipient took action to restore them. This is consistent with historical attacker campaigns which continuously look for notable global events to exploit in their social engineering lures. Microsoft identified the threat actor’s use of specific sender domain names, randomized outage subjects, recipient-specific tracking links, and a common non-Microsoft destination to make the messages appear relevant while complicating simple pattern matching. We assess that the activity was likely credential phishing; however, the available evidence does not confirm credential capture, malware delivery, or post-compromise activity. Multiple threat actors may continue to look for ways to exploit notable global events in any social engineering pretext.
