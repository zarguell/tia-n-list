Australia’s Parliament on Wednesday greenlit legislation creating sweeping online safety reforms that open the possibility of billions of dollars in fines for big tech companies that fail to preempt online harms before they occur. Known as the Digital Duty of Care Act, the legislation sailed through Parliament with strong backing from Prime Minister Anthony Albanese. His administration has been a global leader in fighting against online safety harms and spearheaded Australia’s pioneering strict age requirements for social media use that took effect in December 2025 and have since been copied by governments worldwide. The pending law will mandate that online providers establish protocols to ensure Australians are protected from threats of violence and rape as well as behavior that could cause significant harms. Platforms are required to detect and decrease risks from harmful content and must bolster protections for users under 18 against pornography, suicide and eating disorders. They also must undertake annual written risk assessments and call out major platform design changes such as endless scrolling that create new harms. Under the law, users also will be able to opt out of algorithmic feeds and will be required to prove their age to access porn. After the law passed, Albanese reportedly told Parliament it will be a “powerful instrument to protect our children and our society from online harms by placing the onus on the tech platforms to ensure a safer online environment.”
