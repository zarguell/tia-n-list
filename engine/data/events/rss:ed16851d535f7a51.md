Japan Airlines (JAL) has announced that a data breach at Amadeus’s Passenger Service System exposed the data of about 92,000 customers. JAL Group, which operates the airlines JAL and Japan Transocean Air, uses the system to handle passenger reservations, ticketing, and check-in. According to its announcement , Amadeus notified the airline on October 23, 2026, that unauthorized access to its systems occurred between August 10, 2023, and September 23, 2026. As a result of this lengthy breach, the threat actor accessed reservation codes (PNRs), names, flight dates, routes, and travel classes for approximately 90,000 customers. An additional 2,000 customers had the above data types exposed, plus their email addresses, phone numbers, passport numbers, nationalities, frequent flyer numbers, and flight, ticket, and seat numbers. JAL says credit card numbers and passwords were not compromised, and assured that it currently has no evidence of customer information being misused. The company said Amadeus has now implemented the necessary security measures and flight operations are unaffected. Amadeus is an IT firm that provides solutions for travel agencies, airlines, airports, and hotels. It is a major tech supplier to global aviation, used by over four hundred airlines , 210 airport operators, 34 cruise and ferry lines, and 2 million hotels.
