CLOSEDQUORUM, a malware binary discovered through Cisco Talos’ CAIRN project , exhibits fully autonomous command and control (C2). While we do not have confirmation of in-the-wild deployment, artifacts from the binary were used to connect the developer to postings on criminal forums related to carding, dating back to 2025. This malware is a useful reference example of how attackers can collapse the decision space of a particular attack phase into a constrained set of choices, allowing AI models to provide reasoning and act independently. CLOSEDQUORUM represents a shift in effort displacement for attackers, in which expanding portions of the attack chain can be executed without operator involvement. AI’s impact on offensive cyber operations has thus far mainly focused on two dimensions: speed and scale . Attackers can generate phishing lures faster and produce more malicious code variants with less effort. These are real effects, visible in the proliferation of AI-generated coding samples and agent-assisted intrusions that have become common in the past few years. But in each case, the human operator remains present: directing the tooling, selecting targets, and guiding the execution. AI makes the operator faster and more productive but does not remove them from the operation. A third dimension has received less attention in the malware space: effort displacement.
