A cyberattack attributed to Iran-linked hackers forced a small British power facility offline for four consecutive days in July, raising fresh concerns about the exposure of UK energy infrastructure to geopolitically motivated threats. The incident, first reported by The Telegraph , is believed to represent the first known case in which hackers affiliated with Iran successfully caused a UK power-generation site to shut down completely. While the disruption did not endanger the national electricity grid, officials and security analysts view it as a significant demonstration of capability against critical infrastructure. The Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ) confirmed that the event affected a “small-scale energy generator.” A department spokesperson stressed that the UK’s wider energy system remained secure and that the incident posed no risk to national supply. Government sources characterized the targeted generator as “less than a rounding error compared to grid capacity.” The facility was reportedly below the statutory threshold requiring major electricity generators to formally report significant cyber incidents.
