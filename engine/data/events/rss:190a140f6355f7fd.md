The New York attorney general (AG) has reached a settlement over a medical data breach that affected 13.3 million people after basic security failures. Perry Johnson & Associates (PJ&A) and four related companies have agreed to pay $300,000 and implement extensive security reforms following a breach that exposed personal and medical information belonging to more than 13.3 million people. They face another $9.7 million in penalties if they fail to meet the settlement’s terms. New York AG Letitia James announced the agreement on November 6, alongside the attorneys general of Connecticut, Michigan, and New Jersey. Approximately 4.2 million of the people affected were New York residents. The investigation describes a series of failures: remote access without multifactor authentication , nearly identical passwords circulated by email, sensitive files without file-level encryption, and security software that detected malicious activity but did not alert security staff. The result: the companies only discovered the attack when the attackers contacted them on May 2, 2023, and demanded payment. How many people were affected? The stolen database files contained records associated with more than 13.3 million people, including approximately 4.2 million New Yorkers. Earlier reporting put the number of affected patients at approximately 8.95 million. The settlement documents now give the larger figure of more than 13.3 million.
