One of the most challenging aspects of rolling out DLP rules is the friction involved, as with many other data protection tools. The work around DLP should therefore be viewed as an iterative process in which feedback is gathered from stakeholders across the organization: Is the action reasonable for the information? Is it relevant? Is it feasible in relation to existing workflows? Yet driving an implementation based only on theory is not always a winning approach. Start With a Hypothesis Many organizations already have assumptions about potential risks. This is particularly true for supporting functions such as IT and Security. People in these areas often have specialist expertise and insight into the organization’s systems and how they are used within their respective domains. General potential risks include: Personal data must not be shared externally without appropriate controls. Customer data should not be uploaded to personal cloud services. Financial reports must not be sent outside a defined group of recipients. Production schematics should not be copied to USB devices or shared through unknown channels.
