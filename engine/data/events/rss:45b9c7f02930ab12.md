A China-linked espionage group exploited a VMware vCenter zero-day vulnerability for more than two months before Broadcom released a patch on Thursday, according to Google. Tracked as CVE-2026-123890 (CVSS score of 9.8), the bug resides in the VMware Directory Service and could let remote, unauthenticated attackers obtain an authenticated session with administrator privileges. Mandiant, which reported the bug to Broadcom, has linked its exploitation to Armory Castle , a suspected Chinese threat actor formerly tracked as UNC5221 and also known as Warp Panda. According to Mandiant, the hackers gained initial access through CVE-2026-102764 , a recently patched BeyondTrust Remote Support zero-day. They then pivoted to Linux appliances on the internal network and targeted vCenter in early September. They used a custom tool called Bonesaw to exploit CVE-2026-123890, steal the Lightning Memory-Mapped Database (LMDB), extract SAML signing material, and create a single sign-on (SSO) administrator account. Next, the attackers deployed a second tool to forge SAML tokens and installed backdoors. They also took the vCenter master encryption key and database credentials, then moved to an ESXi host and extracted additional information. “Mandiant has evidence that Armory Castle had knowledge of CVE-2026-123890 prior to the disclosure of CVE-2026-59309 on July 29, 2026,” Google notes.
