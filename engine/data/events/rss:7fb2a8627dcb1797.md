Education technology platform Turnitin has disclosed a cyberattack involving certain customer-facing systems. A subsidiary of Advance Publications, Turnitin provides plagiarism checking solutions used by thousands of education and research institutions worldwide. “We are writing to let you know that we recently identified unauthorized activity involving certain customer-facing Turnitin systems,” CEO Chris Caren revealed in a November 23 letter on the company’s website. The company immediately initiated its incident response plan, notified the relevant authorities, and launched an investigation with support from external cyber experts. “We can confirm our systems are fully operational and safe to use and that these customer-facing systems do not collect government identification credentials or institutional financial information,” Caren said. The company continues to monitor the affected customer-facing systems while its investigation into the nature and scope of the incident continues. Customer-facing Turnitin systems include the submission portal for students, a grading interface for instructors, integration APIs for institutions, and reporting dashboards. The company did not say if any or which of these systems was breached.
