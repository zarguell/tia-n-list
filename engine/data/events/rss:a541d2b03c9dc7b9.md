Manufacturers seeking the U.S. Cyber Trust Mark for connected household devices would need to renew their products’ security certifications every three years under an FCC proposal, as the agency works to bring the long-delayed labeling program to consumers. The Federal Communications Commission is also considering whether baby monitors, home security cameras and products marketed to children should face more frequent checks after they go on sale because of the potential harm if hackers compromise them. The voluntary program, developed during the Biden administration, is intended to help shoppers assess the cybersecurity of internet-connected products, much as Energy Star helps them identify energy-efficient appliances. Qualifying products would carry a label with a QR code linking to information such as how long the manufacturer promises security support and how to configure the device safely.
