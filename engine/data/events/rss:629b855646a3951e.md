Federal authorities have charged 14 people and seized roughly 187 computers in their newest effort to disrupt North Korea’s remote IT worker scheme. The Justice Department announced the indictment Tuesday, highlighting two arrests and searches of more than 40 residences in Texas known or suspected to operate as “laptop farms,” where facilitators maintain employer-issued laptops so North Korean engineers can use them while abroad. An indictment unsealed in the Northern District of Ohio charges two U.S. citizens, Michael James Bostelle of Pennsylvania and Juan Cecilio Pena Carrillo of Texas. It alleges the two Americans, along with 10 others located in China, held remote jobs at more than 70 companies from 2019 through 2025 using stolen or fabricated identities, and collected about $3 million in pay. An Ohio resident, Trey Michael Nolte, is also named as a co-conspirator in the indictment, but is not charged. Two unnamed defendants were also charged. “This investigation is about far more than fraud,” Ryan Raybould, a U.S. attorney in the Northern District of Texas, said in a release. “It is about a hostile foreign regime infiltrating American companies through stolen identities and covert access points inside our own borders. Every laptop farm we dismantle shuts down a revenue stream that the North Korean government relies on to fund weapons programs that threaten global stability.
