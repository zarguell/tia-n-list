About 7,000 flights had been delayed or cancelled due to the stop, according to FlightAware, and 4,042 flights were in the air heading to U.S. airports when it was ordered, according to Flightradar24; the communication issue affected all 22 air route traffic control centres. The FAA's post on X described a 'temporary telecommunications outage,' with international flights handled case by case, and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication the stop was triggered by a threat. Bloomberg reported the FBI does not believe a cyberattack or criminal activity caused the failure but was examining whether a software update could have. Air Space Intelligence denied that its SMART system, developed under an $875 million contract awarded in June, was responsible, and Senator Tammy Duckworth called for a thorough and complete investigation.
