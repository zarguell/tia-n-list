Synchronizing Video, Audio, Whiteboard Events, and Distributed Backends at Scale What happens when thousands of students join a live classroom, rewind a lecture by 30 seconds, and still expect the teacher’s whiteboard drawings, voice, and video to remain perfectly synchronized? That challenge sits at the heart of modern real-time streaming systems. Live streaming today is no longer limited to one-way video broadcasting. Modern platforms power interactive classrooms, webinars, gaming streams, collaborative whiteboards, and real-time communication experiences where every participant expects low latency and seamless synchronization. But building these systems introduces difficult engineering problems: How do you synchronize whiteboard actions with live media? How do users seek backward without breaking context? How do backend pods communicate in a distributed architecture? How do you scale real-time communication across thousands of concurrent users? This article explores the architecture and engineering decisions behind scalable live streaming systems, focusing on: Real-time media streaming Whiteboard synchronization Distributed event propagation Seekable playback Horizontal scalability Cross-pod communication The goal is to move from what live streaming is , to why certain technologies are chosen , and finally into how production-scale systems solve real engineering challenges .
