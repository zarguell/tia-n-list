TL;DR Rotation is the last step: Rotating more than 1,000 service credentials across more than 150 applications with zero outages showed that most of the work happens before you change a single password. Controls built for people miss service credentials: Service credentials have no human to prompt, no MFA path, often no current owner, and they fail silently. Their copies also spread into scripts, config files, pipelines, and chat. Inventory first, then SCALER: Know who uses each account, who owns it, and whether it’s still needed. Then follow six stages in order: Scale, Classify, Assign, Lifecycle, Enforce, and Rotate. I once led the service account password rotation of more than 1,000 credentials across more than 150 applications. We finished with zero outages. The rotation itself was the easy part. Most of the work happened before we changed a single password. That order surprises people. Most teams treat rotation as the job. In my experience, rotation is the last step of a longer process. Skipping the earlier steps is how rotation causes outages. The gap a standard identity review misses Picture a typical identity review. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is on for every employee. Single sign-on covers the main applications. Network policies restrict who can reach production. The review passes. Now ask a different question. How many service accounts exist on that platform?
