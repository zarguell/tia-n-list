Hackers who breached the cloud environment of network and email security company Barracuda earlier this month used a stolen cloud access key and the ARTEX AI penetration testing framework. The company says the attacker accessed approximately 10 of its cloud accounts over a 44-hour period between November 1 and 2. The attacker's presence was discovered on November 4, and Barracuda's investigation determined that the open-source AI pentesting framework ARTEX was used for this "machine-speed intrusion." The company explains that the attacker leveraged a cloud access key provided to a third-party vendor for the duration of a previous project. "We did not retire the key when the vendor — a company working in AI — finished that work. That is on us," Barracuda says. The security company believes the attacker stole the key from the unnamed third-party AI vendor in October. It confirmed that the key was stored in a configuration file in the vendor's GitHub repository. The attacker used credentials they found in one system to move through Barracuda’s data analytics environment, source-code repositories, and Kubernetes clusters. Based on the attacker's commands targeting cryptocurrency-related secrets, including private keys, Ethereum keystore files, and seed phrases, Barracuda believes the attack was financially motivated.
