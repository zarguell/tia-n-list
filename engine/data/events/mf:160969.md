This article was co-authored by Gary Katz and Jason Deyalsingh In the prior article , we examined how Visibility, Detections, the Environment, and Threat Intelligence can each serve as starting points for identifying new detection opportunities, as well as how they can be used to filter and prioritize the detections worth pursuing. Having identified an opportunity, the next question is how best to detect it. This follow-on article delineates the different detection capabilities available to detection engineers, providing a framework for evaluating which technique is most appropriate for a given activity. There is no clear-cut answer. Different detection techniques require different types of data and levels of effort, while offering different tradeoffs in robustness, coverage, and complexity. Bianco’s Pyramid of Pain primarily focuses on atomic indicators and signatures, but today’s detection engineering discipline encompasses a much wider range of techniques for identifying malicious activity. The goal of this article is not to provide in-depth instruction on any one technique, but rather to provide detection engineers with an overview of the tools available in their toolbox, along with their strengths, limitations, and data requirements. By understanding the options available, detection engineers can make more informed decisions about which tool is best suited to the task at hand, rather than defaulting to the approach they know best.
