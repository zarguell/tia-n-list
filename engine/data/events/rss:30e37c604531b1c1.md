Intro A novel malware campaign known as PoeLLM has successfully compromised thousands of servers by hiding its command-and-control infrastructure within an innocent-looking GitHub poem . Security researchers at Black Lotus Labs discovered that the malicious software extracts specific words from the verse to dynamically calculate network addresses, bypassing conventional security detection tools. This sophisticated botnet primarily targets open-source artificial intelligence services for illicit activities such as exploit scanning and cryptocurrency mining. Furthermore, investigators noted that the operation establishes a resilient network of AI-enabled proxies that continually rotate their connections without requiring code updates. While the full scope of the threat actor’s objectives remains under investigation, linguistic clues in the code suggest possible Italian origins . Threat Overview The rapid operational adoption of open-source artificial intelligence (AI) framework utilities and proxy management software has introduced a novel vector for threat actors seeking to expand malicious botnet infrastructure. The PoeLLM malware campaign represents a critical inflection point for cybersecurity defenders, illustrating how threat actors can leverage non-traditional command-and-control (C2) mechanisms combined with aggressive targeting of exposed AI services.
