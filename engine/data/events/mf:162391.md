Threat actors are abusing legitimate remote-management tools, including ConnectWise ScreenConnect and Microsoft Quick Assist, to install persistent backdoors on Windows devices. Huntress researchers identified several incidents in late August involving social-engineering lures, rogue ScreenConnect clients, VBScript payloads , and attempts to weaken endpoint defenses. The campaigns affected unrelated organizations but followed a similar pattern. Victims were tricked into granting remote access or downloading a malicious ScreenConnect installer. In one case, attackers posed as technical support staff and convinced a user to open Microsoft Quick Assist and share an access code. Another incident likely began with phishing, while a third used a fake Geek Squad refund lure. After gaining access, the attackers installed unauthorized ScreenConnect clients that connected to command-and-control infrastructure. Observed IP addresses included 45.13.237[.]190, 131.123.40[.]98, and 15.204.185[.]204. Researchers also observed the use of UltraViewer remote desktop software in one environment. ScreenConnect Quick Assist Backdoors The rogue ScreenConnect clients launched Windows Script Host ( wscript.exe ) to execute four VBScript files named 1.vbs , 2.vbs , 3.vbs , and 4.vbs . The scripts formed a staged loader designed to profile the device, download encrypted payloads, decrypt them, and establish persistence.
