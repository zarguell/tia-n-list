The Carnegie Endowment for International Peace said a breach at a vendor it uses for online donations exposed personal contact information and donor giving histories, affecting a prominent foreign policy research institution that is led by former U.S. intelligence chief Avril Haines. An unauthorized party breached the unnamed vendor from Oct. 28 to Oct. 30 and gained access to some information in Carnegie’s donor management and communication database, according to an email notice reviewed by Nextgov/FCW . Carnegie said the information included names, email addresses, phone numbers and, for donors, giving histories. Full credit card information was not exposed. The organization described the affected contacts as a “small percentage” of its database but did not provide a count or identify the vendor. It said it was notifying everyone in the database as a precaution, including people whose information may not have been accessed. The notice did not identify the intruders responsible. The think tank hired an outside cybersecurity firm to ensure the cyberintruder had been removed, the email adds.
