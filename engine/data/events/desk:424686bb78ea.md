Microsoft Security Research (Yash Gund and Sumith Maniath) published the primary disclosure on GSocket abuse, reporting that multiple unrelated threat actors use the open-source GSocket tool and its gs-netcat client to keep encrypted remote access to compromised Linux servers after the initial vulnerability is patched. Microsoft Defender for Endpoint telemetry shows GSocket-related detections rose 356.2% from March to August 2026, with web applications and hosting accounting for 87.1% of attributable incidents and affected organizations concentrated in education and research (30.7%) then technology and telecommunications (22.9%). Because traffic is relayed through the shared Global Socket Relay Network with outbound-only connections, there is no attacker-owned IP or domain to block. In some incidents actors attempted to exploit CVE-2026-43284 (Dirty Frag) to escalate to root and disguised GSocket with kernel-thread-like process names, persisting via cron, systemd, and shell profiles.
