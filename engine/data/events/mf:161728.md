Corporate executives’ most sensitive identity data is being sold on dark-web marketplaces for as little as $0.25 per record. Documented by Rapid7, the finding highlights an increasingly efficient criminal market in which permanent identity attributes, not just passwords or payment cards, are packaged, enriched, and resold for fraud and corporate intrusion. Unlike a stolen credit card, which a bank can cancel quickly, a compromised U.S. Social Security number (SSN) cannot be replaced easily. Hackers Can Buy Corporate Executives’ Social Security Numbers That makes it a durable asset for cybercriminals, who can combine it with addresses, birth dates, phone numbers, employment details, and publicly available biographical information to impersonate high-value individuals. S ample distribution of leaked SSNs by sector (Source: Rapid7) Since early 2026, Rapid7 has tracked 476 compromised SSN records associated with 395 unique corporate personnel. The exposure disproportionately affected senior leadership: C-suite executives represented 44.6% of the leaked profiles, while company presidents accounted for another 28.6%. The data is largely U.S.-centric, reflecting the SSN’s role as a national identifier. Rapid7 found that 95.6% of the observed leaks involved U.S.-headquartered companies. Financial services organizations accounted for more than one-quarter of affected companies, followed by industrial-sector organizations at 17%.
