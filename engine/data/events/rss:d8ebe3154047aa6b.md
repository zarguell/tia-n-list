Compromised passwords and gaps in multi-factor authentication (MFA) remain common ways attackers gain access to sensitive systems. For law enforcement agencies handling criminal justice information (CJI), protecting against these threats is also a matter of CJIS compliance. The FBI's latest CJIS Security Policy sets clear requirements for password management and MFA. these are Priority 1 (P1) controls, so must be implemented immediately to comply. But this can be challenging, particularly across the complex IT environments that are common in law enforcement. This guide contains practical tips to help agencies meet the latest requirements, strengthen their defenses against credential-based attacks, and make ongoing compliance easier to manage. CJIS requires user-selected passwords to be at least eight characters long, or six characters if generated by a credential service provider or verifier using an approved random number generator. However, we recommend going beyond these minimums and encouraging passphrases of at least 15 characters. Longer passwords are generally harder to crack, and passphrases can be easier to remember than shorter passwords with complex combinations of symbols and numbers. CJIS doesn't require traditional complexity rules, so focus on length and strength rather than arbitrary character requirements.
