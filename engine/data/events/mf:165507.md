Signal, the secure messaging app, released version 8.30, completing the rollout of its secure backups feature across all supported operating systems (Android, iOS, Linux, macOS, and Windows). The feature, introduced on Android nearly a year ago, lets users create end-to-end encrypted backups of their chats and restore them on other devices if they lose their phone. The backup system supports two options: Signal-hosted backups, limited to 45 days of media and 128 KB per message for free users (100 GB in paid plans), and on-device, local backups, with no size restrictions. Backups for both options are encrypted and require a recovery key to decrypt, but hosted backups also have a supplemental key that rotates daily inside a Trusted Execution Environment for additional protection and forward secrecy. According to Signal, an on-device backup recovery key can decrypt all past backup files it encrypted, regardless of where they are stored. Threat actors have already taken notice of the key's value and incorporated it into their targeting scope shortly after Signal introduced the feature. The latest update brings on-device backups to iOS and desktop, and revamps Android backups to use the same cross-platform format.
