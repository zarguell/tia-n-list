China-based AI firms are allegedly conducting large-scale campaigns to extract capabilities from leading U.S. artificial intelligence models, according to a joint cybersecurity advisory from the NSA, CISA, and FBI. The agencies said the activity goes beyond normal research-oriented model distillation and involves coordinated attempts to obtain proprietary reasoning, coding, agentic, and domain-specific functions. The advisory attributes the campaigns to companies including DeepSeek , Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun, and Z.AI. It states that these firms allegedly extracted billions of tokens through millions of interactions with U.S. frontier models, including variants of Anthropic’s Claude, OpenAI’s GPT, Google’s Gemini, and xAI’s Grok. Chinese Firms Abuse AI Knowledge distillation is a legitimate AI development method in which a smaller “student” model learns from the outputs of a more capable “teacher” model. However, the agencies warned that the reported campaigns use unauthorized access methods at an industrial scale to create synthetic training datasets. The operators allegedly accessed models through native APIs , remote cloud providers, third-party aggregators, account pools, and gray-market proxy services known as “transfer stations.” These services can conceal user metadata, bypass geographic limits, and make it harder for AI providers to link high-volume requests to a single organization.
