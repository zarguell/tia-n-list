Identity has become a critical attack surface for modern enterprises. Human identities, non-human identities, secrets, and AI Agents operate across cloud, SaaS and on-premises environments. Excessive permissions, dormant accounts and misconfigured access policies leave organizations vulnerable to identity-based attacks. According to the 2026 Unit 42 Global Incident Response Report , identity weaknesses played a role in nearly 90% of investigations, highlighting how attackers continue to exploit gaps in identity security. Yet identity security remains divided between two disciplines. Cloud security teams focus on preventing risk by identifying and reducing excessive permissions, misconfigurations and exposed identities. Identity Security Operations teams focus on detecting suspicious activity and responding to identity-based threats. The challenge is that attackers don't operate within these boundaries. An overprivileged account identified by a cloud security team today could become the compromised identity a SOC analyst investigates tomorrow. Without shared context, security teams are left connecting the same identity risks across separate tools and workflows. Effective identity security requires understanding both where identities are exposed and when that exposure becomes an active threat.
