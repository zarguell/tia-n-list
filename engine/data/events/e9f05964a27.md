Symantec has documented a broad Seedworm campaign targeting at least nine organizations across government, manufacturing, finance, aviation, and education sectors on four continents. The campaign centers on a week-long intrusion into a major South Korean electronics manufacturer in February 2026, during which the Iranian MOIS-linked group spent seven days performing reconnaissance, credential theft via fake Windows prompts and registry hive extraction, and persistent access through registry modifications and 90-second beacon intervals. The group's tooling has evolved: rather than direct PowerShell execution, payloads are now controlled through Node.js loaders. Most notably, Seedworm abused legitimate signed binaries — Fortemedia's `fmapp.exe` audio utility and SentinelOne's `sentinelmemoryscanner.exe` — to sideload malicious DLLs (`fmapp.dll` and `sentinelagentcore.dll`) containing the ChromElevator post-exploitation tool for Chrome-based data theft. Data exfiltration uses `sendit.sh`, a public file-sharing service, to blend with legitimate traffic.
