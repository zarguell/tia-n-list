The Treasury Department has taken some systems offline after a cyberattack, according to a notice posted Tuesday evening. In a notice posted at the top of the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) E-Filing System website, the department said its Financial Crimes Enforcement Network — commonly known as FinCEN — is “investigating a cybersecurity incident.” The incident “resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems,” according to the banner. “As a proactive measure, FinCEN has taken some systems offline. The BSA E-Filing website remains operational; however, some services may be temporarily unavailable,” the agency said. “FinCEN is working diligently to return all systems to normal operations as soon as possible.” The incident was first reported by The Verge. FinCEN said it is extending deadlines for filers affected by the outages. It urged financial institutions to call a hotlin if they need to send suspicious activity reports to law enforcement. The Treasury Department and FinCEN did not respond to requests for comment. No hackers have taken credit for the attack, but several Chinese state-backed groups were accused of breaching the Treasury Department multiple times in 2024 and 2025 .
