Drupal is urging online store owners to patch a highly critical access bypass vulnerability in the Commerce Core module, warning that it is being exploited in the wild. Tracked as CVE-2026-118539 , the issue affects a contributed module that adds e-commerce features to Drupal websites, including a shopping cart and checkout process. Drupal’s statistics show the module is used on more than 42,000 sites. “The module doesn’t sufficiently protect certain routes and entity fields, leading to an access bypass that may financially impact the vendor,” Drupal explains in its advisory. The Drupal security team gave the issue a risk score of 21 out of 25. According to the score, unauthenticated attackers can exploit the flaw no matter how the module is set up. Drupal has confirmed the attacks but has not said how the bug is being exploited, who is behind the activity, or how many sites have been targeted. It has also not said whether the vulnerability was exploited as a zero-day, before patches became available. The vulnerability affects 3.x versions before 3.3.13 and earlier releases before 8.x-2.41. Administrators should update to one of the two fixed versions and review their store orders. “Sites should audit their orders for inconsistencies as this vulnerability is being actively exploited,” the advisory reads.
