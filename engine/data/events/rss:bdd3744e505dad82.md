State-backed hacking groups in China and North Korea are using a powerful AI hacking tool in attacks, according to a new report from Microsoft. Microsoft explained that state-sponsored and financially motivated hackers are using Artex, an AI-powered platform that can automate multiple phases of cyber intrusions at machine speed. The study emerged hours after The New York Times and NextGov reported that Artex was allegedly used in attacks on the CIA, the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence. Microsoft said on Wednesday that Chinese state-backed groups Crystal Typhoon and Salt Typhoon have integrated Artex into their suite of tools, using it for “network reconnaissance, credential attacks, and web application exploitation.” “In the past few weeks, we’ve reported several distinct campaigns that abused Artex to search public repositories for exposed secrets, enable supply chain compromise, and scan internet-facing systems for exploitation,” Microsoft explained. Salt Typhoon is a prolific Chinese hacking group that U.S. agencies believe was behind the breach of at least nine telecommunications giants in 2024. The hack alarmed White House officials in 2024 because it allegedly gave China’s hackers access to communications from the phones of President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance as well as several Democratic leaders.
