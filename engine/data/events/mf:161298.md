Microsoft is rolling out a new Microsoft Teams security control that will allow administrators to automatically block identified external bots from joining meetings . The feature is aimed at AI notetakers, transcription services, recording bots, and virtual assistants that can enter calls and capture sensitive business information. Microsoft says the update provides organizations with a stronger way to prevent unauthorized automated participants from accessing confidential conversations, shared content, chat messages, and meeting transcripts. Microsoft Teams Lets Admins Automatically Block The capability was detailed in Microsoft 365 Message Center notice MC1459141, published on August 21, 2026. It builds on Teams’ bot-identification protections , which Microsoft began introducing earlier in 2026. Those protections use behavioral and infrastructure signals to help distinguish meeting bots from human attendees. Until now, however, administrators could only route detected bots into a meeting lobby and leave the final admission decision to the meeting organizer. That earlier model created an opportunity for human error. In back-to-back meetings, organizers may quickly select “Admit all” without closely inspecting every name in the lobby. A bot disguised with a generic display name could therefore be admitted alongside legitimate participants.
