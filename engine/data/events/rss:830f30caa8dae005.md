The threat actor linked to recent AI-assisted attacks on South Korean financial institutions was testing infrastructure and assembling attack tools as early as August 2026, weeks before the banking campaign became public, according to new research from Recorded Future’s Insikt Group. Subsequent public reporting indicated that the breaches exposed personal data belonging to at least 68,000 people across at least seven financial institutions. Insikt Group’s investigation expands the known timeline and capabilities of the actor, tracked as TAG-217. Insikt Group uncovered tools designed to steal cryptocurrency wallet files, gain greater access to Linux systems, and test weaknesses in mobile devices. The findings illustrate the “ jagged edge ” of AI-enabled attacks: threat actors realizing significant gains while committing basic operational security mistakes. AI helps attackers pursue more targets across more environments, but does not prevent them from making the security mistakes that allowed Recorded Future to uncover preparations and capabilities beyond the reported banking campaign. Those mistakes give defenders opportunities to identify related activity and disrupt operations. Targeting South Korean Banks In early October 2026, South Korea’s Financial Services Commission (FSC) disclosed that multiple financial institutions had been hit by cyberattacks.
