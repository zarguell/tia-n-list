October is Cybersecurity Awareness Month, and it’s a good time to think about how much the world has changed for chief information security officers (CISOs) in the last 12 months. A year ago, we thought we were already in the thick of AI and starting to understand it. Since then, agents have spread across the enterprise faster than most organizations anticipated. According to our Work Trend Index Annual Report , the number of active agents in the Microsoft 365 ecosystem grew 15-fold year over year and 18-fold in large enterprises. Employees are building and deploying AI systems that can access company data, interact with applications, and take actions on their behalf. These systems are built with good intentions, but even well-intentioned agents can introduce risk when access and behavior are not appropriately governed. At the same time, more people inside our companies are building with AI. Many of them haven’t written code in years, if ever, and they aren’t necessarily thinking about security the way a security team would. That isn’t a criticism. It’s how quickly the technology has expanded who can build and what they can accomplish. For CISOs, this creates two fundamental shifts. We have to protect an enterprise that increasingly operates through AI and agents. And we have to rethink how we defend that enterprise as attackers use AI to move faster and operate at greater scale. When I talk with CISOs, these are the two challenges I keep coming back to.
