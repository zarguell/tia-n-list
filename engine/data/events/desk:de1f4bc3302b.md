Germany's BSI said at least 30,000 internet-connected devices sold in the country carried BADBOX malware in their firmware, including digital photo frames and streaming devices. The agency sinkholed the control domains so the devices could no longer reach the operators, and said German ISPs with more than 100,000 customers were required to redirect BADBOX traffic to the sinkhole. The malware could create email and messenger accounts, support advertising fraud and turn household connections into proxies; affected users were told to disconnect their devices.
