Mexican authorities seized roughly 300 GPUs from a clandestine cryptocurrency mine hidden in the mountains of Tlaola municipality, Puebla state, near a hydroelectric plant. Investigators are checking whether the operation stole electricity and whether the mined assets were used for money laundering. Mining itself is not illegal in Mexico; the investigation centers on the origin of the power and the legality of the asset acquisition.

Per Reuters reporting from September 12, 2026, the site sat in hard-to-reach terrain near hydroelectric infrastructure and held about 80 medium-voltage terminals, eight satellite antennas, and a transformer, all pointing to industrial-scale power consumption. The satellite dishes provided connectivity in an area with limited cabled internet. It is the fourth similar site found in this zone since the start of 2025. Officials from the Attorney General's Office, the Mexican Navy, and Puebla's Public Security Secretariat took part in the raid. No authority has said which cryptocurrency was mined, disclosed associated wallets, or announced arrests. Puebla security chief Francisco Sánchez González said energy consumption, noise audible from a kilometer away, and the building's remote location (about two kilometers from the nearest village) drew attention while authorities monitored mining reports near the Nuevo Necaxa dam. Neither the Federal Electricity Commission nor the prosecutors has confirmed the illegal grid connection, and the inspection report and loss estimates have not been published.

Samuel León, an electricity-theft expert at Universidad Iberoamericana, told Reuters that stolen power effectively zeroes out the main operating cost of a mining farm. Comparable cases elsewhere include Malaysian police seizing 73 mining devices in electricity-theft raids in July 2026 and Thai authorities confiscating 996 devices after confirming meter tampering.
