eSentire’s Threat Response Unit (TRU) has uncovered a previously undocumented device-code phishing kit, dubbed “GhostCode,” that abuses Microsoft’s OAuth 2.0 device authorization grant flow to hijack M365 accounts , mint Primary Refresh Tokens (PRTs), and silently enroll rogue devices all within roughly 78 seconds of a victim completing MFA. GhostCode M365 Tokens The campaign, first observed in late August 2026, begins with social engineering rather than malware. Threat actors posed as a procurement officer for BJ’s Wholesale Club and submitted a benign inquiry through a target company’s Salesforce contact form, using a 15-day-old lookalike domain, bjssourcing[.]com. TRU identified over 30 similar impersonation domains registered in August 2026 targeting distributors, manufacturers, and warehousing firms, suggesting a broader BEC-style campaign. After the sales team responded, the attackers followed up with an “NDA” pretext and a WeTransfer link to a password-gated HTML file (“3arhCt9c0p.html”) disguised as a “FlipBook” document viewer.
