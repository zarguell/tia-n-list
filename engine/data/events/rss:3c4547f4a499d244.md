Microsoft collaborated with Japanese authorities, Health-ISAC and several cybersecurity companies to dismantle an expansive, seemingly disconnected ecosystem of Chinese-language cybercrime tools and services, the company announced Wednesday. Since the start of the year, a vast collection of customized phishing kits developed and sold by Chinese-speaking threat actors — a cluster Microsoft tracks as Storm-2990 — have delivered more than 1 billion phishing emails and SMS messages to more than 75,000 organizations globally. More than three-quarters of those malicious messages targeted Microsoft customers, the company said. Cybercriminals purchased the phishing kits, tools and infrastructure from an interconnected network Microsoft and Japanese authorities started investigating in early 2025. The kits were used to target individuals and organizations in healthcare, financial services, logistics and IT predominantly in the United States and Japan. Microsoft said it and Health-ISAC, working with a federal court order, seized and redirected domains that powered the underlying infrastructure of these cybercrime tools and services known as YAM Store, CoGUI and Iron Man.
