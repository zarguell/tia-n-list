Google Chrome has reduced abusive notifications on Android by more than 7 billion per day during the first quarter of 2026, according to a new technical overview from Chrome Security, Safe Browsing , and Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM). The milestone reflects a multi-year effort to curb a web feature frequently exploited by scammers, malware distributors, and fraud operators. While browser notifications can provide legitimate real-time updates from websites, threat actors have increasingly weaponized them to deliver deceptive alerts, fake security warnings, credential-harvesting lures, and fraudulent payment prompts. Google Chrome Blocks 7 Billion Abusive Notifications Chrome’s response is not based on a single detection mechanism. Instead, Google describes the system as a “Swiss cheese” defense-in-depth model, in which overlapping controls target abuse throughout the notification lifecycle from permission acquisition. One key layer automatically removes notification permissions from sites users have not recently engaged with. This limits the long-term impact of sites that obtained consent through misleading prompts, social engineering , or prompt-fatigue tactics. Chrome also revokes permissions from websites that repeatedly generate suspicious notification warnings. Users retain control over the process: revoked permissions can be reviewed and re-enabled through Chrome’s Safety Hub if a site is considered trustworthy or useful.
