In the midst of Black October, I had an idea. It came to me after listening to Michael Aiello, OpenAI’s cyber executive in residence, talk about the AI slop overwhelming open-source maintainers with vulnerability disclosure. I found there was an underlying message throughout his remarks: It was so remarkably easy for people to find vulnerabilities with AI-assisted cybersecurity tools. To drive it home, he at one point referred to AI agents as having the mindset of “a BMX biker gang made up entirely of 12-year-olds.” The joke set off a light bulb in my head: If this guy is talking about this technology this way, how hard could it be to find vulnerabilities? Could I find one? Turns out the answer is yes, I could. And the process by which I found one was easier than I could have imagined. Over the course of six hours and the use of two freely available LLMs, I managed to discover, test, and submit a vulnerability report for a WordPress plugin that has over 40,000 active installations. The vulnerability — a cross-site request forgery — was validated 24 hours after submission to a third party , and assigned a CVE entry: CVE-2026-124251 . The following is a diary of this discovery, which I believe can provide some perspective to the current landscape of AI-assisted vulnerability discovery.
