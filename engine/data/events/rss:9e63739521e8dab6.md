Palo Alto Networks on Tuesday announced PAN-OS updates that patch the Fragnesia Linux kernel vulnerability exploited to root its firewalls. Tracked as CVE-2026-46300, Fragnesia was disclosed in May 2026 as a privilege escalation bug in the kernel’s XFRM ESP-in-TCP subsystem. It allows unprivileged attackers to overwrite sensitive system files to gain root permissions. On October 26, cybersecurity firm Dream warned that a Chinese-speaking threat actor successfully exploited the critical PHP2Shell bugs and the Linux kernel weakness to hack two servers of a US government-affiliated organization. As part of the AI-assisted campaign, the attackers compromised 25 servers (24 firewalls and one internal firewall management server), exploiting PHP2Shell to gain remote code execution (RCE), and executing a Fragnesia exploit to deploy webshells with root privileges. On October 29, Palo Alto Networks confirmed that the threat actor hit PAN-OS firewalls with management interfaces exposed to the internet in attacks chaining PHP2Shell with Fragnesia . By October 30, the company had released patches for PHP2Shell across all supported PAN-OS versions, closing the known initial-access path. “If you are running a fixed PAN-OS listed in the CVE-2026-109618 [PHP2Shell] advisory, you are protected,” Palo Alto Networks notes in a Tuesday advisory .
