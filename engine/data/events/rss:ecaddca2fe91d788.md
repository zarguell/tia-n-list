TL;DR Label the union, not one engine’s results: checking whether engine B finds what engine A found only measures B’s recall on A’s findings. It says nothing about what A misses. Valid-only benchmarks miss most of the problem: generic secrets like passwords and custom tokens can’t be validity-checked, and they make up around 70% of what we detect in customer environments. Test at production scale: strong performance on 500 files may not translate to your entire git history. For context, processing approximately 0.5 TB per day for our customers, a pure LLM scan would cost over $10,000 daily, compared to just a few tens of cents for a rule-based engine.
