Email gateways, endpoint controls, and traditional file-centric sandboxing remain essential components of an enterprise defense strategy. But as phishing campaigns shift away from malicious file attachments and move entirely into the browser, they create a major visibility gap for security operations centers (SOCs). This critical vulnerability serves as the primary gateway for one of the most financially devastating threats facing modern enterprises: Business Email Compromise (BEC). The Scale of the BEC Threat The business impact of this visibility gap is substantial. According to the FBI’s IC3 2025 report, Business Email Compromise caused $3.05 billion in reported losses in a single year. This aligns with a broader surge in enterprise breach severity, with IBM’s Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025 revealing that the average cost of a data breach has reached $4.8 million. Phishing remains the primary entry point for these enterprise breaches, but the nature of the attack is shifting. Verizon’s 2025 Data Breach Investigations Report attributes 53% of breaches to phishing-related credential theft, a trend accelerated by technological evolution. ENISA’s Threat Landscape 2025 reports that 80% of social engineering attacks now use AI-generated phishing, making malicious emails increasingly difficult to distinguish from legitimate business communications.
