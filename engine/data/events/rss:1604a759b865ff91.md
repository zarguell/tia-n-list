Anthropic recently rolled out Claude Mythos 5.2 to selected partners and Fable 5.2 to regular users, with improvements to coding and reasoning. But we also noticed some interesting cybersecurity changes. Anthropic describes Mythos 5.2 as its "most capable model for cybersecurity engineering," which means it's now ahead of Fable 5.2 in security research. However, that doesn't mean Fable 5.2 misses out on security restrictions, as it does come with cybersecurity safeguards, which block most potentially dangerous hacking activities. According to Anthropic, Mythos 5.2 beats Fable 5.2 at finding vulnerabilities, reproducing them, and developing fixes. This makes sense, as Mythos is designed for organizations working on advanced cybersecurity tasks. The company also shared some numbers. For example, Anthropic's CVE Rediscovery evaluation found that the Mythos 5.2 model can detect 10.2% more vulnerabilities than Claude Opus 5.5 and 14.4% more than Mythos 5.1. Moreover, a security company told Anthropic that "one in six confirmed bugs" identified by Mythos 5.2 had been missed by every other frontier model it tested. Although Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 are based on the same underlying parent model, there are obvious differences, and it's totally due to their intended use cases. Unlike Mythos, Anthropic applied additional cybersecurity and biology safeguards to Fable so it can be made available to regular users.
