At school James Robinson was more sporty than STEM, but after school he rose from the track to cybersecurity leader at Netskope. The journey to becoming a CISO The young James Robinson attended a small high school in Central Illinois and describes himself as ‘more sporty than STEM’: “I wasn’t going to knock it out of the park in the English class – but luckily the English teacher was also the track coach, so that helped.” In his final year in high school, the principal suggested going on to something vocational or trades-related rather than university. A friend was already in a vocational school doing electronics engineering, so he went that route. That school had just been included in the Cisco Networking Academy (NetAcad). Robinson threw himself into NetAcad learning. “It was like the world suddenly became my oyster. There was no stopping me. I started doing networking at home, got connected to the internet, and studied and studied.” This was the late 1990s while he was still at school. After school, by the early 2000s, he became a network engineer at State Farm Insurance, but now with a Cisco Certified Internetwork Expert (CCIE) certification. He decided to join Cisco itself. At that time, Cisco was the darling of the tech boom, briefly being the most valuable company in the world with a market cap in excess of $500 billion. But then the dot-com bubble burst.
