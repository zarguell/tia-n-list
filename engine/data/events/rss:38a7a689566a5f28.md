In about a month, five named memory-safety bugs hit foundational software: PHP2Shell (PHP), Brotli2Shell (the Brotli C decoder), Java4Shell (OpenJDK HotSpot), nginx2root (nginx HTTP/2), and Ruby2Shell (Ruby's C runtime). Four of the five are typically reached through a memory-safe language or framework. All five sit in C, C++, or hand-written assembly. The pattern is not “memory-safe languages failed.” It is narrower and more useful: the attacker-reachable code is the native layer underneath the safe language, and AI has made that layer cheap to audit. Rust and Go move the boundary; they do not remove it.
