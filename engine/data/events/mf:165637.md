Election officials said Wednesday that renewed federal cybersecurity assistance comes too late for some of their midterm preparations, following cuts they say disrupted support from the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, though they are not closing the door on federal help. Michael Moore, chief information security officer for the Arizona secretary of state’s office, said the state recently reconnected with CISA’s regional leadership and plans to accept its support on Election Day if cyberattacks disrupt election services. “We haven’t had a cybersecurity advisor in Arizona since March 2025,” Moore said at a briefing hosted by the Partnership for Large Election Jurisdictions, or PLEJ. “When it comes to the services that they’ve recently offered, it is much too late,” he said. “We cannot utilize some of the services that they offer.” CISA CISA pledges election security support after cuts weakened ties with states - Nextgov/FCW ”> released an election security plan last week that assigns its 10 regional directors an election adviser role and describes a free threat-sharing platform connecting election officials, state intelligence hubs and federal partners.
