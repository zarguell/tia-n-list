How to Scan for Malware: The Quick Answer The 5-step answer in one paragraph To scan for malware, update your system, run a full scan with Windows Security, then run an offline scan to catch hidden threats. Next, manually check running processes, startup items, and browser extensions, and confirm with a second reputable scanner. If anything turns up, change your passwords from a clean device. If you are not sure what you are looking for, our guide to what malware is explains the basics, and the steps below work for most Windows computers. Scan vs check vs detect: what each term really means Scanning, checking, and detecting describe three layers of the same job. A scan is an automated sweep of files and memory by security software. Checking is a manual review of things like processes, startup items, and browser settings. Detection is the result: the moment software or a person decides that something is malicious. A thorough approach uses all three. How long does a malware scan take? A quick scan usually takes a few minutes, and a full scan can take from under an hour to several hours depending on how many files the device holds.
