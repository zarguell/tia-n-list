Uber is notifying roughly 150,000 drivers that their personal information was stolen in a data breach involving a third-party services provider. The company learned of the incident on October 9 and immediately severed its data connections with the provider and launched an investigation. On October 13, the investigation determined that the hackers used compromised service provider credentials on October 7 to access data Uber had shared with the provider. According to Uber, the hackers accessed images of driver’s licenses and personal information such as names, addresses, dates of birth, driver’s license numbers, and government-issued ID numbers. The company says that the data was stored in an environment created and managed by Uber’s Cloud Security team. While alerts were enabled, none were triggered because the threat actor used valid credentials. “The service provider’s compromised credentials did not provide access to other Uber systems. The investigation remains ongoing,” the company said. Uber has filed data breach notifications with multiple US states, including California, Iowa, Massachusetts, South Carolina, Texas, and Vermont, and reportedly notified the Texas Attorney General’s Office that a total of 149,346 people were affected.
