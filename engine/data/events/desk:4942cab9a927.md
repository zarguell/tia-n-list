Japan Airlines said Amadeus IT Group, which runs its flight reservation, ticketing and check-in systems, informed the firm last week that hackers accessed a portion of customer reservation data for Japan Airlines and Japan Transocean Air flights. In total 92,000 customers were impacted, with names, reservation codes and flight information accessed, and passport numbers, frequent flyer numbers, email addresses and phone numbers for about 2,000 customers. JAL said flight operations were unaffected and credit card numbers were not compromised.
