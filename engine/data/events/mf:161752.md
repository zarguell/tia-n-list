Russian state-linked threat actor BlueDelta, also known as APT28, Fancy Bear, and Forest Blizzard, has launched phishing campaigns using a lightweight Windows backdoor called HOOKEDGE. The activity targeted government, diplomatic, and defense manufacturing organizations in Romania, Spain, and Türkiye between late September 2025 and early April 2026. Insikt Group assessed with moderate confidence that BlueDelta conducted the activity. The assessment is based on code, infrastructure, and operational similarities between HOOKEDGE and HEADLACE, a batch-script backdoor previously linked to the group. BlueDelta is widely associated with Russia’s GRU military intelligence service. The campaign begins with macro-enabled Microsoft Word documents, likely delivered through spearphishing emails. Early samples used diplomatic-themed lures impersonating Spain’s Ministry of the Presidency, Justice and Relations with the Cortes. Later documents used generic messages that instructed recipients to click “Enable Content.” Once macros are enabled, the malicious document drops several files into the victim’s user profile directory and starts an installer chain. The installer creates a scheduled task to maintain persistence, then deletes itself and related installation files to reduce evidence on the compromised system.
