The campaign shows how threat actors can abuse Android Work Profiles to separate malicious activity from the victim’s normal phone environment and evade banking-app security checks. Gigabud has been active since 2022 and is linked by Group-IB to the GoldFactory threat group . It targets users across Southeast Asia, South Asia, the Middle East, Africa, and Latin America. The malware commonly arrives through phishing websites, messaging apps, and social-media posts that distribute fake applications impersonating airlines, tax agencies, government services, or other trusted brands. After installation, Gigabud requests Accessibility permissions, overlay access, and battery-optimization exemptions. If the victim grants Accessibility access, attackers can remotely view, tap, type, and control the infected device. The trojan can also inventory installed apps, display fake banking-login overlays, capture credentials and lock-screen codes, and hide fraudulent activity behind a black screen. Gigabud Exploits Android Work Profiles Vwork is a weaponized fork of Shelter, an open-source Android tool designed to create and manage isolated Work Profile spaces. Normally, Android Work Profiles separate work-related apps and data from a user’s personal profile. However, Vwork changes this model by exposing cloning and application-management functions through an API that other apps can control.
