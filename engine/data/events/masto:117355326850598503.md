Poland's CERT (CERT Polska) has identified and disrupted an extensive toll fraud campaign abusing premium services against Android devices, in which paid Meta ads steered Polish users to malicious applications on Google Play. Investigators documented 1,235 Meta ads, of which 852 promoted a total of 17 applications linked to the campaign. Six apps were proven to contain the components needed for premium-service abuse, or direct connections leading to them, and another 11 used the same malicious loaders, although researchers could not recover their final payloads.

The campaign used two monetization paths: premium SMS messages and subscriptions activated through mobile carrier billing. CERT Polska checked the three examined premium numbers against the registry of the Polish telecom regulator UKE and found that all three belonged to active paid services.

One entry point was a pair of Facebook ads that deceptively told users their PDF application had expired. The ads led to the Messenger Pro app on Google Play. On the surface the app worked as an ordinary SMS manager, but it carried a malicious loader. Messenger Pro could legitimately ask the user to set it as the default messaging application; in the background it assembled an encrypted DEX file while running and then loaded it. The loader checked the app's package name and the device's mobile country code, then, following instructions from a control server, decrypted and downloaded the code responsible for the fraud. Depending on the victim's country and mobile carrier, the component could send premium SMS messages or launch browser-based carrier billing.

Messages to the examined SMS numbers cost 30.75 zloty, while the direct mobile billing service offered by Teleaudio carried a recurring weekly charge of 17 zloty. The campaign continued even after the malicious apps were uninstalled. CERT Polska reported Messenger Pro to Google on September 15 and then reported all identified apps. Google removed the reported apps from the Play Store and Meta took down the ads the researchers reported, but the removals only blocked new installations; they did not remove already installed apps from infected devices. During the investigation the control server was still operating and kept sending new tasks to Polish subscriptions controlled by the CERT. CERT Polska later detected additional app packages after the earlier apps were taken down, showing the attackers could stand up new distribution channels within a short time. Experts warn that anyone who installed one of the affected apps must also remove it from their device.
