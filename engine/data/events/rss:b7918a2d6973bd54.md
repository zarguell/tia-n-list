TL;DR: Most appliance vendors have shipped php2shell fixes. The remaining exposures linger in devices that will stay vulnerable until someone replaces them. On October 21, 2026, the PHP project fixed two remote code execution flaws in the PHP core code, tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 , both rated CVSS 9.2 (Critical). Researchers at Calif, working with OpenAI and Wiz, published the details as php2shell . PHP powers web management interfaces on firewalls, application delivery controllers (ADCs), network-attached storage (NAS) devices, and many other devices. The owners of those appliances cannot install the PHP fix themselves. Each vendor compiled PHP into its firmware, so each vendor has to ship a fix. Since our October InfraTrust Pulse captured vendor status on October 24, CISA added both flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog on October 26. Palo Alto Networks now says attackers are exploiting PAN-OS management interfaces that are exposed to the internet. Of the 55 vendors in our php2shell tracker, 15 have fixed every product line they list as affected, and 15 say they are not affected. The rest are partly fixed, still investigating, or silent.
