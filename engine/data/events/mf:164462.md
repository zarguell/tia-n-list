Kaspersky’s Global Emergency Response Team (GERT) has uncovered a new campaign by the NightEagle APT group, also tracked as APT-Q-95, deploying a stealthy backdoor called GhostContainer against Microsoft Exchange servers belonging to Russian organizations. The group, active since at least 2023, originally focused on targets in Asia but has now expanded its geographic scope to Russian businesses, signaling a broadening threat surface for enterprise mail infrastructure. NightEagle GhostContainer Backdoor According to research published by Kaspersky , in most observed incidents, NightEagle gained entry using compromised valid credentials to access corporate VPNs, with connections originating from Russian IP addresses linked to Cloudflare WARP tunnels and from European virtual infrastructure providers. Kaspersky could not conclusively determine the exact delivery mechanism for GhostContainer but assesses with high confidence that attackers extracted cryptographic keys from Exchange’s ASP.NET configuration, then overwrote the VIEWSTATE parameter to inject a payload that launched the backdoor directly in memory a technique that leaves minimal disk artifacts. GhostContainer is a .NET assembly built by repurposing components from public open-source projects, including the Neo-reGeorg tunneling tool , an exploit for CVE-2020-0688, and the GhostWebShell class lifted from the ysoserial utility.
