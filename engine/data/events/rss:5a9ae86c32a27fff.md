Welcome to this week’s edition of the Threat Source newsletter. I’ve been following a lot of (sometimes heated) threads and discussions lately about the current vulnerability landscape. I learned new words/phrases such as Summer of Hell (although I’m writing this with 1°C/34°F outside), Vulnpocalypse, Bugmageddon, Bugpocalypse, Breachpocalypse… Let’s look at some numbers: We crossed the 70k mark, and it’s only the end of September. This has never been seen before — I think we can agree this number is skyrocketing. Also, CVE numbering organically grew into the six-digit range. Check your tooling. Some might say we broke that barrier already in 2014 ( CVE-2014-125110 ), or even hit seven digits in 2015 ( CVE-2015-1000013 ), but those were allocated artificially. (History note: Before 2014, 9999 was the highest possible identifier.) So that could be seen as the apocalypse argument. On the other hand, we have the KEV numbers: They didn’t explode… yet? Counting by sheer volume, YoY, CVEs increased by 105%, while KEV only grew 37%. Sounds huge at first, but following a 30% increase from 2024 to 2025, that’s less dramatic — a counter-argument of sorts. CVEs from 2025 or older still account for 33%. In other words, a good third of the KEVs are suggesting there is a patch gap. Looking at the 2026 CVEs, the share where the publication date matches the date added to KEV is 29%, which is only 2% more than (all of) 2025.
