Payment details belonging to members of more than 40 Irish credit unions have been accessed by hackers who exploited a vulnerability in the systems of software supplier Wellington IT. Ireland’s Data Protection Commission told Recorded Future News on Thursday it had received more than 40 breach notifications from credit unions that used the Belfast-based company as a data processor. With a customer base in Ireland and Wellington IT’s headquarters in Northern Ireland, a part of the United Kingdom, the incident straddles two jurisdictions. The U.K.’s Information Commissioner’s Office told Recorded Future News it had not received a report from Wellington IT as of Thursday, but was “starting to see breach reports come through from Credit Unions,” suggesting the incident may also affect credit union members in the U.K. Ireland’s Minister of State, Robert Troy, said in a statement the incident only affected payee details stored by members using credit union mobile banking apps operated by Wellington IT. Members who did not use the apps were unaffected, he said. “At present, there is no evidence that members’ funds have been accessed or that any fraudulent activity involving members’ accounts has occurred,” said Troy. The attack occurred in early October, David Malone, the chief executive of the Irish League of Credit Unions, told Ireland’s national broadcaster RTÉ.
