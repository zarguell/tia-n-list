JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard have disclosed incidents involving outside service providers after the N0n extortion group claimed it stole their customers’ data. JPMorgan said in a post on X on Thursday that some of the companies providing services to the bank had been hit by a cyberattack. “Based on our investigation so far, we believe the attackers accessed certain marketing data held by those vendors, which may include some customer names and email addresses. There is no disruption to our services,” the bank said . Where appropriate, the bank plans to reach out to impacted customers and give them free credit monitoring. It said the incident has nothing to do with the problems some customers saw earlier this week. In a statement to the media, Visa said the claims are “associated with an incident involving a third-party service provider in Asia Pacific.” The payments giant said neither its own systems nor payment data such as card numbers had been impacted. Mastercard said in a statement to Reuters that it had recently learned of an incident “at a third-party provider that resulted in the exposure of limited amount of email addresses — non-payments information — from a small subset of cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit program.” Mastercard said its payment network was not affected and that cardholders can still use their cards.
