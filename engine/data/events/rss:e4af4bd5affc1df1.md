A 43-year-old Kazakh national charged with taking part in a ransomware and extortion conspiracy that targeted U.S. hospitals, schools and healthcare providers made his initial appearance in federal court Monday after being extradited from Italy, the Justice Department said. Roman Khlynovskiy, faces five charges: conspiracy to commit computer fraud and identity theft, conspiracy to commit wire fraud, extortion involving computers, conspiracy to commit money laundering and threats to disclose nonconsensual intimate images under the TAKE IT DOWN Act. If convicted, he faces a maximum penalty of 27 years in prison. Prosecutors allege that Khlynovskiy belonged to the “8-Digits Team,” a group affiliated with the INC and Lynx ransomware groups. According to the indictment, the groups targeted victims in at least six states, gained access to their networks, exfiltrated data and demanded payment in exchange for promises not to release it. The conspirators boasted of holding more than 50 terabytes of data, including more than 70 million confidential health records, and threatened to make the material public to pressure victims into paying. FBI Director Kash Patel said the group “repeatedly targeted hospitals and schools, extorting victims and threatening to release highly sensitive information,” and that the campaign produced more than $500 million in ransom demands.
