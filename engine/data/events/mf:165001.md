A newly identified malware family called Sauron Loader has appeared in attacks against multiple organizations in Germany, according to DCSO. The malware was the final stage in attack chains that began with social engineering, including ClickFix-style lures and voice phishing. DCSO also found a Russian-language underground forum advertisement offering a loader with functions matching the samples it analyzed. In the voice-phishing cases, attackers flooded employees with spam emails before calling them while posing as IT support. In one incident, an attacker instructed a victim to start a Microsoft Quick Assist session. The approach turned an apparent support request into a route for delivering malware. Sauron Loader Spreads via ClickFix DCSO examined a malicious MSI installer that dropped three files into C:\ProgramData\keyroll : a legitimate rnpkeys.exe executable, a malicious rnp.dll , and a second malicious library named tdwp.dll . The legitimate executable side-loaded rnp.dll . That library then loaded tdwp.dll , which decrypted the loader code in memory. The malware delayed execution during this stage, likely to hinder automated analysis.
