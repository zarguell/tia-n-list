In this article Dataset poisoning: Teaching agents to discover and misuse tools Model poisoning: Building reliable backdoors that persist and transfer What the studies show about agent backdoors Mitigation and protection guidance Securing agents beyond the model References Learn More As organizations increasingly deploy specialized AI agents, trust cannot depend solely on a model behaving as expected during evaluation or normal use. As Satya Nadella has argued in Models as Insider Risks in the Super Intelligence Era , trustworthy enterprise AI requires independent observability and controls that sit outside the model. Two studies of the post-training supply chain, from Microsoft Defender Research and the Microsoft AI Red Team, show why. A model backdoor embeds hidden behavior that remains dormant until a trigger appears. In a traditional language model, activation might change a text response; in an agent, it can cause the model to invoke tools, execute commands, modify files, or access data. The two studies examine attacks with different levels of access. First, dataset poisoning hides a small number of crafted examples in a fine-tuning dataset, teaching a victim-trained model an unsafe conditional behavior: rather than memorizing a fixed tool call, the model learns to find any available command-execution tool and misuse it when a routine, even paraphrased, user request appears.
