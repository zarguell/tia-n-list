Cisco Talos has identified UAT-11473, a highly sophisticated advanced persistent threat (APT) actor, who exploited CVE-2026-20127 , an authentication bypass vulnerability in Cisco SD-WAN Catalyst Controller instances to bypass authentication and gain unauthorized access to affected systems. UAT-11473 then installed a custom malware framework we track as “KeyDrift” consisting of multiple components geared towards collecting sensitive keying information, acting as backdoors and intermediate command-and-control (C2) hosts. Observed targeting spans across space and satellite services, defense and aerospace technology, and policy research. Talos assesses with high confidence that UAT-11473’s operating infrastructure overlaps considerably with Volt Typhoon’s “Silent Train” covert access network . KeyDrift is a custom malware framework that is designed to operate in Cisco SD-WAN controller environments. Talos assesses with high confidence that the framework is designed to provide long-term persistent access and automated exfiltration of sensitive keying material. KeyDrift operates inside SD-WAN control-plane processes and is capable of intercepting decrypted control traffic, extracting control-session metadata and data-plane keying material, and staging or transmitting the collected material using encrypted records.
