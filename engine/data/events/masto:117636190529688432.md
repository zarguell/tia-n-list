Another mass cryptocurrency theft worth more than 400 million dollars by North Korea. This one used compromised accounts, fake video conferences, recordings of real people and deepfakes.

After the spectacular attack on a Binance employee and the theft of more than 6 billion dollars, the US Department of Justice announced coordinated actions against plans by the government of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea. This concerns the FaceStealer social engineering campaign, in which APT groups infect more devices, take over social media accounts and extract cryptocurrency wallet data. North Korean APT groups use hijacked profiles, recordings of victims for fake video calls, and malware distribution. Targets include personal wallets and the open-source software supply chain.

The method is straightforward. Attackers take over a profile on Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack or Discord, along with its image and contacts. They contact the victim's friends with a proposal for a cooperation or investment meeting. They create a fake meeting on a page that closely imitates Teams or Zoom, using typosquatted domains such as uu01webzoom[.]us and teams[.]livemeet[.]us. During the call they claim there are technical problems and tell the other person to click an update or copy and run a command in the terminal. The result is another compromised computer and more stolen data, and the cycle repeats.

The victim is recorded during the call, and the material lets the attackers create deepfakes that authenticate later calls. The FBI secured more than 900 recordings. Targets included not only private wallets and individuals but also open-source software supply chains, to spread malware as widely as possible. One victim was the owner of a popular JavaScript library with tens of millions of downloads per week. The FBI lists the APT groups associated with the campaign as Sapphire Sleet, UNC1069, Dangerous Password, CryptoCore, APT38, BlueNoroff and Stardust Chollima. Sources cited by the article are the Justice Department announcement and FBI FLASH AC-111226-MS.
