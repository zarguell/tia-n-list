A DDoS attack (distributed denial-of-service attack) floods a server, website, or network with traffic from many compromised devices at once, overwhelming it so legitimate users can’t get through. Attackers use botnets to generate the volume, making the traffic hard to distinguish from real visitors and hard to block. What Is a DDoS Attack? (Definition and Meaning) A DDoS attack tries to knock a website, server, or network offline by sending it more traffic or requests than it can handle. Because the flood comes from thousands of devices at once, the target can’t easily distinguish attackers from genuine users, so real customers get timeouts and error pages. The aim is disruption, not stealing data. Distributed Denial of Service: What Each Word Means Denial of service means legitimate users are denied access to a service they should be able to use. Distributed means the attack traffic comes from many sources, usually a botnet of hijacked computers, routers, cameras, and other connected devices, rather than one machine.
