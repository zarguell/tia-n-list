Introduction For a threat actor, the perfect malware is one they don’t have to write, and already does all they need. One example of this is shown by the China-aligned APT known as Webworm, which has been known to deploy the open-source SoftEther VPN into the networks of compromised governmental organizations to maintain low-profile, long-term access. After this was publicly documented and defenders could keep an eye out for this tactic, the actor presumably started thinking of ways to make their covert access stealthier. In this post, we share our analysis of what we believe to be a so-far unknown custom SoftEther-based tool, which implements various tricks to hide itself from security tools, and which we attribute to Webworm with moderate confidence. We additionally provide guidance and tooling for detecting its presence on a system and extracting the configuration(s) associated with it. Technical analysis To illustrate how the tool works, we will use the only public sample of this tool we are aware of, which can be found in our IoCs list ( 90e7… ). The sample is a Windows PE32+ file targeting the x64 architecture. Based on cleartext strings and constants in the binary, we identified it as a (modified) build of the SoftEther VPN Bridge executable, specifically version 4.44, build 9807. Similarly, the PDB path included in the executable reads C:\Users\Administrator\Desktop\SoftEtherVPN_Stable\SoftEtherVPN_Stable\SoftEtherVPN_Stable\src\DebugFiles\pdb\x64_Release\vpnbridge.pd
