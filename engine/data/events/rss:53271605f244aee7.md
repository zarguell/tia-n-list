The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), the US Treasury Department’s financial intelligence unit, has disconnected some of its systems while investigating a cyber incident. “FinCEN is investigating a cybersecurity incident that resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems. As a proactive measure, FinCEN has taken some systems offline,” the agency said in a notice on the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) E-Filing System website . The website is operational, but some FinCEN services may be temporarily unavailable, and the agency has extended the deadlines for qualifying filers affected by the disruption. “FinCEN is granting temporary, limited exceptive relief in response to a service disruption that is hindering filers’ ability to adhere to reporting obligations,” the agency said in a November 24 order (PDF). The agency has not shared further details on the nature of the incident and made no mention of a potential data breach, but said it was working to return all systems to normal. It is unclear who was behind the attack and if any data was accessed or stolen. SecurityWeek has emailed FinCEN for additional information and will update this article if the agency responds. FinCEN disclosed the incident amid a wave of cyberattacks and outages impacting US government agencies, including the CIA, which had an unclassified system breached .
