Bulletproof hosting (BPH) providers rent servers and IP space to cybercriminals and ignore abuse complaints and takedown requests, so phishing, malware and ransomware infrastructure stays online. Over the past 12 months, 50 of the BPH providers Silent Push tracks lost their autonomous system numbers (ASNs), the identifiers that tie blocks of IP addresses to a network. Not all of them are publicly known as bulletproof hosters. We identified them as BPH through our own research and data. An ASN can disappear for many reasons. To name a few, the BPH goes out of business, law enforcement action takes its infrastructure offline, or the BPH abandons the ASN and moves its IP ranges to a fresh one under a new front company BPH providers still work the way they always have. What has grown is the pressure on them from law enforcement and private companies, through seizures, sanctions and arrests. The most recent example is the FBI’s seizure of virtualine[.]net, announced by the US Attorney’s Office for the Eastern District of Virginia on October 27, 2026.
