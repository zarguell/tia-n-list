Since early 2026, threat actors have been combining aggressive email bombing — flooding a victim's inbox with thousands of spam messages to create urgency — with direct Microsoft Teams messages impersonating the company's IT helpdesk. eSentire reports the two-step attack achieves a staggering 72% success rate. After gaining remote access through Windows Quick Assist, attackers use the legitimate WinSCP file transfer tool to exfiltrate data, or deploy a Java-based payload from a ZIP archive named "Email-Deployment-Process-System.zip." The campaigns use bulletproof hosting providers (NKtelecom INC, WorkTitans B.V., Global Connectivity Solutions LLP, GWY IT PTY LTD) and create convincing personas with freshly registered .onmicrosoft.com accounts labeled "Windows Security Help Desk." Organizations should restrict external Teams communications, configure prominent warning banners for external users, and train employees to verify IT support requests through established internal channels.
