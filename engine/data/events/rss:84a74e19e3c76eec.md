Disruptions at the National Security Agency entered their second day on Wednesday as the spy organization worked to get its internal communications network back up and fully running. The system, known as NSA Net, experienced intermittent outages on Tuesday, leading the agency to tell its work force to stagger arrivals at its headquarters at Fort Meade, Maryland. It eventually closed its campus to most employees by the evening, according to sources. Non-essential personnel were sent a "Code Gray" notification late Tuesday night, informing them to arrive at 10 a.m. Wednesday. However, that time has already been moved to the afternoon and could change again, said a source familiar with the ongoing situation. Meanwhile, the agency’s cafeteria, and a Starbucks located on its campus, remain closed. The same person said the network is working but NSA leaders are still staggering personnel because the login process is the most taxing process on the system. They also said comments made Tuesday by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton — that the outage was due to maintenance affecting PHP, a common programming language — were correct. It remains unknown if the vulnerability was AI-derived or human-driven. The source speculated that it might take another day or two for full restoration of the classified network, but insisted that the spy agency’s classified national security work remains unimpeded.
