Password management and identity security company 1Password revealed on Wednesday that a hacker stole customer information and proprietary source code. 1Password says customer vaults are not affected. The company launched an investigation on November 19 after discovering that an employee’s corporate GitHub account may have been compromised. The attacker used the account to deploy malicious code to backend services and exfiltrate customer records. The compromised customer records include names, email addresses, account identifiers, public cryptographic keys, authentication parameters, account activity times, and non-secret metadata. In addition, the threat actor obtained source code from private repositories and accessed the company’s CI/CD system, potentially exposing infrastructure credentials. According to 1Password , the hacker also obtained tokens from emails sent to users for account invitations, provisioning, and recovery. A “small number” of individuals who were undergoing provisioning or recovery during the intrusion have been notified. However, the company noted that the exposed tokens cannot be exploited on their own and there is no evidence that the attacker hijacked user accounts. A 1Password representative noted on the company’s community forum that the stolen authentication data included Secure Remote Password verifiers derived from user account passwords and Secret Keys.
