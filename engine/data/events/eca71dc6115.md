The ShinyHunters Oracle PeopleSoft exploitation wave continues to expand with two new developments:

**Nissan Americas** filed breach notifications with the California Attorney General confirming that current and former employee data — including Social Security numbers, banking information, payroll records, tax documents, and beneficiary information — may have been exposed. Oracle told Nissan the attacks impacted "hundreds of companies." Affected employees span the U.S., Canada, Mexico, and Brazil. Nissan has restricted payroll access to company-network-only and VPN connections.

**NAIC** responded to ShinyHunters' data leak claims, stating the 3.1TB of data the group claims to have stolen consists of already-public statutory financial reports, outdated logs, and configuration files — not the critical insurance regulatory platform data (SERFF, OPTins, SBS) the hackers alleged. ShinyHunters updated their leak listing, admitting a previous summary was "exaggerated due to using AI hallucinations when evaluating the files," but maintains a human-validated inventory confirms 105,000 files, 264,000 PDFs, and stored credentials for production environments.

**Context:** *Previously covered June 27 (NAIC breach disclosure, 3.1TB claim) and June 29 (Nissan initial reporting). New today: Nissan confirms employee data scope; NAIC formally disputes data classification; ShinyHunters admits AI-hallucinated claims.*
