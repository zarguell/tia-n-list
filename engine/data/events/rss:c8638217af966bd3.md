Cybercriminals have found another AI tool to do more of the hacking for them. The FBI issued a FLASH advisory on November 13 warning that attackers are abusing ARTEX, a tool originally developed for authorized security testing, to automate intrusions against organizations worldwide. In some observed attacks, criminals went from selecting a target to gaining privileged access in under three hours, leaving defenders little time to respond. ARTEX is an open-source penetration-testing framework. Penetration testing means trying to break into systems with the owner’s permission to find weaknesses before criminals do. An operator gives ARTEX a target and an objective. Its a planning agent divides the work into tasks, while other AI agents use security tools to carry them out and share their findings. Rather than having its own AI model, ARTEX connects to external large language models (LLMs). Once a task begins, it requires relatively little human input. The FBI says attackers have used ARTEX since at least August against sectors including finance, government, healthcare, and technology. Separately, Microsoft reported that a China-state-aligned group used ARTEX to accelerate attacks after compromising ConnectWise application credentials. While these attacks can generate plenty of suspicious activity, their speed leaves defenders little time to respond.
