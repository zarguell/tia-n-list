A little preparation can make your digital life easier for loved ones to manage after your death. Here’s how to record your wishes, protect your privacy, and give the right people a way to act. Start with a digital inventory Your next of kin cannot manage accounts they do not know exist. Create an inventory of the important parts of your digital life, including: Primary and recovery email addresses Phones, computers, tablets, and the mobile carrier associated with them Cloud storage, photo services, and backups Social media, messaging, and professional networking accounts Your password manager and its emergency-access arrangements Online banking, payment, shopping, subscription, and utility accounts Domains, websites, digital businesses, and developer accounts Cryptocurrency exchanges, wallets, and the location of securely stored recovery materials For each service, include the account email or username and a profile link or account reference where relevant. For each account, record what you want to happen: preserve it, download important data, memorialize it, transfer an asset where permitted, or close it. Identify any photos, documents, or other files you want loved ones to keep, and note where they are stored. For websites or digital businesses, record who should take over administration and which domains, hosting plans, or other services need renewing.
