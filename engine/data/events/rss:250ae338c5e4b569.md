Turla, a notorious cyberespionage group aligned with the Russian government, has been wielding a new, customizable toolset to compromise governments around the world over the past year, including Kazakhstan. The toolset was first discovered in November 2025 by cybersecurity firm ESET, which named it “Turla’s Cereal” as a play on its highly modular, interconnected and “serialized” design. It chains and exploits different legitimate functions across Microsoft Exchange, Windows, and .NET assemblies, which Microsoft has described as “the fundamental units of deployment, version control, reuse, activation scoping, and security permissions for .NET-based applications.” “One of the most interesting aspects of this toolset is what we did not find: external command and control infrastructure,” ESET researcher Matías Porolli wrote in the report , released Tuesday. “Instead — adding insult to injury — the victim’s compromised Exchange server is co-opted to act as the C&C infrastructure.” Illustration of Turla’s Cereal compromise of Microsoft Exchange servers using a modular, stealthy toolset. (Source: ESET) Multiple Western governments and threat intelligence firms have attributed Turla as a part of Russia’s Federal Security Service (FSB), which is tasked with gathering intelligence on foreign governments, businesses and organizations.
