CrowdStrike announced Monday it has signed agreements to acquire two artificial intelligence security companies, Fiddler AI and Dreadnode, in deals announced at its Fal.Con Europe conference in Barcelona. CrowdStrike did not disclose what it paid for the companies. A report in the Wall Street Journal listed the price for Fiddler at $400 million, and Dreadnode at $95 million. Fiddler, which specializes in AI agent observability and explainability, will become Falcon Observe on the company’s Falcon platform. Dreadnode builds automated security testing for AI systems, checking models and agents for weaknesses before deployment. The acquisitions address a gap that has widened as companies adopt AI agents: the software can hold valid credentials and approved tools yet still make bad decisions, ignore instructions or claim work it never completed. Traditional security testing, built for code that behaves predictably, does not reach the model layer or the agent’s reasoning process. Fiddler’s technology works after deployment. It evaluates agents before they reach production, traces decisions and reasoning across agent systems, flags drifting behavior and made-up answers, and creates records that can be used in audits.
