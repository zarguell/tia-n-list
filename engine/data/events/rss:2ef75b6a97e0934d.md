A China-linked threat actor has been exploiting the PHP2Shell vulnerabilities to gain root access to firewalls and deploy webshells for persistent access, cybersecurity firm Dream reports. The hackers have been chaining an exploit for the two PHP flaws, tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, with another for Fragnesia , a privilege escalation bug in the Linux kernel tracked as CVE-2026-46300. As part of the observed attacks, the hackers used the Chinese internet asset search engine FOFA to identify 19,000 internet-facing firewalls from a specific vendor, and proceeded to exploit 25 of them on October 23-24, Dream says . After discovery, the hackers first filtered servers in the US, Japan, Taiwan, and South Korea, then filtered those running newer PHP releases, which gave them a pool of 240 potential targets. Using an automated tool, the attackers then targeted the firewalls with the full PHP2Shell-Fragnesia exploit chain, dropping a PHP root webshell on 24 devices for persistent access. While no post-exploitation activity was observed on 22 of the firewalls, the hackers did progress further on two servers belonging to a US government-affiliated environment. The first rooted server crashed shortly after exploitation, on October 23. The attacker launched a second wave of attacks on October 24, which was successful. “The attacker monitored [the first firewall] with 240 automated probes over two hours, waiting for it to recover.
