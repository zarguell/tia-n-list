The campaign used a seemingly legitimate email lure related to financial assistance for a Ukrainian military unit. The email contained a RAR archive named after an official letter from the Sumy Regional Military Administration regarding the redistribution of a subvention for military unit A1836. Inside the archive was a malicious LNK file that triggered a multi-stage infection chain. Our analysis found that the infection chain eventually deploys Azalea Agent 1.21.3, a .NET-based remote access trojan.
