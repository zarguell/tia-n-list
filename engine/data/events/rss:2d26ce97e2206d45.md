Cisco on Wednesday released fixes for an exploited vulnerability affecting its Secure Firewall Adaptive Security Appliance (ASA) and Secure Firewall Threat Defense (FTD) software. Tracked as CVE-2026-76418 and assigned a CVSS score of 8.6, the bug resides in the Remote Access SSL VPN service. Attackers can exploit it remotely, without authenticating, to force the device to restart unexpectedly and disrupt its availability. Cisco attributes the issue to logic errors affecting tunnel groups configured with secondary authentication. Exploitation involves a malicious request sent while connecting to the SSL VPN. “A successful VPN login is not required to reach the affected processing path,” Cisco noted. For an attack to work, the firewall must be running an unpatched release, have its SSL VPN login service reachable, and use secondary authentication in an affected tunnel group. The company’s cloud-delivered firewall and management offerings, Secure Access, and the Secure Firewall Management Center are not vulnerable. Cisco’s product security team learned in October that the flaw was being exploited in the wild. No information has been shared about the attacks or the threat actors behind them. The vulnerability came to light during a Cisco TAC support case, and the company credited Eric Fernald with finding it. No workarounds are available.
