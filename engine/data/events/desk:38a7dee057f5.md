The Korea Times reported that KEPCO detected the intrusion on November 20, blocked external IP access and suspended the two systems, then notified affected people and worked with the National Intelligence Service and outside experts. It listed names, resident registration numbers, bank account details, credit card information and billing records for KEPCO ON users, alongside names, phone numbers and email addresses for 426,827 corporate representatives and names, affiliations and contact details for 442,934 employees. The report noted this was a second breach a month after a separate leak affecting 35,528 people.
