A malware strain called PoeLLM finds its command-and-control server inside a poem and has compromised more than 3,400 servers. According to Black Lotus Labs, Lumen's security research team, PoeLLM is a cryptojacking and self-propagating malware that exploits vulnerable AI services exposed to the internet, primarily servers running LiteLLM and Ollama. Over 3,400 servers have been hit since April, with a peak of more than 800 infected servers active per day. The first traces date to April 13, the date of the first associated GitHub commit; attacks intensified in May with scanning for exposed vulnerable services, and in June Black Lotus Labs identified the PoeLLM infrastructure while investigating Ivanti Sentry flaw CVE-2026-10520. Analysis of compromised systems shows most victims ran vulnerable versions of LiteLLM and Ollama; hundreds of servers running Gotenberg (a PDF converter) or the Gitea development platform were also compromised. PoeLLM stands out for how it resolves its C2: it extracts four words or phrases from a poem titled "On the Nature of Connection" published in a GitHub repository, converts them to numbers using a dictionary built into its code, and the four values form the C2 server's IPv4 address. The operator can migrate infrastructure simply by editing words in the text; the poem has been reworked eleven times since first publication, each version steering infected machines to a new command server. Black Lotus Labs told The Register they had never before observed this kind of "adversarial poetry" in a real attack: to anyone who stumbles on it, it is just a poem on GitHub. The technique differs from a classic LLM jailbreak in that no model interprets the poem; the malware uses it directly to recover its C2 address.
