Hacker Uses Deep AI to Autonomously Attack Servers

A Chinese-language threat actor is using the DeepSeek AI model and the open-source Hermes agent to conduct autonomous cyberattacks on exposed servers with minimal human involvement.

Unit 42 identified an autonomous AI-driven hacking campaign conducted by a Chinese-speaking threat actor. The attacker, operating under the pseudonyms knaithe and KnYuan, used DeepSeek via the Hermes Agent platform as an autonomous offensive operator. Through Telegram, they arranged this operator to perform the following actions: independently enumerating targets and their vulnerabilities using FOFA, acquiring exploit tools, and initiating attacks without human intervention.

Parallel to using DeepSeek as an autonomous operator platform, the actor configured multiple large language models (LLM) including Qwen, GLM, Kimi, and MiniMax. Researchers also identified limited use and testing of Western platforms, including Claude Code for connectivity testing and proxy validation, and signs of Codex use in exploit development directories. This limited use is consistent with evaluating the AI market to determine the preferred toolset.

When initial exploitation failed due to restrictive target environment configurations, the Hermes agent autonomously conducted searches for known critical vulnerabilities and CVEs. It initially searched 10 product families, scanning GitHub for popular proof-of-concept exploits and prioritizing vulnerabilities by attack surface. This research led the agent to focus on seven higher-value vulnerabilities.

The attacker exploited seven vulnerabilities, combining AI-driven autonomous enumeration with manual exploitation to achieve confirmed impact. Although the observed campaign had limited impact, the workflow confirms a functional, comprehensive autonomous offensive capability.
