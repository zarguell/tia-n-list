Microsoft Threat Intelligence has uncovered NeedyMantis, a modular post-compromise malware framework used in a limited set of targeted intrusions against telecommunications providers, government contractors, universities, medical nonprofits, and intergovernmental organizations. The malware deploys after attackers gain access, enabling persistent control, covert command-and-control communications , and modular follow-on activity. Microsoft said NeedyMantis activity dates to at least October 2025 and emerged during its investigation of indicators connected to the DAEMON Tools supply-chain compromise. Microsoft Tracks NeedyMantis Post-Compromise Malware At least one operator, tracked as Storm-3069, has used the malware. Microsoft assesses the activity aligns with threat actors operating from China, but has not attributed Storm-3069 to a specific Chinese state-sponsored group or confirmed that all NeedyMantis incidents involve the same operator. NeedyMantis is not believed to be the initial access vector. Instead, operators introduce it after compromising an environment, making its delivery path flexible across incidents. In one case, attackers used the Impacket toolkit during hands-on-keyboard activity to copy a legitimate application, a malicious DLL, and an associated archive from a network share to a victim device.
