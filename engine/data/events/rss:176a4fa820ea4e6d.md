Key Takeaways Java4Shell (CVE-2026-114938) is a heap out-of-bounds write in the HotSpot JVM's x86-64 Base64 decoder intrinsic. A malformed padded Base64 tail makes the decoder write 62 bytes past the end of its output buffer. Exploitation can crash the JVM, leak heap secrets such as private keys and tokens, or enable unauthenticated RCE. OpenJDK 17.0.6 and newer and its downstream distributions are affected only on x86-64 CPUs with AVX-512 VBMI. Patch the JDK, or disable the vulnerable path with -XX:-UseBASE64Intrinsics or -XX:UseAVX=2 until you can patch. Picus Threat Library threat 69479, Java4shell Web Attack Campaign, tests your defenses against Java4Shell attacks. On November 17, 2026, OpenJDK published a security advisory and Oracle shipped a patch for CVE-2026-114938, a memory-corruption bug in the HotSpot JVM's x86-64 Base64 decoder. The researchers who found it call it Java4Shell. CISA has asked organizations to patch it immediately and to prioritize internet-facing systems [1].
