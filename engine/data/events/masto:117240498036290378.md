Chrome Keeps Your Data Even After You Explicitly Ask It to Delete Everything

Chrome appears to retain certain data related to your Google usage even when you explicitly request that everything be deleted. The concrete issue: the distinction between site data deletion working for third-party sites but not for Google.com.

As developer Jeff Johnson discovered, Google's browser preserves some data from your Google usage even when you expressly ask for it to be removed. The exception seems to apply only to Google's search engine. The problem is not limited to cookies: the data Google records after a search also includes local storage and session storage. Even when you configure your browser to automatically delete site data when closing a window, this data persists.

The behavior can be reproduced: in Chrome settings, go to "Privacy and security," click "Additional content settings," then "Site data on device." Choose "Delete site data that sites have stored on your device when you close all windows." This option normally deletes all cookies and permissions for a site when you close Chrome. However, while it works correctly for third-party sites (like Frandroid.com), data associated with Google.com remains, as visible in "Privacy and security" > "Site settings" > "View permissions and stored data for different sites."

This is not the first time. In 2020, Google was already flagged for a similar issue affecting YouTube, which Google attributed to the search engine appearing on every new Chrome tab. This time, the explanation is harder to accept: even with the browser set to open a blank page at startup, Google site data persists. Jeff Johnson replaced Google with DuckDuckGo as the default search engine, yet Google.com data still reappeared after a simple search and resisted deletion when the browser closed.
