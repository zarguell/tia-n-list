Open-weight AI has gone mainstream. As of September 2026, some estimates suggest 42% of enterprises surveyed were running open-weight models in production, up from 31% just two months earlier. But downloading a model also means taking on responsibilities that a provider might otherwise handle, and many organizations have yet to put clear ownership and controls in place. Renting Capability Vs. Running Your Own Copy When you use an AI model through a provider’s app or service, you’re essentially renting its capabilities. The provider controls the model and handles the infrastructure, updates, and safety controls. You access it through an app or a software connection. An open-weight AI model makes its learned parameters, the ‘weights’ that shape its behavior, available to download and run yourself. You can run the model on your own infrastructure and adapt it for a particular use case using your own data. The license may limit how you can use it, and running it still carries infrastructure and staffing costs. Open-weight models are becoming more capable. One estimate puts the best open-weight models roughly four months behind leading models. Open-weight does not necessarily mean open-source. Access to the weights does not guarantee access to the training code or details of the training data.
