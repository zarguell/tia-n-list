Watch out! Your infected PC now has a price tag! AI evaluates it and criminals put it up for sale.

via Mastodon https://www.redhotcyber.com/post/fai-attenzione-il-tuo-pc-infetto-ora-ha-un-prezzo-lai-lo-valuta-e-i-criminali-lo-mettono-in-vendita/

Group-IB researchers have uncovered BraZetsu, a Python-based malware framework that turns compromised Windows systems into commodities for the cybercrime underground. The framework collects reconnaissance data on victims, uses generative AI to estimate the value of the compromised machine, and then lists access for sale on the Infected Marketplace (also known as Banco de Infects), where other criminals can purchase initial access and deploy their own tools remotely. Entry to the platform requires a $5.80 deposit.

BraZetsu and Infected Marketplace are controlled by a threat actor group known as Exilware, believed to be Portuguese-speaking and primarily targeting Brazil, other Latin American countries, and the Iberian Peninsula. Targets include online shops, corporate networks, financial and industrial organizations, and public safety infrastructure.

The malware's exact distribution method is unknown, but researchers consider social engineering the most likely vector. In the observed attack chain, a loader masquerading as Microsoft Edge is downloaded from the domain caixaentradas1inboxshop[.]site, after which associated VBS scripts advance the attack.

On compromised systems, BraZetsu performs reconnaissance and collects digital certificates, browser history from Chrome, Edge, Brave, Vivaldi, and Opera, takes screenshots, and tracks the active window. It also examines environment variables, open ports and processes, searches for recently opened files and directories containing ERP systems, and enables shell command execution. A persistent connection to Infected Marketplace is maintained via WebSocket.

Particular attention is paid to financial files in the Brazilian CNAB format used by businesses and banks for payment data exchange. This functionality mirrors the CNABHunter tool, capable of searching and analyzing such files and, on operator command, replacing bank details, PIX keys, and barcodes within them to redirect payments to the attackers.

Researchers identified five versions of BraZetsu, the oldest dating to February 9, 2026. Starting from the third version, the malware primarily targets corporate systems in Brazil, though around that same time Infected Marketplace listed access to two compromised hosts in the United States. Analysis of BraZetsu's infrastructure and code linked the malware to the Python backdoor AgentV2, previously used against Brazilian users in phishing campaigns.
