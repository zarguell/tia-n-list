The US Department of the Treasury has cut off the MEXC Global crypto exchange from the US financial system over sanctions evasion and links with cybercrime gangs. Founded in 2018, the Seychelles-based MEXC is one of the world's largest crypto exchanges, giving more than 40 million users from more than 170 countries access to 3,000 cryptocurrencies and tokenized assets. According to a Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) order , MEXC has allowed illicit transactions of at least $16 billion linked to cybercrime operations and Iranian, North Korean, and Russian sanctions evasion between May 2021 and May 2026. The Treasury Department said that, as recently as June 2026, MEXC was still processing transactions with sanctioned Russian entities Garantex and Grinex , and it also facilitated or processed at least $226 million in transactions benefiting or connected to the Russia-based, OFAC-sanctioned A7 sanctions evasion network. "MEXC Global has not only facilitated Russian illicit finance but has also served as a hub for other types of illicit financial activity, including Iranian and DPRK sanctions evasion, ransomware and scam payments, and Russian and other darknet market activity," the Treasury Department said . "Between May 2021 and May 2026, MEXC Global had sent or received approximately $1 billion in direct transactions with more than 40 identified Iranian CVC exchangers, some of which are subject to U.S. and international sanctions."
