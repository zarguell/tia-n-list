We found a wave of websites posing as Calendly booking pages for job interviews at Meta. Visitors pick a slot for a 30-minute phone call with a recruiter, fill in their details, and pass what looks like a CAPTCHA. Then the page asks them to log in to Facebook. The interview is fake. The pages are designed to steal the visitor’s Facebook login details, including the two-factor authentication (2FA) codes meant to protect the account. Visitors are then asked to sign in to their Google account too, supposedly “for security.” These pages are not affiliated with Meta, Calendly, or Google. If you’ve already entered a password or approved a sign-in prompt, secure the affected accounts straight away. See “How to stay safe” below. Why Calendly makes a convincing disguise Calendly is a widely used scheduling tool, and recruiters use it to book interviews. Clicking a booking link, picking a time, and typing in your name and email is something people do without a second thought. There’s no account to create and nothing to download. That routine makes it a good disguise. By the time the page asks for something unusual, the visitor has already picked a slot and shared their details. Going along with one more step can feel easier than starting to doubt the whole thing. For someone who has sent out application after application without hearing back, an invitation to interview at Meta may be exactly the kind of message they hope for.
