In September, the AI company Anthropic published a blog post that claimed its models were approaching “ superhuman capabilities for geolocating outdoor photos”. Anthropic made this claim by comparing its models’ performance to that of players of the popular online game GeoGuessr, which challenges users to geolocate Google Street View images. Bellingcat has been running its own benchmark tests on AI models since June 2025, right around the time when Large Language Models (LLMs) began to challenge reverse image searches for assisting with geolocation tasks. In our first round, GPT 4o-mini from OpenAI appeared to be the best tool for geolocating, a title which was taken by Google AI mode when we performed our second round of tests a few months later in August 2025. This time, we significantly upped the ante for the models, providing increasingly challenging photos and asking for only one set of coordinates. We tasked not only closed-weight models from Claude , Grok , Gemini , ChatGPT , and Mistral , but also open-weight models from Moonshot AI’s Kimi , commercial provider GeoSeer and the non-generative geolocation model GeoClip . In total, we fed each model 52 photos that had not been published anywhere on the internet before.
