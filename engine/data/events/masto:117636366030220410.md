Hackers likely used AI agents to attack South Korean banks.

South Korean authorities are investigating cyberattacks using artificial intelligence against several of the largest banks, including Hana Bank, KB Kookmin Bank and Shinhan Bank. According to The New York Times, credit data belonging to at least 25,000 Shinhan Bank customers leaked last week. At Kookmin Bank, 99 customers and 20 current or former employees were affected. Another 89 Hana Bank customers were victims of attacks that led to data theft. South Korea's National Investigation Bureau is investigating all three cases, and President Lee Jae Myung has drawn attention to them.

Lee said there are indications that the attackers behind these incidents used AI models, causing considerable concern and public anxiety, and that he had ordered his cabinet to confirm the situation and minimize the damage. "It is now possible to easily use artificial intelligence for hacking even without specialized skills," he said at a live cabinet meeting. There is no information yet about who is behind the attacks on South Korea's banking sector. Attribution can be difficult, especially when attackers cover their tracks. AI agents are also known to carry out unintended cyberattacks on their own. The Australian government complained that OpenAI needed 84 days to inform it of a hacking incident involving the Australian national health portal. The article cites Euronext as its source.
