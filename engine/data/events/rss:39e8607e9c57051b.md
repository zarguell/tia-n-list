All threat hunters are looking for evidence of nefarious activity, hopefully before an incident happens. Threat hunting on the network involves actively searching through packet data for malicious activity. Threat hunting on an endpoint depends more heavily on logs. Think of it as on the wire (or in the air) versus on a host itself. Don’t worry, you don’t have to look through every packet in a pcap file. That would make your head explode! We would like to think our firewalls and Intrusion Detection/Prevention Systems (IDS or IPS) are blocking all evil traffic from coming in, but those appliances have limits. Adversaries evolve their techniques faster than the static signatures used on our perimeter defenses. Updates from vendors and threat intelligence feeds can’t keep up. A network threat hunter looks for patterns beyond specific byte sequences, file hashes, and documented signature exploits. In contrast to signatures, behavior analysis lets network threat hunters draw insights from network baselines to understand expected protocols and traffic flows. Threat actors may change their techniques and procedures, but they still have fundamental network behaviors.
