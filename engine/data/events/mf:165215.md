Kiteworks has urged customers to temporarily take their systems offline after receiving credible threat intelligence from federal authorities that a threat actor may attempt to target Kiteworks deployments. The company stressed that the action was preventive, not a response to a confirmed security breach , and said it lifted the shutdown recommendation on September 27. The San Mateo, California-based secure data exchange provider issued the advisory on September 25, 2026, recommending a nine-hour shutdown window in customers’ respective local time zones. Kiteworks Warns Customers to Shut Down Servers Organizations that self-manage Kiteworks instances on-premises or in AWS and Microsoft Azure were instructed to power down their environments during the designated window, while Kiteworks said it would shut down systems it hosts for customers. Frank Balonis, Kiteworks’ chief information security officer, said the company received “credible threat intelligence from federal intelligence authorities” indicating that a threat actor may target some Kiteworks systems. Kiteworks said it notified customers directly and coordinated with authorities while reviewing the potential threat. The vendor said it had no indication that its infrastructure or customer deployments had been compromised. It also stated that all vulnerabilities currently known to the company are addressed in Kiteworks version 9.5.1, which it continues to recommend customers deploy.
