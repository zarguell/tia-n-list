You’ve probably encountered devices that are powered via a USB-C connector, but that don’t work when you connect them to a USB-C charger or powerbank. They only work with a USB-A to USB-C cable, not with a USB-C to USB-C cable. I used to encounter such devices regularly a couple of years ago, but far less nowadays. Until we went on holiday this summer, and my wife used a small portable fan. She connected it to her USB-C charger, and it didn’t work. So I fixed this with a USB-A to USB-C cable plugged into a charger with USB-A connector. But this got me thinking: surely, there must be a small USB-C adapter that solves this problem? The problem occurs because in the USB-C standard, a power source doesn’t put 5 Volts on VBUS, until it detects there is a device connected that requires power. It detects the presence of such a device because of current flowing between the CC pins and GND. This happens because the device has resistors between the CC lines and GND. And some devices, like this fan, lack these resistors, and thus no power is present on VBUS. While with USB-A, there is always power on VBUS, whether a device is connected or not.
