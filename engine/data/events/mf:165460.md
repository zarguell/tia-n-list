Key takeaways: Venmo scams trick you into sending money or sharing sensitive information via the payment app, Venmo. Common Venmo scams include accidental payments, fake buyers and sellers, QR codes, impersonation, phishing and overpayment scams. Most Venmo scams rely on getting you to act before you have time to think, so slowing down and verifying unexpected requests can help you avoid them. Venmo scams usually look harmless because scammers use everyday interactions to convince you to send money or share sensitive personal information yourself. Someone might send you a payment by mistake and ask you to return it. You send the money back, only to find out later that the original payment came from a stolen account or card and has been reversed. In July 2026, a woman in New Jersey agreed to make a small Venmo donation to someone who said he was raising money for his basketball team. While she was making the payment, he took her phone and used her Venmo account to transfer $2,500 to another account.
