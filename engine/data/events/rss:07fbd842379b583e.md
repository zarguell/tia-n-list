On November 14, 2026, a seller on DarkForums opened a thread for SeverVault, a shop offering infostealer logs and combo lists. The pitch leans on scale and automation: an inventory said to run into the tens of millions of logs, more than a billion parsed accounts, entry prices of $0.25 and free registration. The headline feature, though, is that each log arrives with an AI-written summary of the machine it was stolen from. That summary changes what a buyer is paying for. A stealer log has always been a pile of saved passwords, cookies, autofill entries and system details that someone had to sift through by hand. SeverVault does the sifting in advance and presents the result as a profile: who appears to use the computer, where they seem to work and which of their accounts might open a door into an organization. For security teams, the takeaway is uncomfortable. An infection on a government worker’s home computer or a bank employee’s family PC no longer sits anonymously in a bulk dump. It arrives labeled with the employer, the sector and the kind of corporate access it may hold, from government portals to Citrix sessions.
