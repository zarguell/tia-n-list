Threat actors breached at least three AI inference providers and stole customer prompts, but none detected the intrusions until they were notified, according to cybersecurity firm Dragos. AI inference providers host AI models and sell access to them through APIs. The hacks were detected after a team led by Dragos chief research officer HD Moore sent out roughly two million prompts to widely used AI services between July and November. These prompts contained a unique, trackable bait such as URLs, hostnames, and credentials, including AWS keys. The security firm then set up a monitoring system to see whether anyone used the bait information in the wild, which meant the prompt had been compromised. Dragos soon found evidence indicating that someone had stolen information from one of the tested AI inference providers. “Although our prompts were bait, they were processed along with real customer requests, and those were almost certainly copied too,” Moore noted . The biggest breach involved roughly 200 AWS test keys sent to a provider, which were stolen in late October. “Some of the stolen keys came from requests that the provider rejected before a model ever ran – this tells us that the attacker was copying requests at the provider’s front door, and not somewhere deeper in the inference stack,” Moore said.
