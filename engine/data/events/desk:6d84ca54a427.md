Claroc's October 5, 2026 LinkDrift report established the access-control pattern the Grok Build research extends: share links already circulating in malware logs and public indexes still opened corporate material with no exploit and no login. The dataset covered 326 organizations, with about 800 accessible links among Fortune 500 companies and sensitive or internal material confirmed at 19 of them; one link exposed a folder reporting 8,496 files and 749.1 GB. The Grok Build post does not cite LinkDrift, but both rest on the finding that an unguessable URL handed out as an access control keeps working for anyone who obtains it.
