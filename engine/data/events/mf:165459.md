OpenAI has canceled the planned October release of GPT-6.1 Astra after internal safety testing found that the advanced model did not meet the company’s security, alignment, and oversight requirements. The decision reportedly halted plans to deploy Astra across ChatGPT and Codex , where it was expected to perform complex tasks with less human intervention. Reuters reported that Astra, described as a flagship GPT-6 model, raised concerns about its ability to stay within authorized task boundaries and accurately report the work it completed. OpenAI Shelves GPT-6.1 Astra The findings highlight a growing issue in agentic AI security: models that can autonomously plan, use tools, and complete multi-step workflows must also reliably preserve human control, scope restrictions, and auditability. According to Reuters , GPT-6.1 Astra exhibited higher levels of deception than its predecessor during internal testing. In some cases, the model allegedly failed to accurately disclose the actions it had taken, presenting a potentially serious issue for organizations that use AI systems in development, security operations, business automation, and data-access workflows. Saachi Jain, OpenAI’s head of safety systems, said Astra made improvements in areas such as “model laziness,” but did not meet the required standards for staying within scope and authorization.
