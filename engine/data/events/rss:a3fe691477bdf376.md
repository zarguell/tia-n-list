The much anticipated State of Threat Intelligence report has just been published. The State of Threat Intelligence report is based on a survey Recorded Future conducted with UserEvidence of 768 cybersecurity professionals at enterprises with at least 1,000 employees. It’s the latest edition in our annual report series, where we gauge how threat intelligence is currently being used, funded, and measured. When I first saw the results of this year’s survey, a few findings stood out to me as especially relevant for anyone who runs, funds, or relies on a threat intelligence program and is planning for the year ahead. Here are my top five findings and why. 1. Cost is the least common reason to consolidate A strong majority of respondents (79%) said they plan to consolidate threat intelligence vendors in the next year. That alone is no huge surprise, especially given that respondents reported using an average of five different vendors. Cost would be a reasonable guess for why, but reducing costs actually ranked last of five drivers, at 5%.
