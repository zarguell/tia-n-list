A new UEFI bootkit named BlackX, deployed in an espionage campaign in early 2026, exploited the old Baton Drop vulnerability to bypass Secure Boot and load a malicious driver designed to interfere with security solutions from Kaspersky and Avast. The operation has been attributed with medium confidence to APT41, a Chinese state-sponsored threat group also known as Brass Typhoon, Wicked Panda, and Barium. It was discovered in early 2026 and targeted diplomatic agencies in Central Asia, particularly in Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, and Turkmenistan. Kaspersky researchers analyzed the BlackX bootkit and found similarities with BlackLotus , which has been circulating on hacker forums since October 2022 and leveraged the Baton Drop vulnerability (CVE-2022-21894) to bypass Secure Boot protection. ESET confirmed in March 2023 that BlackLotus exploited Baton Drop, and at the time it could compromise even fully patched Windows 11 systems. According to Kaspersky researchers, BlackX's implementation of the exploit is "remarkably similar" to the one used by BlackLotus, allowing the attacker to enroll custom Machine Owner Keys (MOKs) and load arbitrary EFI files to bypass Secure Boot protections. However, BlackX differs from BlackLotus and deploys a different kernel driver and follow-on payloads. Kaspersky suggests that BlackX was either derived from reverse-engineering BlackLotus or from its source code.
