Key Takeaways Identity technical debt is the accumulated security, governance, and operational burden created by unmanaged identities, excessive permissions, outdated access, and fragmented controls. It’s caused by identity sprawl, privilege creep, dormant and orphaned accounts, excessive permissions, unmanaged non-human identities (NHIs), and persistent policy exceptions. It increases breach blast radius, Security Operations Center (SOC) investigation time, audit fatigue, operational disruption, delivery friction, and tool complexity. It should be measured using a five-part framework that covers inventory and ownership, dormancy, privilege, credentials, and remediation, rather than a single score. It can be reduced through four maturity stages and a six-step roadmap: Discover, Classify, Prioritize, Remediate, Prevent, and Measure. Qualys ETM Identity correlates identity sprawl, privilege exposure, and NHIs with Enterprise TruRisk , so this debt can be discovered, prioritized, and reduced through verified, closed-loop remediation. The Hidden Cost of Identity Technical Debt Most identity decisions are made under pressure: a contractor needs access today, a migration needs a service account now, an acquired business needs users provisioned on day one. Each solves an immediate need and quietly leaves risk behind.
