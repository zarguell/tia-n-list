Matt Burgess and Lily Hay Newman reported that two unrelated, unauthenticated databases exposed more than 3.5 billion usernames, passwords, national identity numbers and documents. Greg Pollock's China-linked trove held 1.64 billion documents, including at least 840 million distinct national ID numbers and 300 million phone numbers, while Jeremiah Fowler's credential store held about 2 billion logins, 8,471 plaintext folders and about 400,000 per-victim documents. The article says Pollock could not identify an owner and that the database became inaccessible during his investigation, seemingly as part of a ransomware attack or destructive campaign. Fowler contacted the hosting provider to restrict access; neither researcher could establish how long the data was exposed or whether others downloaded it.
