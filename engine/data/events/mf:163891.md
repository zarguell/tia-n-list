Threat researchers have uncovered a previously undocumented malware-as-a-service (MaaS) platform called VectraRAT, a full-stack toolkit that gives cybercriminals enterprise-grade intrusion capabilities for as little as $250 a month. Unlike most commodity RATs that fork leaked code from families like AsyncRAT or XWorm, VectraRAT was built entirely from scratch by a single developer the Windows implant, the Linux control server, the proprietary communication protocol, and even the licensing system that enforces subscription payments. VectraRAT MaaS SOCRadar’s Threat Research Unit (STRU) stumbled onto the platform on June 23, 2026, after discovering an exposed open directory that led investigators across more than ten servers, dozens of malware samples, live operator panel logs, and eventually a Telegram conversation with the developer, who operates under the alias “Nyxel,” according to research published by SOCRadar . Evidence suggests Nyxel has run the operation undetected for nearly four years, with a YouTube channel tracing back to August 2022. VectraRAT’s C2 traffic runs over a proprietary protocol on TCP port 3308 rather than reusing known malware frameworks, making it harder for signature-based defenses to flag. The developer reportedly upsells add-on services too, including “fully undetectable” crypting for $100–$350 monthly and bundled packages exceeding $2,000.
