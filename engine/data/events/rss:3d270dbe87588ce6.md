McDonald’s Japan recently experienced a cybersecurity breach that exposed customers’ purchase histories and mobile device location data. The fast-food giant said Friday that an unauthorized third party had gained access to a database associated with its official mobile app. The company has not disclosed how many customers were affected, when the intrusion occurred or who was behind it. The compromised information included customers’ purchase histories, IP addresses, internal customer identification numbers and location data from mobile devices used to access McDonald’s services. The company said the internal identification numbers could not be used on their own to identify individual customers. Names, email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses and credit card information were not affected by the breach. McDonald’s Japan said it was working to invalidate the authentication credentials used in the attack but did not explain how the hacker obtained them. The company has not detected any unauthorized use of its app or related services as a result of the incident and said customers did not need to change their passwords or take other security measures. McDonald’s Japan is investigating the affected systems and has reported the incident to Japan’s data protection authorities. It remains unclear whether the attacker stole any customer information.
