Cybersecurity policy has long treated data protection as a systems problem: encrypt the database, secure the network, restrict access, monitor activity, and keep attackers out. All of that still matters. But those defenses have a major blind spot. Once inside the wall the data itself is at risk. Every time data is used, copied, or moved, the risk that a malicious actor will steal and expose the data significantly increases. Policymakers need to consider a tested method that reduces the value of sensitive information if it compromised. Hospitals, banks, defense contractors and government agencies cannot operate on permanently unreadable data. They need to process records, share information, analyze intelligence and prepare data for artificial intelligence models. The reality is data moves. It travels through cloud platforms, vendors, backups, analytics tools, payment processors, software services, AI pipelines, and partner systems. Every handoff creates another place where information can be copied, cached, logged, and indexed, and another place where it can be mishandled or stolen. This is the gap encryption does not close. Encryption protects data when it is stored or traveling, only partially protects it when it is transmitted, and does not protect data at all when it is processed.
