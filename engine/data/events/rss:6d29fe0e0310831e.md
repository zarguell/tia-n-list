The Fugaku supercomputer operated by Japan's RIKEN Center for Computational Science has been taken offline following a security breach. The organization says that between November 10 and 13, an unauthorized party exploited the public dashboard displaying Fugaku's operational status to gain access to multiple internal servers. Although the research center has yet to determine the access method, RIKEN’s investigation confirmed that the intruder accessed some servers to plant malware that targeted user data. RIKEN’s Center for Computational Science operates the two supercomputers, making them available to researchers in academia and industry sectors. Fugaku, which entered shared use in March 2021, is a 537 petaflops supercomputer used in drug discovery, severe-weather forecasting, and simulations of airborne virus transmission. Rikyu is a 64 petaflops supercomputer used in AI scientific research. In response to the incident, RIKEN shut down Fugaku on November 14, disabled users’ login keys, and asked users to revoke and replace any credentials they had stored on the supercomputer. RIKE
