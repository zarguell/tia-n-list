A 36-year-old Ukrainian national was sentenced to 70 months in prison for participating in a string of ransomware attacks targeting various critical infrastructure operators and businesses from the middle of 2018 to late 2021, the Justice Department said Tuesday. Artem Aleksandrovych Stryzhak was involved in ransomware attacks on hospitals that disrupted patient care, government entities, a dentist office, a company in the oil and gas transportation industry and law firms. He joined various ransomware groups during his crime spree, including Nefilim and Hive, which attacked about 1,500 victims worldwide. “Ransomware attacks like those committed by the defendant wreak enormous havoc — harming victim companies that pay millions in remediation and ransoms, preventing hospitals and other critical facilities from accessing data, and putting sensitive information in the hands of bad actors,” Joseph Nocella Jr., U.S. attorney for the Eastern District of New York, said in a statement. Stryzhak pleaded guilty to multiple cybercrimes in late 2025. He was arrested in Spain in June 2024 and extradited to the United States in April 2025. Authorities are still looking for his alleged co-conspirator Volodymyr Tymoshchuk and previously announced a $11 million reward for information leading to his arrest or conviction.
