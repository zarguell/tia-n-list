Rockwell Automation on Tuesday informed customers that patches or workarounds are available for more than a dozen vulnerabilities discovered across its industrial automation products. Only one of the new advisories describes critical vulnerabilities. It covers four critical and high-severity denial-of-service (DoS) issues affecting the RSLinx Classic communications software. Exploitation can cause the RSLinx Classic service to crash, requiring a restart for recovery. Rockwell’s advisory for CVE-2026-9637 , a high-severity DoS flaw in ControlLogix and CompactLogix controllers, flags the vulnerability as exploited. However, it’s likely an error, as it’s only listed as such in the document’s header; elsewhere it’s listed as not exploited. Hands-On Cyber-Physical Systems Training at ICS Cybersecurity Conference CISA’s own advisory for CVE-2026-9637, published by the agency on Tuesday along with other Rockwell advisories, also says it’s not aware of exploitation. DoS vulnerabilities have also been addressed by Rockwell in 1756-ENBT, Logix controllers (third-party component), and FactoryTalk Historian Machine Edition. In FactoryTalk Historian the company fixed a high-severity remote code execution issue. In FactoryTalk Activation Manager, Rockwell resolved a high-severity flaw that allows an authenticated attacker to access files, processes and system resources with elevated privileges.
