Three weeks before the Grok Build findings, the same team published the first GhostShares report on Meta's Muse assistant, which publishes shared conversations as pages that open for anyone holding the link. The post explains the technique the Grok research later reused: Muse named each share's address after the conversation title, passive DNS and subdomain discovery listed those hostnames, and an incognito session with no login returned personal files and internal company documents. On October 20, 2026, after Ars Technica asked about the findings, Meta told the outlet it was suspending the feature; Claroc rechecked 40 shares that day and none opened, though the hostnames and titles remained in passive DNS history. The Grok Build post cites this earlier work directly and applies the same findability argument to every published AI app that gets its own hostname.
