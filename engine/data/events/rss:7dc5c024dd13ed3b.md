Attackers don’t have to steal information to cause serious harm. Sometimes, changing a temperature setting is enough. On November 5, the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), and three other federal agencies issued a warning urging operators to secure industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems. The warning follows cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration systems across Europe. Separately, France’s national blood service confirmed that the monitoring and control systems at three of its cold-storage sites had been affected. Staff intervened before any blood products or samples were compromised. The incident showed how a connected cooling system can put essential supplies at risk. On October 13, after the first attacks in France , refrigeration supplier Danfoss advised its Dutch customers to disconnect their systems from the public internet and reconnect only through a secure network. A Danfoss spokesperson told NOS that most stores followed the advice. When similar attacks reached the Netherlands on November 2, major supermarket chains were reportedly unaffected. At least 17 businesses, mainly butcher shops and small neighborhood stores, had their refrigeration systems compromised. Danfoss said that the affected systems were still accessible through unsecured connections and used default passwords.
