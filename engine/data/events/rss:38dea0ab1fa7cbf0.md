Summary This article examines DoppelCart, a large-scale shopping fraud network encompassing approximately 261,700 suspected domains identified by Recorded Future. These fraudulent storefronts copy legitimate retailers’ branding and product listings to deceive shoppers into disclosing personal and payment information, with some also capturing bank-issued verification codes to authorize fraudulent transactions. This research highlights the network’s widespread impersonation of US brands, evolving hosting infrastructure, and use of shared payment-routing configurations and the Medusa e-commerce platform. With approximately 40% of observed DoppelCart websites remaining active, understanding these patterns can help retailers identify brand impersonation and help consumers recognize fraudulent shopping sites. Background In September 2026, German cybersecurity company Nebty exposed DoppelCart, a large-scale online shopping fraud network with over 119,000 websites. The fraudulent storefronts copy legitimate retailers’ product listings, images, and descriptions to deceive shoppers into entering payment information. Researchers identified shared website designs, code, and infrastructure across thousands of suspicious storefronts, eventually linking the domains to the DoppelCart cluster. Most websites used .shop web addresses, and many advertised steep discounts, including offers of 65% off, to attract shoppers.
