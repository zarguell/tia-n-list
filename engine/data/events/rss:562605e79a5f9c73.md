Executive Summary Since mid-2025, the Kremlin has intensified restrictions on digital communications and infrastructure, very likely to suppress the organization of domestic political dissent and conduct digital surveillance within Russia. Increasingly strict measures targeting virtual private networks (VPNs), state-managed internet shutdowns, limitations on civilian communications platforms, and monitoring of domestic and foreign entities very likely represent an escalation from the approaches to censorship and surveillance pursued prior to 2024. However, despite intensifying internet restrictions, technical and political limitations will likely prevent the Kremlin from achieving its long-held objective of a fully “sovereign internet” in the near term. The most likely scenario is that Moscow exerts extensive censorship and monitoring of online activity with increasingly strict –– though not absolute –– controls on circumvention and anonymizing tools.
