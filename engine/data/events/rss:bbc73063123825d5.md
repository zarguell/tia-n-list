A proxyjacking campaign has impacted devices in 34 countries, monetizing victims’ internet connections through a commercial residential proxy network. The operator installed Pawns.app, a legitimate bandwidth-sharing application that lets users earn money by sharing their internet connections, on devices without the owners’ consent. Pawns.app account holders are rewarded for allowing traffic from other users to flow through their network, provided it runs on trusted, non-blocking IP addresses. By installing the app on multiple systems, the attacker likely accumulated larger payouts. Threat intelligence company GreyNoise discovered the campaign while investigating suspicious activity at an unnamed organization, and identified at least 143 compromised devices worldwide. According to the researchers, the attacker leveraged several vulnerabilities to breach edge devices and install the Pawns.app software: The researchers also observed targeting of Hikvision and Dahua DVR/NVR devices, but did not specify the vulnerabilities exploited in these systems. GreyNoise says that, in some cases, the attackers configured SSH key authentication as a backup access method. Even legitimate proxy networks can route suspicious traffic through volunteer-operated nodes due to insufficient oversight, potentially resulting in IP address blocks for participating users or exposing them to allegations of involvement in illegal activities.
