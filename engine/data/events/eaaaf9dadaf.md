Ryan Goldberg (Georgia) and Kevin Martin (Texas) each received four-year prison sentences after pleading guilty to conspiracy to obstruct interstate commerce by extortion. A third defendant, Angelo Martino (Florida), pleaded guilty with sentencing scheduled for July 9. All three were employed at cybersecurity firms — two specifically as ransomware negotiators — when they pivoted to conducting attacks themselves using the BlackCat/Alphv ransomware. The group received approximately $1.2 million from a single victim, paying 20% to BlackCat administrators and keeping 80%. BlackCat targeted over 1,000 organizations between November 2021 and December 2023 before being disrupted by authorities; months later, operators executed a $22 million exit scam. The case represents a deliberate corruption of trusted defensive roles and underscores insider risk within the incident response and negotiation ecosystem.
