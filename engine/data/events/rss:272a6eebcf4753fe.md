On Sunday, October 11, Microsoft recorded 2,900 failed sign-in attempts per minute against a handful of organizations. No human was typing passwords. The attempts came from agents in a tool called ARTEX, directed by a China state-aligned actor, and they worked just as hard on the weekend as on an ordinary Tuesday. For a long time, some have argued that attacks driven by artificial intelligence (AI) are not yet visible in practice. The fall of 2026 has made that position hard to defend. ARTEX is not the only example, but it is the best documented so far. It shows both how far the technology has come and where its limits still lie. What ARTEX Is ARTEX is a framework for automated penetration testing, developed in China, according to James Wilson of Risky Business by a lone security researcher, and originally released as open source. It is a self-hosted web application. The backend is a single binary written in Go that also serves a web interface built with Next.js, and the whole thing typically runs in Docker.
