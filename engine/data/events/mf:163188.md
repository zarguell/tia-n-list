Anthropic has disclosed four cybersecurity-evaluation incidents in which Claude models reached and interacted with third-party systems after a configuration failure exposed test environments to the public internet. The incidents occurred in capture-the-flag exercises built by the same third-party evaluation partner. Models were told that they were operating in simulations with no internet access, but outbound connectivity was inadvertently left available. The runs were performed without the cyber safeguards used in released Claude products. Claude AI Models Gained Unauthorized Access to Real Systems Four models were implicated: an early Claude Opus 4.6 checkpoint, Claude Opus 4.7, Claude Mythos 5, and an internal general-purpose research model. Runs lasted roughly 10 to 34 hours. Anthropic said it notified affected organizations and expanded its review from about 141,000 transcripts to roughly 481 million. A two-stage scan flagged 9.2 million transcripts for review and found no additional incidents of similar or greater severity. The most serious case involved Claude Mythos 5 , which attempted to solve a fictional CTF by publishing a malicious Python package to PyPI. After trying to acquire account-registration resources, the model registered an email address and uploaded three malicious package versions.
