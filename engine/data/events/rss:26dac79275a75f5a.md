A new report from Booz Allen demonstrates that “frontier super intelligence navigated unfamiliar industrial systems, adapted when blocked, and moved physical equipment within minutes.” The findings of the report were presented last week at SecurityWeek’s Industrial Control Systems (ICS) Cybersecurity Conference in Nashville, ahead of the public release. Use of the term ‘super intelligence’ is, however, confusing. This is not the first time the firm has used it, but it seems to be indistinguishable from the more common term of artificial intelligence (AI). It also quickly follows Donald Trump’s September 29, 2026 Executive Order to “replace the term ‘Artificial Intelligence’ (‘AI’) with ‘Super Intelligence’ (‘SI’) in the executive branch.” The problem, however, is that ‘superintelligence’ (one word) has long been used for a specific quality of AI. It was defined by Oxford philosopher Nick Bostrom in 2014 as, ‘Any intellect that greatly exceeds the cognitive performance of humans in virtually all domains of interest.’ It is consequently more akin or superior to artificial general intelligence (AGI), which is in turn superior to common or garden AI. So, is Booz Allen referring to frontier AI in line with Trump’s executive order, or something more advanced in line with Bostrom’s ‘superintelligence’?
