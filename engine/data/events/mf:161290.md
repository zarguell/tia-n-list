A newly disclosed five vulnerabilities in Palo Alto Networks GlobalProtect, the VPN and endpoint agent deployed across enterprise environments. The findings include local privilege-escalation flaws and an alleged credential-recovery technique that could expose Active Directory passwords from an affected device. The disclosure has also sharpened scrutiny of vendor response practices when researchers follow coordinated vulnerability disclosure procedures. Five GlobalProtect Vulnerabilities Martijn van Ramesdonk, who published the research on a dedicated website, said he first reported the issues to Palo Alto Networks in early April 2026. He said two findings were ultimately consolidated into CVE-2026-0251. Palo Alto’s advisory describes CVE as a group of local privilege-escalation vulnerabilities affecting GlobalProtect , allowing a low-privileged local user to obtain SYSTEM privileges on Windows or root privileges on macOS and Linux. The NVD assigns CVE-2026-0251 a CVSS 3.1 base score of 7.8. That rating reflects the impact of a weakness that requires local access but can grant an attacker complete operating-system control. In practical terms, malware or an untrusted user account that has already landed on a corporate endpoint could execute arbitrary commands with the device’s highest privileges, bypassing user-level security boundaries.
