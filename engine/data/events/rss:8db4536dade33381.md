Intro Black Friday remains a defining moment of the holiday shopping season and a key period for cybercriminals seeking to exploit the surge in consumer activity. The scale of this demand is reflected in seasonal spending patterns. According to PwC’s post-holiday analysis , holiday spending rose by 6.4% year over year in 2025, with Black Friday once again being the peak sales day generating nearly $20 billion. Consumer behavior points to the same trend: KPMG’s 2026 Consumer Pulse Survey found that Black Friday remains the most important sales event, with 62% of respondents pointing to its importance. Moreover, it is strongly associated with online shopping, with 83% of consumers preferring to buy online or combine online and in-person purchases on this occasion. To find the best offers, 55% of respondents in 2026 planned to research purchases through retailer websites and 46% through brand websites. As shopping activity concentrates online during peak sales periods, threat actors gain broader opportunities to exploit consumer demand, trusted brands, and heavy promotional traffic to steal credentials, payment data, and money.
