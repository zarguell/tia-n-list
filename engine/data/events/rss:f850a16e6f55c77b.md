Taking down a banking Trojan does not necessarily stop financial fraud. Cybercriminals can switch malware families, reuse existing infrastructure, and exploit legitimate software to keep their operations running. In LATAM, this adaptability creates a persistent challenge for businesses trying to protect financial assets and customer data. Using ANY.RUN , we explore the connections behind Brazilian-linked financial fraud and identify opportunities for earlier detection and more effective response. Key Takeaways Financial fraud operations in LATAM are highly interconnected , combining banking Trojans, infostealers, phishing kits, and legitimate remote access software. Vidar and StealC dominate the analyzed Brazilian infostealer submissions , appearing in 73% and 49% of sessions, respectively. Attackers exploit legitimate websites and software to evade detection, extend their reach, and maintain unauthorized access.
