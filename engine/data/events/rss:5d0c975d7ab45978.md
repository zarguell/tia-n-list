The evolution of malware command-and-control (C2) infrastructure has shifted away from fragile, centralized servers and hardcoded domains toward resilient, decentralized blockchain networks that eliminate single points of failure. Threat actors are leveraging immutable public ledgers such as Bitcoin transactions, EVM smart contracts via techniques like EtherHiding, TRON wallets, and Solana memo programs to store, mask, and persistently update their operational endpoints. By executing read-only remote procedure call (RPC) queries to retrieve encrypted C2 configurations directly into compromised endpoint memory, adversaries can continuously bypass traditional perimeter defenses and domain seizures. Modern cybersecurity requires a fundamental shift away from static domain blocklists toward monitoring anomalous outbound JSON-RPC traffic, analyzing endpoint memory, and treating blockchain infrastructure as an active threat communication channel. The command-and-control (C2) channel is the operational backbone of any persistent malware campaign. It is also the most fragile component. The moment an actor's C2 infrastructure is identified and reported to the hosting provider, the domain registrar, or law enforcement, the campaign's operational capability collapses.
