In September 2023, an infostealer infection hit a small print shop in Qayyarah, Iraq – a walk-in vendor that, it turns out, at least four Iraqi police units and an army brigade all paid to type up their paperwork. Neither the police nor the army was breached. But their documents were on that machine, including a 189-name list that labels each person “the ISIS member” beside his village, his job, and whether he still owns his house. How This Surfaced The file that started this investigation was a spreadsheet-style Word document, recovered from an infostealer log dated 30 September 2023 . Its title, in the compiling institution’s own words, was an inventory of ISIS-linked houses within the responsibility sector of the Imam Gharbi Police Station – and it ran to 189 named individuals across eight villages, one row per person, each labelled in the name column as اسم الداعشي: “the ISIS member’s name.” A police station’s internal list of alleged ISIS affiliates should be on a police computer.
