Most of the people who depend on a SOC will never log into it. Here’s how we’re getting the right report in front of them. There’s a question I hear in almost every client conversation, usually right after we’ve walked through their dashboards: “This is great, but can we get a report?” It took me a while to appreciate how much that question matters. Reports are how security work gets seen Most of the people who depend on security monitoring will never open a SIEM. The CEO wants to know whether things are under control. An MSSP’s end clients want proof they’re getting what they pay for. Auditors and insurers want dated evidence that monitoring actually happened. The IT director wants a weekly view without having to log in and click around. For a lot of those people, the report is the only part of the SOC they ever see. When it shows up regularly and it’s clear, it builds trust in the work your team is doing. When it doesn’t show up, the work is mostly invisible to them, however good it is. The gap Open-source SIEM is great at the hard part. You get the detections, the data and the dashboards without a per-GB license bill. What it hasn’t given you is a way to turn all of that into a report that goes out on its own. Grafana open source, which is where most of these dashboards live, doesn’t schedule reports; that’s a Grafana Enterprise feature. So teams fill the gap by hand.
