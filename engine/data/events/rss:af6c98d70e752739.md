The Tor Project has released new security fixes for its core software as a distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attack continues to strain the volunteer-run relays that make up its network. Tor 0.4.9.17 , announced on November 13, patches several security issues. The project singled out one fix related to how relays’ built-in DoS protections deal with IPv6. “This release comes in rapid response to the ongoing DDoS attack on the network, which has affected most relay operators. This is not an easy problem to address, as relays across the network are being overwhelmed by a massive flood of client circuits,” Tor developer David Goulet said in the announcement. According to the project, more defenses are under consideration, and new versions should follow shortly. Anyone running a relay has been urged to update without delay. The latest version comes less than three weeks after Tor 0.4.9.15 , which introduced three DoS protections against what the project described as a “thunderstorm” of directory requests. Writing to relay operators at the time, Goulet noted that a single relay could be hit with hundreds of circuits per second. He cautioned that relays would remain under heavy load for as long as the attack lasted. Reports from relay operators suggest the latest update hasn’t ended the disruption.
