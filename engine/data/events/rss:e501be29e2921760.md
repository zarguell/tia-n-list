As U.S. policymakers focus on outages that appear partly caused by widespread patching of AI-identified vulnerabilities, the U.K.’s top cyber intelligence agency says it is seeing more cyberattacks carried out with AI assistance. On Tuesday, three officials from the National Cyber Security Centre said they had observed “a marked rise” in AI-assisted cyberattacks since September. “Organizations across multiple sectors are reporting incidents as a result of threat actors using ‘agentic offensive frameworks’; software originally designed for red teaming being applied beyond its defensive purpose,” wrote David Chismon, chief technology officer for architecture, and two partially-named NCSC staffers, Kris B., incident handler, and Ellen T, lead data scientist on the vulnerability management team. The incidents resemble past AI-enabled hacks, defined by rapid, automated vulnerability discovery and attempted exploitation of all of a target’s internet-facing assets “in quick succession.” The impact on victims ranged from outages to system compromise. “What has changed, is how quickly and persistently threat actors can conduct attacks – at low cost – using these frameworks combined with cheap, capable open models or illegal API resellers,” the officials wrote. The NCSC officials called the threat sobering but noted that “none” of the vulnerabilities or attacks are novel.
