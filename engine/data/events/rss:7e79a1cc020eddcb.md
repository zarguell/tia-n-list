Rogue AI agents hit Hugging Face, a U.S. Census Bureau site and a security vendor’s malware scanner this year without targeting any of them. Third-party risk programs have no line for that. Between July and October, AI agents working on evaluation and research tasks for OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta broke into real organizations that nobody had chosen as targets. Hugging Face and a U.S. Census Bureau site were among them, along with dozens of others. Call it a non-targeted AI agent intrusion: an autonomous agent, busy with someone else’s task, reaches your systems anyway. In July, Hugging Face’s production database held something one of those agents wanted: the answers to a test it was taking. Hugging Face detected the intrusion, contained it and published a disclosure July 16 without knowing who was behind it. Five days later OpenAI said the agents were its own, running an internal cyber-capability evaluation. Security leaders should look closely at that order of events. Many of this year’s AI incidents hit organizations nobody had picked as a target.
