The European Commission confirmed Thursday that it had been hit by a cyberattack last month involving a publicly available artificial intelligence tool, following media reports of the incident. Speaking to journalists, Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier said the first signs of the attack were detected October 22. He described the incident as an “AI-generated cyber campaign” but did not identify the tool used or the attackers’ apparent motivation. POLITICO first reported that the Commission had been impacted by the cyberattack. It said CERT-EU had identified the attackers’ use of ARTEX, an open-source AI hacking platform that the FBI last week warned was being used both by criminals and state-sponsored groups. The attack reportedly disrupted the European Commission’s personnel systems for more than 10 days, including payroll, leave requests, medical reimbursements and translation services. A commission spokesperson confirmed to Recorded Future News they had “taken the necessary steps in response to a detected cyber campaign,” including taking several services offline as “a protective measure.” They said they had notified affected individuals and were “preventively checking other systems” while keeping in touch with CERT-EU and the European Data Protection Supervisor. Regnier said investigators had found no evidence of a data breach affecting Commission staff broadly and were unaware of any ransom demand. He said the investigation remains open.
