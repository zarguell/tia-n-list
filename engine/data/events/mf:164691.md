EvilTokens emerged in February 2026 and quickly gave cybercriminals a ready-made platform for large-scale business email compromise attacks. It combined phishing templates, malicious landing pages, delivery tools, token-management capabilities, and AI features designed to create more convincing messages. The platform reportedly offered dozens of phishing themes, including fake invoices, shared documents, request-for-proposal messages, password-expiration alerts, voicemail notices, and cloud-service notifications. Operators could customize pages, redirect victims through multiple sites, use CAPTCHA-style checks , and track targets after they interacted with a lure. A key feature was an AI assistant that helped criminals tailor phishing emails to a target’s role. Once an inbox was compromised, the same capability could review email activity, locate high-value employees, and identify information useful for fraud, such as invoices, wire-transfer discussions, executive correspondence, and vendor relationships. EvilTokens Hijacks Microsoft Accounts EvilTokens primarily targeted Microsoft accounts through device code phishing. The technique abuses a legitimate OAuth device-code sign-in process intended for devices with limited input options, such as smart TVs, printers, Teams devices, and conferencing equipment.
