How Investigative Capabilities Help Countries Strengthen AML/CFT Effectiveness and Move Off the FATF Grey List TL;DR The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) measures outcomes such as investigations, prosecutions and asset recovery. These outcomes depend on the operational ability of financial intelligence units, law enforcement and regulators to detect, investigate and disrupt illicit financial activity. Modern investigative technology strengthens that ability by connecting financial transactions, accounts, people and companies to reveal the hidden networks behind financial activity. Investment in these capabilities is one of the clearest ways a government can show real commitment to fighting financial crime. Countries leave the FATF grey list by demonstrating effective AML/CFT investigations, not by introducing new legislation, publishing more compliance guidance or gathering more data. Being placed on the FATF grey list (a.k.a. Jurisdictions under Increased Monitoring) puts a government under sustained pressure to prove it can fight financial crime.
