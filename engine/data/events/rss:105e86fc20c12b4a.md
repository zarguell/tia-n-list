Threat actors have been exploiting SAP NetWeaver and Cisco Expressway vulnerabilities in the wild, the US cybersecurity agency CISA warned on Friday. The NetWeaver flaw, tracked as CVE-2025-42884 (CVSS score of 6.5), is a medium-severity issue that could allow unauthenticated attackers to leak or modify server information. Successful exploitation could allow attackers to “inject JNDI environment properties or pass a URL used during JNDI lookup operations, enabling access to an unintended JNDI provider,” reads a NIST advisory . SAP patched the bug with its November 2025 security updates . There appear to have been no reports of the security hole being exploited in the wild before CISA’s Friday alert. Tracked as CVE-2026-76466 (CVSS score of 9.8), the Expressway defect is a critical hardcoded credentials weakness that could allow attackers to execute system commands with root privileges. The use of persistent cookies, combined with insufficient validation of client certificates in certain conditions, could allow attackers who possess the cookies to connect to the database service and invoke specific RPC commands. It impacts Expressway Core (Expressway-C) and Expressway Edge (Expressway-E) devices running vulnerable software versions and configured in a cluster, and was patched on October 21 in Expressway Series release X15.5.20.
