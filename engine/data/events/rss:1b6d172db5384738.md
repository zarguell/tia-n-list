The dependable way to get Palo Alto or FortiGate logs into a file you can analyze is syslog. Forward the firewall’s logs to a small Linux collector running rsyslog, and export the collector’s file. For history that’s already on the box, FortiGate can download raw logs from its GUI. On PAN-OS, the practical source of history is usually Panorama, Strata Logging Service or your SIEM. Firewalls see every connection in and out, and they’re now a target in their own right. Their logs answer “where did that host talk to?” and “who logged into the firewall at 3 a.m.?” Prime Core has 2093 detection rules for firewall logs, mapped to 554 MITRE ATT&CK techniques. Among them: command-and-control over common protocols (T1071), exfiltration over alternative channels (T1048) and network scanning (T1046). Get Started for Free Before you start Check what’s actually logged. Firewalls log only what their rules tell them to, and the defaults are thinner than people assume. On PAN-OS, the predefined intrazone-default and interzone-default rules don’t log by default. Denied traffic that falls through to them leaves no trace unless someone overrode them to log. On FortiGate, logging is set per policy (security events only, or all sessions). Logging of traffic hitting the implicit deny policy is off by default. Check where history lives.
