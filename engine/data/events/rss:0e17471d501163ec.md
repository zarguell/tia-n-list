The CIA and Office of the Director of National Intelligence have discovered a data breach of an open-source intelligence analysis system, CyberScoop has confirmed. The New York Times first reported the breach Tuesday, writing that the breach had exposed some unclassified information. A source familiar with the breach said there was a disruption, but the exposure was limited. ‘The hack did not involve our classified networks or classified information, and no other systems are currently at risk,” the source said. The discovery is the latest in a series of intrusions affecting U.S. security agencies, including two at the FBI and another at the National Security Agency . Ben Bernstein, manager of cybersecurity advisers at Huntress, said in an emailed comment that while the reported breach didn’t hit classified data, open-source (OSINT) information can still be sensitive. “Calling this ‘unclassified’ doesn’t make it harmless,” he said. “These systems pull in public and commercial info like news, satellite imagery, and paid data feeds, so the raw material isn’t a state secret.
