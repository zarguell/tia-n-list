In his LABScon 26 keynote, Dino Dai Zovi, SVP and Head of Security at Adyen, argues that defenders can’t keep pace with AI-enabled attackers through software alone. He makes the case that the hardware-based security already protecting everyday payments can close that gap. Using tap-to-pay as an example, Dai Zovi notes how a secure element in a smartphone completes a transaction that malware on the device cannot touch. At the same time, data from the UK’s AI Security Institute on autonomous cyber capability shows end-to-end intrusions that once took days or months are now approaching minutes, with open-weight models only months behind frontier models. Arguing that the main risk from AI is not AI agents turning on humanity but humans using AI to achieve malicious goals, Dai Zovi proposes engineering for resilience using hardware-based controls. He draws on analogies with bank vaults and nuclear weapon safety principles to show how hardware controls have long contained risks that would otherwise depend on people or software behaving correctly. He then works through an AI cyber range on the assumption that none of its vulnerabilities can be patched, and shows how a handful of hardware boundaries leave an agent with nothing useful to take. He closes with a question. If the technology to contain AI already runs at scale in systems used many times a day, should we choose to apply it?
