North Korea’s Hangro VPN and mail platform has deployed a new management certificate across infrastructure in Pyongyang and Russia’s Far East, indicating that the services may be jointly administered across both locations. The new certificate hierarchy appeared around July 2026 on port 6006. It was found on Hangro servers in North Korea and on two Russian-hosted systems. Hangro is a North Korean VPN client that also includes email and real-time chat features. It has reportedly been used by North Korean trade representatives and officials working abroad. The management certificate is identical on servers using the North Korean addresses 175.45.176.21, 175.45.176.22, and 175.45.176.32, as well as Russian addresses 188.43.136.115 and 188.43.136.116. Its Subject Alternative Name field lists all five public-facing systems. It also includes 100.100.100.170, an address within carrier-grade NAT space that is not publicly routable. The presence of this internal-style address in a public certificate provides an unusual view of how the Hangro environment may be addressed internally. Hangro Reuses Pyongyang-Russia Certificate (Source: synapticsystems) The certificate uses a newer RSA-4096 configuration and is valid from July 26, 2026, through July 23, 2036. It is issued by a root certificate named “KEVIN ROOT CA.” The management interface requires client certificates, preventing unauthenticated users from accessing the service after the TLS handshake.
