Chinese hackers used an AI tool called Cairn to allegedly discover a zero-day while breaching several universities in Asia, according to a new report from Amazon’s Threat Intelligence team. CJ Moses, CISO of Amazon Integrated Security, said their investigation began on September 10, when they discovered a publicly accessible server with a publicly hosted instance of Cairn — an open-source autonomous AI penetration-testing framework. They were able to view the operational history of the platform, including previous prompts and more. The operator wrote their prompts in Simplified Chinese and the timestamps were configured to China Standard Time. The targeted victim organizations were universities in East Asia. Moses explained that Cairn is ideal for attackers because, when given a starting point and objective in plain language, it can coordinate multiple AI agents to complete a task. In this incident, the attacker provided IP addresses and asked Cairn to “obtain shell access” — meaning find a way to gain enough access to a system for various actions to be taken. “Based on those inputs, the AI agents planned, executed, and adjusted their approach as they learned, without the operator choosing each next step,” Moses said. One of the attempts involved 148 actions taken over 16 hours that were ultimately unsuccessful, including a failed attempt at exploiting the Log4Shell vulnerability.
