Britain’s National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) said Tuesday it has observed “a marked rise” since September in cyberattacks assisted by artificial intelligence. Echoing a message the agency has repeatedly stressed, the issue is not that AI is making established cybersecurity practices obsolete, but its adoption by attackers is reducing the margin for error among organizations that are failing to do the basics. In a blog post , the NCSC warned the technology was increasing attackers’ speed, scale and persistence while also reducing their costs — largely due to the widespread availability of the hacking frameworks “combined with cheap, capable open models or illegal API resellers.” “Organisations across multiple sectors are reporting incidents as a result of threat actors using ‘agentic offensive frameworks’,” said the NCSC, referring to the software “originally designed for red teaming being applied beyond its defensive purpose.” The agency said it was “worth noting” that “none of the weaknesses being exploited are new or novel” and that the tools being used by these AI agents “provide no new capability the typical threat actor would have access to.” The post — co-authored by David Chismon, the NCSC’s chief technology officer for architecture, alongside incident handler Kris B and lead data scientist Ellen T — identified Artex as the predominant framework receiving attention at the moment.
