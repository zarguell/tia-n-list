Detection engineering is both a role and an activity. It’s the discipline of building and maintaining the logic that identifies malicious or risky behavior within your organization. It’s where security analysis meets software engineering, turning raw logs and telemetry into actionable detections that defenders can rely on. Detection engineers bridge the gaps between security operations, threat intelligence, and infrastructure. They understand how attackers operate, how defenders respond, and how systems generate the data that supports those detections. A well-built detection can mean the difference between an alert that matters and one that drowns in noise. Precision and Recall: The Balancing Act Every detection faces a trade-off between precision and recall. Precision measures how much you can trust your alerts: of everything that fired, how much was actually malicious? High precision = fewer false positives = analysts trust the tool. Recall measures how much you’re catching: of everything malicious that happened, how much did you detect? High recall = fewer blind spots = threats don’t slip through. Detection engineering lives in the tension between the two: too much precision risks missing subtle threats; too much recall floods analysts with false positives.
