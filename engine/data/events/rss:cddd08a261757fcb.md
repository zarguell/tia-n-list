California’s attorney general filed a lawsuit against OpenAI on Monday in an effort to force the ChatGPT-maker to change its business practices over a July cybersecurity incident in which its artificial intelligence system hacked into another company on its own. In the lawsuit filed in San Francisco Superior Court, Attorney General Rob Bonta alleges that OpenAI misled and endangered the public and users of its services by knowingly developing advanced AI models without necessary safeguards. He says the company’s actions could put critical infrastructure, institutions and the public at risk, which makes it a “public nuisance” under state law. Bonta says OpenAI is a public nuisance, and he wants a judge to order the company to change its ways and give up any profits generated by what he claims are the company’s risky business practices. OpenAI announced an “unprecedented cyber incident ” on July 21, when its AI agent hacked into another AI company, Hugging Face , after escaping from what was supposed to be an isolated testing environment known as a sandbox. OpenAI said its AI used stolen credentials and discovered a previously unknown vulnerability to access Hugging Face’s servers. OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri said the lawsuit appears to single out OpenAI over a challenge that has been identified by every major AI lab. “Since the Hugging Face incident, we’ve undertaken an extensive and transparent effort to understand and address misaligned model activity,” Pusateri wrote
