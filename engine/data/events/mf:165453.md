RemoteThreat emerged from stealth mode on Tuesday with $7 million in pre-seed funding from Osage University Partners and DataTribe for its offensive operations platform. The company was founded by Chris Thompson (CEO) and Shawn Jones (CTO), both IBM X-Force red team leaders. The Nakasone Group , the company launched recently by retired US Army General Paul M. Nakasone, will serve as a strategic advisor to RemoteThreat . SecurityWeek interviewed Thompson earlier this year for our Hacker Conversations series. RemoteThreat’s O/C/O Platform is built for offensive cyber operations, serving enterprise and critical infrastructure red teams, US government mission teams, and vetted defense partners. It brings together eight connected systems: mission planning and oversight, command and control (C2), implants, an initial access framework, a set of composable capabilities, an obfuscation pipeline, targeting, tasking and analysis engines, and AI assistants. The platform provides implants that work across operating systems and architectures and support in-memory execution, along with a C2 system that has configurable traffic profiles and can run alongside a team’s existing C2. Operators can combine payloads, implants, and post-exploitation tools for each engagement.
