As many of you already know from my previous articles, I have always been a strong supporter of Indicators of Attack (IoAs) over Indicators of Compromise (IoCs). IoCs are useful, and I use them almost every day for investigations, Threat Hunting, and building my own Threat Intelligence feeds. But I believe we can get much more value from them than simply collecting indicators such as IP addresses, domains, or hashes. Every week, we see cybersecurity articles describing new campaigns, malware, or variations of attacks we already know. Sometimes the only difference is a new payload, a different domain, or a small change in the execution chain. Interesting research, of course, but what additional value can we extract if our detections already cover the attack behaviour? Let’s take this recent CyberSecurityNews article about ClickFix as an example. It describes malicious activity involving ClickFix and provides several IP addresses, domains, and other indicators. Great! We can import them into MISP, OpenCTI, Defender XDR, or Sentinel, investigate historical connections, and potentially block the malicious destinations. https://cybersecuritynews.com/hackers-use-clickfix/ But what happens tomorrow when the attacker creates another domain? Will the researchers update the article? Publish another one? Will someone add the new IoCs to a public feed?
