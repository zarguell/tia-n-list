The U.S. government has disrupted a North Korean social engineering campaign called ‘FaceStealer’, responsible for stealing more than $400 million in cryptocurrency. The attacks used multiple social engineering techniques, including deepfakes and ClickFix, to target individuals in the cryptocurrency and Web3 sectors, as well as open-source developers. The FaceStealer campaign ran for 18 months before the U.S. seized the underlying cloud-based infrastructure. U.S. authorities identified more than 900 webcam videos that hackers secretly recorded of tech workers, who were deceived in the massive North Korean operation. An FBI alert earlier this week notes that the North Korean threat actor hijacked accounts on Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack, and Discord, and used them to invite victims to video meetings under various pretenses. During the video calls, the attacker showed recorded footage of people the targets knew and who had previously fallen victim, too. The FBI explains that while the target waited for the meeting to start, the hacker secretly recorded a video, which was later used with other targets. The meeting sometimes featured recordings of multiple familiar individuals to add pressure on the current victim in the next stage.
