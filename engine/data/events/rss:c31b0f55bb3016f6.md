Recent incidents indicate that Russia is now targeting Starlink infrastructure in Eastern Europe. On 23 September, power equipment at a Starlink ground station near Warsaw was set on fire. Polish authorities suspect that the perpetrators acted on behalf of Russian intelligence. On 9 October, attackers damaged Starlink antennas at the Plana Teleport facility south of Sofia. A Bulgarian court has since approved European arrest warrants for two German suspects.[2][3] During the night of 17–18 October, an attempted arson attack targeted Starlink equipment at a data center in Vilnius. The facility supports nine satellite antennas whose communications are used partly by Ukraine. Lithuanian authorities linked the incident to Russia. Increased security prevented significant damage or disruption.[2] Starlink and its military counterpart, Starshield, support Ukrainian battlefield communications, reconnaissance and drone operations. Starlink and Starshield enable Ukrainian long-range drone operations by supporting command and control, near-real-time video transmission and the exchange of targeting and reconnaissance data.
