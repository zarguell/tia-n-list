Google on Wednesday announced Gemini 4 Carbon, a frontier AI model that, according to the company, beats rival models on benchmarks for detecting, exploiting and patching vulnerabilities. The model is not broadly available yet. This week, Google is opening access to a select group of cyber defenders, then expanding it in stages to a wider pool of approved partners via its Fairwind Program . Fairwind offers governments, critical infrastructure operators and major technology platforms early access to advanced models for cyber defense. The company says more than 650 organizations currently participate. Carbon follows Gemini 4 Argon , which Google launched two weeks ago. Some Fairwind partners already have exclusive access to Argon’s cybersecurity defense capabilities. Google said Carbon scored 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro, which tests a model’s ability to uncover flaws in real-world software. It also achieved 54.8% on the ExploitGym exploitation benchmark and 71.0% on Collinear’s CWE-bench patching benchmark, topping OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.1 in the company’s comparison. These results were obtained with no guardrails in place, the company noted.
