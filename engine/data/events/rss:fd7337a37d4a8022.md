A cyberattack has forced hundreds of supermarkets across France to throw away refrigerated and frozen food after attackers interfered with the systems that keep it cold. The attack caused significant financial losses and food waste. Some installations reportedly reached temperatures of around 50°C (122°F), with damaged equipment and reports of fires adding to the disruption. The attack, which struck overnight from October 11 to 12, affected stores belonging to Intermarché, Netto, Coopérative U, Carrefour, E.Leclerc, and Monoprix. These stores do not share a single parent company, but they all use refrigeration-control equipment supplied by Danish manufacturer Danfoss. These commercial refrigeration-management systems let operators manage the equipment that keeps supermarket food refrigerated or frozen. Le Monde reported that the attackers extended defrost cycles from one hour to eight hours, delaying alarms and giving staff less opportunity to intervene before stock was ruined. Danfoss told AFP that most affected equipment consisted of legacy products that no longer receive software or security updates. It identified AK-SM800 and AK-SC355 systems among those affected and said it had long advised customers to move away from obsolete models and keep supported equipment updated. Les Mousquetaires, the group behind Intermarché and Netto, has estimated its losses at several million euros. The damage goes beyond spoiled food.
