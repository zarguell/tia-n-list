Back in college, I took an anthropology course called “Peoples and Cultures of Southeast Asia.” While the title sounded like your everyday anthro course, this one was a little different. It had fewer students, maybe around 10, and took place from 7 – 9 p.m., most college kids’ nightmare — but it was the perfect time of day to videochat with Malaysian students on the other side of the globe with a 12-hour time difference. My university had a 14-week semester, while the university in Kuala Lumpur had seven weeks, so our class had time before meeting the students to study Malaysia’s pre- and post-colonial history, political climate, and cultural and religious dynamics; watch famous Petronas Deepavali and Chinese New Year ads ; and (of course) drool over pictures of nasi lemak and teh tarik. When both classes began videochatting with one another (yeah, we used Zoom before it was cool), our questions were quite tame.
