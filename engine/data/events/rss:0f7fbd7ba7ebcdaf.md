France’s national cybersecurity agency is investigating a suspected ransomware attack that impaired the computer systems of the University Hospital of Guadeloupe, forcing doctors to postpone surgeries, suspend some cancer treatments and revert to pen and paper. France’s national cybersecurity agency, known as ANSSI, said Tuesday the incident appeared to involve ransomware and that investigators were examining whether hackers gained access to the hospital’s systems through an unnamed IT supplier that was reportedly compromised several months ago. French prosecutors have launched a criminal investigation into the incident. It remains unclear who was behind the attack. The hospital’s management told local media on Monday that it had not received a ransom demand and ruled out paying a ransom. The hospital — the largest on the French Caribbean island of approximately 380,000 residents — said on its website it had canceled some surgical procedures, medical consultations and imaging tests through at least Friday. Radiation therapy has been temporarily suspended over safety concerns, while chemotherapy sessions scheduled for Monday were canceled. The hospital is reviewing upcoming appointments based on each patient’s medical needs. Emergency services, dialysis treatments and urgent cardiac procedures remain available.
