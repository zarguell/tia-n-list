Introduction In early 2026, we identified a new, active intrusion campaign that we have named Operation ShadowCloud . The threat actor behind it is abusing the legitimate MeshAgent utility to drop two brand-new backdoors. These implants hide their command‑and‑control (C2) traffic behind cloud services Microsoft SharePoint, GitHub, and Ably , allowing the implants to blend in with normal traffic, effectively evading network-based detections. We’ve called the implants GraphDoor and GitDoor , reflecting the cloud platform they use for C2. While their C2 protocols differ, the two backdoors share key capabilities: On‑demand plug-in download : fetching additional modules as needed In‑memory shellcode injection : loading malicious code directly into memory to extend functionality without touching the disk The threat actor specifically deploys a modified Erfrp client and Ably messaging service-based plug-ins to proxy traffic between infected systems and C2 servers. In addition to the plug-ins, we uncovered several shellcode families, most of them delivered as COFF (Common Object File Format) binaries.
