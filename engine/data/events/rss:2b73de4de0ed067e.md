Hackers have made millions of attempts to exploit newly disclosed flaws in PHP, widely used software that helps run websites worldwide, as government agencies and businesses work to install emergency fixes, cyber threat intelligence firms say. Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince said the company detected thousands of attempts across its network during the 10 hours before the vulnerabilities were formally announced. That rose to hundreds of thousands within two hours of the announcement and eventually millions, he wrote in a post on X. Others have reported similar activity. Cisco Talos said it observed attack attempts within hours of disclosure. Arctic Wolf detected attempts to make targeted servers download additional software, though it could not confirm those downloads succeeded. The vulnerabilities, known as PHP2Shell, could let hackers run commands on a website’s server without logging in, potentially giving them access to sensitive data or control over the site, officials and experts say. It’s unclear how many hackers are involved in the efforts or how many systems have been successfully breached.
