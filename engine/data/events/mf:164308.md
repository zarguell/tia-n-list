Security researchers have discovered a new Android banking Trojan called RatHat. This malware combines artificial intelligence (AI) , abuse of accessibility features, and Android Debug Bridge (ADB) capabilities to steal financial credentials, PINs, and one-time passcodes. RatHat Android Malware According to MalwareBytes , RatHat differs from traditional Android malware because it does not rely solely on pre-programmed automation scripts. Instead, it provides a live AI assistant with access to the Android accessibility tree, allowing it to interpret the content displayed on the victim’s screen and dynamically decide where to tap, scroll, or input data. This AI-driven interaction model makes RatHat more difficult to detect. Conventional mobile security products typically identify malicious activity based on fixed behavior patterns, known overlay templates, or hardcoded automation sequences. In contrast, RatHat can vary its navigation path depending on device settings, installed applications, and the screens presented to the user. The infection process begins with social-engineering tactics, such as smishing messages and malicious advertisements. Victims are redirected to fraudulent download pages that impersonate trusted applications, such as streaming platforms or popular web browsers.
