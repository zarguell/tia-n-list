A new version of MacSync is using a longer, more complex infection chain to install an information stealer and a backdoor on macOS devices . Kaspersky researchers first spotted the variant in September 2026. Unlike earlier versions built largely around AppleScript, it uses binary droppers and final payloads written in Swift and Objective-C. MacSync is sold as malware-as-a-service, so operators can choose how to lure victims. The latest samples arrive in malicious DMG disk images disguised as software. In one campaign, the attackers promoted “Toria,” a nonexistent cryptocurrency wallet, through a website and posts on X and Telegram. The binaries support both Apple silicon and Intel Macs. MacSync Malware Evolves Its Tactics In the more complex infection chain, the app inside the DMG removes its quarantine attribute and decrypts a hidden URL to find the next stage. In at least one sample, that URL led to a public iCloud calendar. The downloader passed the calendar’s contents to the zsh shell; commands placed after the event’s DESCRIPTION: line then fetched an archive containing another malicious app. Other samples used an attacker-controlled server instead of the calendar.
