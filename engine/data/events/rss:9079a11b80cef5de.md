A ransomware attack discovered Monday at Vanderbilt University in Nashville, Tennessee appears to have compromised vast amounts of student and employee personal data, prompting the university to take several digital services intermittently offline as it continues responding to the incident. As of Tuesday morning, the university shared an update that select campus-wide systems and applications were safe to use again, including campus WiFi, Okta, Outlook, Brightspace, Zoom and YES, while others remain restricted, including CampusPrint. Students, faculty and staff were also allowed to resume use of Vanderbilt devices, following orders to shut them down Monday. “Vanderbilt Information Technology continues to work with government agencies and other partners to manage this situation, and we will continue to send updates today and throughout this week for as long as necessary,” the update said. A ransomware group known as BYOD has taken responsibility for the attack, according to a post on the group’s leak blog. The group, which emerged in September, has since taken credit for several attacks targeting academic and health care institutions including one last month on the University of New Mexico Health Sciences Center that caused system outages, disrupted patient care and impacted academics.
