Senior Trump administration officials on Wednesday insisted that recent service outages underpinning much of everyday life — and U.S. national security — are not the result of a coordinated digital attack on the country. Over the last week, a global blackout of common Microsoft products, a nationwide ground stop by the FAA, glitches in the banking system and National Security Agency, the country’s largest spy agency, have rattled the public and policymakers on both sides of the aisle who fear the high-profile disruptions are secretly the work of foreign adversaries. “With the processes that we put in place, what we have seen over the last ten days is not an orchestrated attack by a foreign malign actor,” Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton testified before the House Homeland Security Committee. “We do not have any indication that it would be.” Throughout the panel’s annual worldwide threats hearing, Clayton, along with Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin and FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia, sought to address bipartisan concerns, chalking up the interruptions to emerging AI capabilities, aging infrastructure, and increased system patching and upgrades.
