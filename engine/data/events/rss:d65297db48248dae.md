FCC Commissioner Olivia Trusty said Wednesday she hopes consumers will begin seeing products bearing the U.S. Cyber Trust Mark within the next year, offering a new outlook for the device-security labeling program as officials work to finalize requirements for its rollout. Speaking at a Consumer Technology Association event, Trusty said the Federal Communications Commission expects to seek public comment on technical requirements and testing procedures in the coming months. The agency on Tuesday opened a separate proceeding covering label design, certification renewal and checks on products after they enter the market. FCC Chairman Brendan Carr assigned Trusty to oversee implementation of the voluntary program, which is intended to help shoppers compare the cybersecurity protections of connected household products such as cameras, appliances and baby monitors. The update is the first in several months to address the program’s status. First envisioned during the Biden administration, the mark is akin to the Energy Star label that helps shoppers identify energy-efficient products.
