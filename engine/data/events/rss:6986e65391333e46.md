There’s mounting evidence of spyware attacks growing more indiscriminate, leading to more everyday citizens being hit as opposed to traditionally “high-value” targets. The AdFection spyware attacks , which could infect anyone visiting a website that had advertising on it, represented the most recent revelation. And iVerify published a report Tuesday analyzing customer infections and threat notifications from Apple that contributes to the trend. “Conventional guidance on spyware risk focused on people with a public profile, a sensitive job or a role that put them on a government’s radar,” the company said. “Some Apple Threat Notification cases iVerify saw this year don’t fit that pattern.” “In Ottawa, Apple Threat Notifications reached the children of a city bus driver and a stay-at-home mom,” it continued. “In Australia, one went to a limousine driver. None of them had a public profile, a sensitive job or an obvious connection to anyone worth spying on. These cases matter because they show the threat reaching people who had no reason to expect it. For an enterprise, the employees most likely to be hit look much more like the limousine driver than like the CEO.” Part of that is that when spyware attacks are less targeted, they might be more likely to hit those who aren’t as diligently focused on routine cyber hygiene, as opposed to CEOs who are probably regularly upgrading their defenses, iVerify told CyberScoop.
