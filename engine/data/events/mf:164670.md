The malware campaign, tracked as “Steamtv Esp,” abuses interest in free IPTV and streaming content. Victims who click malicious advertisements are sent to a phishing site that appears to offer a TV-streaming application. The site checks whether visitors use Android devices and hides the download option from non-Android users. Users who proceed are instructed to enable installation from unknown sources and grant Android Accessibility permissions. The downloaded APK is a dropper linked to infrastructure previously associated with the Mirax banking trojan. However, the final payload is a previously unseen malware family, now named StreamRat. The campaign reflects a wider mobile-malware trend in which attackers use pirated sports, movie, and IPTV applications as lures. ThreatFabric has observed similar streaming-themed campaigns across several countries, using social-media ads to direct victims to malicious Android apps. StreamRat Hijacks Android Devices After installation, the dropper attempts to become the device’s default launcher. This means pressing the Android Home button can redirect the victim back to the malicious app interface, helping the attackers maintain control during the infection process. The dropper also downloads the StreamRat payload into the public Downloads folder under a filename such as update_{timestamp}.apk . It then requests permission to install the second-stage application and launches it. A notable technique involves a deliberately non-functional VPN
