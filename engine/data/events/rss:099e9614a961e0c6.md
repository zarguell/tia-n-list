Ask many IT teams how they would recognize ransomware, and the answer is still “when files start changing extensions.” By then, the intrusion may already include credential theft, privilege escalation, defense evasion, recovery sabotage, lateral movement and data exfiltration. Encryption is often a late-stage action, not the beginning of the attack. In data-extortion attacks it never happens at all: the operators steal data and threaten to leak it without encrypting anything. Recent attacks against on-premises SharePoint servers illustrate that sequence. After exploiting internet-facing systems, operators used stolen credentials and legitimate administration tools to move through victim environments before deploying ransomware. The practical lesson is simple: defenders need to detect the behaviors that precede encryption, not only the encryptor itself.
