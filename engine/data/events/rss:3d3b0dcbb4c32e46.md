There’s an active investigation into a hack of the FBI’s National Crime Information Center, bureau director Kash Patel said Tuesday, but he otherwise declined to comment on details of the breach. Patel said “the matter is ongoing” when asked at a new conference about who was behind the hack reported Friday and what information they obtained from the NCIC, a national database for sharing information on warrants, fugitives, missing persons and background checks. “We’ve publicly released, as we always do, information we are permitted to at this time, but it’s an ongoing investigation,” he said, adding that the FBI was working with other agencies on the matter. “So we’re working with the interagency, and as we’ve done in the past, we will make all the information public either through our partners at the Department of Justice or through our engagements on Capitol Hill.” President Donald Trump over the weekend said the breach was “not good” and that he’d just learned about it, but otherwise downplayed it, something he often does in response to cyberattacks against the United States. “I don’t like that,” Trump said. “But maybe we will learn things that we would’ve never learned otherwise.” It was the second breach in recent months at the FBI, which suffered an attack from ShinyHunters in September that saw the bureau’s website defaced and the hacking group claiming it stole sensitive information on FBI agents and jobseekers.
