Your banking app won’t load. Someone posts that an airline is having computer problems. Then you see a screenshot showing trouble at a cloud provider. By the time you’ve scrolled through a few more posts, someone is telling you to withdraw cash and prepare for a nationwide blackout. That is quite a journey from an error message. Recent technology disruptions have provided plenty of material for this kind of speculation. As CNN describes , real outages have become mixed up with exaggerated reports, unrelated events, and conspiracy theories about what might happen next. A routine emergency alert test was even folded into the narrative. The outages are real and so is the inconvenience. The problem with reporting on computer failures is how quickly “the system is down” becomes “the system is under attack.” Non-malicious causes include faulty software updates, configuration mistakes, equipment failures, power interruptions, and damaged cables. Cyberattacks can also disrupt services by overwhelming them with traffic or using ransomware to make systems unusable.
