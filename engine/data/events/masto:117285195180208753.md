CHOSEN BRICK, Iran's surveillance malware. Hackers linked to the Iranian state are using a new strain of malware to spy on dissidents, activists, and journalists worldwide. The alert was issued jointly by the UK National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), the FBI, and Dutch authorities. The malware spreads through social engineering on Telegram and WhatsApp, with attackers posing as trusted contacts or technical support. Malicious files are disguised as legitimate applications like Norton Antivirus, Adobe Flash Player, or KeePass, and often appear as fake medical exam results such as MRI documents.

After execution, CHOSEN BRICK installs silently, maintaining persistence through Windows Registry keys and adding Defender exclusions. The operators control infected devices via a single Telegram bot per victim, gaining full access to screen capture, microphone recording, email extraction, and WhatsApp and Telegram data harvesting. Exfiltrated data is sent through VultrObjects, StorjShare, or Telegram, with recent variants using SOCKS5 proxies. Stolen data frequently appears on pro-Iranian leak sites as a harassment tactic. Iranian intelligence has been linked to international kidnapping and lethal operations against dissidents.
