Cyberattacks almost certainly aren’t responsible for the procession of major tech outages over the past week, Trump administration officials say — but there’s a growing sense that patching fueled by a race to close vulnerabilities uncovered by artificial intelligence was a contributing factor. The top Democrats on the House and Senate intelligence committees are among those observing that the rise in vulnerability discoveries resulting from AI are at least partially to blame for tech service interruptions like those ranging from the aviation ground stop to incidents with major communications and banking systems, rather than the direct result of hacking. “What appears to be happening instead is a consequence of how quickly artificial intelligence is changing the cybersecurity landscape,” Sen. Mark Warner, D-Va., said Tuesday. “AI is making it possible to identify vulnerabilities across enormous numbers of systems at unprecedented speed, creating enormous pressure on companies and government agencies to patch those vulnerabilities immediately, often all at once, putting tremendous strain on systems that weren’t built to handle that pace of change.” And Rep.
