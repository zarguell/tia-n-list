Vulnerabilities discovered in Bluetooth-enabled brake controllers made by Australian firm Elecbrakes could allow hackers to manipulate trailer brakes, potentially causing accidents, researchers warned. The affected products include the EB2, VM1 and ELBC 2000 brake controllers from Elecbrakes. According to the cybersecurity agency CISA, which last week published an advisory on the vulnerabilities, the affected brake controllers are mainly used in Australia and New Zealand. The controllers regulate the braking force applied to trailers, with the EB2 unit mounted on the trailer and the VM1 unit on the towing vehicle. The devices can be controlled remotely via a special remote or a smartphone app. Researchers from Australian IT and cybersecurity consultancy Element Digital — with help from AI — discovered three vulnerabilities in the braking controllers, including two critical missing authentication and insufficient replay-attack protection issues. They demonstrated that an unauthenticated attacker who is within Bluetooth range of the targeted vehicle (up to 100 meters or 328 feet) can use a laptop or phone to engage or release a trailer’s brakes or adjust the braking force, without any interaction required from the victim. “Your trailer’s brakes slamming to maximum while your tow vehicle keeps going is the exact recipe for a jackknife – one of the more reliably fatal things a vehicle combination can do,” the researchers warned.
