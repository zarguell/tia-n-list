Here’s a counterintuitive truth: the ultimate goal of threat hunting isn’t to find more security incidents—it’s to drive continuous improvement across your entire security program. We’re not hunting merely to find present bad; we’re hunting to decrease the probability of future bad. Think of it like debugging. You don’t debug just to find bugs—you debug to build better software. Each bug you find teaches you something about your system, improves your testing, and makes future bugs less likely. Threat hunting works the same way. The Cyclical Nature of Threat Hunting Every threat hunting framework can be thought of as a cycle that improves with every revolution. Step 1: Determine Your Focus The biggest challenge in threat hunting? Answering this question: “Where do we even start?” You can’t just randomly poke around in logs hoping to find something interesting. You need a method to focus your efforts. The good news is that there are several effective approaches, and you don’t have to pick just one. In fact, most mature programs blend approaches over time. Hypothesis-driven hunting starts with an educated guess, perhaps informed by the latest threat intelligence. Maybe something like, “I bet adversaries are using scheduled tasks for persistence on our web servers.” Baseline hunting flips the question entirely: instead of rooting around for specific threats, you map what “normal” looks like in your environment, then investigate anything that doesn’t fit the pattern.
