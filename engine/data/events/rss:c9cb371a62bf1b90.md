Some donors to the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace had their information leaked due to a cyberattack on a third-party vendor named Soapbox Engage. The organization sent emails to past donors on Friday warning of the data breach, writing that the incident occurred on October 28. The threat actors were removed from the vendor’s system on October 30, they said. Carnegie Endowment for International Peace spokesperson Sharmeen Aly confirmed to Recorded Future News that the vendor was Soapbox Engage — an online fundraising and engagement software platform for nonprofits. Carnegie uses Soapbox Engage for online donations. The hackers stole names, email addresses, phone numbers and giving history. “Due to an integration with Carnegie's database, the unauthorized users were able to temporarily gain access to contact information maintained by Carnegie,” Aly said. “Based on our investigation, a small percentage of our database was affected. No full credit card numbers were exposed.” Aly added that the organization notified everyone in their database about the breach due to European data privacy laws. Carnegie hired a cybersecurity firm to confirm the hackers were removed from the system. Aly did not say how many people were impacted by the incident.
