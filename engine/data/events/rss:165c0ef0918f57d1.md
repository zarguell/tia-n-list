A bipartisan group of federal lawmakers on Monday sent a letter focused on security practices to several identity verification companies, citing a September breach that led to scans of more than 150 million people’s drivers’ licenses and other identification documents appearing for sale on the dark web. The breach of IDScan led to the theft of customer data stored in the company’s cloud platform, spurring an FBI probe and identity theft and fraud concerns for the vast number of Canadian and U.S. victims. The six lawmakers behind the letter pressured the identity verification firms to provide detailed answers about how they manage customers’ sensitive data and what they are doing to protect it against increasingly common and devastating hacks enabled by artificial intelligence. They cited not only the risk of fraud and identity theft, but also pointed to the fact that foreign adversaries are increasingly able to “infer Americans’ behavior through commercial data linked to government credentials,” said the letter, signed by Sens.
