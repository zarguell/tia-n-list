The Register reported that TfL had not disclosed how the perpetrators entered its systems and had found no evidence that the compromised data had been used. It noted that holders of Zip Oyster cards, students, apprentices, care leavers, veterans and Londoners aged 60 and over were among those potentially affected, and that TfL would ask all photocard customers to change passwords and security questions and answers. The article compared this with the September 2024 TfL attack, when more than 7 million customers were notified and about 5,000 were contacted because bank details might have been accessed; two teenagers were later charged and authorities linked that attack to Scattered Spider, with no evidence at publication that the incidents were related.
