Right now, people on security and GRC teams are spending their entire workday answering security questionnaires. At some companies it is a full-time role: a GRC analyst or compliance manager who arrives, opens a laptop, and works through these forms until they go home, two or three hundred of them in a year. I’ve heard the stories. The dinner missed, left a kid’s game at halftime, lost the weekend to a form asking whether the company still keeps tape backups. They do it because the questionnaire sits on the path to revenue. If the form stalls, the deal stalls with it. There is a double standard built into the process. The same team buried under those questionnaires also sends them out to the company’s own vendors, collects the answers, and files them as proof those vendors are safe to trust. They know how little the answers are worth when they fill them out, because they have watched how little scrutiny the yeses get under deadline. They rely on them anyway when a vendor sends them back, because for a long time there was nothing better to rely on.
