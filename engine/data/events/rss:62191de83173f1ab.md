This post summarizes the work we presented at the 2026 IEEE 4th Conference on Information Technology and Data Science ( CITDS 2026 ) in Debrecen, Hungary. My name is József Sándor, and I’m a PhD student in the CrySyS Lab. FAME is joint work with Endre Glocker and Roland Nagy. Machine learning (ML) has become a popular tool for detecting malware on IoT devices. These devices have very little CPU, memory, and energy to spare, so detectors need to be lightweight. One such detector is SIMBIoTA-ML , which feeds the TLSH similarity hash of a file into a classifier. It is cheap to compute and detects new malware that resembles what the model saw in training. But like every ML-based detector, SIMBIoTA-ML can be fooled by adversarial examples (AE). In our paper we ask: can we build a systematic, reliable pipeline that produces functional evasive malware against it? The answer is FAME (From Adversarial feature vectors to Malware Evasion). The hard part: the inverse feature-mapping problem Most evasion attacks from the ML literature work on continuous feature vectors. You nudge the numbers until the classifier flips its decision.
