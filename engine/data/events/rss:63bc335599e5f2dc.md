A keylogger is software or hardware that records every keystroke typed on a device, usually without the user knowing. Attackers use keyloggers to steal passwords, card numbers, and private messages, while some employers and parents use them for legitimate monitoring. What Is a Keylogger? (Definition and Meaning) A keylogger is a tool that secretly logs what a person types. The sections below cover what that means in practice, what a keylogger collects, and where you are most likely to run into one. Keylogger Definition in Plain English A keylogger is a program or physical device that monitors the keyboard and records every keypress. Think of it as a hidden stenographer sitting between your fingers and the screen. The word is short for keystroke logger, and the activity itself is called keylogging. Whether a keylogger is harmful depends entirely on who installed it and whether the device owner agreed to it. Keylogger vs Keystroke Logger: Are They the Same? Yes, keylogger and keystroke logger mean the same thing. Keylogger is the common shorthand, and you will also see key logger, keystroke recorder, and keyboard logger used for the same tools.
