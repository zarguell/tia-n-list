A caller offers you a free phone upgrade or an exclusive deal from your mobile provider. A new phone arrives at your home. So far, everything seems to check out. Then comes another call: There’s been a shipping mistake. You received the wrong phone, and the company needs you to send it back. You send it back, but the phone never reaches your provider, leaving you with a large charge or long-term contract to pay. Now police are warning about this scheme after residents lost devices and were left facing the associated payments. How phone return scams works The scam usually starts with a call from a spoofed number. In the US we’re seeing several examples in anonymized Malwarebytes Scam Guard records of the impersonated company being Canadian mobile provider Rogers. The Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre warns: “After collecting personal information required for credit checks, they’ll order a new phone in your name.” Next, a genuine device arrives through a legitimate delivery service. That delivery makes the next part of the story easier to believe. The caller gets back in touch and claims something went wrong. Perhaps the company sent the wrong device, or an extra phone arrived by mistake. To put things right, you need to send it back using the label or shipping QR code they provide. The shipping label is the real trick. Rather than directing the package to the provider’s returns facility, it sends the device to an address chosen by the criminals.
