China-aligned cyberespionage group FamousSparrow has expanded attacks against government organizations across eight Latin American regions using a newly discovered custom backdoor named SparroWocky. ESET researchers said the threat actor has targeted entities in Argentina, Ecuador, Guatemala, Honduras, Panama, Peru, Puerto Rico, and Venezuela. The campaign began focusing heavily on Latin America from July 2025, with the new malware appearing a month later. According to ESET telemetry, about 90% of FamousSparrow’s observed targets from mid-2025 into 2026 were located in Latin America. This is a notable shift for the group, which had previously targeted organizations across multiple regions and sectors. FamousSparrow is believed to have been active since at least 2019. The group was publicly documented in 2021 after exploiting the Microsoft Exchange ProxyLogon vulnerability, tracked as CVE-2021-26855. It initially targeted hotels but later expanded operations against governments, engineering companies, international organizations, trade groups, and law firms. FamousSparrow Hits Latin Governments The attackers appear to be moving away from their older custom implant, SparrowDoor, in favor of the more advanced SparroWocky backdoor. ESET linked the malware to FamousSparrow with high confidence after observing SparrowDoor deploy SparroWocky in early attacks.
