A newly identified advanced persistent threat (APT) group tracked as UAT-11473 was observed exploiting CVE-2026-20127, an authentication-bypass flaw in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Controllers, to deploy a custom malware framework called KeyDrift. CVE-2026-20127 is a max-severity flaw that Cisco patched in February 2026, which allows remote attackers to gain privileged access to Catalyst SD-WAN Controller and Manager and add rogue peers. UAT-8616 has exploited the vulnerability as a zero-day since at least 2023 , and recent research also linked its exploitation to the Chinese Volt Typhoon state-sponsored hacking group. According to Cisco Talos researchers who reported on the activity, the UAT-11473 threat cluster appears to overlap with Volt Typhoon’s "Silent Train" covert access network, but they made no definitive links. In a report Lumen released yesterday, researchers revealed that the Silent Train framework allowed Volt Typhoon to evade detection while conducting zero-day vulnerability attacks. The attacks Cisco researchers observed targeted space and satellite services, defense and aerospace technology, and policy research organizations, using the KeyDrift malware, which is a custom malware framework designed to operate on Cisco SD-WAN controller instances. "Talos assesses with high confidence that the framework is designed to provide long-term persistent access and automated exfiltration of sensitive keying material," explained Cisco Talos .
