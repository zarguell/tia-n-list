Cybersecurity researchers have uncovered a social-engineering operation named Spring Ring that used external Microsoft Teams accounts to impersonate IT help desk staff. Between January and April 2026, the campaign targeted more than 150 employees at least 10 organizations across multiple industries. The attackers did not exploit a flaw in Microsoft Teams . Instead, they abused the trust employees place in workplace communication tools. They created external Teams identities with names such as “IT Help Desk,” “IT Assistance,” and “Support Staff,” then contacted employees through direct chats and voice calls. The operation shows how attackers are shifting beyond traditional phishing emails. Rather than sending a malicious link and waiting for a victim to click it, Spring Ring operators spoke directly with employees and persuaded them to run software or download files. Teams Scammers Hit 150 The attackers used external Microsoft 365 tenants with deceptive .onmicrosoft.com addresses designed to look like internal corporate services. Examples included names containing words such as “internal,” “network,” “infrastructure,” “certified,” and “helpdesk.” After sending a Teams chat request, the threat actors quickly launched an unsolicited audio call. This voice-phishing technique, also known as vishing , gave the attackers a more convincing way to pose as company IT personnel.
