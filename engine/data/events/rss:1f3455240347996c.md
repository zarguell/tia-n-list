On Monday, the Council of the EU sanctioned 30 individuals and two entities for Russian information manipulation and malicious cyber activity. The influence side hits the Social Design Agency, the Moscow political-technology firm behind years of fake-news operations. The cyber side lists NoName057(16), the pro-Russian group that has spent four years knocking European websites offline with DDoS attacks. A sanctions listing won't take down a botnet, and nobody in Brussels expects it to. What it changes is the legal exposure for every European company that might pay, host, employ or do business with anyone on the list. It also puts a propaganda shop and a DDoS crew in the same announcement, which tells defenders how the EU now sees them - two arms of one hybrid campaign. What the Council announced The Council's announcement covers two separate legal regimes: Russia's destabilizing activities regime , created Oct. 8, 2024, adds the Social Design Agency, 24 of its staff and six pro-Kremlin commentators. It now lists 111 individuals and 21 entities. The EU's diplomatic service calls this the largest EU sanctions package targeting foreign information manipulation and interference (FIMI) to date.
