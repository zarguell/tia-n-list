Australian uranium company Peninsula Energy (ASX: PEN; OTCQB: PENMF) has requested a trading halt after a cybersecurity incident hit its uranium mine in the United States. The incident, the company told the Australian Securities Exchange (ASX), occurred on Friday night and impacted the Lance Project in Wyoming, US. “The company is undertaking a preliminary investigation of the incident and an assessment of the timing and impact, which will temporarily affect its operations,” Peninsula Energy said in a Monday filing . According to the filing, the incident resulted in a breach of its “operating system”, while administrative systems in the US and Australia were not affected. It did not specify which technical systems were compromised or whether uranium production had been interrupted. “Peninsula wishes the trading halt to remain in place until the earlier of the commencement of normal trading on 25 November 2026 or when the company releases the announcement regarding the cybersecurity incident,” the company said. The wholly owned Lance Project, located in Crook County, relies on in situ recovery to extract uranium, which is then processed into yellowcake, which can be turned into nuclear fuel. In 2025, the US added uranium to the federal list of critical minerals, and the Lance Project, which is expected to become a key supplier, has likely become an attractive target for hacking groups.
