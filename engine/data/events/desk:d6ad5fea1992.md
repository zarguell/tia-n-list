Kaspersky's second installment analyzes GraphDoor, identified by MD5 14ec33ea808e697870d00c5e434c2952, which opens a PID-derived named pipe and creates a GUID-named directory under C:\ProgramData\Intel_Log before harvesting system information. It documents the scr.ts, pr.ts and kl.ts plug-ins for screenshots, process listing and keylogging, plus a modified Erfrp proxy and Ably channel proxies that tunnel traffic through legitimate cloud messaging. The report notes that the backdoor obtains C2 details from proxy channels rather than hardcoded domains, and that two observed IPs resolve to twnic[.]top and ekmt[.]tw. Kaspersky reports overlaps with FamousSparrow and CopperTurtle staging on victim systems within a one-day window, while stopping short of a definitive attribution.
