Introduction Preinstalled malware on mobile devices is a major threat to user security and privacy. When we buy a new smartphone or tablet, we expect it to be ready to use and secure right out of the box. In some cases, however, devices ship with malware already on them. Such malware can do almost anything: display ads, collect personal data and credentials, track user activity, and more. Preinstalled malware can also turn the infected device into a tool for fraud, such as stealing credit card numbers or other sensitive information, causing financial loss and reputational damage. We’ve previously reported cases where users bought smartphones on marketplaces with malware already preinstalled. Over the past few months, however, several large organizations have encountered malware embedded in the firmware of mobile devices. The specific risks to corporate security depend on how the devices are used and on the company’s line of business. But because the Trojan sits in the device firmware, any app can be compromised, and the potential damage is hard to overstate.
