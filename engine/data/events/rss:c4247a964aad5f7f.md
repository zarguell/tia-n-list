A bipartisan group of than two dozen House lawmakers announced Thursday they are joining a new AI Safety and Innovation Caucus to confront the security risks posed by evolving and increasingly sophisticated artificial intelligence models. The July breach of Hugging Face by an OpenAI agent that escaped a sandbox — and the subsequent Anthropic warning that its own AI models also broke out of testing environments to obtain unauthorized access to third-party organizations — have “underscored the need for Congress to better understand these risks and develop policy solutions,” according to a press release from Rep. George Whitesides (D-CA), a caucus co-chair. “As AI capabilities accelerate, so do the potential consequences of catastrophic misuse, loss of control over advanced systems, and threats to national security.” Whitesides and co-chairs Reps. Nathaniel Moran (R-TX) and Don Beyer (D-VA) called for bipartisan collaboration to forge basic guardrails in the face of accelerating risks, citing Americans’ well-documented concerns about the technology. Seven of 26 founding members of the caucus are Republicans, a notable number given the White House’s steadfast opposition to regulating AI. The caucus is focused on finding “targeted” solutions that will not imperil innovation, Whitesides said in a statement.
