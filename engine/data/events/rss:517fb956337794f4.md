A critical app used by thousands of Colorado residents for state services was taken down by a cyberattack last week. The Governor’s Office of Information Technology (OIT) said the incident was discovered on the evening of November 18 and involved hackers breaching the myColorado app — which Colorado residents use for digital identification, document storage and accessing state government services. OIT shut down the app and began an investigation. The app is still unavailable as of Tuesday afternoon. The state noted that the app has 2.1 million users and the cyberattack likely involved the theft of data tied to nearly 150,000 people. Investigators declined to provide more information about what data may have been stolen by the cybercriminals. “We know how much Coloradans rely on these services, and we understand how this is frustrating and inconvenient. Protecting our users’ data is our first priority, and we will bring systems back online as quickly as we can once security is restored,” said Sarah Tuneberg, executive director of OIT. Colorado urged residents to bring their physical IDs if they are traveling for the Thanksgiving holiday. Those seeking to access state services can still do so through Colorado agency websites. The state previously dealt with major cyberattacks on the Department of Higher Education and ransomware attacks on several smaller municipalities .
