Ransomware attackers are increasingly targeting managers and senior employees rather than focusing only on technical administrators. New research from Zscaler ThreatLabz found that 62% of victims in a ransomware-linked campaign held manager-level positions or higher. The findings show that attackers do not always need to compromise an IT administrator to damage a company. Employees with business authority, access to sensitive documents, financial systems, vendors, customers, and internal communication channels can provide an equally valuable entry point. ThreatLabz tracked 351 victims across 334 organizations during a one-month period. The campaign was linked to a ransomware group known for gaining initial access, stealing corporate data, and encrypting selected systems to increase pressure on victims. More than a dozen organizations had multiple employees compromised, showing that attackers may not stop after gaining access to one account. Instead, they can use a first victim to identify more targets, steal additional data, and move deeper into the corporate environment. Ransomware Targets Senior Management Nearly two-thirds of victims held managerial titles or more senior roles. These employees may not have direct administrator privileges, but they often hold what researchers describe as business privilege.
