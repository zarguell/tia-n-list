A newly disclosed active exploitation of a JFrog Artifactory authentication bypass warns that attackers are minting administrator tokens on vulnerable internet-accessible instances. Tracked as CVE-2026-82329, the flaw can enable unauthenticated network attackers to obtain administrative privileges when Artifactory runs with its default configuration. The vulnerability carries a CVSS v3.1 score of 9.8 and is classified as critical. Its vector, AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:U/C:H/I:H/A:H, indicates that exploitation is remotely reachable, requires low complexity, requires no existing account, and requires no user interaction. Critical JFrog Artifactory Authentication Bypass Exploited Artifactory is a repository manager for software packages, container images, binaries, and build artifacts. Administrative access to such an environment could give an intruder control over repository settings, identities, access tokens, and the artifacts consumed by developers and automated build systems. That makes the incident a significant software supply chain risk , rather than simply an exposed management console issue. watchTowr’s observation that attackers are creating their own admin tokens is particularly concerning. Security teams should treat the compromise of an affected instance as a possible identity and artifact-integrity incident: determine whether new administrative principals or tokens were created, identify their scopes and expiry dates, and establish whether repositories.
