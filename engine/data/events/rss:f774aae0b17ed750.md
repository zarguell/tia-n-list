The European Commission has confirmed falling victim to an AI-assisted cyberattack that disrupted several of its services. Hackers hit the European Union’s main executive body in late October, and the attack has been largely contained. The investigation into the incident continues, Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier said during a press conference . “As a protective and preventive measure, we temporarily took down several applications, and most of them have since then been restored. We are taking any necessary steps based on the findings, as they come,” Regnier said. The attack was detected on October 22 and was confirmed on October 26. The investigation has found no evidence of a data breach affecting the Commission’s staff, Regnier added. According to Politico , the hackers potentially compromised large amounts of data and caused disruptions that lasted over 10 days. Impacted services included the Commission’s HR platform Sysper, medical reimbursement system MyPMO, and translation systems. While Regnier has confirmed that the hackers used an AI tool during the attack, he did not attribute the incident to a known group and said he was not aware of the Commission receiving a ransom demand related to the attack. Regnier has not named the AI tool used in the attack, but the disclosure came roughly two weeks after the European Union’s cybersecurity arm CERT-EU warned of threat actors using the AI-powered penetration-testing framework
