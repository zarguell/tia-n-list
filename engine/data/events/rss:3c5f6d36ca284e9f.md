President Donald Trump’s pick to lead the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency on Thursday vowed to uncover any “gaps” within the organization that have been created by massive personnel and funding cuts. “I'm going to get involved and understand where the gaps are and I will come back to this committee with the gap analysis,” Mike LoSapio, Palantir’s chief information security officer, told the Senate Homeland Security Committee. LoSapio was nominated earlier this month to helm the nation’s cyber defense agency, which has been without a Senate-confirmed chief for almost two years. A previous nominee, Sean Plankey, withdrew from consideration earlier this year after spending months in limbo on Capitol Hill. Nick Andersen has led CISA in an acting capacity since February. The agency has been a target of Trump’s ire over the role it played in debunking the conspiracy theories he spread that the 2020 presidential election was rigged. Since he returned to the Oval Office, CISA has lost one-third of its staff and had its budget slashed by millions. In a brief nomination hearing, LoSapio described CISA as “effectively the cybersecurity version of the 911 system. You pick up the phone, you say you have an emergency, you need help.” “If confirmed, I intend to bring together the community and the national labs to help collaborate and coordinate and to give defenders the tools they need to defend their networks,” including AI tools, he told the panel.
