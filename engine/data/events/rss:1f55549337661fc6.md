Spain’s professional soccer league LaLiga said Monday hackers stole personal information belonging to fans in a cyberattack affecting its digital services and those of several Spanish clubs. The intrusion occurred between November 14 and 16 and targeted a technology platform that provides digital services to fans, LaLiga said in a statemen . The organization has since contained the attack but is still investigating the extent of the breach. LaLiga represents 42 clubs across Spain’s top two professional men’s soccer divisions. In addition to organizing competitions and managing broadcasting rights, it operates several digital platforms and services for clubs and their fans. LaLiga said the incident did not disrupt soccer competitions or affect its other systems. Its security teams identified the source of the unauthorized access, stopped the hacker from stealing more data and blocked access to the platform. The league has not disclosed how the hacker broke in or how many fans were affected. Spain’s National Police is investigating the breach, and LaLiga has notified the country’s data protection regulator. At least five Spanish soccer clubs — CD Eldense , Real Oviedo , Real Zaragoza , Cordoba CF and CD Leganes — have confirmed that some of their fans’ personal information was compromised.
