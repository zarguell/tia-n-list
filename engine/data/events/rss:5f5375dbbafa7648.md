Indian IT security company OpsShield on Monday fell victim to a cyberattack that resulted in malicious versions of cPGuard being served to its customers. OpsShield specializes in server protection and web security software, and cPGuard is its most notable product, designed for Linux web hosting servers. According to the company’s incident notice , the attackers used compromised credentials to access its update distribution infrastructure (CDN) and distribute a malicious script via cPGuard’s update channel. cPGuard versions 5.88.12–5.88.19 contained the malicious code. The supply chain attack was discovered within 12 hours and the fake updates were distributed for roughly 70 minutes. The attackers first targeted the company’s billing and licensing server. They registered a customer account and ordered a free cPGuard trial to study its communication with the licensing system, then started sending crafted requests to the portal. After obtaining command execution on the billing and licensing server, the hackers started collecting data and then uploaded a fake cPGuard update using a stolen access key, the company explains in an updated report . The update was executed as root on the servers that polled OpsShield’s update infrastructure during the compromise window. At the same time, the attackers copied three of the company’s cloud servers.
