The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency has identified approximately 100 companies that own critical infrastructure for talks on hardening their systems because of how vital they are to the nation, CISA acting director Nick Andersen said Tuesday. That figure does not cover everyone CISA wants to speak with, because those companies have so many interdependencies with other firms, he said. And the conversations are going to vary wildly from company to company, he told CyberScoop. “For the initial workstream, we’re probably talking about around 100 companies,” Andersen said after speaking at FedTalks, produced by FedScoop. “We want to be focused on being able to gather what are those lessons learned that are most appropriate coming out of it, what works and what doesn’t, and the problem with each one of those different companies.” To generate the list, CISA went through an intergovernmental Homeland Defense Working Group that identified companies that provide functions deemed most important to the economy, public health and safety, national security and defense critical infrastructure. Then, Andersen said, the agency overlaid five to six years of intelligence about what cyber adversaries appeared to value most, such as where they were pre-positioning or where their research and development budgets were going.
