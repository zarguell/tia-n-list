A data leak happens when sensitive information is exposed to unauthorized parties, usually by accident, rather than through a targeted cyberattack. It can be as simple as a misconfigured database left open online or as damaging as an entire customer list uploaded to the wrong server no hacker required, just a mistake with real consequences. The scale of that risk is only growing. According to IBM’s 2026 Cost of a Data Breach Report, the average breach now costs organizations $4.99 million, a record high driven largely by AI-assisted attacks and slower detection times. For individuals, the fallout looks different but is just as real: leaked emails, passwords, and personal records feed directly into phishing campaigns, account takeovers, and identity theft. This guide breaks down what a data leak is, how it differs from a data breach , why they happen, and, most importantly, how to check whether your information has already been exposed.
