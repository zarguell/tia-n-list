Your managed detection and response (MDR) provider flags suspicious activity, and your analyst spends the next hour finding the account, checking logs, and deciding whether anything needs to happen. By the time the investigation is underway, the alert feels less like help and more like a task assignment. MDR should bring your team capacity and expertise when it needs them. If the service keeps handing the hardest work back to your analysts, these five signs can help you work out where the arrangement is falling short. 1. Every alert arrives with homework If your analysts are still sorting through the same recurring false positives, the extra coverage may be creating more work than it removes. Even an alert worth investigating is only so useful when your team has to gather the basic context before it can make a decision. Take a few recent alerts and look at what your provider had already checked when it contacted you. You should be able to see how it separated routine activity from a credible threat, and what it did to stop familiar noise from coming back. 2. It spots the fire, but cannot see the fuel A suspicious login involving an account with broad access deserves a different response from the same activity on a tightly restricted account. The systems within reach, their importance to the business, and any known weaknesses all help determine what needs attention first.
