Following recent cyber attacks on French blood banks and European supermarkets, U.S. federal agencies are urging critical infrastructure organizations to immediately secure industrial refrigeration systems against remote tampering and temperature manipulation. Key takeaways Cybercriminals are actively targeting unpatched flaws, misconfigurations, and weak or hard-coded passwords in commercial cold-chain and refrigeration controllers globally. Attackers are gaining remote access to manipulate temperature controls and spoil essential products, such as blood and food. CISA, the FBI, and other U.S. agencies are advising organizations to isolate OT systems, secure or remove remote access, patch critical vulnerabilities, and replace end-of-life devices. Critical infrastructure organizations in the healthcare, chemicals, transportation, and food and agriculture sectors must urgently harden their industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems to protect food, medicines, and other essential products against ongoing cyber attacks. That’s the call from the U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) and other U.S. government agencies, following a series of alarming breaches against refrigeration systems in French blood banks and European supermarkets . “Every organization that depends on refrigeration and monitoring systems should assume it could be a target,” CISA Acting Executive Assistant Director for Cybersecurity Chris Butera said in a statement.
