Key Takeaways MITRE ATLAS describes how attackers operate against AI systems, while the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications ranks the risks LLM application developers should fix first. Seven of the ten OWASP risks have a clear ATLAS counterpart, and the other three (LLM07, LLM08 and LLM10) only partly match. Only ATLAS covers attacker and victim actions around the application, attacks on non-LLM models and documented case studies. Only OWASP covers risk prioritization, risks that aren’t attacks and design-level prevention guidance. Neither framework shows whether your own controls stop these attacks, which Picus AI Security Validation tests continuously against guardrails and LLM firewalls. MITRE ATLAS and the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications are the go-to frameworks for assessing the security of AI systems. The two were built from different perspectives and answer slightly different questions, but they also overlap enough that they’re often confused. They work best together for mapping AI-related attacks, building defenses, writing detections, and ultimately, validating an organization’s protection against both proven and theoretical attacks.
