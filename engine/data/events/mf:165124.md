Kiteworks has urged customers worldwide to temporarily shut down their servers after receiving law-enforcement threat intelligence indicating that a cyberattack targeting Kiteworks systems may be imminent. The company said the move is preventive and that it has no evidence of a confirmed compromise. The warning affects organizations using Kiteworks’ secure file-transfer and communications products, which enterprises and public-sector entities widely use to exchange sensitive data. Kiteworks Customers Told to Shut Down Servers In a customer notification attributed to Kiteworks CISO Frank Balonis, the company said it had received “credible threat intelligence from law enforcement” that an attack could occur during the weekend. Kiteworks recommended a coordinated six-hour shutdown window on Saturday, September 26. Customers in Central Europe were advised to take systems offline from 4:00 a.m. to 10:00 a.m. local time, while organizations on the U.S. East Coast were reportedly given a 10:00 p.m. Friday to 4:00 a.m. Saturday window. Administrators were also encouraged to power down systems before the designated period where possible. The company’s support team reportedly described the action as protection against “potential zero-day attacks,” suggesting the threat may involve an unknown vulnerability with no available patch.
