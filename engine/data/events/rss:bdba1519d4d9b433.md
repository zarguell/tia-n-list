Executive Summary SentinelLABS evaluated how frontier models reverse engineer binaries and annotate disassembler databases, finding recurring problems with inaccurate labels and incomplete analysis. To improve performance, we developed and tested a standard for LLM-driven reverse engineering that keeps annotations tied to evidence, verifies database changes, preserves project state, and requires review of the model’s work. We open-sourced VerifiedIDA , a reference implementation that applies the standard to autonomous reverse engineering in IDA Pro. Despite the growing capabilities of new models, our research shows that scaling reverse engineering requires that the analysis they produce remain inspectable, continuable, and reusable by humans, other models, and downstream security workflows. Overview The use of large language models (LLMs) for reverse engineering creates the possibility of examining binaries at a scale and speed exceeding that of manual analysis. It also makes reverse engineering accessible to those without specialized expertise in the discipline. The combination of scale, speed, and accessibility introduces a major shift across multiple domains. In cybersecurity, for example, defenders can analyze larger volumes of malware in less time, while attackers can misuse the same capability to considerably accelerate and scale vulnerability research and exploit development, shortening the time to exploitation and increasing the volume of intrusions.
