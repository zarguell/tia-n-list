Changing your password should be part of recovering a compromised account. The developers of Remus Stealer are advertising a Gmail-reading feature they claim can survive that step and even session revocation. That is a serious claim. It is not, however, a demonstrated capability in the published investigation. Threat intelligence company Hudson Rock examined Remus’s promotional material and questioned both its developer and a self-described customer. Its conclusion is more cautious than the sales pitch: The material depicts a mailbox-reading interface, but it does not establish that Remus can retrieve new messages after an account has been comprehensively secured. Nor does it establish a Gmail zero-day vulnerability. What Remus is advertising According to Hudson Rock, Remus’s version 1.3.9 announcement promotes a feature that lets operators open a compromised Gmail mailbox directly from the service’s dashboard. The supplied screenshots show message lists, individual emails, and a “Mail” option attached to records of compromised devices. The announcement makes several additional claims: Operators can intercept email verification codes without collecting browser cookie files. Password changes and session revocation do not stop access. Mailbox access can be shared with other operators through links. Asked how it works, the developer described interaction with browser memory and said the malware executes and exits.
