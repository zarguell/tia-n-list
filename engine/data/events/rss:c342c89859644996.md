A British hacker tied to the Scattered Spider cybercrime group was sentenced Tuesday to six years in federal prison for his part in a sprawling phishing campaign that stole more than $11.6 million in cryptocurrency from individuals in the United States. Tyler Robert Buchanan, 24, of Dundee, Scotland, was previously described as the suspected ringleader of the hacking collective, which Microsoft called “one of the most dangerous financial criminal groups” around. Buchanan was arrested at Palma Airport in Spain in June 2024, and named among five defendants charged that November with targeting victims including Coinbase , Twilio , Mailchimp and LastPass , as well as with the ransomware attack on casino giant MGM Resorts . That attack left parts of Las Vegas paralyzed for days and cost MGM an estimated $100 million. Buchanan additionally admitted using “the information stolen from company intrusions to identify and gain access to virtual currency accounts and wallets belonging to individual victims to steal millions of dollars’ worth of virtual currency,” stated the U.S. Attorney’s Office, Central District of California. Those thefts “had severe, life-altering consequences for many victims and underscore both the seriousness and far-reaching impact of this conspiracy,” wrote prosecutors in a sentencing memorandum.
