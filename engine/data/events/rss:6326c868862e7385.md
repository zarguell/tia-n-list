Japan’s education ministry disclosed earlier this week that hackers had breached its government procurement system, potentially exposing records containing business information and email addresses. The Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT) said it detected the intrusion on October 1, nearly two months before publicly disclosing the incident. An investigation found that hackers may have obtained information stored in the system’s database. The potentially compromised data includes nearly 1,700 email addresses and 164,000 records containing company names, the names of employees and other business information, the ministry said in a statement Wednesday. The affected system is used to publish information about government tenders, contracts and procurement opportunities. The potentially exposed business information relates to companies eligible to participate in the ministry’s bidding processes. The ministry said it had blocked all communications between the affected system and internal and external networks and found no evidence that the potentially compromised information has been publicly disclosed or misused. The incident was reported to Japan’s data protection authorities, and the ministry is continuing its investigation in coordination with relevant government agencies.
