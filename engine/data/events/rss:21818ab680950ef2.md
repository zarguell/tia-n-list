OpenRouter removed three AI providers from its platform after discovering that they had violated their stated data-handling policies and exposed sensitive data. The company also revoked Zero Data Retention (ZDR) eligibility for 125 endpoints as a precaution and notified all potentially affected customers. OpenRouter is an AI model broker that lets developers access AI models from different companies using a single API key. When an app sends a request, OpenRouter forwards it to a provider that runs the selected model and passes the answer back. Operational technology security company Dragos alerted OpenRouter after sending two million test prompts through the platform, each containing a unique tracking link or decoy credential, and detecting "activity indicating that data from at least three AI providers was exposed." Dragos discovered that, in some cases, those credentials were leaked to outsiders and used in the wild, indicating that the test prompts had escaped. “We observed activity indicating that data from at least three AI providers was exposed. Our seeded test prompts were used in attempts to access our controlled test systems and canary AWS accounts,” Dragos says . OpenRouter says there was no breach on its own systems, making the leak point at the provider level and leading to the decision to remove the three leaking inference providers: Together AI, Phala, and Novita.
