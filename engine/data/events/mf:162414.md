The FBI has opened an investigation into an apparent data breach after a dark web marketplace began selling scans of more than 153 million U.S. and Canadian driver’s licenses. The service, called Nexus, allegedly offered highly detailed identity records that may have originated from a Louisiana-based identity-verification provider Nexus was advertised on the Russian-language cybercrime forum Exploit on August 31, claiming access to identity documents belonging to more than 170 million people across North America. FBI Probes Data Breach Exposing 153 Million Driver’s License Scans Its operators said the collection included over 153 million driver’s licenses, 10 million identification cards, more than three million travel or international identity documents, and at least 579,000 medical cards. An unauthenticated blank search reportedly returned roughly 11.5 million result pages, each displaying about 15 records, figures broadly consistent with the claimed scale. While the data set included Canadian documents, most records appeared to belong to U.S. residents. Searches for Canadian driver’s licenses alone reportedly produced around 1.1 million results, including 473,673 records from Ontario. The marketplace’s listings included more than basic personal details. Some entries reportedly contained front-and-back license images, standard scans, infrared captures, ultraviolet images, photographs, and timestamps associated with the scans.
