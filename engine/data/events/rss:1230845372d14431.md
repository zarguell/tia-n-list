McDonald's Japan, the fast-food giant's Japanese subsidiary, has disclosed a data breach after attackers accessed a database containing sensitive information for users of the company's official smartphone app. Founded in 1940, McDonald's is now a multinational fast-food chain that operates more than 40,000 restaurants in more than 100 countries and serves tens of millions of customers daily. In a Friday statement, the company's Japanese subsidiary confirmed that threat actors gained access to its official app's database and said the exposed information includes the users' product purchase history, IP addresses, mobile device location data, and customer management IDs (which cannot be used to identify the customers' email addresses or member IDs). McDonald's Japan also added that the exposed data doesn't include the affected customers' names, email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses, or credit card information. While it has yet to reveal when it detected the attack or how many individuals had their data stolen, the company said a follow-up investigation found no service disruption or misuse of official apps as a result of the incident. "We are working to invalidate any credentials used for unauthorized access, investigate the relevant systems, and report to the Personal Information Protection Commission," it said .
