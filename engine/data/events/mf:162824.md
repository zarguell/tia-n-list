Switzerland’s federal administration is preparing to test a sovereign, open-source digital workplace designed to reduce long-term dependence on Microsoft 365 for sensitive government operations. The Swiss Federal Council was informed on September 2, 2026, about the results of a feasibility study examining whether browser-based open-source office software could support defined federal workflows . Following the assessment, the Federal Chancellery will begin a follow-up program to build and deploy an independent workplace platform for selected government employees. Switzerland Launches Open-Source Alternative The initiative is not an immediate replacement for Microsoft 365 across the federal government. Instead, Swiss authorities intend to operate the sovereign workplace alongside Microsoft’s cloud productivity suite, initially providing critical employees with an alternative environment during outages, supply chain incidents , geopolitical disruptions, or other crisis scenarios. By the end of 2027, approximately 3,000 federal employees involved in particularly critical business processes are expected to receive access to the new platform. The planned environment will include the core services required for day-to-day government work: email, calendar functions, document and presentation editing, telephony, file storage, collaboration tools, and audio and video conferencing.
