Earlier this year, I showed how to break encrypted USB drives with time-travel debugging ( slides ) at RE//verse . After having a lot of recent success with AI-assisted reverse engineering and vulnerability research, I wanted to see how well AI could handle the same tasks. The results exceeded my expectations. For one encrypted USB drive, the investigation uncovered a backdoor that let me decrypt files with alternative passwords. AI also reproduced my earlier research in seven minutes using static analysis. I tried it on two more USB-protection products I had not studied before, and it broke those too. A copy-protected USB drive The first drive was YutianDun (御天盾), sold by SecZure . It hides encrypted files from Windows Explorer and opens them through bundled readers that claim to block copying, screenshots, and printing. Fun fact: I had studied this drive before. In “Examining USB Copy Protection” in Paged Out! , issue 5 (November 2024) , I showed that a DLL injected into its PDF reader process could read a protected file that another process could not read.
