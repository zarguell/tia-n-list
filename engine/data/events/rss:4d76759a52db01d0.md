U.S. National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross is leaving the White House post, creating upheaval among the Trump administration’s digital security leadership as the country wrestles with recent technology outages. Axios first reported Cairncross’s exit. A White House official confirmed his departure, which reportedly had been pushed by President Donald Trump’s chief of staff Stephen Miller, who argued Cairncross lacked credentials to perform the job. Kirsten Davies, the Pentagon’s chief information officer, has been named the acting cyber czar, which advises the president on cybersecurity matters and coordinates digital policy across the federal government. “Our nation stands in the crossroad of analog, digital, and quantum technologies, our American way of living increasingly interconnected. Our opportunity is to dominate the cyber landscape for generations to come … but only if we modernize and drive resilience into the foundations our great nation runs on,” Davies, who the Senate confirmed for her role about a year ago, wrote on X . Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Secretary of State Marco Rubio congratulated Davies in social media posts on Thursday. The high-profile departure comes amid a series of technology outages, including a nationwide aviation ground stop, a global Microsoft Outlook outage and disruptions to the National Security Agency’s classified communications network.
