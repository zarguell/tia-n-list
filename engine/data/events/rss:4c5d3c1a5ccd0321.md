If someone asked me to name the biggest shortcoming in DFIR, and in cybersecurity, in general, I'd have to say documentation. We miss a lot of value because activity and analysis goes undocumented, and it is lost. Without documentation clearly delineating what was done, and what was observed, we can't identify and address issues that need to be fixed. Without that documentation, capabilities cannot be updated, and new capabilities cannot be added. We end up not being able to operationalize what was observed or learned. Without that documentation, every analyst needs to learn the same lessons, from their own efforts, and cannot benefit from the efforts of others. This is inefficient, and leads to a great deal of corporate knowledge simply be unrealized and lost. I learned lessons about the value of documentation a long time ago. In 1990, during the first Desert Shield, my unit deployed to South Korea for an exercise named "Valiant Blitz". After the exercise, we had an after action review, and one of the radar operators stood up and just flat out said, "Comm sucked." That's it. Being the Communications Officer, and having a senior officer from my own unit stand up and say that, hurt. Particularly because I could not remember a single complaint during the entire exercise.
