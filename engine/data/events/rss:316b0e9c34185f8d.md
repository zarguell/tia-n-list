Water utilities in 26 states have already signed up to receive free cybersecurity support on the heels of the Iran-linked attacks and other tech snafus, a group launched this year said. The Water Watch Center said more than 100 water and wastewater utilities, which all serve populations of 10,000 people or fewer, have expressed an interest in receiving its free cybersecurity monitoring and hygiene support, with many already receiving those services. The center is a joint effort between the National Rural Water Association and DEF CON Franklin, a project of the Cyber Policy Initiative at the University of Chicago Harris School of Public Policy.
