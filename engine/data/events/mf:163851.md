Iranian state-linked hackers are using a Windows malware family called CHOSEN BRICK to spy on dissidents, activists, journalists, and other individuals viewed as threats to the Iranian regime. The malware has been active since at least 2025 and has targeted people in the UK, United States, Netherlands, and other countries. A joint advisory from the UK National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), the FBI , and the Netherlands’ AIVD intelligence service said CHOSEN BRICK can steal emails, social-media messages, contacts, screenshots, audio recordings, and system information. The collected data could help operators track a victim’s communications, movements, and daily activities. The warning comes amid continued concerns that Iran uses cyber operations to identify, monitor, intimidate, and harass critics abroad. In several previous cases, personal information stolen from victims was published on pro-Iranian leak websites, creating additional privacy and personal-safety risks. CHOSEN BRICK Steals Messaging Data The operators begin their attacks through WhatsApp , Telegram, and other social-messaging platforms. They spend time building trust with targets, often posing as someone the victim already knows or as technical-support staff from a messaging service. The attackers then persuade targets to download and open a file that appears legitimate.
