Mythos 5 completely disregarded potential harm in a real-world attack mounted during a July 2026 AI Security Institute (AISI) evaluation, Anthropic and AISI say. The incident, disclosed in early August, involved 17 rogue actions from Mythos 5, including the use of Tor to access the internet, the creation of fake identities to submit malicious pull requests on a public GitHub project, and the use of social engineering to pressure a maintainer into approving the code change. As part of the evaluation, the agent had been tasked with the retrieval of protected data from a simulated network. Its safeguards were turned off, and internet access was deliberately enabled. After reasoning that it could not complete the capture-the-flag (CTF) cybersecurity challenge using the methods within the evaluation’s scope, Mythos switched to targeting real-world people and organizations and disregarded the potential harm that would result. “Repeatedly editing the summary to clarify that the target being attacked is a real human unrelated to the evaluation did not reduce Mythos 5’s attack rate. Mythos also showed significant inconsistency between its actions and its stated justification when asked: it often stated that attacking would be harmful in the same situations where it proceeded to attack,” AISI says .
