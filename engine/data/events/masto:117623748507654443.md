Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN in an APT group's crosshairs. Cybercriminals use the CVE-2026-20127 flaw to take over devices. Researchers from Cisco Talos published a detailed analysis of an APT group tracked as UAT-11473, which exploited the critical flaw rated 10.0 in CVSS v3.0 in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN controllers to distribute the KeyDrift malware. That tooling is used for C2 communication, persistence and exfiltration of data including IPSec tunnel encryption keys. The flaw is in the authentication of peering connections, allowing a crafted request to bypass authentication and log in to a privileged account such as v daemon, which opens NETCONF access to the SD-WAN configuration. The affected components are the Catalyst SD-WAN Controller (formerly vSmart), Manager (formerly vManage) and Validator (formerly vBond). Cisco fixed the flaw in February 2026, there is no workaround, and patching is the only remedy; administrators who deferred the update gave the attackers a short window to break in. KeyDrift consists of the PolyFlame and LittleFilch modules and runs inside SD-WAN system processes, with a loader that locates vdaemon PIDs in /tmp/system-vdaemon, launches a component named rsshd, and injects libdssl[.]so into memory using direct syscalls while checking TracerPid for sandboxes.
