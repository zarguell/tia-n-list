Two people shopping for the same product shouldn’t have to wonder whether one is paying more because a company knows more about them. That’s the concern behind a bill passed by the New York City Council on November 12. The legislation targets surveillance pricing, defined as using personal data and algorithms to offer different people different prices for the same goods or services. Introduction 891-A would prohibit these practices in transactions with consumers in New York City. It would also restrict collecting, selling, or sharing personal data to facilitate surveillance pricing. The bill is at the mayor’s desk and would take effect one year after becoming law. For consumers, the issue goes beyond an unexpectedly expensive shopping cart. It’s about companies turning information about your life and online behavior into a tool for deciding what to charge you. What exactly is surveillance pricing? Surveillance pricing uses information about you, or a profile a company has built about you, to help determine the price you see for a specific product or service.
