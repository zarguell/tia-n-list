Levi Strauss &amp; Co. has disclosed a cybersecurity incident in which an unauthorized third party used social engineering techniques to gain access to files on three company-issued employee computers, resulting in the exfiltration of unspecified corporate information. The apparel company revealed the incident in a Form 8-K filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) on August 7, 2026. Levi Strauss said its preliminary investigation found that attackers accessed and extracted certain corporate data, but emphasized that no consumer information was affected and that business operations continued without interruption. The intrusion began when attackers used social engineering against three employees, enabling unauthorized access to their company-issued endpoints. Levi Strauss did not disclose the specific delivery mechanism, such as phishing emails, voice phishing, fraudulent IT-support requests, or multi-factor authentication fatigue attacks, nor did it identify the threat actor. That distinction is significant: the disclosure attributes the compromise to employee manipulation rather than to a known software vulnerability, an unpatched system, or an exposed application. According to the SEC filing , Social-engineering operations remain effective because they target identity, trust, and routine business workflows the human layer around enterprise security controls.
