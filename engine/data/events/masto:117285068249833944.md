Iranian malware CHOSEN BRICK spies on activists through fake medical exams. Hackers linked to the Iranian state are using a new strain of malware to spy on dissidents, activists, and journalists worldwide. The alert was issued in a joint statement by the UK National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), in collaboration with the FBI and Dutch authorities.

The attack begins with social engineering schemes shared on platforms like Telegram and WhatsApp. Attackers impersonate trusted contacts or technical support agents to trick users into opening malicious files. These files are presented as legitimate applications including Norton Antivirus, Adobe Flash Player, or KeePass, and often appear under the pretext of medical results such as MRI documents. The hackers even suggest targets run the tools on personal devices to bypass corporate security blocks.

After execution, CHOSEN BRICK is silently installed in the background, maintaining persistence through Windows Registry keys. To avoid detection, the program adds security exceptions to Microsoft Defender. Control of the infection is handled through a single Telegram bot associated with the victim's identifier. From that point, the attackers gain full access to the device. The threat can capture the screen, record audio through the microphone, and extract email content as well as WhatsApp and Telegram data. The malicious code downloads additional payloads to the C:\Windows\SysWOW64 folder and can delete files or completely wipe the host system.

Exfiltrated data is sent to attackers through cloud services such as VultrObjects and StorjShare, or via Telegram itself. Recent variants use SOCKS5 proxies to hide network traffic. According to the security agencies' report, stolen data frequently ends up on leak sites associated with pro-Iranian groups, serving as a harassment method that increases physical risk to dissidents abroad. The document notes that Iranian intelligence services have already planned kidnappings and lethal operations internationally against individuals considered enemies of the regime.
