GitLab has released emergency security updates after discovering a critical vulnerability that could allow unauthenticated attackers to remotely modify or delete public projects and user data. The flaw, tracked as CVE-2026-19478, affects GitLab Community Edition and Enterprise Edition installations across multiple supported versions. Administrators operating self-managed GitLab servers are being urged to update immediately, particularly organizations with internet-facing instances , public repositories, or externally accessible GraphQL services. GitLab Critical Vulnerability GitLab Dedicated is already running the patched release, meaning customers using those hosted services do not need to take action. The company issued GitLab versions 19.2.4, 19.1.6, 19.0.8, and 18.11.11 on August 17, 2026. The releases were published as an ad hoc critical patch rather than part of GitLab’s normal twice-monthly security update schedule. CVE-2026-19478 is a code injection issue involving a GraphQL directive. Under certain conditions, the vulnerability could enable a remote, unauthenticated user to alter or delete public projects and user data. The issue received a CVSS severity score of 9.4 out of 10, reflecting its potential for high impact on integrity and availability without requiring credentials, victim interaction, or complex exploitation conditions.
