The Artex agentic AI penetration testing framework has become a common tool in Chinese threat actors’ arsenal, and Barracuda is one of its victims. Published on GitHub in July 2026, Artex relies on a planner to formulate an objective and create tasks based on it, and on agents to perform those tasks, while the platform itself records the progress, findings, and assets. It reportedly used DeepSeek v4.1-flash as the initial model, but can use other LLMs as well, and GLM-5.3 and Grok 4.6 were observed in certain instances. Overall, however, its users mainly rely on Chinese models, which are more permissive compared to US-made ones. Artex was designed for security testing, but threat actors have been using it as part of in-the-wild intrusions since at least August, and its occurrence in real-world attacks has increased significantly during the past several weeks. “The framework enables threat actors with minimal technical expertise to conduct complex, multi-target operations simultaneously, fundamentally accelerating attack velocity and expanding operational scale,” the FBI warns in a new alert (PDF). Mentioning the framework’s use in attacks on South Korean financial firms and ConnectWise , the FBI warns of the risks Artex poses: faster attacks through automation, exploitation of neglected peripheral devices, and the ability to find and exploit supply chain attack vectors.
