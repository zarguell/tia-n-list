Google has launched Gemini 3.8, a family of reasoning and coding models that includes Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber, a specialized system built to help security teams discover software vulnerabilities and create working patches. The release arrives three weeks after Gemini 3.7 Flash and marks Google’s third Flash-tier launch in six weeks. Both Gemini 3.8 Flash and its Cyber counterpart share the same underlying architecture, but Google tuned them for distinct use cases. The general-purpose version focuses on long-horizon software engineering, agentic workflows, tool use, and debugging. Flash Cyber is instead directed toward defensive vulnerability research, validation, remediation, and code development. Google Launches Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber Google said Gemini 3.8 Flash improves software engineering performance while keeping its price at $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output tokens. In DeepSWE v1.1, which measures end-to-end engineering work, Google said that the model outperformed larger frontier systems at a lower cost. It scored 54.9% on HLE-Verified. Gemini 3.8 Flash DeepSWE v1.1 Evaluation (Source: Google) Google attributed the improvements to a willingness to take additional reasoning steps and call tools repeatedly when a task requires investigation. Higher-effort settings can consume more tokens but may yield stronger results for planning, code review, troubleshooting, and multistage execution.
