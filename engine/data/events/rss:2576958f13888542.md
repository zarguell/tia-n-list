The eMudhra CA key compromise is a certificate authority (CA) security incident in which eight intermediate certificates issued by eMudhra, an Indian digital trust provider, carried private keys that anyone can recover from the public certificate. An independent researcher reported the weakness on October 17, 2026, and Chrome and Firefox blocked the intermediates within hours. The keys were P-384 elliptic-curve values below 256, generated during an April 30, 2025 key ceremony. Six of the intermediates issue Transport Layer Security (TLS) certificates and two sign code . Browsers are now protected, but software that relies on its own CA bundle and skips revocation checks may keep trusting the compromised chain. This FAQ covers what happened, who is exposed and which updates close the gap.
