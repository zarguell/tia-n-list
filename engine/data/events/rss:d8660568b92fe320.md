A Syrian man tied to the Syrian Electronic Army ( SEA ) has received a 42-month prison sentence in the US, more than 12 years after he was first charged. Ahmad Umar Agha, who used the online handle Th3Pr0, was sentenced on Thursday by a federal court in the Eastern District of Virginia. According to the Justice Department, he evaded US authorities for more than a decade. Prosecutors say the hacking conspiracy Agha took part in was directed by figures close to Bashar al-Assad, among them the head of Syria’s General Intelligence Directorate. The scheme began as early as August 2011 and continued until January 2024. The DOJ described Agha as a central figure in the operation. He compromised around 47 targets, a mix of Western news outlets, companies, NGOs, and government bodies in the US and allied countries. Among the intrusions highlighted by prosecutors was the 2013 takeover of the Associated Press’s Twitter account. Agha used it to publish a fabricated report claiming that President Barack Obama had been hurt in two blasts at the White House. The fake post triggered a flash crash that briefly wiped $136 billion off the combined value of the Dow Jones Industrial Average’s component stocks. Agha also gained access to the company hosting the Marine Corps’ recruiting website and sent its traffic to an image calling on Marines to defy their orders.
