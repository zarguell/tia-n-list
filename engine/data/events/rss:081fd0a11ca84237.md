The British government served Apple with a secret legal notice targeting the company’s cryptographic protections for iCloud accounts, a tribunal has confirmed, ending almost two years of official refusal to acknowledge what had been widely reported. In a judgment handed down Thursday, the Investigatory Powers Tribunal — the only court in the country that can hear certain national security cases — rejected the government’s “neither confirm nor deny” policy based on the argument that even confirming the existence of the notice would damage national security. The court’s three judges stated explicitly that the government “has given Apple a technical capability notice,” referencing the legal power which the government used to demand that Apple retain the ability to access the content of iCloud accounts. At the core of the dispute is Apple’s Advanced Data Protection (ADP) feature which generates and stores encryption keys on users’ own devices instead of Apple’s servers. Because Apple never holds the keys, it is technically unable to access the content of iCloud accounts even when served with a lawful request. Last February, Apple withdrew ADP for British users entirely after receiving the technical capability notice, as was first reported by The Washington Post. Although the details of the notice are not public, it appears that Apple effectively complied by withdrawing ADP for British users.
