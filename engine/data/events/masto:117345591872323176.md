Researchers from OPSWAT Unit 515 (Khoi Tran and Thai Do) found two vulnerabilities in the popular TP-Link Tapo C200 smart camera. Both allow an attacker on the same local network as the camera to bypass authentication and gain administrator privileges, or to disrupt the device management service. TP-Link confirmed the flaws and published fixes. The researchers also mention a third, potentially critical flaw enabling complete camera takeover, withheld under responsible disclosure until a patch ships.

CVE-2026-15315 (CVSS v4.0 7.1) is an authentication bypass in the camera's local HTTPS management interface on port 443. The challenge-response login implementation failed to properly verify the response to the issued challenge: the camera accepted a value it had generated itself and that the attacker replayed back to it. An attacker on the LAN can therefore establish a valid administrator session without knowing the password, with no user interaction, no existing authenticated session, and no physical access; LAN reachability is the only requirement. CVE-2026-15316 (CVSS v4.0 8.7) is improper input validation in the configuration service that processes encrypted credentials. Deliberately modifying the length of the encrypted payload causes an unhandled exception that crashes or reboots the device, producing denial of service that lasts until restart. The same LAN-access requirement applies.

TP-Link has released fixed firmware; users should update to V5_1.4.6 or later. Recommended hardening includes keeping camera management interfaces off untrusted networks and restricting the admin panel to trusted devices in isolated subnets or VLANs.
