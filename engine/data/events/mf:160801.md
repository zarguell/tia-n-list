Finding — and fixing — vulnerable dependencies with AppVA Building software right now, especially in the era of AI, is easier than it has ever been. You describe what you want, you get back working code, and something that used to take weeks can take an afternoon. I’m not complaining about that. I lean on AI in my own development workflow every day. But easier to build doesn’t mean safer to run. When that code comes back, it doesn’t just come back with your logic in it. It comes back having already made a pile of decisions on your behalf. And the biggest one is which outside code it pulled in to do the work. Quick detour: what a library actually is If you spend your day on the defensive side rather than in an IDE, this part matters for everything below. Nobody writes an entire application from scratch. If you need to talk to a database, or handle a file upload, or parse a date — somebody already solved that in your language of choice and packaged it up so you can just use it. That package is a library. A dependency. Same thing, different words. And that’s good. Reusing working code is the whole reason software moves as fast as it does. Nobody should be writing their own crypto. Here’s the catch. You pull in one package. That package needs three others to do its job. Those three need nine more.
