CVE-2026-123007 is a 64-bit integer underflow in nginx's HTTP/2 connection flow control. The fix is six lines. We reproduced the accounting bug with a few megabytes and the crash with about 60 GiB. The full RCE needs about 128 GB of traffic, and the reason is arithmetic, not bandwidth: a 64-bit window fed by 31-bit increments. We did not run the RCE. The public strategy needs glibc's AVX-512 copy routine, and our test host is a Haswell Xeon. The short version: Vulnerable: nginx 1.9.5 through 1.31.8. Fixed: 1.31.9 and 1.30.7. The bug is in ngx_http_v2_filter_cleanup() , ngx_http_v2_state_window_update() , and ngx_http_v2_send_chain() . A client can make cleanup refund more connection credit than the connection ever had, push the counter past 2^31-1 , and then walk it past 2^63 . At that point the counter becomes a negative off_t , nginx builds a buffer whose end is before its start, and the TLS path passes a huge unsigned length to memcpy . A few megabytes proves the accounting failure. About 60 GiB walks the counter to the signed midpoint and crashes a worker. About 128 GB walks it far enough for the public RCE's bounded out-of-bounds copy. The patch keeps send_window + send_queued , which is the invariant the old check assumed.
