Hidden cameras concealed inside hotel-room objects, such as clocks, chargers, smoke detectors, and adapters, remain a serious privacy concern for travelers and short-term rental guests. Researchers have unveiled SweepLED, a low-cost smartphone-based detection system that uses a compact LED attachment and artificial intelligence to identify the distinctive reflections produced by hidden camera lenses. The system, developed by researchers from KAIST, Singapore Management University, and the National University of Singapore, is designed to make hidden-camera inspections faster and less dependent on specialist equipment. $7 SweepLED Gadget Helps Detect Hidden Cameras Its core hardware components cost less than $7, while a complete scan of a suspicious object takes under five seconds. SweepLED is currently a research prototype, not a commercially available consumer gadget. However, the researchers said its compact form factor could allow the technology to be built into smartphone cases, travel accessories, or hotel-provided privacy inspection tools. SweepLED (Source: Ink.library.smu) Most conventional hidden-camera detectors use a single LED or laser source. Users must slowly scan a room and visually look for bright reflected spots through a detector’s viewfinder. This can create false alarms because shiny plastics, glass, polished metal, mirrors, decorative objects, and recessed surfaces can all reflect light in ways that resemble a camera lens.
