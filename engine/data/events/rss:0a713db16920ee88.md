The European Commission, the European Union's main executive body, has confirmed that attackers breached its EU Academy online learning hub and stole the personal data of an undisclosed number of users. This follows the Commission's Thursday disclosure that hackers breached its systems on October 22 using the ARTEX AI-powered penetration testing framework , causing service disruptions that also affected its internal Sysper HR platform. "As a protective and preventive measure, we temporarily took down several ​applications and most of them have since been restored. The investigation is still ongoing," spokesperson Thomas Regnier told reporters at the time. "We are taking any necessary steps based on the findings as they come. We have not detected any evidence of a data breach affecting Commission staff as a whole." While the Commission said it hasn't received a ransom demand and has yet to attribute the attack, Regnier confirmed in a Monday press briefing that the attackers also breached the EU Academy platform, used to learn more about EU policies and follow EU online courses. Regnier also confirmed a data leak following the EU Academy incident, which is still under investigation and has affected users' personal information, including their names and email addresses. "And as a precautionary measure, given that the fact that there is a data leak that is confirmed including names and email addresses,today there was a message to all users on the EU academy website," he added.
