West Virginia’s state legislature is responding to a cyberattack that has taken down its website and impacted IT systems. Ann Ali, deputy chief of staff for the House of Delegates, told Recorded Future News that the attack started on Thursday and has continued through Friday. “Upon discovery, the relevant teams immediately took steps to contain the incident and began an investigation with the help of outside cybersecurity specialists,” Ali said. “In connection with the investigation, access to certain Legislature IT systems has been temporarily disabled. However, based on what we know so far, the incident appears to be limited to the Legislature’s computer systems.” She added that no other state agencies or offices are impacted by the cyberattack. An investigation into the incident is ongoing but Ali did not respond to follow-up questions about whether it was a ransomware attack. No hacking groups have taken credit as of Friday. The West Virginia legislature is split between two bodies, a Senate and a House of Delegates with 134 total members representing the state’s 1.7 million residents. Multiple municipalities in the state have dealt with ransomware attacks in recent years, including the cities of Beckley and Westmoreland . Security research firm Comparitech tracked 132 ransomware attacks on government bodies across the U.S. in the third quarter — a 91% increase compared to Q3 last year.
