Frontier AI models (like Mythos) are rapidly mining decades of technical debt across ubiquitous legacy libraries, driving monthly Microsoft Patch Tuesday vulnerability advisories up more than tenfold (reaching 1,415 this month). This volume is overwhelming open-source maintainers and putting pressure on the traditional 90-day coordinated disclosure model. Despite a 105% year-over-year surge in CVEs, Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEVs) grew by only 37%, and only 3 of September's 975 Microsoft advisories saw in-the-wild exploitation, demonstrating that the vast majority of AI-discovered bugs are not practically exploited. We may be able to take new-bug-class vulnerability spikes as evidence that these vulnerabilities will eventually level off and decline, once the historical code backlog is remediated and AI verification is embedded directly into new development pipelines. With attackers able to parse patches faster via AI, defenders cannot cover for all possible threats. Organizations should continue to build robust defense-in-depth. Prioritize high-exploitability flaws in their specific environments, implement interim mitigations (such as LiveProtect), deploy behavioral anomaly detection, and integrate current AI evaluation models into their software build chains.
