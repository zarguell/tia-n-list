A threat actor has leaked online a database containing the personal and work information of thousands of US Department of Homeland Security employees. The hacker, who uses the handle Department of Peace, has leaked more than 41,000 records. “They have our information, so it’s only fair that we have theirs,” the hacker wrote in a note. According to the hacker, the data was stolen from an Okta single sign-on system used by DHS employees and contractors to access its central environment. “We are aware of this report. Okta is secure, and we have no evidence our data or systems were compromised,” an Okta spokesperson told SecurityWeek . Most of the leaked records reportedly contain information on US Customs and Border Protection (CBP), Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), and Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) employees. Roughly 1,000 CISA employees, along with personnel at several other DHS agencies, had their information leaked as well. The compromised data includes names, work email addresses and phone numbers, job titles, and office locations, as well as records showing which systems and internal applications each account can access. An online tool to search the data is also available. “DHS is aware of a potential breach and is actively working to address the situation swiftly and effectively.
