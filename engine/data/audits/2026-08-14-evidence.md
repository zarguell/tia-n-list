The day's defining story is the [RingCentral data breach](stories/ringcentral-data-breach-exposed-info-of-1-6-million-accounts/): Have I Been Pwned now puts the July intrusion at 1.6 million accounts, confirming ShinyHunters' claim that it stole 623GB from the cloud communications provider and leaked a 280GB archive after RingCentral refused to pay the ransom, so businesses on its calling, messaging, and voicemail platform should treat breach-themed phishing as likely and watch for unauthorized account activity. The [Beacon CRM data breach](stories/beacon-crm-data-breach-exposes-entire-customer-database-after-aw/) exposed the entire customer database of a platform used by more than 1,000 UK charities after a leaked AWS access key sitting in public JavaScript build artifacts gave the attacker access to data and attachments, a supply-chain class breach for the non-profit sector, so assume donor personal data is out and scan repositories and build artifacts for committed credentials.

Microsoft's August [Patch Tuesday release](stories/microsoft-s-august-2026-patch-tuesday-addresses-398-cves-cve-202/) covers roughly 400 CVEs, led by the exploited AFD.sys WinSock elevation-of-privilege flaw CVE-2026-68820 that CISA added to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Aug 11, and follow-on coverage this week adds the Exchange Server branch, six flaws spanning remote code execution, privilege escalation, denial of service, spoofing, and a security-feature bypass disclosed Aug 11 with CVE-2026-62913 updated the next day, plus the Windows 10 KB5120249 and Windows 11 KB5121003 cumulative updates and a CVSS 8.8 Outlook RCE in CVE-2026-70329 reachable via crafted Office files. The same release finally carries official patches for [LegacyHive](stories/windows-legacyhive-zero-day-privilege-escalation-on-fully-patche/), the Windows User Profile Service privilege escalation disclosed in mid-July by Nightmare Eclipse: a non-admin user can modify the classes registry hive for code execution at the next administrator login on fully patched builds, so verify the fix is deployed and hunt the published Defender for Endpoint queries.

The [Trezor breach at ShipMonk](stories/14-000-trezor-customers-impacted-by-data-breach-at-shipmonk/) exposed order-related personal data for roughly 13,689 customers, 11,742 of them with full name, email, phone, and shipping address, after ShipMonk notified Trezor of unauthorized access to its systems on Aug 10; Trezor's own Aug 13 [disclosure](stories/trezor-discloses-data-breach-affecting-nearly-14-000-customers/) puts the affected window at orders shipped May 10 through Aug 8 across the US, UK, Sweden, Colombia, Brazil, Italy, and Portugal. Private keys, wallet backups, and holdings were never at risk, so the exposure is downstream fraud, and Trezor customers should treat unsolicited contact referencing a real order as hostile by default. Apple sent a new batch of high-confidence [Threat Notifications](stories/apple-sends-new-threat-notification-alerts-over-mercenary-spywar/) on Aug 13 to iPhone users in 110 countries, warning of individual targeting by mercenary spyware without naming the family, so recipients should treat the alert as active-monitoring risk and consider lockdown mode.

Jamf Threat Labs uncovered [AmnesiaStealer](stories/amnesiastealer-macos-malware-steals-data-controls-browser-sessio/), a macOS infostealer delivered through a ClickFix campaign fronted by a counterfeit GitHub portal at github.aoitour[.]com that asks victims to paste a Terminal command and enter their password; the payload steals credentials and browser data and silently takes control of live Chromium sessions, so never paste commands into Terminal from a web page and alert on browser-initiated Terminal execution. Attackers began exploiting an [unpatched GeoServer zero-day](stories/hackers-exploiting-unpatched-geoserver-zero-day/) within hours of its disclosure, an SQL injection in the jsonArrayContains filter usable with PostGIS and Oracle JDBC data stores that can reach remote code execution, with no CVE assigned and no patch available, so identify and quarantine exposed instances now.

Fortinet's August patch wave fixes an [unauthenticated FortiWeb login bypass](stories/fortinet-fortiweb-and-forticlient-flaws-let-unauthenticated-atta/) (CVE-2026-26035, a CWE-287 improper-authentication flaw that lets a remote attacker log in to the GUI or CLI with a random username and password when the wildcard admin setting is enabled) and arbitrary code execution in FortiClient for Windows (CVE-2026-70465), while the [CCCS advisory AV26-812](stories/fortinet-security-advisory-av26-812/) adds CVE-2026-70468, an authentication bypass that lets a remote attacker impersonate any FortiGate managed by FortiManager; no exploitation is reported yet, but disable the FortiWeb wildcard setting and patch FortiManager and FortiClient as the priority. The [vCenter campaign](stories/hackers-exploit-critical-vmware-vcenter-flaw-to-deploy-reverse-s/) exploiting CVE-2026-59310, a CVSS 9.8 directory traversal with no workaround, keeps expanding as more than half of the 361 compromised IPs across 47 countries sit in Germany, the US, Turkey, Iran, and France, with an assessed APT operator planting reverse_ssh backdoors and a generic YARA rule published for the tool.

President Trump signed a national security presidential memorandum that lets vetted private US companies run [offensive cyber operations](stories/trump-turns-to-private-sector-in-offensive-hacking-operations-me/) against transnational criminal organizations, with DHS and DOJ program executive directors granting authorizations and 60 days to draft the company-vetting standards; critics warn the arrangement legitimizes hack-back, so the rules of engagement and liability for cyber effects are the near-term question. The [Fairlife ransomware attack](stories/when-patching-isn-t-enough-what-the-fairlife-ransomware-attack-s/) became the case study in why patching alone is not a complete defense: the Anubis group entered the Coca-Cola-owned dairy through CVE-2025-5777, the CitrixBleed 2 flaw already on CISA's KEV, encrypted Nutanix systems, stole roughly 1 TB of data, and forced a production suspension across US facilities. ----- dysphoria-hijacks-routers-gateways-and-ip-cameras-to-build-massi.md (em_dashes=0) -----
A Shadowserver Special Report put the Dysphoria botnet at approximately 296,000 compromised devices, classifying every listed infection as critical and offering operators retrospective data for remediation. Dysphoria targets internet-facing embedded Linux gear: routers, gateways, IP cameras, and DVRs. QiAnXin XLab separately tracked it to about 200,000 devices globally and linked the malware to the JackSkid and FBot families, with the first sightings on March 25, 2026 and rapid variant churn since.

The botnet's evolution matters more than the raw count. Earlier variants were DDoS-capable; newer ones convert infected hosts into relay infrastructure that carries attacker-controlled traffic, and researchers have observed a relay-only variant that drops DDoS functionality entirely and turns devices into proxy nodes. That lets operators launder traffic through residential and small-business broadband connections, hide origin, bypass IP-based restrictions, and run automated abuse. Each endpoint is simultaneously an attack participant and a traffic source.

C2 design makes Dysphoria unusually hard to disrupt. It resolves command infrastructure through Ethereum ENS and Solana SNS domains, with C2 addresses concealed inside fake IPv6 strings recovered by a custom byte-transformation algorithm, so takedown cannot rely on traditional DNS sinkholing. Hunters should watch for anomalous ENS and SNS DNS queries from endpoints with no reason to resolve blockchain domains, and for outbound traffic on non-standard ports. No CVEs are attached; the exposure is configuration, not a patchable flaw.


----- malicious-svg-reconstructs-dcrat-archive-entirely-inside-victim.md (em_dashes=0) -----
Trellix Advanced Research Center, working from a customer escalation, detailed a DCRat delivery chain that builds the malware load entirely inside the victim's browser. The lure is a fake Colombian judicial notice named "Resolución Denuncia Jurídica" sent as a phishing email with an SVG attachment. The SVG carries double Base64-encoded JavaScript that reconstructs a password-protected archive, DOC-16-ENE-2026 RESOLUCION DENUNCIA JURIDICA.7z, in memory via a Blob object and steers the victim to a fake judicial portal. That is HTML smuggling: the malicious payload never crosses email filters as an executable.

The archive holds a decoy executable, supporting DLLs, and the hidden malware chain. The loader borrows Brotli naming, libbrotlidec.dll and libbrotlienc.dll, so the DLLs sit next to the decoy and Windows loads the attacker-controlled versions when it runs, a DLL sideloading trick that drops DCRat inside a legitimate Windows process. Process hollowing and trusted-process masquerading keep the remote access trojan blended into normal endpoint activity.

The campaign is worth watching because the delivery method, SVG plus browser-side archive reconstruction, sidesteps attachment scanning and needs no exploit. DCRat is a commodity RAT with broad post-exploitation reach, so any organization with users exposed to legal-themed Spanish-language phishing should expect follow-on activity. No CVE is involved and no in-the-wild victim counts were published; detection should focus on 7z creation from browser processes and unexpected DLL loads alongside Brotli-named files.


----- ringcentral-data-breach-exposed-info-of-1-6-million-accounts.md (em_dashes=0) -----
ShinyHunters stole personal information from 1.6 million RingCentral accounts, according to Have I Been Pwned, after breaching the cloud communications company in July. RingCentral disclosed the incident on July 28, attributing the compromise to what it described as a sophisticated social engineering campaign. The company said it has seen no new unauthorized activity since taking remediation efforts, that the incident did not impact the core RingCentral platform, and that customers not contacted by RingCentral are not affected. RingCentral has not publicly attributed the breach to a specific actor, but ShinyHunters claimed responsibility on July 27, saying it had stolen 623GB of data, and after the company refused to pay a ransom the group leaked a compressed archive of 280GB on its dark web leak site.

RingCentral is a cloud collaboration and communication platform used by over 600,000 businesses for calling, messaging, and voicemail, so the breach touches a large customer base even though the company downplays scope. Personal data for 1.6 million accounts now sits behind a public leak, and ShinyHunters is a prolific extortion group that routinely monetizes stolen corporate data. The disclosure on July 28 came one day after the group's claim, and the company has yet to share further technical detail on the intrusion.

Watch for confirmation of exactly what data types are in the leaked archive, any credential material that enables account takeover, and downstream phishing or extortion targeting RingCentral customers. Businesses using RingCentral should treat breach-themed phishing as likely, watch for unauthorized account activity, and reset credentials if their data appears in the dump.
