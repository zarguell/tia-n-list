## Recommendations

1. **Codify re-report/no-delta demotion in `score.py` (fourth repeat, now the
   single largest auditor time sink).** 544 of 657 overrides are demotes and the
   dominant reason is "new events restate covered facts". `score_breakdown`
   already counts `rereports` and `novelty` but neither penalizes a story whose
   only new events are outlet re-reports of the same fact set. A zero-delta
   penalty on the hourly score would stop these reaching the digest slate at all.
2. **Make duplicate detection entity-based, not just title-similarity.** The
   check that would have caught all six leftovers: same victim/actor entity +
   ≥2 shared distinctive title tokens + first_seen within ~10 days + no distinct
   CVE sets. Title jaccard alone misses OpenCode (0.25), Ahold (0.25) and
   NightmareStresser (0.57 but only after token normalization). Cheap to add to
   `merge.py` and it would have flagged 4 of today's 6 hand-found pairs before
   the digest linked them.
3. **Give score-0 duplicates a path to merge automatically.** Trezor (3),
   BambooToken (3) and NightmareStresser (2) are all 0.0 and were flagged by the
   probe on consecutive days without ever being merged, because the human budget
   always goes to the visible pairs. An unattended rule for jaccard ≥ 0.5 with
   zero analysis file and zero digest references would drain this backlog
   without spending the daily merge budget.
4. **Finish the title lint job.** Yesterday's recommendation worked: raw-toot and
   aggregator titles fell from 207 live stories to 27, the worst now scoring 0.1,
   and the Chrome 153 story's truncated-toot title has been replaced
   ("Google security advisory (AV26-1045)"). The remaining 27 are all ≤0.1 and
   aged out of the hot view — worth a final pass, not urgent.
5. **Stale-story lifecycle (unchanged from 2026-10-19).** A story can still be
   demoted by consecutive digests without ever being corrected (the Chrome 153
   counts case). A "demoted 2+ digests with no new fact" signal should surface
   such stories to the desk for a rewrite or a supersede-merge.
