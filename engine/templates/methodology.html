Methodology

How we research, score, and report

A plain explanation of how Tia N. List works. The process is deterministic where it can be, analyst-written where it needs judgment, and transparent about both.

How we research

Continuous ingestion. Every hour we poll the publication feeds on our source list — 48 outlets, published as an OPML you can import yourself — plus the r/cybersecurity hot feed and the Mastodon/X accounts we follow. Each article is ingested exactly once (a seen-list of feed IDs tracks what we already have), and non-English articles are detected and translated.

Stories, not articles. New articles are merged into stories by matching their URL, the CVE they name, or their title against what we already cover. A story is one incident: the original reporting, then updates from other outlets as they land.

The timeline is the story. The original article appears in full. Each update shows only what is new — sentences that add information the story did not already have. Reports that merely restate known facts collapse to a bare mention (source, date, link).

Analysis where it matters. Every story that crosses into hot gets a written desk review: what the story is, why it matters, what to watch. When reporting that predates our coverage turns up later — the vendor advisory, the researcher's disclosure, early exploitation telemetry — it is added to the story's timeline at its original date, labeled as backfill, so the history reads honestly instead of starting at the moment we noticed. The daily brief at 07:15 EDT summarizes the day's landscape.

How we score

Six factors, one formula. Every story is scored 0–10 from explicit factors, and the story page shows the breakdown (open "Why this score"):

Severity (0–5, the dominant axis): the real CVSS base score of the CVE(s) involved, plus KEV/active-exploitation status, content signals (zero-day, ransomware, OT/ICS, APT, breach scale), and the EPSS exploitation probability from FIRST's daily model (a CVE the model expects to be exploited any day now outranks its CVSS alone). Breadth (0–2): how many distinct outlets cover the story — deliberately capped low, because repetition is weak evidence of significance. Authority (0–2): the weight of the best outlet covering it. Velocity (0–2): updates in the last 48 hours. Pickup speed (0–0.6): how quickly the second source arrived after the first. Recency: a temporal multiplier that decays with a 36-hour half-life. Community (+0–1.5): reddit presence.

The formula: score = (breadth + authority + severity + velocity + pickup) × recency + community, capped at 10.0. Bands: 0–3.2 new, 3.3–4.9 warm, 5.0–7.4 hot, 7.5+ hottest. The hot ranking re-computes every hour, so a story cools off naturally as it ages — and a story is hot because it is significant, not just because it is new and covered by several outlets.

How we report

Five feeds. Every article, hot stories (score ≥ 5.0), the daily brief, new KEV catalog entries, and the KEV candidate list — all under feeds.

The daily brief, published each morning after the 06:30 ET run: a headline, a one-to-two-sentence executive summary, the analyst's landscape analysis, and the stories it covers — each linking to its full timeline.

Corrections and limits. Story clustering is deterministic and can over- or under-merge at the edges; scores are a ranking signal, not a verdict. When we change methodology, this page changes; the archive never does.

How we cover remediation

The KEV catalog, enriched. Every entry in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog gets a page of its own: proof-of-concept availability, exploitation preconditions, the SSVC assessment, the BOD 26-04 timeline for an internet-facing and an internal-only asset, the EPSS score with a 21-day trend, and hunting hypotheses. CISA's own remediation due date is a countdown on the deadline board, and the catalog can be narrowed to the vendor/product pairs you actually run ("my stack" — stored in your browser only, never on our side).

Vendor patch releases. For vendors whose disclosure cadence is predictable — Microsoft, Adobe, Oracle, Cisco, Red Hat, SUSE, Siemens — we normalize each release into one filterable table: CVSS, EPSS, KEV status, exploitation signals, the BOD class, and a link to the advisory for that specific CVE. Any view exports to CSV with every field, including the ones the table does not show.

Candidates before the catalog. A CVE that credible sources report as exploited, but CISA has not listed yet, appears under candidates — the early-warning set, with the same enrichment.

How we cover detection

Cases, not feeds. Each researched story becomes a CTI case: actors, malware, campaigns, CVEs, victim sectors and ATT&CK techniques, joined into the coverage matrix. Where we can, the case also ships detection content — Sigma rules with derived Splunk and KQL variants, and YARA rules — all behind a publish gate that fails the build on a rule that will not convert or parse. Every case carries a curated IOC set, and the whole state is pinned daily as immutable STIX snapshots, so a past claim can always be re-checked against what we actually published that day.
