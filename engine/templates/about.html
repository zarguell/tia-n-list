About

Why I built Tia N. List

Tia N. List — threat intel analyst — started as a simple idea: turn the flood of cybersecurity news into something actionable. This is the story of how it got here.

The problem

Too much news, too little signal. Every day there are new vulnerabilities, breaches, and exploits being reported on, and staying on top of them is difficult and time-consuming. The reporting rarely tells you what matters — that part is on you.

Where it started

A one-shot prompt. The system began as a simple n8n workflow that collected articles into a single “summarize this into a daily digest” prompt. Back then I was running GLM 4.6, and I had to cap the digest at 40 articles to stay within reasonable context limits for a one-shot prompt.

Agents, then a real harness. As it grew, I realized one-shot prompting wouldn’t cut it, so I moved to a Hermes agent that collects articles and sifts through content over multiple turns to produce the daily digest. More recently I switched to oh-my-pi as the agentic harness, with OpenHands automations as the scheduling layer — and the pipeline went from a once-a-day digest to a continuous system.

Today

Open-weight models, cheap to run. The backend model changes over time, but I usually use open-weight models. Right now the whole system runs on the OpenCode Go coding subscription with deepseek-v4-flash — it hardly makes a dent in my subscription usage.

A digest I actually read. I’m a cybersecurity practitioner, and I read these digests every day. That’s the whole point: a tool that helps me stay on top of a threat landscape that grows faster than any one person can follow.

Feedback welcome. I’m always open to feedback — reach out on GitHub with any thoughts you have.
