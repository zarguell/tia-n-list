About

Why I built Tia N. List

Tia N. List (or threat intel analyst) began as my idea to turn the mass amount of cybersecurity news reporting into something actionable.

Every day, there's tons of vulnerabilities, breaches, exploits, being reported on, and staying on top of them is difficult and time consuming.

The system started off as a simple n8n workflow that collected a bunch of articles into a single "summarize this into a daily digest" prompt.

As it grew, I realized that a one-shot prompt wouldn't cut it, so I transitioned to using Hermes agent, collecting articles and sifting through content through multiple turns to produce a daily digest.

Since then it has become a continuous system: an agent harness (pi) driven by a small Python cron runner (cronman) that owns the schedule, the locks, the secrets and the git commits, with a static site generator rendering whatever the data says on every push.

The backend model changes constantly — that is the least interesting part of the system and the quickest to go stale, so I do not pin a model name here. I use open-weight models on a flat-rate coding subscription, which is what makes an hourly agent loop affordable. When I started, I was using GLM 4.6 and had to cap the n8n digest at 40 articles to keep a one-shot prompt inside its context window; the current harness runs many small, tool-using turns instead of one giant prompt.

As a cybersecurity practitioner, I actually read these digests every day and find it useful to stay on top of the quickly growing threats. I'm open to feedback to please reach out on GitHub with any thoughts you may have!
