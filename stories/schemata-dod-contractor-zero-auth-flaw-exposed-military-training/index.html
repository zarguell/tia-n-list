    Schemata DoD contractor zero-auth flaw exposed military training data and service member base deployments for 150 days

    gbhackers.com · 97d ago · May 6th, 2026

    A critical authorization vulnerability in Schemata, an AI-powered virtual training platform backed by Andreessen Horowitz with active DoD contracts, allowed unprivileged accounts to access sensitive military training materials and personnel records across all tenant boundaries. Security researcher Alex Shapiro, using the open-source AI hacking agent Strix, discovered that the platform's API lacked any meaningful organizational scoping or permission checks — returning the entire platform's data regardless of the requesting user's tenant. Exposed data included the complete user directory with full names, emails, and enrollment data, as well as the specific military base deployments of active U.S. service members, metadata and direct AWS S3 links for confidential naval maintenance training materials, and restricted Army field manuals on explosive ordnance deployment. Write-enabled routes lacked authorization checks entirely, meaning attackers could have manipulated or deleted critical training infrastructure. Despite being contacted on December 2, 2025, Schemata did not patch until May 1, 2026 — 150 days later — after initially dismissing the report as a bug bounty solicitation.
