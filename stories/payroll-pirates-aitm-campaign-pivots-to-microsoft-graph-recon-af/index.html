    Payroll Pirates AiTM Campaign Pivots to Microsoft Graph Recon After Session Theft

    gbhackers.com · 1d ago · August 10th, 2026

    The Storm-2755-linked "Payroll Pirates" campaign documented by Arctic Wolf now shows a full post-compromise playbook: 11–24 hours after an adversary-in-the-middle phishing login, operators refresh stolen Microsoft 365 sessions every ~8 hours from rotating residential proxies, then enumerate tenant users tied to payroll, finance, HR, invoices, and banking via Microsoft Graph — activity Defender flags as "Suspicious Exchange Online Graph Reconnaissance." Mailbox collection across tenants occurred in tightly synchronized bursts, with malicious MailItemsAccessed events landing inside the same 26-second window, and the actors avoided MFA changes, password resets, and inbox-rule creation to stay under the radar. Initial access rides voicemail-themed lures that chain legitimate Google Meet redirectors, Google Ads trackers, Campaign Manager, and AWS S3-hosted content into AiTM proxy pages. Hunt for SessionID reuse, anomalous Outlook user agents, and residential-IP sign-ins on finance-adjacent accounts.

    Payroll Pirates Abuse Microsoft Graph to Find HR and Finance Staff After Account Compromise

    gbhackers.com · 1d ago · August 10th, 2026

    A widespread phishing operation that compromises Microsoft 365 accounts through adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) infrastructure, then uses Microsoft Graph to identify employees handling payroll, finance, HR, benefits, invoices, and banking workflows. The activity closely overlaps with Microsoft’s “Payroll Pirates” cluster, tracked as Storm-2755. Researchers also found similarities with activity previously documented by Security Risk Advisors, indicating that the operation’s targeting may be broader than initially reported. Initial access begins with voicemail-themed phishing emails masquerading as automated call notifications. The messages use Microsoft branding, fabricated caller details, and an “OPEN [Organization] VOICEMAIL PORTAL” lure to create urgency. Clicking the link sends victims through a layered redirect chain that abuses legitimate Google Meet redirectors, Google advertising infrastructure, Campaign Manager click trackers, and Amazon S3-hosted content before landing on attacker-controlled AiTM phishing domains . The phishing kit proxies the legitimate Microsoft authentication process in real time rather than presenting a static credential-harvesting page.
