    Remus infostealer evolves from Lumma — bypasses App-Bound Encryption with 51-byte shellcode, uses EtherHiding for C2

    cyberpress.org · 97d ago · May 6th, 2026

    Gen Digital researchers have documented Remus, a 64-bit information-stealing malware representing the next evolution of the Lumma Stealer lineage. Following the public doxxing of alleged Lumma core members in late 2025, the malware was first seen as "Tenzor" test builds in September 2025 before entering active circulation as Remus in February 2026. The most significant capability is its bypass of Chromium Application-Bound Encryption: Remus injects a custom 51-byte shellcode directly into the browser process memory to hunt for the protected v20_master_key, enabling extraction of stored passwords, cookies, and autofill data. Remus also replaces Lumma's traditional dead-drop resolvers with EtherHiding — using Ethereum smart contracts hosted on the blockchain to resolve C2 server URLs, making infrastructure practically immune to standard takedown requests. The malware incorporates rigorous anti-analysis checks, scanning for sandbox and honeypot indicators, with silent self-termination if detected. IOCs include four C2 IP addresses on the 217.156.122[.]x range and 45.151.106.110.
